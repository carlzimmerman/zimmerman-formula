## 1. Lensing in the nonlocal theory

Gravitational lensing is determined by the **spatial** metric perturbation
`Ψ(r)` (the `ij` components) for null geodesics. In the weak-field,
slow-motion limit,
```
ds² = -(1+2Φ_N/c²)c²dt² + (1-2Ψ/c²)δ_{ij}dx^i dx^j .
```
The deflection angle for a light ray passing a mass `M` with impact parameter
`b` is
```
α = (4/c²)(Φ_N' + Ψ') b  (integrated)  ->  α = 4GM/(c² b)  in GR (Φ_N = Ψ).
```

## 2. The key lensing question: is `Ψ = Φ_N`?

In GR, the `ij` and `00` components give `Ψ = Φ_N` (equal potentials), so the
deflection is `4GM/(c²b)` (the observed value). In many modified gravity
theories, `Ψ ≠ Φ_N`, changing the deflection.

**DERIVED (from the field equation structure):** The nonlocal M-term
`E_{μν}` contributes to **both** the `00` and `ij` components. Whether
`Ψ = Φ_N` depends on the **trace** and **anisotropic stress** of `E_{μν}`.

- The M-term is `-(a₀²/c⁴) M[g] g_{μν}`-like at leading order (it multiplies
  the metric in the action as a scalar). This tends to give `Ψ ≈ Φ_N`
  (conformal coupling), **but** the full `E_{μν}` has non-trivial
  anisotropic stress from the transport structure (Phase II).

**UNKNOWN:** The exact relation `Ψ/Φ_N` in this theory is **not determined**
without the full `E_{μν}` (which requires resolving the T-gap and the ghost).
The lensing prediction is therefore **UNKNOWN** in the frozen candidate.

## 3. The ghost effect on lensing

The ghost `b` (Phase VI) is sourced by `R_{uu}` and back-reacts on the metric,
including the spatial components. The ghost therefore **corrupts the lensing
signal**: the deflection angle receives a ghost contribution that grows with
distance (the ghost is massless/IR). Lensing measurements would therefore see a
**systematic ghost contamination** that is not present in GR or in healthy MOND
theories.

lensing signal. The candidate does **not** give a clean, testable lensing
