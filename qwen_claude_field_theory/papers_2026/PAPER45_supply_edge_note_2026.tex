\begin{abstract}
\noindent We study the law $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_\Lambda}$ ($\kappa=\tfrac12$, fitted) with the kernel $\nu(y)=1/(1-e^{-\sqrt y})$ and a cold fluid of fixed cosmic amount. In particle-mesh simulations, letting the law act wherever matter has collapsed makes small-scale structure too clumpy: the excess in the matter power spectrum at $k\le1\,h$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ is $+15.3\%$/$+19.3\%$ on the two footings at $256^3$ and $+17.4\%$ at $512^3$, against an allowed $10\%$. We test one rule with no tuned radius. The law's extra (phantom) mass is the cold fluid itself, so a halo can build the phantom only until its own cold fluid is used up; for an undepleted halo this happens at $r_{\rm edge}=r_M/\ln[1/(1-f_b)]\approx5.85\,r_M$. The cold fluid used is drawn from the same halo's turnaround sphere, so mass is conserved halo by halo. With this rule the excess is $2.7\%$ at $256^3$ and $3.3\%$ at $512^3$, with $\sigma_8$ within $0.6\%$ of a control run without the law (the cold fluid evolved as ordinary cold matter, i.e.\ $\Lambda$CDM-equivalent growth). It holds in three random realisations, on both footings, with $a_0$ tracking dark energy, and when the two settings inherited from earlier work are varied. Turning the mass conservation off restores the excess ($15.4\%$). Earlier rules that set the edge by hand, or from measured baryon censuses, sit at the $10\%$ limit at $512^3$. The rule adds no tuned radius or width; its framework inputs are $\kappa$ and the cosmic baryon fraction, and it inherits standard ingredients (a $\Lambda$CDM initial power spectrum, a spherical-collapse turnaround density, a halo finder and the pass thresholds). The cold fluid is handled as bookkeeping, not as simulated particles, and $\kappa$, the cold fluid's amount and $\rho_\Lambda$ remain undetermined.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
The acceleration scale is $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_\Lambda}$ with $\kappa=\tfrac12$ fitted. We report two footings, $9.3603\times10^{-11}$ and $1.1312\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, separately. The phantom density is $\rho_{\rm ph}=\nabla\!\cdot[(\nu-1)g_b]/4\pi G$. A cold fluid with the cosmic amount $\Omega_c/\Omega_b=5.364$ is assumed; no particle species is specified and the amount is an input. In the working model the phantom is not extra mass from nowhere: it is cold fluid that has settled into the profile the law requires.

\section{The problem}
Particle-mesh runs: box $200\,h^{-1}$\,Mpc, initial redshift 49, 150 steps, the same initial conditions as a control run without the law, in which the cold fluid is evolved as ordinary cold matter (so the control has $\Lambda$CDM-equivalent growth). Pass means $|\sigma_8-1|\le5\%$ and $\max|P-1|\le10\%$ for $k\le1\,h$\,Mpc$^{-1}$, both relative to the control.

\section{The rule}
For a point mass with this kernel the enclosed phantom is $M_{\rm ph}(<r)=M_b/(e^{s}-1)$ with $s=r_M/r$ and $r_M=\sqrt{GM_b/a_0}$. If the phantom is the halo's own cold fluid, it can grow only until it equals that cold mass. For a halo that has kept its cosmic share of baryons this gives
\[ r_{\rm edge}=\frac{r_M}{\ln[1/(1-f_b)]}=5.85\,r_M ,\]
and the general form with retained fraction $f_{\rm ret}$ is $r_M/\ln(1+f_{\rm ret}\,f_b/(1-f_b))$ (equal to $5.850\,r_M$ for $f_{\rm ret}=1$). Version 1.0 used measured retention ($f_{\rm ret}=0.07$--$0.10$), which places the edge at $0.24$--$0.54$ of the turnaround radius for KiDS lenses and $0.24$--$0.33$ for the simulated groups and clusters. The simulation does not remove baryons, so the self-consistent choice there is $f_{\rm ret}=1$.

In the simulation, inside each halo's edge the excess of the phantom over the local cold density is added as a source. The same amount is then removed from the cold fluid inside that halo's turnaround sphere (in proportion to where the cold fluid is), so the added source sums to zero on every catchment. Nothing in this rule is tuned: there is no confinement radius and no catchment width. Two settings are inherited from earlier lanes: the width of the collapse switch and a gas-phase filter on the baryon source. We vary both below.

Every run of the rule passes, 15 of 15. The excess changes from $2.7\%$ to $3.3\%$ between $256^3$ and $512^3$, a much smaller rise than the earlier rules showed; with two resolutions and one $512^3$ realisation, convergence is not established. No catchment is ever asked for more cold fluid than it holds. The largest share of a catchment's cold fluid used is 30\% at $256^3$ and 57\% at $512^3$; that share rises with resolution and is the quantity to watch at higher resolution. The control shows that the mass conservation, not the edge alone, removes the excess.

\section{What this does and does not show}
It shows that within this framework, structure growth can be kept within 10\% of $\Lambda$CDM-equivalent growth by a rule that follows from treating the phantom as conserved cold fluid, with no radius or width chosen to fit. Its framework inputs are $\kappa$ (fitted to rotation curves) and $f_b$; it also inherits a $\Lambda$CDM initial spectrum, a spherical-collapse turnaround density, a halo finder and the pass thresholds.

It does not show the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item The cold fluid is bookkeeping in these runs. Its settling into the phantom profile is imposed, not derived from an action.
\item One random realisation was run at $512^3$ (a second is running); the mesh does not resolve galaxy scales, and the share of cold fluid used (57\% at $512^3$) could reach 100\% at higher resolution.
\item The pass criteria compare with a control that has $\Lambda$CDM-equivalent growth, not with survey data. A pass means the law does not spoil standard growth by more than 10\%.
\item $\kappa$, the cold fluid's amount and $\rho_\Lambda$ are not derived, and no dark-matter particle is identified.
\end{itemize}
