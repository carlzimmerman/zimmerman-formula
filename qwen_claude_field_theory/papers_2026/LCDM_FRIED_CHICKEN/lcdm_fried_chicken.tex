\begin{abstract}
\textbf{This is a research experiment. The author does not believe the $\Lambda$CDM recipes, halos or particle readings below.} It works out how a $\Lambda$CDM group could reverse-engineer the law, so that the route is on record first. Nothing here is a result of the author's framework or feeds it. ``Fried chicken'' is a nickname and carries no physics. This paper tunes the knobs of $\Lambda$CDM galaxy modelling, supernova feedback first, until halos reproduce the law $g_N=g^2/(g+a_0)$, with $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}=9.36\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ and $\kappa=\tfrac12$ fitted, on the measured baryons of 171 SPARC galaxies. It is easy to get close. Twelve feedback knobs, as one global recipe and with no knob set by a galaxy's own curve, land the fitted acceleration within 0.3\,\% of $a_0$ and sit 0.0415\,dex from the law, 0.0445 on galaxies held out of the tuning. Against the observed rotation curves that recipe scores 0.1953\,dex, and the law 0.1951. Adding two concentration knobs gives 0.0275\,dex and 0.1933. Six knobs per galaxy, inside 2$\sigma$ priors, reach 0.0152\,dex and pass the 0.02\,dex target frozen in advance, though at least half the galaxies then sit at the low bound on concentration. The law's tie to dark energy did not come from the knobs. It took five terms written in by hand, at extreme values, and $\kappa$ still narrowly missed. A halo can also be written to equal the law exactly, including the tie, by defining it from the formula. That passes every target by construction, and it needs a coupling to dark energy that $\Lambda$CDM does not contain. On KiDS-1000 lensing, the law, the recipes and that halo all fail. Version 1.2 tries for a complete $\Lambda$CDM recipe, using the law as its basis, against a frozen scoreboard. One global recipe with 26 knobs and hand terms is not complete. Every line can be passed somewhere only by a patchwork of about 1066 parameters, which is not one theory. It also fits X-COP and KiDS better than the plain law does. These recipes were tuned to the law, so a match means freedom, not explanation. This is not evidence for the law or against $\Lambda$CDM, and every knob is disclosed.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is being imitated}
The target is the law's curve on each galaxy's own baryons, not the observed rotation curve. The law is not new in form: the scale goes back to Milgrom \cite{milgrom83,milgrom99}, and this interpolating form has appeared before \cite{limbach08}. Only the coefficient looks new, and it is fitted. The law carries its own failures, including IC~1613 and X-COP, listed in PAPER67 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23883741). A perfect imitation inherits them.

SPARC \cite{lelli16} supplies the baryons, with $\Upsilon_{\rm disk}=0.5$, $\Upsilon_{\rm bul}=0.7$ and no per-galaxy adjustment, giving 3373 points. Four of the 175 galaxies have too few usable points. Against the observed curves on these points, the law's rms is 0.1951\,dex.

\section{The recipe: twelve supernova knobs}
Galaxy simulations are run with subgrid feedback models that carry many tunable parameters. We can't run hydrodynamic simulations here, so R11 is an analytic stand-in, and the mapping from knob to halo is ours, not any simulation's parameter file.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stellar mass outcome, 4 knobs.} The stellar-to-halo relation \cite{moster13}: normalisation, $\log M_1$, $\beta$, $\gamma$.
\item \textbf{Feedback energy, 4 knobs.} $e=\epsilon\,(M_*/0.01M_{200})^{a_1}\,(\Sigma_b/100)^{a_2}\,(f_{\rm gas}/0.5)^{a_4}$, and the core exponent is $n=\tanh e$ \cite{read16}.
\item \textbf{Core size, 2 knobs.} $\eta=\eta_0(M_*/10^9)^{a_3}$, core radius $\eta\,R_{1/2}$.
\item \textbf{Contraction or expansion, 2 knobs.} $f=f_0+f_1\log_{10}(M_b/10^{9.5})$ \cite{blumenthal86}.
\end{itemize}
Halos are Einasto with $\alpha=0.16$ and $c(M)$ from \cite{diemer19}. The tuned values are $\log$ norm $-0.319$, $\log M_1$ shift $+0.705$, $\beta$ shift $-0.884$, $\gamma$ shift $-0.600$, $\log\epsilon=-0.639$, $a_1=-0.760$, $a_2=+0.091$, $a_4=-0.097$, $\log\eta_0=+1.000$, $a_3=+0.466$, $f_0=+1.000$, $f_1=-0.335$. Three sit at the bounds of the ranges we chose: $\gamma$, $\eta_0$ and $f_0$. Halo masses stay near abundance matching, with $\log M_{200}$ shifted by $-0.23$, $+0.11$, $+0.30$ at the 16th, 50th and 84th percentiles.

The recipe gives $a=9.331\times10^{-11}$. Tuned on half the galaxies, it scores 0.0405\,dex there and 0.0445 on the other half, so it transfers. With $c(M)$ norm and slope also free (R11b, 14 knobs), it reaches 0.0275, and 0.0326 on unseen galaxies, with $M_{200}$ up by a median of 0.39\,dex and both $c$ knobs at their bounds.

\section{Targets, frozen before any number}
E1: pooled rms to the law at most 0.02\,dex. E2: 95th percentile of per-galaxy rms at most 0.04\,dex. E3: no half-dex $g_{\rm bar}$ bin off by more than 0.02\,dex. E4: $d\ln a/d\ln\rho_{\rm DE}=0.50\pm0.05$ at fixed $\omega_m$, $\omega_b$, $A_s$. E5: $a(z)/a(0)$ inside the law's band at $z=1$, 1.5 and 2.33. Most recipes here were added after first numbers, at the owner's request, and are marked Amendments A1 to A3 in \texttt{FROZEN\_RECIPE.md}. The targets never moved. The knob ranges and priors are our judgement.

\textbf{The level comes cheaply.} Four knobs bring the fitted acceleration from $15.76$ to $9.12\times10^{-11}$, within 3\,\% of $a_0$, with the concentration knob at its bound. Getting the right number from tuned halos is easy, so the number alone proves little.

\textbf{The shape does not.} Halos set by mass alone can't follow galaxies of the same mass and different surface density. Coupling $c$ and $M_{200}$ to $M_b/R_d^2$ helps, to 0.0408\,dex, with the abundance-matching normalisation at its bound. It still misses all three targets. Measured against the observed curves, R7 has rms 0.1964\,dex, R11 0.1953, and R11b 0.1933, against the law's 0.1951. None was tuned to those curves. With these fixed mass-to-light ratios, this statistic barely tells them apart, so a good fit to rotation curves alone cannot pick the law over a tuned halo.

\textbf{The thermal halo misses.} An isothermal halo in the baryon potential looked like the natural imitator \cite{kaplinghat16}. With two global laws it fails at 0.2245\,dex, from either starting point, and with 342 free parameters it still reaches only 0.0609. Its per-galaxy fits do land near $\sigma^4=GM_ba_0/4$, median ratio 1.017, with wide scatter (0.568 to 3.174).

\section{The exact dish, and its bill}
\textbf{R9} is the closest match on $\Lambda$CDM parts inside stated priors: per galaxy $M_{200}$, $c$, Einasto $\alpha$, contraction fraction, core exponent and core radius. With 2$\sigma$ priors (0.22\,dex in $c$, 0.6\,dex in $M_{200}$) it passes E1 to E3 at 0.0152\,dex. The bill: median $\log c$ shift $-0.22$, at the bound, and median $\alpha$ 0.1, also at the bound. Scatter can't push half the galaxies to the same edge, so this is really a population shift in $c$. With 1$\sigma$ priors it fails, at 0.0376. NFW \cite{nfw}, Blumenthal contraction \cite{blumenthal86}, a coreNFW core \cite{read16}. Each galaxy gets its own $M_{200}$, $c$, contraction fraction and core exponent. R8 passes E1 to E3. The bill, relative to abundance matching \cite{moster13} and $c(M)$ \cite{diemer19}: $\log M_{200}$ shifts by median $+0.29$ and $+1.2$ at the 84th percentile, and $\log c$ by median $-0.42$, reaching $-0.81$ at the 16th. Contraction is near full (median 0.9). Under priors of 0.11\,dex in $c$ and 0.3\,dex in $M_{200}$, R8p is 0.0396 and fails E1 and E2.

\textbf{R10} writes those six knobs as quadratic functions of $M_b$, surface density and gas fraction: 42 coefficients, one recipe. It stops just short at 0.0213\,dex, and 0.0270 on unseen galaxies, but with median $\log M_{200}$ shift $+0.46$, $\log c$ shift $-0.74$, and 0 of 171 galaxies inside 1$\sigma$ of both relations.

\textbf{R6} is one line, the published empirical form \cite{mcgaugh16}, with $g_\dagger=9.585\times10^{-11}$, or 1.024 $a_0$. It passes E1 to E3 with one number, but writes the law's content as a halo response with no process behind it.

\textbf{R5} is the law renamed: $M_{\rm DM}(r)=r^2(g_{\rm law}-g_{\rm bar})/G$. We checked whether it could exist at all, as a spherical halo with baryons smoothed by 0.1\,dex. Density is positive in 171 of 171, the isotropic Jeans dispersion is positive in 171 of 171, and the Eddington distribution function is non-negative to 1\,\% of its peak in 171 of 171. The same Eddington code returns $+0.0000$ for Hernquist and $-0.2555$ for a cored tracer in a point-mass potential, so it can fail. Stability is therefore no argument against the imitation.

Its origin is the weak point. Undoing Blumenthal contraction gives a monotonic initial profile in 158 of 171 galaxies. An NFW fit to that initial profile has median rms 0.057\,dex and 0.261 at the 84th percentile, with median $c$ at 0.32 of the $c(M)$ value. Against abundance matching, the implied $\log M_{200}$ has median $+0.16$, but $-0.76$ at $\log M_b$ 11 to 12.

\section{The dark energy tie}
This is the part the knobs don't supply. On R10 with no hand term, the slope with $\rho_{\rm DE}$ is $+0.283$, $\kappa$ runs 0.459, 0.614, 0.735 across $(\Omega_\Lambda,H_0)=(0.70,67.4)$, $(0.685,73)$, $(0.65,73)$, and $a(z)/a(0)$ is 1.58, 2.12, 3.44 at $z=1$, 1.5, 2.33. Five terms written in by hand change that. $c\propto\rho_{\rm DE}^{3.00}H_0^{-1.61}$ and $M_{200}\propto H_0^{-12}$ give slope $+0.496$, which passes, and $\kappa$ of 0.485, 0.506, 0.525, a narrow miss of the 5\,\% target. Two of those exponents sit at the bounds we allowed, and no process produces them. $c\propto(1+z)^{-0.26-0.89z}$ gives 0.93, 0.91, 0.92, inside the band, with baryons held fixed in redshift, which no real galaxy does. So the tie can be imitated by hand, almost, and then it is the law's physics written into the halos. R5 and R6 pass E4 and E5 only if $a_0$ is written into them directly. The thermal halo R3, with the same scale written in, gives $+0.810$ and fails.

\section{The halo exactly like the formula}
\textbf{R12} is the reverse-engineered dish: one function for every galaxy, added after the owner asked for it and frozen as Amendment A6.
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm DM}(r)=\frac{r^2}{G}\left[g_{\rm law}(g_{\rm bar};a)-g_{\rm bar}\right],\qquad a=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}},\quad\kappa=\tfrac12\ \mbox{by hand}.
\end{equation}
In $\Lambda$CDM's language this is a halo of collisionless dark matter particles, with the particle left unspecified. Only the mass profile enters, so the model says nothing about the particle's mass or identity. R5's Eddington check is what allows that reading: a spherical, isotropic, collisionless equilibrium exists for this profile inside the measured radii. Particles appear here only as the rival's language. The framework this paper imitates has none.

It passes everything by construction. Its rms to the law is 0.0000\,dex, its rms to the observed curves is 0.1951, $\kappa$ is 0.500 at all three cosmologies, the slope with $\rho_{\rm DE}$ is $+0.500$, and $a(z)/a(0)$ is 1.000 for constant $\rho_{\rm DE}$, inside the band. None of that is evidence. The formula went in, so the formula comes out.

The bill has three items. First, a particle halo has no term through which it could know $\rho_{\rm DE}$, so the tie needs a new coupling that is not one of $\Lambda$CDM's knobs. Second, no formation path is supplied, and the origin problem from R5 stands. Third, implied $\log M_{200}$ sits a median $+0.11$\,dex from abundance matching (16th to 84th percentile $-0.23$ to $+0.27$), but $-0.80$ at $\log M_b$ 11 to 13. Baryon fractions are not a problem: $M_b/M_{200}$ stays below the cosmic 0.156 in all 171.

\section{If the halo were a particle}
The author does not hold this reading; it is the rival's language, worked out to show what a reverse-engineered particle would need.

This is an effective reading in the rival's language. The author does not hold that the dark sector is a particle, and these results don't show that one exists. A static scalar with $y^2/(a_0-y)=g_{\rm bar}$ reproduces the law to $3.9\times10^{-13}$ on all 3373 points, in spherical symmetry, so $y<a_0$ always. This is not new: it is the scalar function $\mu_s=\tilde\mu/(1-\tilde\mu)$ of \cite{zhao06} for the simple $\tilde\mu$ of \cite{famaey05}, since $s=y/a_0=\tilde\mu$. If instead a real particle halo supplies all of $g_{\rm law}-g_{\rm bar}$, no force acts on baryons and double counting goes away. That halo can't bind to the Sun beyond 0.076\,AU, so Cassini passes, and wide binaries should be Newtonian, unlike a field reading of the law.

A boson that condenses in all of SPARC and not in clusters at 100\,kpc lands between 9.2 and 13.7\,eV on our thresholds. But $\log P$ at fixed $\log\rho$ scatters by 0.521\,dex across galaxies, against a limit of 0.1, so no single equation of state holds these halos up, which undercuts a simple condensate. A collisionless route from an NFW start passes the mixing theorem in 139 of 171 galaxies, all failures below $\log M_b=10$; a frozen control there failed because our expectation was wrong, and is disclosed. The halos need a median 0.516 of all supernova energy at full coupling. Tracking baryons by scattering would need $\sigma/m$ of 0.63 to 40\,cm$^2$\,g$^{-1}$. A fermion needs a mass above about 56\,eV for a typical galaxy and 245\,eV for the most demanding. No part of this derives $\rho_{\rm DE}$ in the halo.

\section{Tying the field to dark energy}
Amendment A12 writes the link as one action with one scale, $\rho_{\rm DE}$:
\begin{equation}
L=-\rho_{\rm DE}\left[1+\frac{\kappa^2}{4\pi}H(s)\right]-\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{8\pi}}\frac{\phi\,\rho_b}{M_{\rm Pl}},\qquad s=\frac{|\nabla\phi|}{\sqrt{\rho_{\rm DE}}},\quad H'(s)=\frac{s^2}{1-s}.
\end{equation}
It gives $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$, with coupling $\beta=0.0997$ and $c=0.0199$. It reproduces the law on 3373 points to 0.00004\,dex. The field's gradient energy is capped at $\rho_{\rm DE}$, and the field's own energy stays below it, 0.0579\,$\rho_{\rm DE}$ at SPARC's maximum and 0.328 at 1\,AU.

This is a posit, not a derivation. $\kappa$ stays fitted. An earlier lane in the repository, UW1, found that this function leaves the vacuum constant free and turns complex for timelike gradients, so the action has no cosmology yet. Baryons source $\phi$ and so feel it, which conflicts with the particle reading unless their coupling is about 0.2 of the particle's, an order-of-magnitude figure. The field is also not the mass: a boson at $m=\rho_{\rm DE}^{1/4}$ with a potential of that height misses the dark matter density by about 49 decades.

\textbf{Prior art.} The capped function $H$ was first written by the author's own programme on 22 October 2026, as the free function of an AeST embedding (lane C1), and is part of the v4 theory in PAPER59 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23585319). The v3 form there, $J=\Lambda-2a_0^2[y-\ln(1+y)]$, already carries $\Lambda$ and $a_0$ in one function. Version 1.3 of this note omitted that citation. Outside the programme, a limited literature search found no exact match for this action with $\kappa=\tfrac12$, which is not proof that none exists. Close neighbours: Milgrom's $2\pi a_0\approx c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/3}$ \cite{milgrom99}, equivalent to $\kappa\approx0.46$; the scale $g_\Lambda=c^2\sqrt{\Lambda}=8.4\,a_0$, where a gravitational field's energy density reaches the dark energy density \cite{banik22}, which is the same idea as the cap here, with $\beta$ absorbing the numerical factor; a Skordis--Z\l{}o\'snik extension with $J\propto Y^{3/2}/\sqrt{V_{\rm de}}$, which ties the MOND scale to the dark energy potential \cite{tian23}; and unified dark sector models \cite{zhao07,blanchet08}. The law itself is the simple interpolating function.

\section{Can $\Lambda$CDM supply the tie itself?}
Amendment A13 asks whether any acceleration scale already in $\Lambda$CDM gives $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$ with no hand term. Gates frozen first: $\kappa_{\rm eff}$ within 0.05\,dex of $\tfrac12$, slope $0.50\pm0.05$ with $\rho_{\rm DE}$ over three cosmologies, inside the law's redshift band, one constant over $\log M$ 10 to 15, and acting on galaxy dynamics. No route passes. Milgrom's coincidence $c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/3}/2\pi$ matches the number, $\kappa_{\rm eff}=0.461$, slope exactly $+0.500$, flat in $z$, but $\Lambda$'s pull at 10\,kpc is $1.1\times10^{-5}\,a_0$, so it acts on nothing in a galaxy; that outcome was written down before the run. $cH/2\pi$ gives $\kappa_{\rm eff}=0.551$ but rises 3.432 times by $z=2.33$. The NFW central acceleration $2\pi G\rho_s r_s$ is 0.830\,$a_0$ at $10^{12}\,M_\odot$, but spans $-0.46$ to $+0.48$\,dex over mass and scales with cosmology at slopes $+1.381$ and $+3.561$. The Tully--Fisher scale at the cosmic baryon fraction is 0.202\,$a_0$ and spreads 0.505\,dex. So a rival can quote $\Lambda$ for the value, but no $\Lambda$CDM process here carries it into halos.

Amendment A14 tries the next step a rival would take, $\Lambda$CDM plus one named ingredient, on the same gates. None passes. Feedback as encoded in the stellar-to-halo mass relation, $V_{\max}^4/GM_*$ with gas left out, gives 1.715\,$a_0$ at $10^{12}\,M_\odot$ but spreads 0.830\,dex over mass and rises 3.200 times by $z=2.33$. A halo edge set by $\Lambda$, at the zero-gravity radius, is flat in $z$ but only 0.001\,$a_0$ and grows as $M^{1/3}$. Coupled quintessence \cite{amendola00} rescales $G$ for dark matter and carries no acceleration scale. A posited product of halo gravity and $\Lambda$'s pull at the scale radius gives 0.005\,$a_0$, and no $\Lambda$CDM term makes it. Knobs that carry $\rho_{\rm DE}$ remain hand terms by definition.

\textbf{Correction and the strongest swoop (Amendment A15).} Version 1.6 understated the halo route: A13 judged the NFW central acceleration over $\log M$ 10 to 15, wider than disc hosts. Over 11 to 12.5 it spreads only 0.089\,dex, the argument of Navarro et al.\ \cite{navarro17}; this was found in an unfrozen check, then graded. Per SPARC galaxy through the stellar-to-halo relation it gives a median 0.612\,$a_0$, spread 0.156\,dex, $\kappa_{\rm eff}=0.306$, and rises 1.493 times by $z=2.33$, so it does not supply the tie. But with one global factor, 1.404, the law with each galaxy's own halo scale sits 0.0586\,dex from the law and 0.1941\,dex from observed curves, against the law's 0.1951. One knob on $\Lambda$CDM halos ties the law on today's rotation curves; what it lacks is the dark energy tie and a flat $a_0(z)$. On observable gates alone, since one universe has one $\rho_{\rm DE}$, Milgrom's coincidence cannot be told from the tie; only a mechanism separates them. A self-interacting core with one cross-section for all galaxies \cite{kaplinghat14} fails, 0.121 to 0.126\,dex from the law. Hydrodynamic simulations, fuzzy dark matter and interacting vacuum are not graded here.

\section{The other dark matter models}
Amendments A16 to A20 ask whether any other $\Lambda$CDM family, or any extra knob block, reaches the law exactly: rms to the law at most 0.02\,dex, 95th percentile per galaxy at most 0.04, worst bin at most 0.02, and the same on unseen galaxies at most 0.025. None does, and none ties $a$ to $\rho_{\rm DE}$.

A16 adds four knob blocks to the 14-knob recipe and keeps what helps. The best set, 15 knobs, reaches 0.0263\,dex; with widened bounds 0.0252, at a median halo mass 0.64\,dex above the stellar-to-halo relation. On unseen galaxies it gives 0.0267, and the per-galaxy 95th percentile, 0.053, blocks it. This is the closest single recipe so far.

A17, wave dark matter, a soliton on an NFW halo \cite{schive14}: the boson mass alone gives 0.1494\,dex, six knobs 0.0921. The fits improve by switching the soliton off, present in 0 of 171 galaxies at six knobs, and the soliton's acceleration scale grows with halo mass.

A18 uses the hydro-calibrated halo responses as published: Di Cintio et al.\ \cite{dicintio14}, coreNFW \cite{read16}, and the FIRE-2 form of Lazar et al.\ \cite{lazar20}. They sit 0.116 to 0.177\,dex from the law; with three knobs each, 0.073 to 0.084, wanting lighter halos. These are formulas fitted to simulations, not simulation outputs.

A19, self-interacting dark matter with a velocity-dependent cross-section, gives 0.122\,dex; with three halo knobs added, 0.0797. Fitting $M_{200}$ and $c$ per galaxy at $\sigma/m=0.5\,{\rm cm^2/g}$, in the style of Ren et al.\ \cite{ren19}, gives 0.0487 with 343 parameters, 77 at a bound, and 0.110 on unseen galaxies at prior centre. Warm and mixed warm plus self-interacting dark matter give 0.116 to 0.120. A coding error in the first control was fixed; the wrong output is kept.

A20, interacting vacuum, $Q=\xi H\rho$ \cite{wang16}, is the only route that puts $\rho_{\rm DE}$ into the halos. Near $\xi=-0.55$ it gives $\kappa_{\rm eff}$ 0.47 to 0.54, but the halo scale drifts with redshift, spreads 0.153\,dex over galaxies, and put into the law it sits 0.0576\,dex away. A20 also asks whether the law is only a reading of measurement error on ordinary halos. Mocks with SPARC's errors match the total scatter, a weak test since the errors drive it, but a fitted $a_0$ comes out $+0.228$ and $+0.415$\,dex high, without and with contraction.

So the closest a rival gets is 0.0252\,dex with 15 knobs and stretched halos, and the only tie to $\rho_{\rm DE}$ comes from a coupling that drifts in $z$. Coefficients for the wave and warm dark matter rows are from memory and were not checked against the papers. Real hydrodynamic simulation outputs are not graded here.

\section{Simulations and the near-MOND dark sectors}
Amendments A21 to A24 grade four further routes on the same gates. None gives the tie natively.

A21 asks whether the law simply emerges in $\Lambda$CDM hydrodynamic simulations, using published results. No measured simulation scale reaches the law: the best is Magneticum, 0.0290\,dex, then MassiveBlack-II faint galaxies 0.0306 and ZOMG 0.0359; EAGLE's raw fits sit 0.17 to 0.19 away. Only round order-of-magnitude figures of $10^{-10}\,{\rm m\,s^{-2}}$ pass. The EAGLE runs with varied $\Lambda$ report no acceleration relation, so the slope with $\rho_{\rm DE}$ cannot be tested from any simulation on record, and where redshift numbers exist the scale rises into the past, 1.305 to 1.428 times in ZOMG. These values are read from the papers' summaries and are provisional.

A22, superfluid dark matter \cite{berezhiani15}: the deep phonon force added to baryons, $g_b+\sqrt{a\,g_b}$, sits 0.0392\,dex from the law, 0.0378 on unseen galaxies; at the published fiducial values with the condensate's gravity 0.0810; with four knobs 0.0389; with 346 parameters 0.0370. Setting its energy scale by $\Lambda^4=\rho_{\rm DE}$ gives $\kappa_{\rm eff}$ 5.014; reaching 0.5 needs its coupling set by hand, so $\kappa$ moves into that coupling, and nothing in the model pins the scale to $\rho_{\rm DE}$. The matching rule was changed after a first test matched none of 171 galaxies; this is disclosed.

A23, dipolar dark matter \cite{blanchet08}: the paper's cubic function gives the same $g_b+\sqrt{a\,g_b}$, 0.0392. Its free function can, however, take the law's shape exactly: the law is one such choice, and a fourth-order expansion with three knobs reaches 0.0036, 0.0033 on unseen galaxies. That is a fit of shape in a reformulation of MOND, not a $\Lambda$CDM mechanism. Its link to $\Lambda$ is stated as an order of magnitude and imposed; at the quoted coupling $\kappa_{\rm eff}$ is 0.576, and as written the slope with $\rho_{\rm DE}$ is 0.

A24, emergent gravity \cite{verlinde17}: in no reading does it reach the law, best 0.0392 with one knob, 0.0378 on unseen galaxies. With today's $H_0$ it gives $\kappa_{\rm eff}$ 0.583 and rises 3.510 times by $z=2.33$. With the de Sitter rate $\sqrt{\Lambda/3}$ it gives $\kappa_{\rm eff}$ 0.482, slope 0.5 and no drift, but the effective scale spreads 0.115\,dex between galaxies, and the choice of rate is imposed: it is the $\Lambda$ coincidence of Amendment A13 with 1/6 in place of $1/2\pi$.

Three different theories reduce in their plain forms to $g_b+\sqrt{a\,g_b}$, which misses the law by the same 0.0392\,dex because its transition has the wrong shape.

\section{Reading the menu}
The point of the exercise is how little it takes. A dozen feedback knobs get the level, nearly the shape, and a tie with the law on observed curves. So a future $\Lambda$CDM paper showing a tight relation at this scale has not, on that alone, derived anything. A paper claiming to derive this law from $\Lambda$CDM should show these five things: the prior on $M_{200}$ and $c$ per galaxy, the fit against surface-density diversity and not only mass, the slope with $\rho_{\rm DE}$ with baryons evolved, $\kappa$ across cosmologies, and a formation path to the required halo. Failing them doesn't refute $\Lambda$CDM, since real simulations could do things this model can't. Passing them would be a real result, and the law would then have to be read as an output of the halo side.

\section{Limits}
This is a semi-analytic model, not a simulation. Baryons are spherically approximated in the halo response. Mass-to-light ratios are fixed. Priors and knob ranges are our judgement. The feedback mapping is ours and has not been tested against any simulation, so whether real subgrid physics can reach these knob values is open. Masses $M_*$ use one $\Upsilon$ and one SHMR. Among clusters only X-COP is graded. A coding error in the cosmology rows was found and fixed during this work; the wrong output is kept in the repository. Three coding errors in the lensing two-halo code were fixed before its first full table. All numbers are AI-run and have not been reproduced independently.
