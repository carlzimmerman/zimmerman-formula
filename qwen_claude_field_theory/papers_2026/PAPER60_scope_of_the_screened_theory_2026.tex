\begin{abstract}\noindent
The owner's programme studies a gravity framework whose galactic law is $g_N=g^2/(g+a_0)$, with $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$ and $\kappa=\tfrac12$ fitted; its extra gravitating mass is a collisionless ``cold energy'' sector, not a particle. PAPER59 wrote the screened theory covariantly; its Khronon form, v3, is the current best theory. Since then the tests on the programme's status board have been recomputed for v3 alone (one, radiative stability, is still grey), each by a lane that froze its criteria before computing and ran a mutation control, on both $a_0$ footings ($9.36\times10^{-11}$ and $1.13\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, never pooled). This note states what v3 is a theory of, and why it fails where it fails.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{What passes.} The acceleration scale (MeerKAT's local $a_0$ sits $+0.032$\,dex from SPARC's with the same recipe), isolated galaxy groups, Cassini and the planets (screened), gravitational waves and binary pulsars, static black holes and neutron stars, and, with no knob, the tidal dwarfs and NGC~1052-DF2/DF4. Conditional: the SPARC radial-acceleration relation (rms 0.054\,dex, but the $\chi^2$ line fails), inner galaxy lensing, red lenses, strong coupling.
\item \textbf{Galaxy-scale failures.} The Milky Way: the AQUAL disc's extra field is disc-shaped (vertical force $K_{z,1.1}$ 92.3 / 96.9 against $66.4\pm2.2$) and the outer slope is excluded ($Z=-4.37$ / $-4.62$). Dwarf satellites ($+2.00$ / $+1.99$\,dex) and Coma's ultra-diffuse galaxies ($+1.41$\,dex) fail structurally: v3's held dust is weightless in a static host, the same theorem that passes the tidal dwarfs.
\item \textbf{Clusters: a scoped no-go.} The cores sit 1.68--1.85 (canonical) / 1.54--1.70 (alternative) times above the law on their baryons. v3's one cluster-scale freedom, the dust mass $\mu_K$, fixes no cluster cell (0 of 1,804): its static dust is attractive inside the well, then oscillates negative, and the oscillation reaches galaxies before clusters.
\item \textbf{Linear growth: no allowed $\mu_K$.} The dust holds only below the scale $\mu_K$, which Blanchet and Skordis cap at 14.8\,$h$/Mpc; at the cap v3 reaches 0.391 of the dust-limit $\sigma_8$, and reaching 0.95 needs $\mu_K\ge361$--2542\,$h$/Mpc, 24--172 times the cap. The galaxy passes need $\mu_K<0.1$\,Mpc$^{-1}$, so in the static branch no single $\mu_K$ serves both. The literal dust sector also has no radiation era; Blanchet and Skordis's later DBI form fixes BBN only.
\item \textbf{Saddles: ill-posed.} Where the khronon density is negative, at every two-body saddle and in the external-field lobes, the screening term turns the khronon/dust pair into an unbounded gradient instability. The term that passes Cassini is the term that breaks well-posedness.
\item \textbf{The pattern.} Each repair tried so far moves the failure rather than removing it (Table~\ref{tab:repairs}). v3 is an effective theory of the acceleration scale and the mean radial-acceleration relation of isolated systems, with a screened Solar System. It is not a cluster theory, not a cosmology, and not well posed at saddles.
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}

\section{Galaxy-scale failures and their mechanism}
\subsection{The Milky Way: the AQUAL disc has the wrong shape}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Vertical force (P4, FAIL).} v3's static limit is AQUAL, and AQUAL's extra field around a disc is itself disc-like. At 1.1\,kpc $K_z=92.3$ / 96.9 against $66.4\pm2.2$ ($\chi^2=178$ / 251 against a line of 67.5), for all four baryon models, every $L_\star$ and with the external field. v3 has no rule that rounds the extra field.
\item \textbf{Outer slope (S3, FAIL, robust).} The law's slope is $-0.126$ / $-0.114$ against the data's $-0.325$: $Z=-4.37$ / $-4.62$, worse than the record's $-3.63$ / $-3.90$. The algebraic kernel, $\nu_1$, the AQUAL disc and the external field all fail; screening is inert at these radii.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hosted small systems: the dust sits in the wrong place}
U1 asked what v3's own dust can supply, and proved two theorems.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{T1: held dust never exceeds the AQUAL phantom density} (14,373 equilibria; maximum ratio 0.666).
\item \textbf{T3: held dust is weightless in a static host:} $\psi=\Phi-\Xi$ is flat across it, so a system that falls with its host carries no dust, and dust it does hold is bound only by the baryons' Newtonian well.
\end{itemize}
The consequences cut both ways, and they must be read together:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tidal dwarfs and DF2/DF4 pass natively.} Dust-free systems are Newtonian, which is what they show: $+0.055\pm0.114$\,dex and $+0.30$ / $-0.24$ in ln. Static AQUAL (P4) had them $-0.59$ / $-0.62$\,dex below v3.
\item \textbf{Dwarf satellites fail structurally:} $+2.00$ / $+1.99$\,dex above v3 under its own dynamics (isolated AQUAL alone would leave $+0.52$ / $+0.47$). With the static host field solved instead (S1) the gap is $+1.33$ / $+1.30$\,dex at the $L_\star$ floor. A weaker external field does not rescue them (T1): at zero external field they still sit $+0.91$ / $+0.89$\,dex high, and one external-field strength does not fit the data sets together (goodness-of-fit $p=0.023$ / 0.014; Coma wants $\epsilon\le0.21$ / 0.24 of the full field, isolated-galaxy lensing wants $\epsilon\ge0.67$ / 0.81).
\item \textbf{Coma's ultra-diffuse galaxies fail:} $+1.41$\,dex ($z=5.4$) under v3's dynamics; $+1.12$ / $+1.08$\,dex in the static host field (S1).
\item \textbf{The Milky Way's circular speed at the Sun} under v3's dust is at most 215.6 / 220.6\,km\,s$^{-1}$ in every branch U1 tested, against $229.1\pm4.6$.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Mechanism.} v3's dust couples to $\psi$, which is flat across a freely falling system, so the dust sector cannot put extra mass where hosted dwarfs need it. U1's conclusion: what would have to change is the dust coupling (and $J$), not a parameter.

\section{Clusters: a scoped no-go (F1, H2, S4)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The failure.} Rebuilt from the raw eRASS1 and KiDS data (F1, 326 clusters), the cores (0.3--1\,Mpc) sit 1.68--1.85 / 1.54--1.70 times above the law on their baryons, $Z\approx+5$ to $+6$. The field equation, native $h$, gas calibration, $R_{500}$ and miscentring each move it by at most 4\%; any added cold profile that fixes the cores overshoots the outskirts.
\item \textbf{v3's only cluster-scale freedom does not fix it (S4).} Blanchet and Skordis's static $\mu_K^2\psi$ term is v3's dust in hydrostatic equilibrium. In spherical symmetry the Khronon static equation is AeST's (H2) with $\mu\to\mu_K$, reproduced to 0.0058. Over every allowed $\mu_K$ and dust offset, 0 of 1,804 cells pass even one cluster cell, and the best profile $\chi^2$ anywhere on the grid is 32.0 / 27.5 against a pass line of 11.07.
\item \textbf{Mechanism: the ordering is backwards.} The static dust is attractive inside the well, then turns negative and oscillates. For $\mu_K\le0.03$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ the clusters are untouched. At $\mu_K=0.1$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ the cluster cores move by about 1\%, while isolated-galaxy lensing is already ruined ($\Delta\chi^2=+109.7$ / $+189.7$); the galaxy guard fails for every $\mu_K\ge0.1$\,Mpc$^{-1}$, up to the cap. The first zero of $\psi$ is at 1.21\,Mpc around a galaxy and 4.21\,Mpc around a cluster at that $\mu_K$, and the onset scales as $M^{1/6}$, so galaxies are hit before clusters, the reverse of what the data need. At $\mu_K^{-1}=1$ / 0.3 / 0.1\,Mpc the cluster MID amplitude becomes 1.33 / $-3.84$ / $-9.86$ (canonical), and the absolute dust is negative over 46--89\% of the cluster volume for $\mu_K=0.3$--1\,Mpc$^{-1}$.
\item \textbf{Other cluster components tried} (PAPER59 and the draft): AeST's static dust has the wrong radial shape (H2); a potential-depth selector, braneworld Weyl closures and a screened tidal charge all fail (J1, K1, M2). M2's lesson is the useful one: anything attached to every mass element acts like a halo on small systems; a cluster component would need its own scale between galaxies and clusters.
\end{itemize}

\section{Linear growth and the early universe (F3, M1, Q6, Q7)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The kernel (M1, corrects F3 and PAPER59).} The exact dust kernel of the Blanchet--Skordis post-Newtonian system is $s(k;f_0)=(\mu^2-f_0k^2)/((1-f_0)k^2+\mu^2)$ with $f_0=1+J'(0)$. Both $J_x$ ($f_0=0$) and $J'(0)=0$ give $s\to1$ as $k\to0$: the Newtonian term survives on large scales, and $J_x$ holds the dust better. The failure is the cap on $\mu_K$, not $J'(0)=-1$ as PAPER59 stated.
\item \textbf{Mechanism: the dust holds only below the scale $\mu_K$.} At Blanchet and Skordis's cap, 14.8\,$h$/Mpc, $\sigma_8/\sigma_{8,\rm dust}=0.391$ for $J_x$ (0.121 for $J'(0)=0$), starting at $a_i=10^{-3}$. Reaching 0.95 needs $\mu_K\ge361$--2542\,$h$/Mpc for $J_x$ ($a_i=10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$; 24--172 times the cap, F3) and 411\,$h$/Mpc for $J'(0)=0$ (27.8 times, M1). No analytic $K$ shape escapes: the late Jeans scale is $K''(1)/2$. Above the cap the dust forms galaxy halos and double-counts against the law (F3).
\item \textbf{The full Boltzmann calculation is worse (Q6).} On a dust background with the literal perturbations, $\sigma_8=0.061$ at the cap ($R_B=0.073$, against the ODE's 0.391); even $\mu_K=2542$\,$h$/Mpc reaches only $R_B=0.88$. Planck lite gives $\Delta\chi^2=+13{,}891$ (not converged) down to $+18.7$ over that range.
\item \textbf{No radiation era (Q6).} The quadratic $K=\mu_K^2(Q-1)^2$ carries a stiff $a^{-6}$ partner fixed by today's dust density; it beats radiation before $z=1854$ at the cap and before $3.2\times10^{5}$ at 2542\,$h$/Mpc. BBN needs $\mu_K\ge1.84\times10^{8}$\,$h$/Mpc, $1.24\times10^{7}$ times the cap.
\item \textbf{Blanchet and Skordis's DBI $K$ fixes BBN only (Q7).} It makes the early khronon dust-like ($\Delta N_{\rm eff}$-equivalent at 1\,MeV $4.3\times10^{-6}$ / $7.2\times10^{-6}$ at $\lambda_D=30$ / 1, against $7.7\times10^{13}$ for the quadratic $K$), but growth stays at $R_B=0.26$--0.32, Planck lite $\Delta\chi^2=+350$ to $+748$. Their own CMB claim compares only the background $w(a)$; they ran no Boltzmann code. A DBI scale of $\lambda_D\approx7.8\times10^{9}$ restores $\sigma_8$ but leaves $P(k)$ at 1\,$h$/Mpc at 0.33 of $\Lambda$CDM and, by an order-of-magnitude estimate, makes $K$ complex inside cluster potentials.
\item \textbf{The one open escape is nonlinear} (PENDING, L1): a Khronon-native particle-mesh run tests whether the full $J_x$ hold term restores $\sigma_8$ at the cap. Its indicative $128^3$ run is labelled not a verdict; no production result is committed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Saddles: v3 is ill-posed where the khronon density is negative (H4)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The dispersion.} In the weak field, to first order in background gradients and with self-gravity, the khronon/dust pair obeys $\omega^2=\rho_\tau[k^2/\chi-4\pi G]$ with $\chi=k^2/(4\pi GM_\theta+\gamma_Lk^2)+\mu_K^2/4\pi G$, where $\rho_\tau=D\cdot P+{\rm dust}$.
\item \textbf{Mechanism.} Where $\rho_\tau<0$ the growth rate $\Gamma=L_\star k\sqrt{2\pi G|\rho_\tau|}$ rises linearly with $k$ without a cap: a gradient (Hadamard) instability. The stiffness term makes it unbounded; without $\gamma_L$ the same pair grows by only 0.006--0.20 e-folds.
\item \textbf{Where.} $\rho_\tau<0$ at every two-body saddle (the Milky Way and M31, the LMC and the Milky Way, galaxy pairs, cluster pairs) and, with an external field, in lobes around every system. Wavelengths below 4.2--326\,pc grow by more than one e-fold over a region's life; a 1\,AU wavelength grows by $8.6\times10^{5}$ to $6.7\times10^{7}$ e-folds. The Solar System is healthy: screening keeps $D\cdot P\ge0.045\,M_\odot$\,pc$^{-3}$.
\item \textbf{Why it is not a detail.} No dust offset or $\mu_K$ removes the regions. A cutoff would have to sit near $L_\star$, but the stiffness must act at AU scales to screen the Sun. The AeST form (v4) does not escape: the same term makes it ill-posed wherever its aether twists (I1; relayed signals can close loops in time, by 4\,ms to 3.9\,s around an illustrative galaxy), and it runs away on FRW for the dust shape tried (C7, $P/P_{\Lambda\rm CDM}$ up to $2.8\times10^{41}$ at $k=10\,h$/Mpc). The screening routes without $\gamma_L$ fail too (M1): galileons screen but cost SPARC 0.68 / 0.54\,dex (cubic / quartic, median), potential keying has the wrong ordering, density keying is a conditional no-go, and Weyl keying carries an Ostrogradsky ghost.
\end{itemize}

When each repair fixes one regime and breaks another, the family is missing an ingredient; more lanes of the same kind will not close it. Three requirements follow from the mechanisms, each stated by its lane:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a dust coupling that lets hosted systems carry extra mass (U1);
\item a cluster component with its own scale between galaxies and clusters, not one attached to every mass element (M2, S4);
\item a screening operator that acts at AU scales and stays well posed where the khronon density is negative (H4).
\end{enumerate}
None of these is a fitted knob; each changes the action. Adding a particle species is excluded by the programme's rules.

\section{Scope}
v3, as it stands, is an effective theory of:
\begin{itemize}
\item the acceleration scale $a_0$ and, conditionally, the mean radial-acceleration relation of isolated, rotation-supported galaxies, and isolated groups;
\item a screened Solar System, Cassini and the planets, with $L_\star$ bounded below by the outer globulars;
\item the relativistic sector for static, non-rotating compact objects and the tensor waves.
\end{itemize}
These passes hold in the small-$\mu_K$ limit ($\mu_K<0.1$\,Mpc$^{-1}$), where linear growth fails; no single $\mu_K$ serves galaxies and growth in the static branch, and whether collapsed dust at growth-level $\mu_K$ changes this is L1's question. It is not a theory of galaxy clusters, of linear structure growth or the early universe, of systems hosted in a larger potential, or of the Milky Way's disc in detail, and it is not well posed at two-body saddles. Not decidable today: isolated-galaxy lensing at all radii (too few spectroscopically isolated lenses), the SLUGGS central ellipticals (tension 2.8 to 3.1$\sigma$ with v3's kernel), high-redshift discs and CRISTAL / ALESS.

\section{What this is not}
\begin{itemize}
