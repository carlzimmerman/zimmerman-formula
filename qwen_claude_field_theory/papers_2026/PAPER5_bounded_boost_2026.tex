\begin{abstract}
Any modified-gravity kernel \cite{milgrom83,bm84,qumond,fm12} that writes the observed acceleration as $g=\nu(y)\,g_N$, $y=g_N/a_0$, with a Newtonian limit $\nu\to1$ and a deep-MOND limit $\nu\to y^{-1/2}$, makes a prediction that a dark-matter halo structurally cannot make: the \emph{acceleration excess} $\Delta \equiv g_{\rm obs}-g_{\rm bar}$ is bounded above by a pure number times $a_0$. For the exponential carrier $g_{\rm bar}=g\,(1-e^{-g/a_0})$ the bound is exact and elementary, $\Delta\le a_0/e=0.367879\ldots a_0$, attained at $g_{\rm bar}=(1-1/e)a_0$; the completed kernel saturates there, so the bound is attained on a plateau. Across five standard kernels the constant lies in $[0.250,1.000]$, so the ceiling is kernel-independent up to an $O(1)$ factor. In a halo model the same quantity is $g_{\rm halo}$, set by $M_{200}$ and concentration, which span decades and are not tied to $a_0$; the ceiling is therefore a one-sided hard wall with no fitting freedom, and a single system above it falsifies the kernel. We test it. (i) On 2352 SPARC \cite{sparc} rotation-curve points beyond 2\,kpc from 144 galaxies (the survey's own $Q<3$, $i>30^\circ$ cuts), $99.23\%$ obey the widest kernel bound at both $a_0$ footings; the eighteen $>3\sigma$ exceptions lie in five named galaxies, thirteen of them in NGC\,5985 alone. (ii) The ceiling is tight enough to discriminate between kernels: $9.8\%$ of those points (263, spread over 32 of 144 galaxies) exceed the exponential carrier's own ceiling $a_0/e$ at $>3\sigma$, and between $g_{\rm bar}=1$ and $4.5\,a_0$ the measured excess is $2$--$3\times$ that ceiling against $1.2$--$1.9\times$ the wider $\nu_{\rm RAR}$ \cite{rar} ceiling. The coherent stellar-normalisation freedom is independently pinned to $+1.8\%$ by the Newtonian limit, and raising $a_0$ cannot absorb the discrepancy (it would need $12\times$ the canonical value). (iii) In X-COP \cite{eckert19,ettori19} cluster cores, after correcting a radius-unit error we verify directly from the FITS headers, the ceiling fails by a factor $9.1$ against the exponential carrier's bound and $3.4$ against the widest bound of any kernel, so no choice of interpolation function can absorb it. Both escapes close quantitatively: nonthermal support would need $\sigma_{\rm 1D}=673$--$1155\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ against the Hitomi Perseus measurement of $164\pm10$ \cite{hitomi}, and unseen baryons would need $3.9$--$5.2\times$ the directly imaged X-ray gas. Clusters therefore require a genuine extra source \emph{as a theorem rather than a fit}. The required source is baryon-tracing, but so is the published NFW \cite{nfw} fit, so its profile shape does not discriminate; we report that as a null. We then carry the swap the theorem selects through the framework's whole constraint ladder. The kernel enters every gate through a single function, $J_Y(s)=s/\Delta(s)$, whose saturated slope is $e$ for the exponential carrier and $1/C_{\rm RAR}=1.544$ for $\nu_{\rm RAR}$: one ratio, $1.76$, is the entire content of the swap. It is admissible everywhere. Post-Newtonian $\alpha_1$ is untouched because the screening crushes its scalar drag to under $2\%$ at either kernel's own floor; the cosmological linear-source screening is untouched because, re-derived from the action, it contains no kernel dependence at all; and the dark sector's deep-MOND length is \emph{identical} for the two kernels, only its saturated value differing, by $0.568$. Clusters, as the theorem requires, are not rescued: the excess is $5.2\times$ $\nu_{\rm RAR}$'s wider ceiling against $9.1\times$ the carrier's. Finally, we close the loop the corrections open: the programme's own wide-binary prediction was evaluated at floors the carried kernel does not permit and with an interpolation that is wrong above the saturation point, and we recompute it. The pre-registered estimator returns $\gamma_v=1.0375$ and $1.0275$ for the exponential carrier at its corrected floors and $1.0450$ and $1.0300$ for $\nu_{\rm RAR}$ carried, against a stale $1.032$/$1.040$. The two corrections act in \emph{opposite} directions and are of comparable size, so the net change is not monotone in the coherence length and had to be computed rather than assumed. Every value remains far below the pre-registered band, so the prediction stays sharply distinguishable, while the anisotropy sign-flip radius moves out of the survey's usable separation range. This is a preprint of an AI-assisted draft. It claims no completed relativistic theory, no derivation of $a_0$ or of $\kappa=\tfrac12$, and no empirical detection.

\section{The action and the kernel}
The candidate under test, in the lineage of relativistic MOND completions \cite{aest}, is general relativity plus a clock scalar $\tau$ with unit normal $n_\mu=-\partial_\mu\tau/\sqrt{-(\partial\tau)^2}$ and projector $q_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+n_\mu n_\nu$, and one MOND scalar $\phi$ with $Q=n^\mu\partial_\mu\phi$, $V_\mu=q_\mu^{\ \nu}\partial_\nu\phi$, $Y=V\!\cdot\!V$:
\begin{equation}
S=\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\Big\{\tfrac{1}{16\pi G}\big[R-2\Lambda\big]-c_1(\nabla_\mu n_\nu)(\nabla^\mu n^\nu)-c_2(\nabla\!\cdot\!n)^2-c_3(\nabla_\mu n_\nu)(\nabla^\nu n^\mu)+c_4(n\!\cdot\!\nabla n)^2
\end{equation}
\[
\qquad\qquad+\,2(2-K_B)\,J^\mu\partial_\mu\phi-(2-K_B)\,\mathcal J\!\left(Y+\xi^2q^{\lambda\sigma}q^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda V_\mu\nabla_\sigma V_\nu\right)-K(Q)\Big\}+S_m[g,\psi_m],
\]
with $J^\mu=n^\nu\nabla_\nu n^\mu$ the clock's four-acceleration, $K(Q)=K_2(Q-Q_0)^2$, $c_1=-c_3=K_B$ so that $c_{13}=0$ and $c_T=c$, and matter minimally coupled. Nothing below depends on this particular action: the theorem applies to any kernel of the stated class. We carry both acceleration footings, $a_0=9.3619\times10^{-11}$ and $1.1279\times10^{-10}\,{\rm m\,s^{-2}}$, throughout; $\kappa=\tfrac12$ in $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_\Lambda}$ is fitted, not derived.

In the static weak-field limit the scalar carries an exponential kernel. Writing $y=g/a_0$ for the total field, the matter-sourced carrier obeys $g_{\rm bar}=g\,(1-e^{-y})$ for $y\le1$, and above $y=1$ the scalar force saturates at $a_0/e$.

\section{The theorem}
Define the \emph{acceleration excess} in units of $a_0$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta(g_{\rm bar})\;\equiv\;\frac{g_{\rm obs}-g_{\rm bar}}{a_0}\;=\;y\big[\nu(y)-1\big].
\end{equation}
For the exponential carrier this is exactly
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Delta=y\,e^{-y},\qquad \frac{d\Delta}{dy}=(1-y)e^{-y},\qquad \Delta_{\max}=\frac1e=0.367879\ldots\ \text{at } y=1,\;}
\end{equation}
verified symbolically: the critical point is unique, the second derivative there is $-e^{-1}<0$, and both endpoint limits vanish. The maximum is reached at $g_{\rm bar}=(1-1/e)\,a_0=0.6321\,a_0$. Because the completed kernel saturates at $y=1$, the excess does not fall back but stays at $a_0/e$: the bound is attained on a plateau.

The statement is not special to this kernel. $\Delta$ vanishes as $\sqrt{y}$ at small $y$ and tends to $0$ or a finite constant at large $y$ whenever $\nu\to1$ at least as fast as $1/y$; being continuous in between, it is bounded. Table \ref{tab:C} gives the supremum for five standard kernels, computed on a $2\times10^6$-point logarithmic grid.

\section{Why a dark-matter halo cannot make this prediction}
In a halo model the same observable is $g_{\rm obs}-g_{\rm bar}=g_{\rm halo}$, the halo's own acceleration at that radius. It is set by the halo mass and concentration, which vary by orders of magnitude across the galaxy population and have no relation to $a_0$. There is no mechanism placing a common upper bound on $g_{\rm halo}$, still less one at a fixed multiple of an acceleration defined by the cosmological constant. A ceiling holding across every system and every radius is therefore a coincidence a halo model must absorb as a fine-tuning of the mass--concentration relation against the baryon distribution.

Two properties make this sharper than the radial-acceleration relation from which it descends. It is \emph{one-sided}: only excursions above the wall count, so scatter, distance errors and inclination errors cannot manufacture a false confirmation, only a false violation. And it is \emph{parameter-free} once $a_0$ is fixed: there is no amplitude, slope or scatter to fit, so a single system above the wall falsifies the kernel outright.

\section{Test on rotation curves}
We use the SPARC Rotmod curves \cite{sparc} with the survey's published sample cuts ($Q<3$, $i>30^\circ$) and its fixed $3.6\,\mu$m mass-to-light ratios, $\Upsilon_{\rm disk}=0.5$, $\Upsilon_{\rm bul}=0.7$, keeping points with $\delta V/V<0.10$: 144 galaxies, 2681 points, of which 2352 lie beyond 2\,kpc. The uncertainty on $\Delta$ combines the velocity error with a $0.1$\,dex stellar-population spread.

Against the \emph{widest} kernel bound $C=1.000$, $99.23\%$ of points beyond 2\,kpc comply at both footings. The eighteen $>3\sigma$ exceptions lie in five galaxies: ESO\,563-G021, NGC\,5907, NGC\,5985, NGC\,6674 and UGC\,12506. Thirteen are in NGC\,5985 alone, which has $Q=1$ and $i=60^\circ$ and so passes every standard cut; its $V_{\rm flat}=294\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ against a baryonic Tully--Fisher expectation near $195\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ makes it a genuine exception, and we keep and name it rather than cutting it. Three of the remaining four are near edge-on ($i\ge83^\circ$) and contribute one or two points each.

The ceiling is tight enough to discriminate between kernels, and it does not favour the kernel this action carries. Table \ref{tab:trans} compares the binned median excess with the exponential carrier's saturated ceiling and with $\nu_{\rm RAR}$.

Beyond 2\,kpc, $9.8\%$ of points (263 of 2352, spread over 32 of 144 galaxies) exceed the exponential carrier's own ceiling $a_0/e$ by more than $3\sigma$. That is the ordinary population, not a tail. Two escapes are closed. A coherent shift of the stellar normalisation is pinned independently by the Newtonian limit, which every kernel shares: at $g_{\rm bar}>10\,a_0$, where all kernels require $\Delta\to0$, the measured median of $g_{\rm obs}/g_{\rm bar}$ over 90 points is $1.018$, so the normalisation is already correct to $1.8\%$. Raising $a_0$ does not help either: the ceiling is $a_0/e$ in physical units, and accommodating the largest measured excess would require $a_0\ge1.14\times10^{-9}\,{\rm m\,s^{-2}}$, twelve times the canonical value and far outside the $20\%$ spread between the two footings. We note that a joint profiling of $a_0$ and $\Upsilon$ is the formal comparison and is not repeated here; what this statistic adds is that the coherent part of the $\Upsilon$ freedom is externally constrained.

\section{Test on clusters}
We use the twelve X-COP clusters \cite{eckert19,ettori19}, taking hydrostatic masses from the FORW reconstruction, gas masses from the published $f_{\rm gas}$ profiles, and stellar profiles for the seven clusters that ship one; the remaining five receive a radius-dependent stellar import measured from those seven and are excluded from every headline.

A radius-unit error had to be corrected first, and we verified it directly from the FITS headers rather than from any script: the gas profile's \texttt{RADIUS} column carries \texttt{TUNIT1 = R/R500}, with $R_{500}$ given in each file's own header (for example 1250\,kpc for A644), whereas earlier analysis multiplied it by $10^3$ as if it were Mpc. Since $R_{500}$ ranges from 1054 to 1430\,kpc across the sample, that misplaced every gas measurement by a different per-cluster factor.

With the radii corrected, the median excess over the seven clusters with measured stars is
\[
\Delta/a_0 = 3.37,\;2.94,\;2.48,\;2.28,\;2.10,\;1.91,\;1.63,\;1.52,\;0.99,\;0.69
\]
at $r=40,50,75,100,150,200,300,420,750,1000$\,kpc. At 40\,kpc that is $9.1$ times the exponential carrier's exact bound $a_0/e$ and $3.4$ times the widest bound of any kernel in Table \ref{tab:C}. \emph{No choice of interpolation function can absorb this}, which is the point of proving the bound before measuring.

Both escapes close quantitatively. Restoring the ceiling by nonthermal pressure alone, with thermal and nonthermal components of similar logarithmic slope and $kT=5$\,keV, requires a one-dimensional turbulent velocity of $1155\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ at 40\,kpc falling to $673\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ at 300\,kpc, against the Hitomi measurement of $164\pm10\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ in the Perseus core \cite{hitomi}: a factor $4.1$ in velocity and $17$ in pressure at the most favourable radius. Restoring it with unseen baryons requires $3.9$ to $5.2$ times the observed baryons inside 300\,kpc, where the X-ray gas is directly imaged and dominant.

That clusters resist MOND-class gravity has been known since \cite{sanders03}, and neutrino or other unseen components have been proposed to absorb it \cite{angus08}; what the bound adds is that the requirement is exact and kernel-independent. Because the bound is a theorem, this conclusion does not depend on a fit, on a choice of interpolation function, or on a dark-matter model.

\section{What the cluster residual must be, and a null}
Applying the kernel to the total source, the required source mass runs $M_{\rm src}/M_{\rm b}=6.5$ at 40\,kpc, $8.2$ at 100\,kpc and $5.2$ at 750\,kpc, a logarithmic slope of $-0.05$: it tracks the baryons. That looks like a strong constraint until the control is run. The published NFW \cite{nfw} fits to the same clusters give $M_{\rm NFW}/M_{\rm b}$ with slope $+0.03$, and the Newtonian hydrostatic ratio $M_{\rm HSE}/M_{\rm b}$ likewise $+0.03$, both equally flat. Baryon-tracing therefore does \emph{not} discriminate between an extra source and a dark-matter halo, and we report it as a null. A rescaled acceleration scale does not fit either: the $a_0$ that would reproduce each cluster point runs from $24$ to $8$ times the canonical value with slope $-0.32$, so it is not a constant.

What survives as the discriminating statement is the ceiling itself, not the profile shape.

\section{The carrier theorem is the bounded-boost theorem}
The bound has a structural consequence for any theory in which the modification is carried by a matter-sourced scalar. Such a scalar obeys $\nabla\!\cdot\![J_Y\nabla\phi]=4\pi G\rho$, so on a sphere $J_Y(g_\phi)\,g_\phi=g_N$: the scalar force must be a single-valued function of $g_N$, and its longitudinal stiffness $dg_N/dg_\phi$ must be positive or the static problem is ill posed. Writing $g_\phi=a_0\Delta(s)$ with $s=g_N/a_0$, that stiffness is exactly $1/\Delta'(s)$. Hence
\begin{quote}
a matter-sourced scalar can carry a kernel only up to the \emph{maximum} of $\Delta$, and must saturate at $C\,a_0$ above it.
\end{quote}
This is the same statement as the theorem: $\Delta$ is bounded, so it has a maximum, so $s\mapsto\Delta(s)$ is not invertible beyond it. The saturation is therefore universal, not a defect peculiar to one kernel, and \emph{every} kernel of the class leaves a constant residual scalar force $C\,a_0$ at high acceleration. We verify numerically that the stiffness changes sign at $g_N=0.633\,a_0$ for the exponential carrier and at $2.540\,a_0$ for $\nu_{\rm RAR}$, exactly at the two maxima.

That residual must be screened in the Solar System, which in this action is done by the coherence length $\xi$ in the $\xi^2|\nabla_\perp V|^2$ operator. We price the swap on the same three gates used elsewhere in the programme --- a quadrupole ceiling, the phantom mass inside Saturn's orbit, and a sunward ephemeris gate of the kind that constrains MOND in the Solar System \cite{hees14} --- at three external-field inputs and both footings, first validating the machinery by reproducing the previously published floors exactly on their own grid.

\section{The wide-binary prediction, recomputed}
Wide binaries at $10$--$30$\,kAU probe accelerations below $a_0$ and have become a contested test of gravity in that regime \cite{chae23,banik24}; this programme carries a frozen, pre-registered estimator for the velocity ratio $\gamma_v$ and a registered expectation band of $1.16$--$1.23$ for standard MOND. Its standing prediction, $\gamma_v=1.032$ (canonical) and $1.040$ (alt), carried \emph{two} defects that the preceding sections expose. It was evaluated at $\xi=0.03$/$0.05$\,pc, the floors of the unsaturated partner. And it solved with $\mu=1-e^{-y}$ throughout, although the wide-binary external field sits at $y_e=1.9$, \emph{above} the saturation point $y=1$, where the carried interpolation is $\mu=1-1/(ey)$ instead --- a $5.5\%$ difference in $\nu$ at that field.

We correct both, using the programme's own three-dimensional pair solver and its frozen estimator, which is imported and not edited. As a control, the estimator code used here reproduces the published $1.032$/$1.040$ to within $0.0005$ when fed the original tables, so every change below is physics rather than re-implementation.

A dedicated decomposition, running the carried kernel at the \emph{old} floors, separates the two effects: at fixed $\xi$ the carried kernel \emph{raises} the boost by $+0.0405$ (canonical) and $+0.0225$ (alt), because $\nu$ is larger at $y_e$, while raising the floor \emph{lowers} it by $-0.0350$ at both footings. The two are of comparable size and opposite sign.

Two consequences. Every recomputed value lies roughly $33\sigma$ of the estimator's own error below the registered band, so the prediction remains sharply distinguishable: the candidate returns a near-Newtonian answer where standard MOND expects $1.16$--$1.23$. But the companion prediction weakens. The anisotropy sign-flip radius scales as $s_\times=2.49\,\xi+0.31$\,kAU, so it moves from $15.7$ and $26.0$\,kAU at the old floors --- inside the usable $4$--$30$\,kAU range --- to $36$--$77$\,kAU at the corrected ones, at or beyond the edge of it.

\section{The full gate ladder for the swap}
Selecting a kernel on one test is not the same as adopting it. We therefore carry $\nu_{\rm RAR}$ through the framework's whole constraint ladder. The exercise is tractable because the carried kernel enters every gate through a single function, the coefficient of the scalar's gradient term,
\begin{equation}
J_Y(s)=\frac{s}{\Delta(s)},\qquad s=g_N/a_0 ,
\end{equation}
which above each saturation point grows linearly with slope $1/C$: $e=2.718$ for the exponential carrier and $1/0.6476=1.544$ for $\nu_{\rm RAR}$. That single ratio, $1.76$, is the whole content of the swap.

Three of these deserve comment. \emph{Post-Newtonian gravity is free.} In this action the scalar contributes a drag $-4(2-K_B)/[J_Y(1+\xi^2k^2)+1]$ to $\alpha_1$ \cite{will14,jacobson01}; at either kernel's own Solar-System floor the screening factor $(\xi k)^2$ at Saturn's orbit exceeds $10^6$, so the drag falls below $2\%$ of $\alpha_1$ and the total is $-4c_{14}=-4.1\times10^{-5}$ for both, an order of magnitude inside the bound. \emph{The cosmology is free.} Re-deriving the linear-source screening from the action, its coefficient depends only on $K_B$, $c_2$ and $K_2$; the kernel's $J_Y$ at zero field does not appear, so the closure relation between $c_2$ and $|K_2|$ and the upper bound that follows from it carry over unchanged. \emph{The dark sector barely moves.} The hydrostatic length $H=c_s^2/g$ is \emph{identical} for the two kernels in the deep-MOND regime, $0.42\,c^2/(|K_2|a_0)$; only its saturated value differs, by $0.568$, so the admissible $|K_2|$ window shifts by that one factor and remains non-empty.

The costs are a factor $1.4$--$1.5$ in the coherence length and, through it, the anisotropy sign-flip radius pushed out of the usable separation range, as the previous section records. And the one thing the swap does \emph{not} do is the thing the theorem already forbade: clusters violate $\nu_{\rm RAR}$'s wider ceiling by $5.2\times$ at 40\,kpc against $9.1\times$ the carrier's. Better, and nowhere near enough. That is the point of proving the bound before measuring --- the cluster requirement is a property of the class, not of a choice within it.

\section{What is not claimed}
The theorem is exact; everything else here is a measurement with stated systematics. Hydrostatic equilibrium is assumed for the cluster masses and is tested rather than granted in the nonthermal-support calculation, but the FORW reconstruction, the distance scale and the sphericity of these systems are inherited. The SPARC test uses fixed mass-to-light ratios; a per-galaxy stellar population model would move individual points, though the Newtonian-limit calibration bounds the coherent part of that freedom. The gate ladder of Section 11 is this programme's own constraint suite, evaluated with its own solvers and proxies, not an independent audit. This work does not derive $a_0$, does not derive $\kappa=\tfrac12$, does not present a completed relativistic theory, and does not claim that any data set favours this framework over $\Lambda$CDM. Its two positive results are that the ceiling is a genuine parameter-free prediction of the modified-gravity class that a halo model cannot reproduce without fine-tuning, and that cluster cores violate it by a margin no interpolation function can absorb.
