\begin{abstract}\noindent
This note follows PAPER63 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23708507), which asked where cold dark energy switches on. The programme studies a galaxy law $g_N=g^2/(g+a_0)$, with $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$ and $\kappa=\tfrac12$ fitted. It adds a gravitating $w\approx0$ field component called \emph{cold dark energy}; this is not matter and not a particle.

We report frozen, reproduced calculations. Each was pre-registered and committed before its numbers, run with a mutation twin, and rerun to byte identity. Together they reduce the switch to one condition. A system carries a cored cold dark energy halo iff the collapse surface density of the \emph{gravitational basin} it belongs to beats the galaxy floor:
\[
\eta(L)\,\Sigma_{\rm coll}(M_{\rm basin})>a_0/2\pi G .
\]
The same $\max(a_0/2\pi G,\ \eta\Sigma_{\rm coll})$ sets the halo core. The threshold mass is therefore not a separate number: it follows from the concentration level $L$.

On the canonical footing this rule passes every graded calibration line:
\begin{itemize}
\item SPARC;
\item 63 groups;
\item 15 cluster lensing keys;
\item five Local Group dwarf classes;
\item KiDS isolated-lens lensing;
\item the Milky Way.
\end{itemize}
Random settings in the explored box pass 10 times in 24.

An X-ray-bright group ``wrong sign'' is withdrawn as an estimator artefact. A held-out elliptical failure becomes a hot-gas prediction. IC~1613 remains a genuine two-tracer failure.

Two lensing predictions are sealed by content hash. The second is unique to the basin rule: at fixed stellar mass, a central in a massive basin should lens about half as strongly at 0.3~Mpc as an isolated one.

A candidate field equation keys condensation on the expansion of the matter flow. This is not evidence for the framework over $\Lambda$CDM.
\end{abstract}

\section{The four equations}
The four equations, as graded (canonical footing $a_0=9.3603\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$; footings never pooled):

(1) $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$.

(2) $g=\nu(g_N/a_0)\,g_N$, with $\nu(y)=\tfrac12+\sqrt{\tfrac14+1/y}$.

(3) $\Sigma_{\rm core}=\max(a_0/2\pi G,\ \eta(L)\Sigma_{\rm coll})$, with $\eta=\tfrac{4}{3B(1)}L^{5/4}$ and $B(1)=3\ln2-\pi/2$. A halo exists iff the second term wins on $M_{\rm basin}$. The concentration $c_f(M)=L\,c_{\rm nat}(M)/c_{\rm nat}(10^{14})$ follows the framework's own zoom slope.

(4) The halo holds the native fair share ($\Omega_c/\Omega_b=5.2715$, from BAO+BBN with no CMB) of the measured baryons, out to the turnaround radius.

A \emph{basin} is the region inside the zero-velocity surface a system belongs to. For the Local Group it is measured from the radial-velocity zero crossing in the Updated Nearby Galaxy Catalog: $R_0=794.7\pm151.5$\,kpc, with $\log M_{\rm basin}=12.392$.

\section{Sealed predictions}
\textbf{SP1} (isolated lenses, sha256 \texttt{09cf7da6\ldots04bf}). Above $\log M_\star\approx11.21$, the outer $\Delta\Sigma$ of isolated centrals drops below the law's, with $S_{\rm step}=0.47$.

\textbf{SP2} (the basin step, sha256 \texttt{75b5fcdb\ldots8b3d}). At fixed $M_\star$ below the own-mass switch, a central in a basin above $T$ carries a cored halo truncated at $R_{200}$. An isolated central follows the law. The predicted ratio of their own $\Delta\Sigma$ at 0.3\,Mpc, averaged over $\log M_\star$ 10.5--11.0, is $S_{\rm env}=0.484$ (canonical; 0.442 alt). The sign is opposite to halo-model expectations for group centrals.

Both falsifiers are frozen. A KiDS-Legacy or Euclid environment split that isolates the central's own term decides them.

\section{A candidate field equation (not derived, not graded)}
Every selector keyed on potential depth, $\sigma$ or acceleration has failed in the record, because satellites sit in shallower potentials than the Sun. A pressure fluid is excluded by the Bullet Cluster.

The basin, as measured, is where the coarse-grained expansion $\theta=\nabla\!\cdot\!v$ of the matter flow has turned negative. $\theta$ is native to Khronon/AeST-type actions through $c_2(\nabla_\mu u^\mu)^2$. A candidate order parameter $\chi$ has
\[
m^2=m_0^2\big[\Theta(\theta_u)-1+(a_0/2\pi G)/(\eta\Sigma_{\rm coll})\big],
\]
which condenses inside turned-around regions exactly when the $\max(\cdot)$ rule's collapse term wins. An oscillating condensate behaves as cold, collisionless matter. The step function and the smoothing scale are posited. Kill tests are listed in the repository.
