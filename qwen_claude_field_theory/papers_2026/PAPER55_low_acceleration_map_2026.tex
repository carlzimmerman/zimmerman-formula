{\Large\bfseries Where a Dark-Energy-Tied Galaxy Law Holds and Where It Breaks:\\ Low-Acceleration Tests, a Two-Footing Shear Pass, and the Satellite-Dwarf Excess}\\[6pt]
{\small Working note, 2026-10-19, v1.0. AI-assisted research programme; not peer reviewed. $\kappa=\tfrac12$ is fitted; the theory is not closed.}
\end{center}

\begin{abstract}\noindent
We report one day of pre-registered tests of a gravity framework whose galactic law is $g_N=g^2/(g+a_0)$ with $a_0=\tfrac12 c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$ (the $\tfrac12$ is fitted). Its extra gravitating mass is a collisionless ``cold energy'' component. Every lane froze its criteria before computing and ran a mutation control; both $a_0$ footings ($9.36\times10^{-11}$ and $1.13\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$) are reported and never pooled.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Shear.} The framework's own 1024$^3$ particle-mesh structure, scored against raw KiDS-1000 cosmic shear, is CONSISTENT on both footings ($\Delta\chi^2=-7.70$ and $-7.18$ against its matched control). The pass is conditional on inherited initial conditions; a native-input rerun is running.
\item \textbf{Where the law holds.} Isolated late-type galaxies obey the law down to $g_{\rm bar}\approx7\times10^{-14}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ in KiDS-1000 lensing (amplitude $1.016\pm0.086$; within $\sim$0.1\,dex across robustness variants).
\item \textbf{Where it breaks.} Spheroidal / red isolated lenses sit $1.45\times$ above the law at the same accelerations after a stellar-mass correction (4.0$\sigma$ split). They follow $g_{\rm obs}=g_{\rm law}(g_\dagger/g_{\rm bar})^{0.060}$. Milky Way and M31 satellite dwarfs carry a larger, host-independent excess ($\approx+0.44$\,dex) that ten framework-native mechanisms fail to produce.
\item \textbf{Theory.} Lensing equals dynamics in the framework to better than $2\times10^{-5}$. In the candidate particle model a uniform field produces no internal response at all, so the law's cold mass must be real mass assembled from afar, not a local polarisation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}

\section{The shear test on both footings}
The framework's combined ``theory-v2'' particle-mesh engine (per-host supply, condensed source, gas expulsion; 1024$^3$ particles, 100\,Mpc$/h$) was scored by an unchanged pipeline on raw KiDS-1000 and DES~Y3 $\xi_\pm$ against a matched control run.

\begin{tabular}{lccc}

\midrule

The frozen rule requires both footings, so the shear side passes. It is conditional on inherited $\Lambda$CDM-shaped initial conditions and background, a post-preview module selection and a post-hoc control, all disclosed. Runs with the framework's own initial conditions are in progress and decide the condition.

\section{Isolated galaxies obey the law at ultra-low acceleration}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The test.} We computed the full-covariance amplitude $A$ of the Brouwer et al.\ (2021) isolated-lens radial acceleration relation over the law, in the window $10^{-13.3}\le g_{\rm bar}<10^{-11.9}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, which is where the satellite dwarfs sit.
\item \textbf{Late types.} Blue (late-type) lenses give $A=1.016\pm0.086$, excluding the dwarfs' $\times2.75$ at 20.2$\sigma$.
\item \textbf{Robustness.} Window shifts, a $\pm0.2$\,dex stellar-mass systematic, a S\'ersic definition and the alternative footing move $A$ between 0.902 and 1.312. A frozen robustness lane therefore classes ``exactly on the law'' as fragile, but the dwarf excess stays excluded by $\ge12.2\sigma$ in every variant.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Lensing probes 30--300\,kpc. This tests the law's $g(g_{\rm bar})$ relation, not extra mass concentrated inside small systems.
\end{itemize}

\section{The spheroidal excess}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Both splits.} At the same accelerations, red and spheroidal ($n>2$) lenses sit $\sim1.6\times$ above the law, and blue and disc-like lenses on it. The colour split is 5.0$\sigma$ and the S\'ersic split 4.6$\sigma$; the two labels cannot be separated with the published bins.
\item \textbf{Stellar-mass correction.} Applying the measured $+0.123$\,dex offset between LePhare and colour-based masses for old early types leaves the split at 4.0$\sigma$ (red $A=1.451$). With $+0.20$\,dex it is 2.9$\sigma$.
\item \textbf{An empirical law.} Red lenses follow $g_{\rm obs}=g_{\rm law}(g_{\rm bar})\,(g_\dagger/g_{\rm bar})^{b}$ with $b=0.060\,(0.050$--$0.070)$ and $\log_{10}g_\dagger=-10.05$. This gives $\chi^2=16.1$ against 157.9 for the law alone on 15 points; it is a description, not a prediction.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item With corrected masses on the canonical footing the onset lies near $a_0$. This is footing-dependent and is flagged as a coincidence to test.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Not one law with the dwarfs.} Extrapolated to the satellite dwarfs, the same law predicts $+0.147$\,dex against an observed $+0.438$ (4.4$\sigma$ low).
\end{itemize}

\section{The satellite-dwarf excess and what does not explain it}
\textbf{The excess.} With stellar masses from each dwarf's own star-formation history (E-MILES $\times$ Weisz et al.\ 2014), 25 of 28 independent dwarfs sit above the law, with mean $\ln(\sigma_{\rm obs}/\sigma_{\rm law})=+0.38$. There is no age dependence (Spearman $\rho=+0.09$); an apparent age trend at fixed $\Upsilon=2$ was a stellar mass-to-light artefact.
\begin{itemize}
\item It lives only at $g_{\rm bar}<10^{-12}$. Where dwarfs overlap SPARC discs they agree ($+0.05\pm0.07$\,dex).
\item It is the same for MW and M31 satellites at matched acceleration (offset $+0.055\pm0.117$\,dex).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Mechanisms excluded}, each by a frozen, mutation-controlled test:
\begin{itemize}
\item stiffening of gravity: globulars need $L_*\ge24$\,pc, dSphs $\le22$\,pc;
\item free cold energy of expelled gas spread to the turnaround radius (too far out);
\item the catchment budget packed into $r_{1/2}$: tight at $\Upsilon=2$ (median 0.30 of the whole supply), failing at $\Upsilon=1$;
\item present-day tides ($\rho=-0.00$) and pericentric tides from Gaia orbits in the framework's own Milky Way field ($\rho=+0.08$);
\item capture of the host's cold energy: the Liouville ceiling is $10^{-8}$--$2\times10^{-5}$ of the need;
\item an early-collapse free core in every small system: it reaches the densities but over-predicts SPARC's small rotating dwarfs ($+0.305$ against $+0.032$\,dex, 10.4$\sigma$);
\item maximum-entropy relaxation of the two-population cold medium: wrong sign or ill-posed;
\item gas loss traced by metallicity deficit ($\rho=-0.11$, with power shown by injection);
\item dynamical-friction transport by fragmented cold energy: it over-fills Fornax $\times6.0$ and Leo~I $\times5.8$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The constraint any mechanism must meet.} Fainter dwarfs need more absolute extra mass at fixed radius and dispersion. Leo~I has Draco's size and dispersion and six times its stars, but needs less extra mass.

\section{Theory results}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lensing equals dynamics.} For parents of the record's type (dust-like cold energy plus the stiffened field), the gravitational slip $|\Phi-\Psi|/v_c^2$ is $\le1.9\times10^{-5}$ from dwarfs to clusters on both footings, far below lensing precision. This follows largely from the dust-like premise. The field's own energy and stiffness are smaller still. A well-posed covariant parent is not shown.
\item \textbf{No response to a uniform field.} In the candidate particle model, a uniform acceleration produces only a rigid translation of the total centre of mass: no internal change at any order, for any state, with host and recoil included.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item A local response can generate at most $\approx3\ell/r$ of the law's medium mass, for carriers displaced by $\ell$.
  \item The cold mass must therefore be real mass assembled from afar, which independently supports the catchment-supply rule.
  \item The first missing selection equation is a medium kernel $4\pi G\Sigma_{\rm med}=a_0g/(a_0+g)$ across the galactic band.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Kinetic confinement.} For the two-population cold medium, the full linear feedback work is bounded uniformly in time if there is no embedded neutral mode and the orbital frequencies are non-degenerate (a conditional theorem). The energy method alone cannot close it, because the operator's continuous spectrum covers every receiving frequency. Unconditional all-time confinement remains open.
\item \textbf{Shape test.} A calibration-free shape test (from an independent AI-assisted review) between the simple law and a quadratic rival is not diagnostic on SPARC, because the gas-dominated discs lack transition-regime leverage.
\end{itemize}

\section{What this establishes}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{What holds:} rotating and gas-rich systems obey the law at every acceleration tested; the shear side passes conditionally on both footings; lensing traces dynamics.
\item \textbf{What is open:} spheroidal systems carry a slow excess below $\sim a_0$, and satellite dwarfs a larger one that no framework mechanism yet produces.
\item \textbf{What is not derived:} $\kappa=\tfrac12$, the cold-to-baryon supply ratio and the retained fraction remain inputs.
\end{itemize}
The sealed external tests (ALMA, Gaia DR4) are unaffected.
