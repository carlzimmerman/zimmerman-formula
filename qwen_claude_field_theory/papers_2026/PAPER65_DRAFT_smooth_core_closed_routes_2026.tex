\begin{abstract}\noindent
This note follows PAPER64 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23771199). The programme pairs a galaxy law with $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$ ($\kappa=\tfrac12$ fitted) and a gravitating $w\approx0$ component called \emph{cold dark energy}; this is not matter and not a particle. Its halos obey an empirical core rule:
\[
\rho_0r_0=\max\!\big(a_0/2\pi G,\ \eta\,\Sigma_{\rm coll}\big).
\]

We report three things. Each comes from frozen lanes: criteria committed before any number, a mutation twin, and identity checks against the committed predecessor.

\textbf{(i) The floor is not derived, and five routes to deriving it are closed.} A local tether, three gated extra pulls on the cold component, phantom-halo tracking, pressure floors and halo reshaping all fail. \textbf{(ii) The floor is empirically preferred.} Softening it in low-acceleration galaxies worsens the affected half of SPARC by 43--55\%. \textbf{(iii) A smooth-cored halo} $\rho_0/(1+x^2)^{3/2}$, with the collapse coefficient re-derived for that shape, passes every graded line on the canonical footing at the assumed stellar fraction. With measured stellar fractions, one key fails at $Z\approx2.3$: the heaviest-cluster outskirts.

\section{The floor is preferred by SPARC}

\begin{itemize}
\item Low-$X_0$ galaxies are 93 of 175 (median $X_0$ 0.29), not a small tail.

\end{itemize}
