\begin{abstract}
\noindent We test a gravity framework in which the acceleration scale is tied to dark energy, $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$ ($\kappa=\tfrac12$, fitted), and a cold, collisionless component (``cold energy'', $\Omega_c/\Omega_b=5.364$) settles inside bound systems into the profile the law demands.

The test is framework-native. We run the framework's own $1024^3$ particle-mesh simulations in a $100\,h^{-1}$\,Mpc box and score the gravitating-field matter power spectrum on the \emph{raw} KiDS-1000 and DES~Y3 shear correlation functions $\xi_\pm$. The comparison is a same-pipeline control run without settling. No $\Lambda$CDM-calibrated nonlinear machinery enters the verdict.

The original settling rule, in which all baryons source the law and supply is pooled across overlapping catchments, puts 19--25\% too much power at $k=0.3$--$1\,h$\,Mpc$^{-1}$ and is excluded: $\Delta\chi^2_{\rm KiDS}=+68.7$. Limiting the settling source to a census cap of condensed baryons gives $+14.0$, still narrowly excluded. Two physically motivated rules together bring the framework's own boost down to $1.005/1.012/1.023$ at $k=0.3/0.5/1$:
\begin{itemize}
\item each halo settles only its \emph{own} catchment's supply (no pooling);
\item only \emph{condensed} baryons (stars plus cold gas) source the settled profile.
\end{itemize}
With these rules KiDS-1000 is \textbf{consistent}: $\Delta\chi^2=+2.03$, robustness maximum $+3.28$, sensitivity tooth passes. The absolute fit, $\chi^2=146.3$ ($p=0.011$), matches the control's $144.3$ ($p=0.014$). DES~Y3 is not diagnostic at this box size.

The passing combination was selected after small-box previews. The primary pre-declared combination, which adds the framework's own gas expulsion, is running. The initial conditions remain inherited ($\Lambda$CDM-fitted); a native-input rerun is prepared and its preview is unchanged. The alternative footing is pending. We state these limits plainly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Framework and what is assumed}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Law.} $g=\nu(g_N/a_0)\,g_N$ with the repaired monotone kernel. The saturating-vacuum form $g_N=g^2/(g+a_0)$ gives the same verdicts to within 0.01 (CFG658).
\item \textbf{Acceleration scale.} $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}\simeq0.173\,cH_\infty$, with $\kappa=\tfrac12$ fitted. Two footings, $9.3603\times10^{-11}$ and $1.1312\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, are never pooled.
\item \textbf{Cold energy.} Its amount is measured, not derived. It is required: without it, clusters, the Bullet Cluster, Local Group timing and cosmic shear all fail (CFG624).
\item \textbf{Settling.} Inside bound systems, cold energy drifts into the law's ``phantom'' profile, drawn from each system's turnaround catchment. The switch reads baryons.
\item \textbf{Provenance.} All numbers come from committed lanes (identifiers CFG\,$n$) of the programme's public repository.
\end{itemize}

\section{The test}
\paragraph{Simulations.} We use a lean copy of the programme's census engine:
\begin{itemize}
\item $1024^3$ particles, $L=100\,h^{-1}$\,Mpc, 150 steps from $z_i=49$;
\item snapshots at $z=1$, 0.755, 0.5, 0.256 and 0;
\item the same seed for every run.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Statistic.} We use the framework's own boost $B(k,z)=P_{\rm grav}/P_{S0}$ on the \emph{gravitating} field (particles plus settled source; CFG555), applied to the control spectrum. We project it to $\xi_\pm$ with the published redshift distributions and covariances of KiDS-1000 and DES~Y3. Points are kept only if at least 90\% of their integrand lies inside the box's $k$-range. Intrinsic alignment is free.
\paragraph{Classes.} EXCLUDED if $\Delta\chi^2\ge9$ and every robustness variant gives $\ge9$. The robustness variants are: widened covariance, no intrinsic alignment, held-out bins, and DESI distances. A sensitivity tooth (``T-OWN'') must show that the framework's own boost, injected into the control, is detectable. Otherwise the result is NOT DIAGNOSTIC.
\paragraph{Freezing.} Criteria were committed before each production run. MUTATE checks confirm the null ($F\equiv S0\Rightarrow\Delta\chi^2=0$).

\paragraph{Where the original excess came from.}
\begin{itemize}
\item 56--79\% of CFG596's KiDS $\Delta\chi^2$ sits at $k<1$, mostly 0.3--1, i.e.\ the halo scale of groups and clusters (CFG646).
\item The engine itself conserved mass and never exceeded supply. But the original rule pooled supply across merged catchments, so dominant hosts drew their neighbours' cold energy: about 10\% of the settled mass (CFG647).
\item The phantom of hot gas added settled mass where most baryons in massive halos are hot (CFG634).
\item The two rules remove exactly these two effects.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations (plainly)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Post-preview selection.} The per-host + condensed combination was chosen after seeing $256^3$ previews (CFG650 addendum A1). The pre-declared primary run (T1x, which adds gas expulsion) is in progress.
\item \textbf{Relative classifier.} The classes compare the framework with a same-pipeline control. Both are marginal in absolute terms ($p\approx0.01$), so CONSISTENT means ``as good as the control'', not ``a perfect fit''.
\item \textbf{Inherited initial conditions.} The runs use Planck-fitted parameters and $\Lambda$CDM growth to set the starting state. A framework-native-input rerun is validated at $256^3$, with an unchanged preview, but has not been run at $1024^3$ (CFG654).
\item \textbf{Box size.} The box loses 54\% (KiDS) and 77\% (DES) of points; no baryonic feedback is modelled in either arm; the alternative footing is pending.
\item \textbf{Unexplained source rule.} The condensed-only source is not yet derived from an action. The one-action form of the law responds to all baryons (CFG658/722).
\item \textbf{Open failure.} Massive systems carry extra core mass not explained by the law: early types $+0.28$\,dex at $Z\approx3.7$; eRASS1 cluster cores $\times1.8$ at $7\sigma$ (CFG669/687). Two candidate mechanisms failed (CFG721/726).
\item $\kappa$ is fitted and $\Omega_c/\Omega_b$ is measured. Nothing here closes the theory.
\end{enumerate}
