{\Large\bfseries A Galaxy Law Tied to Dark Energy,\\ and a Map of What It Leaves Unexplained}\\[6pt]
{\small Working note (PAPER67), version 1.1, 2026-11-04. AI-assisted research programme; not peer reviewed. $\kappa=\tfrac12$ is fitted. The theory is not closed. Nothing here says the data favour this framework over any other.}
\end{center}

\begin{abstract}\noindent
We report what is left of a research programme after its own final grade. A galaxy law, $g_N=g^2/(g+a_0)$ with $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$ and $\kappa=\tfrac12$ fitted, describes isolated disc galaxies well on the canonical footing: SPARC rms 0.0541\,dex at median $\Upsilon$ 0.52, no tilt with speed, no slope with surface brightness at fixed speed. The law is a restatement of MOND's simple form with a proposed origin for its scale. Earlier authors proposed that origin, and the scale's tie to dark energy, as distinct from $cH_0$, has no decisive test yet.

The law fails IC~1613, several Local Group classes, cluster shear and X-COP, whose hydrostatic masses it misses by a factor of 2.93, and the unscreened Solar System. Its Milky Way result depends on a vertical form it doesn't supply, and at 60--150\,km\,s$^{-1}$ it asks for about twice the stellar mass estimated from photometry. It has no action and supplies no dust-like term for the CMB. To cover those we developed a cold dark energy sector. On measured data every graded candidate fails, including the law alone, and the sector is now retired. We publish the scorecard, the two walls that stopped it, and twelve requirements any successor must meet. This is numerical evidence for the families tried, not a no-go theorem. We close with dated tests that can still kill the law.

Version 1.1 adds lanes run after the retirement (Section~\ref{sec:after}). None was blind, all were run by AI subagents, and no outside group has reproduced them. A gas term fitted on X-COP reaches 14 of 15 keys on the canonical footing, fragile, never 15, and tied to present gas it is dead on the Bullet. A repaired test gives no statistical support that the $a_0$ formula is special; that doesn't refute it, and the test lacks power. A line of v1.0 about v9's $\alpha_1$ is corrected. A published rise of $a_0$ with redshift (MUSE-DARK III), which v1.0 omitted, counts against the law's flat band at face value; we read it as mass-route dependent and not decisive, and we say what that reading rests on. Formula searches found no hit. A definition audit moves single lines of the scorecard, and each candidate still fails at least two on every reading run. Lanes through 2026-11-04 add four results. No unscreened action gives the law and passes Cassini; only the frozen screened theory's screening length passes the Solar System, and that theory still fails cosmology. The record's lead for the Milky Way and Cassini together, a phantom mass smoothed over a length, has no surviving form once the full 3D field is used. The law's lensing shortfall, about 0.09\,dex inside 0.3\,Mpc, survives every estimator on record and the measured hot gas. A light fermion beside the law, tried under a one-lane lift of the no-particle rule, fails the early universe.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}
This is a programme report, not a complete theory. Three owner decisions on the record set its form: the $a_0$ floor was removed from the cold sector (2026-10-31), the search for a derived threshold stopped (2026-11-01), and the cold sector was retired (2026-11-01). Earlier deposits stand as dated records. The two footings, canonical $a_0=9.3603\times10^{-11}$ and alternative $1.1312\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, are never pooled. This paper's audit script checks the quoted numbers against committed files. It doesn't check the prose.

\section{The law, and whose idea it is}
\[
g_N=\frac{g^2}{g+a_0},\qquad a_0(z)=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}(z)},\qquad \kappa=\tfrac12\ \text{(fitted)}.
\]
\textbf{Credit.} The first relation is Milgrom's modified dynamics in its simple form \cite{M83}. The numerical nearness of $a_0$ to $cH_0$ and to $c^2\sqrt{\Lambda}$ is his observation \cite{M83,M99}. Limbach, Psaltis \& \"Ozel \cite{LPO} proposed the tie to the dark energy density. None of this originated here.

\textbf{$\kappa$ isn't derived.} Of 550 derivation or selection attempts on the record, none derives $\kappa=\tfrac12$. The record's closure (KC1) reduces every route to one missing length: $\kappa=\tfrac12$ is the same as a length of exactly $c/\sqrt{G\rho}$, and no field equation on record supplies it. A screen of 16 further premises found none that does (KC2). It isn't singled out by SPARC either. A profile likelihood over 175 galaxies has a free best fit of $1.0766\times10^{-10}$. Against it, $\kappa=\tfrac12$ on $\rho_{\rm DE}$ reads $\Delta\chi^2$ 63.90 (1.82$\sigma$ with galaxy-clustered errors), the alternative footing 7.04 (0.60$\sigma$), and Milgrom's own $cH_0/2\pi$ 5.34 (0.53$\sigma$). So Milgrom's value fits at least as well as ours. Data can't settle the coefficient yet. Telling $\tfrac12$ from 0.461, the value a $2\pi$ horizon form gives, needs $a_0$ to 2.8\,\% at 3$\sigma$. The shared galaxy-mass floor is 9.47\,\%, and the best case with $\rho_{\rm DE}$ is about 5\,\% (KC1). Only new observations, not more archive data, would lower it.

\textbf{What this programme adds:} a reconstruction of $a_0(z)$ from raw BAO and supernovae with a stated ruler, the graded comparison below, and the failure record.

\textbf{The band, and what it rests on.} The reconstruction gives $a_0(z)/a_0(0)$ of 0.91 [0.76, 1.03] at $z=1$, 0.94 [0.71, 1.12] at $z=1.5$ and 0.91 [0.33, 1.21] at $z=2.33$ (68\,\%), against 1.76, 2.35 and 3.49 for the rival $a_0\propto H(z)$. These ratios are the law's predictions from the measured $\rho_{\rm DE}(z)$, not measured galaxy values. Constant dark energy isn't rejected (1.97$\sigma$, primary). \textbf{This band isn't free of the retired sector's input:} it takes $\rho_m(z)=\omega_m(1+z)^3$ with a cold mass from the CMB, and with positivity alone, no CMB and no $H_0$, its check fails. The law doesn't explain that matter term (Section~\ref{sec:gaps}).

\textbf{The distinctive claim has no decisive test.} At $z\approx0$ the law can't be told from $a_0\propto cH_0$. Separating them at 3$\sigma$ needs $a_0$ to 0.055\,dex at $z=1$ and 0.084\,dex at $z=2.33$. The best public sample reaches 0.4--0.5\,dex. A frozen decision rule (AT1, its arithmetic checked in Lean) puts the binding term in a sample's common mode, the part of its mass error that doesn't cancel against a local reference. That must sit under the same 0.055--0.084\,dex. At 0.05\,dex, with 0.14\,dex per galaxy, 41 galaxies decide at $z=1$ and 5 at $z=2.33$. At 0.075\,dex only $z=2.33$ stays open, and even a sample that passes the rule's power gate can land inconclusive.

\textbf{A published rise that counts against the flat band at face value.} MUSE-DARK III \cite{C26} fits the radial acceleration relation in 79 galaxies at $0.33<z<1.44$ and reports $a_0(z\sim1)=2.38^{+0.12}_{-0.10}\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ and, for $a_0(z)=a_0(0)+a_1z$, $a_1=1.59\pm0.10$ in the same units (95\,\% intervals). Our band at $z=1$ on the canonical footing is $0.91\times9.3603\times10^{-11}$, a factor of about 2.8 lower. Alcock \cite{Al26}, an independent author, fits the survey's four bins as read from its figure: a constant $a_0$ is excluded at 5.3$\sigma$ if the bars are 1$\sigma$ and at $\ge$10$\sigma$ if they are 95\,\% intervals, the reading the survey's stated convention for its main fits points to, statistical errors only. On that reading $a_0\propto E(z)$ is itself in tension (4.2$\sigma$). The Letter notes that frameworks tying $a_0$ to the cosmological constant are under the same pressure. That is this law. It was received 2026-07-10, before our first deposit using these data (2026-09-06), and we claim no priority on it.

\textbf{Why we read it as non-diagnostic, not as a pass.} The survey's main fit leaves stellar mass free of its SED estimate. Our own frozen run (CFG262, a different sample of 109 galaxies, not a direct $a_0$ measurement) finds the rise travels with the mass route: the implied change from $z$ 0.52 to 1.20 is $+0.570\pm0.094$\,dex on fitted masses, $-0.115\pm0.489$ on SED plus H$_2$, and $+0.036\pm0.138$ on SED alone, with a spread of $\times12.7$ in level in the top third. The SED routes are consistent with flat and also with $H(z)$, so they rescue nothing. A redshift-dependent mass drift of 0.13--0.19\,dex across the bins would remove the rise; the survey puts its unmodelled molecular gas near 0.2\,dex, but nothing here shows that drift is present. MUSE-DARK II \cite{J26} finds no baryonic Tully--Fisher zero-point evolution at $z\sim1$ ($0.00\pm0.06$\,dex), with a further $\pm0.16$\,dex from the local zero point in its own model comparison. On the survey's masses, the band and the dark energy tie fail. That reading stands until a per-galaxy reanalysis with independent baryon masses says otherwise.

\textbf{A caution on the $\Upsilon$ line.} The 0.50--0.80 line was first written for this law, and its lower edge is a population model's central value, not a measurement. So passing it is weaker than it looks; Section~\ref{sec:fails} sets it against measured masses.

\textbf{Surface brightness is the strongest result.} At fixed speed, a halo set by mass alone can't follow the disc: every version of the retired sector's halo tilts, $-0.17$ to $-0.29$ per dex, and the law doesn't. That's a real discriminator on the data used, between the law and these halos only.

\textbf{Isolated-galaxy lensing is shape, not a clean amplitude.} KiDS-1000 prefers the law's shape over the retired halos ($\Delta\chi^2$ +366.0 against the best native halo). But the law's amplitude depends on colour and on the baryon inventory: blue lenses read $A=1.016\pm0.086$ in one lane and 0.878 in a later forward model that adds a hot gas inventory, and red lenses 1.587 and 1.313. No tested model fits these profiles in absolute terms. Inside 0.3\,Mpc the data sit above the law under every grader on record, by +0.0887\,dex (alt +0.0500), and no single swap of estimator brings the full-profile grade within $|Z|$ 2 (LE1, ESTIMATORS DISAGREE, NO DEFECT). The hot gas measured through kSZ doesn't close it (CW6, GAP UNCHANGED). That gas sits mostly at 200--500\,kpc. Red lenses move from 1.313 to 1.306, still 4.87$\sigma$ above 1, blue from 0.878 to 0.861, and the full-profile $Z$ worsens from +9.27 to +10.45 (alt +6.52 to +13.97). Gas held inside the virial radius narrows red, but one gas rule for both colours can't bring red within 2$\sigma$ without pushing blue over.

\section{Where the law fails}\label{sec:fails}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Milky Way.} The law gives only the in-plane force, so the vertical test needs a form it doesn't supply. The field-equation form (AQUAL) is INFEASIBLE, 0 cells, on both footings. So is a real QUMOND solve, which differs from AQUAL by at most 0.13\,\% in $K(R_0)$, and varying heights and thick-disc share doesn't reach the line: in priors the lowest is 75.9 (alt 76.2) against its top of 70.8 (UX5, K\_z FAILURE SURVIVES). That rests on a kinematic summary fitted inside Newtonian potential families. The round rule, which has no field equation behind it, passes in 1 in-prior cell (alt 6) (LW1), 72 (alt 196) with a shorter thick disc, and there it still misses the $K_z$ scale length. On an older, weaker gate, 7 of 175 cells pass (alt 4), all with a short disc and the lightest local column; those have $K_z(R_0)$ 76.7--80.9 against $66.4\pm2.2$, $+4.7$ to $+6.6\sigma$, and leave the outer slope excluded ($Z$ $-3.88$). FG1 counts this as FAIL. A filter line tried to pass the Milky Way and Cassini together by smoothing the law's phantom mass over a length (KT14--KT32, CZ1--CZ5). No form survives. Fixed widths fail Cassini or the planets (CZ2, CZ3). Tidal rules fail the Milky Way once the vertical tide is in (KT29--KT32). The local rule fails Cassini on the 3D field, 13.3 times the ceiling (alt 18.1), because the Sun's and the Galaxy's pulls cancel at a saddle where its smoothing length goes to zero (CZ5). Its earlier passes rested on a 1D approximation. The first Milky Way pass also needed a thick disc heavier than star counts give: at their share of 0.12, 0 of 450 cells pass (KT18).
\item \textbf{IC 1613.} $E=-0.593$, $Z=-7.9$; it fails under every halo-free inclination. One galaxy: disfavoured, not excluded. $Z=-7.9$ has no velocity error; with 15\,\% on $V$ the base is $-3.94$, and $-2.35$ and $-2.83$ at RF2's central inclinations 34.4 and 37.9. Only inclination could close it: $E=0$ needs $i$ 22.1 (alt 21.1). A disturbed gas disc is a live explanation, not computed (UX4).
\item \textbf{Local Group classes.} $Z$: MW ultra-faints +5.22, bright M31 +2.20, other M31 +2.29, MW classicals +1.69, field +0.57. Adding the external field makes it worse: ultra-faints +6.76, bright M31 +3.97. And at fixed $M_V$ the data are flat in host field, $-0.05\pm0.19$ dex per dex, where AQUAL with its external field predicts $-0.76$, so neither version describes these satellites (LW1). Listed systematics don't close them (UX4, FAILURES STAND, both footings). A central stack of binaries, tides, membership and heating leaves ultra-faints at $Z$ +5.08 and other M31 at +2.75; bright M31 reaches +1.47 only with an assumed orbit nobody measured. A ceiling stack overshoots and breaks what passes, field $Z$ $-7.67$. The field's pass doesn't test this regime: 41 satellites are fainter than the faintest field dwarf with a dispersion. The filter rules don't lift the dwarfs: on the 3D field the local rule passes 17 of 24 Local Group dwarfs, as plain QUMOND does on canonical (alt 18) (CZ5). A sweep of every external field inside $\nu$ on record found no defect in these rows or in the Solar System line below (CW3).
\item \textbf{Measured stellar masses, 60--150\,km\,s$^{-1}$.} The law's $\Upsilon$ is 0.62 and 0.65 where GSWLC stellar masses give 0.277 and 0.304 ($Z$ +3.4, +5.6). These are estimators against W1 luminosity, not a 3.6\,$\mu$m measurement. Gas dominance doesn't excuse the 100--150 bin. Offsets, distances and inclinations don't carry it (UX5, NOT CARRIED). The level miss against GSWLC is narrow, best $|Z|$ 2.12 at +0.204\,dex; Taylor needs more than a Salpeter shift; and no offset changes the step between 100--150 and 150--200, which rests on 23 and 25 galaxies. Galaxies with accurate distances still read 0.622$\pm$0.063.
\item \textbf{Disc weighings.} On this law's own form, DiskMass $\sigma_z$ reads round rule $r=1.239$ (alt 1.204), outside 0.90--1.10, a FAIL as frozen (LW1). Earlier lanes read $r=1.250$ and 1.342 with a different interpolating function. Same-population dispersions \cite{A16,A18,A21} correct $r$ by $c$ of 1.414--1.517, giving 0.824--0.884 one-zone and, with exact Jeans and measured thickness, inside the line. But the sources' own tables run $c$ from about 1.24 at the relevant radius to 1.88 in integrated light, so the question is open (OD1, NOT SEPARATED). NGC~628 reads $\Upsilon_{3.6}=0.75\pm0.18$ and NGC~6946 about 0.4.
\item \textbf{The Solar System.} The unscreened law fails Cassini's $Q_2$ by 2.44--4.59 times the $2\sigma$ line (alt 2.85--5.19). The pass in earlier reports belongs to a screened covariant theory. That line's final verdict (v3s) was that it holds as a galaxy law and fails as gravity plus cosmology. So the law as written here needs a screen it doesn't contain. A high-precision rerun cleared the one failed check behind the screened pass, so that theory's Solar-System gate is clean on both footings, $Q_2$ at least $4.8\times10^7$ (alt $5.7\times10^7$) inside the line (C6P). Its verdict stands.
\end{itemize}

\section{What it leaves unexplained}\label{sec:gaps}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cluster cores and shear.} From measured gas, in 318 eROSITA clusters with raw KiDS shear, the law is short by $A=1.91\pm0.12$ at 0.5--1.2\,Mpc (alt $1.75\pm0.11$) and matches at 1.2--3\,Mpc, $A=1.01\pm0.09$. On the measured chain it fails 10 of 15 keys on both footings, G-OUT-M3 +4.76, joint $p$ 3e-8. On this statistic that is worse than the retired native profile (LW1).
\item \textbf{X-COP.} The law's own number is $+0.467$, rms 0.476: $M_{\rm HSE}/M_{\rm law}$ of 2.93 (alt 2.72), all 7 clusters short. Taking 0.12\,dex off the hydrostatic masses still leaves 2.23 (alt 2.06). An earlier scorecard showed another rule's row here; LW1 corrects it.
\item \textbf{What is missing in clusters, as a description.} Inside about $R_{500}$ the deficit is near 8 times the gas at 0.2--0.5\,$R_{500}$ and 5 times at 0.5--1\,$R_{500}$, with no detected mass dependence, no BCG link and no preferred scale found, picked from 31 looks (LM1). Trained on KiDS, it predicts X-COP's hydrostatic masses out of sample, but HSC lensing reads it about 1.7 times higher, and tracking gas can't be told from tracking $M_{500}$, so its frozen label is NOT (GM1, which issued labels after a failed check and says so). No mechanism follows.
\item \textbf{Outer shear against gas mass.} $\Delta\Sigma_{\rm OUTER}\propto M_{\rm gas}^{s}$ with $s=0.90\pm0.21$, against the law's 0.47 and Newton's 0.84. The amplitude agreement above is an average over a slope the law misses.
\item \textbf{The CMB and growth.} $a_0$ is absent from the linear FRW equations, so the law can't act on the background. It can act on the perturbations. The potential that CMB lensing maps at $z=0.5$--2.33 has gradients of 0.003--0.22\,$a_0$ (Planck PR4; WG1), inside the law's range. Run from a baryons-only start with no cold term (WG2), the law misses CMB lensing on both sides. Off on the web, it gives 0.0002--0.0009 of the measured amplitude with the measured expansion, and 0.08--0.31 with baryons alone in the expansion. As QUMOND on the peculiar field, it gives 11--14 times too much, 560--660 with baryons alone in the expansion, and more again with $a_0\propto H(z)$. The measured value lies between. No rule tried lands there. The record's one web rule, a switch that turns the law on only past turnaround, never turns on from this start and fails with the law off (WG3). A variant without its turnaround threshold lands within a factor of 2 with the measured expansion, but only because its ramp then sits at one half everywhere, halving the phantom uniformly. That is a setting, not a rule. WG2's labels rest on one change, made after the results, to an initial-conditions check. The record has no CMB pass without a dust-like component. With one, a window exists at $z_{\rm on}\in[3\times10^4,10^6]$ on the channels run. Retiring the sector leaves this unanswered. \textbf{The law offers no replacement, and we don't claim it needs none.}
\item \textbf{The matter term.} The law takes $\rho_{\rm DE}$ from an expansion history whose matter term is several times the baryons. That term is unexplained here.
\item \textbf{An action.} None is selected, and the $a_0$ sector's action stays open. No action is selected: unscreened classes fail Cassini (AX1), v3s fails as gravity plus cosmology, and a smoothed QUMOND with one Gaussian length passes no length on the static gates (FN1b). Attempts on record are this programme's own versions on AeST \cite{SZ} and Khronon \cite{BS}, and don't test those theories as published. \textbf{Correction to v1.0:} v1.0 said v9, an AeST embedding, fails the preferred-frame bounds with $\alpha_1=-2(K_B+2)$. That is a static-branch ratio. A source moving through the aether has $\alpha_1=\alpha_2=0$ at $G=\tilde G$ (AS3). v9 stays non-viable, because it then loses the law for moving systems. v3, Khronon with an added term, fails linear growth: the published cap $\mu_K\le14.8\,h$/Mpc sits 24--172 times below what $\sigma_8$ needs. v4, AeST with that term, is ill-posed where the aether twists and runs away on FRW for the dust shape tried. v3s, v3 with switched stiffness, is frozen as a galaxy law that fails cluster cores, linear growth, hosted dwarfs and the Milky Way disc. AX1 grades every action class on record against this law. No unscreened class gives $g^2/(g+a_0)$ and passes the Solar System. Those whose static limit is AQUAL or QUMOND miss Cassini's $Q_2$ by 1.69--5.18 times its $2\sigma$ bound. Those that keep the interpolation relativistically fail on gravitational waves, BIMOND's on a source read only in part. Only v3s's screening length passes the Solar System on both footings (C6P). That adopts nothing, and $a_0$ enters by hand in every class.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
Each lane commits frozen criteria alone before any script or number, runs a MUTATE twin that must fail, reruns byte-identically and publishes failures under frozen labels. Most lanes were not blind. Grading was done by the same programme that proposed the models; the clean-room regrade tested code, not independence of definitions. No outside group has reproduced these results. Lanes in Section~\ref{sec:after} were run by AI subagents after the retirement, and their failed checks are listed in each README.
