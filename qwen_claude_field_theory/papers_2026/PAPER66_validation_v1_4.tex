 \section{Validation of the repaired scorer}\label{s:validation}
The corrected code passes six regression tests covering disagreeing or missing starts, invalid speeds, the 15\,\% boundary, the fixed error floor, and refusal to overwrite historical outputs. The end-to-end five-class selftest passes 10/10 checks, including the velocity-axis mutation. No release data enter these checks.

\textbf{Recovery and error coverage.} At archive noise the compact Tadaki P-B branch has 9.0\,\% RMS, truth within the reported error in 1/3 accepted fits; the compact moment-0 P-B branch has 30.2\,\% RMS, truth within the reported error in 1/2 accepted fits. In the sister-like suite the smooth Tadaki P-B branch has 2.6\,\% RMS, truth within the reported error in 6/6 accepted fits, and the compact Tadaki P-B branch has 2.2\,\% RMS, truth within the reported error in 6/6 accepted fits. The sister-like compact moment-0 P-B branch still has 13.7\,\% RMS, truth within the reported error in 4/6 accepted fits. These small-sample frequencies are descriptive, not a coverage calibration. Passing the fit gate does not remove inclination bias. Consequently the fixed-floor intervals and the labels ``consistent''/``in tension'' are operational diagnostics, not calibrated confidence statements or model exclusions.

The archived 8.9\,\% floor is retained, not refitted on these outcomes. The validation record reports bias, fractional RMS and empirical error coverage for every accepted inclination/pressure branch against the generating curve at the radius actually scored. It keeps a poorly recovered or rejected branch visible. Six noise seeds per case cannot establish general confidence coverage. The two power suites retain the original generating speed, flux and smooth/compact shapes; clumpy gas, bulges and a broad velocity grid remain untested. Detection and fit validity are distinct, and an accepted numerical fit alone does not establish that a real galaxy is a thin disc.
