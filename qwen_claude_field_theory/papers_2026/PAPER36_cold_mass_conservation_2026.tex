{\Large\bfseries The Cold Component Keeps Its Collapse Mass: A Derived Dark-Sector Rule for a $\Lambda$-Tied MOND Law, Tested with Weak-Lensing Collapse Masses}\\[6pt]
{\small Carl P. Zimmerman (Briar Creek Tech) --- 2026-09-28, version 1 --- AI-assisted research programme; not peer reviewed; nothing here is claimed closed.}
\end{center}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent The programme ties the MOND acceleration scale to the vacuum, $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_\Lambda}$, with $\kappa=\tfrac12$ fitted, not derived [Z26a]. Its best current version adds a cold fluid with the cosmic amount $\Omega_ch^2=0.12$. Inside bound systems the fluid supplies the law's phantom density, and its cosmic share is written against the baryons a system holds \emph{today}. That bookkeeping leaves baryon-depleted systems short. Twenty X-ray groups fall 1.4$\times$ short at $R_{500}$ and 1.9$\times$ short inside $R_{2500}$, by an amount that tracks their lost baryons (Spearman $\rho=-0.96$). Seven X-ray-bright ellipticals fall short by a factor 1.9.
\emph{The rule.} The fluid is pressureless and conserved, so feedback cannot remove it: a bound system keeps the cold mass it collapsed with. The fluid first builds the law's phantom, and any leftover stays as collapse debris with the collapse profile. No constant is added, but the collapse mass must be \emph{measured}. Here it comes from weak lensing (Mandelbaum et al.\ 2016), which separates passive from star-forming central galaxies.
\emph{The tests.} With a colour-blind stellar-to-halo relation the rule breaks the most massive spirals (up to $+0.30$\,dex in rotation speed). With the measured colour split:
\begin{itemize}
\item every star-forming spiral is left on the law;
\item 16 passive early types with extended HI discs follow the law ($+0.026\pm0.085$\,dex) and receive no debris;
\item the X-ray ellipticals half-close ($+0.125$\,dex, 1.0$\sigma$);
\item the globular-cluster dispersions of 19 SLUGGS early types, which the law leaves $+0.080$\,dex short (3.3$\sigma$), are fitted at $+0.007\pm0.017$\,dex (0.4$\sigma$).
\end{itemize}
Added to the programme's 48-gate harness, the rule moves SPARC by 0.0009\,dex and the cold budget by at most 0.007 of $\Omega$. One massive S0 in SPARC disagrees by $+0.14$\,dex. $\Lambda$CDM on the same measured halos sits within about 2$\sigma$ of the globular clusters, so these data do not favour the framework over $\Lambda$CDM.
\end{abstract}

\section{The law and the dark sector it needs}\label{sec:law}

The relation is $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_\Lambda}$ with $\kappa=\tfrac12$. Its form is forced and its coefficient is fitted [Z26a]; it predicts $a_0$ flat in redshift [Z26c]. Every number below is given on two footings: canonical, $a_0\approx9.36\times10^{-11}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$; and alternative, $\approx1.13\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$. Pairs are written canonical/alternative. The galaxy law is $g=\nu(g_N/a_0)\,g_N$ with the programme's contract kernel $\nu_{\rm mono}$. It fits SPARC [LMS16] to 0.100\,dex rms (lane CFG4).

The programme's best candidate adds four ingredients (lanes CFG4, FG001):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{T4:} a cold fluid with $\Omega_ch^2=0.12$, fitted as in $\Lambda$CDM, which behaves as CDM wherever the law is off;
\item \textbf{T5:} the identity that inside a bound region the fluid \emph{is} the law's phantom, with dark mass $\max(M_{\rm ph},(\Omega_c/\Omega_b)M_b)$;
\item \textbf{an edge:} the phantom ends at $x_e=0.4$ of the turnaround radius;
\item \textbf{hierarchical ownership:} accreted systems obey the isolated law of their infall baryons, and embedded ones are Newtonian.
\end{itemize}
With these it passes 42 of 48 harness gates (lane CFG19).

\section{Where the as-written bookkeeping fails}\label{sec:fail}

T5 reproduces the X-COP clusters, whose baryon fraction is nearly cosmic: 0.946 of the hydrostatic mass (lane CFG4). It does not reproduce systems that have lost baryons:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Groups.} For twenty X-ray groups (Lovisari et al.\ 2015 [LRS15]; stars from the Kravtsov et al.\ 2018 relation [KVM18]) the hydrostatic mass exceeds B's by 1.41/1.33 at $R_{500}$. That is 1.8/1.5$\sigma$ with a generous 20\% hydrostatic allowance, and far more on the group-to-group error alone. Inside $R_{2500}$ the excess is 1.88 (2.6$\sigma$). Across groups and clusters the shortfall tracks the baryon fraction, $\rho=-0.958$ ($p=7\times10^{-18}$; lane CFG34).
\item \textbf{X-ray ellipticals.} Seven X-ray-bright ellipticals (Humphrey et al.\ 2006 [H06]) need a factor 1.91/1.79 more mass than the law gives (1.7/1.6$\sigma$ with the IMF floor), and the shortfall grows outward in all seven (lane CFG32).
\end{itemize}

\section{The rule, derived}\label{sec:rule}

T4's fluid is pressureless and its amount is conserved. Baryonic feedback is a pressure force on gas, so it moves gas and leaves the fluid. A bound system therefore keeps the cold mass it collapsed with, $M_c=(1-f_b)M_{\rm coll}$ with $f_b=\Omega_b/\Omega_m$. T5's ``cosmic share of today's baryons'' is only a floor.

T5's identity says the fluid first \emph{is} the phantom. Conservation says the leftover stays, and the law does not use it, so it keeps its collapse (CDM) profile. This is the \emph{conservation form} (lane CFG35):
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm dark}(<r)=M_{\rm ph}(<r)+f_{\rm ex}\,(1-f_b)\,M_{\rm NFW}(<r;M_{\rm coll}),\qquad f_{\rm ex}=\max\!\Big(0,\ 1-\frac{M_{\rm ph,edge}}{(1-f_b)M_{\rm coll}}\Big).\label{eq:rule}
\end{equation}
Here $M_{\rm ph}$ is the law's phantom from today's baryons, and $M_{\rm ph,edge}$ is its mass inside the edge. $M_{\rm NFW}$ has the Dutton \& Macci\`o concentration [DM14]. For galaxies whose phantom already uses up their collapse mass, $f_{\rm ex}=0$ and the rule is the law.

No constant is added, but $M_{\rm coll}$ is history, not a prediction, so a non-circular test needs it measured. Before turnaround the fluid behaves as CDM, so the programme inherits $\Lambda$CDM's collapse statistics. For single galaxies weak lensing measures the collapse mass directly.

\section{Tests with measured collapse masses}\label{sec:tests}

\textbf{Colour-blind masses break the spirals (lane CFG35).} The Moster et al.\ relation [MNW13] puts every galaxy with $\log M_*\approx11.4$ in a $\sim10^{14}\,M_\odot$ collapse. The rule then closes the X-ray ellipticals ($-0.013$/$-0.024$\,dex). It also raises the outer rotation speed of the most massive SPARC spirals by up to $+0.30$\,dex (UGC 2885), and their rotation curves follow the law. The rule cannot take a colour-blind relation.

\textbf{Measured colour-split masses (lane CFG36).} Mandelbaum et al.\ 2016 [M16] measured the mean halo mass of central galaxies by weak lensing, separately for passive and star-forming systems. Their Table 3 is transcribed with provenance in the repository. At fixed stellar mass, passive centrals sit in halos 3--7$\times$ heavier. With the red relation for early types and the blue one for late types:
\begin{itemize}
\item every late-type SPARC spiral in the table's range has $f_{\rm ex}=0$;
\item the X-ray ellipticals half-close ($+0.125$/$+0.122$\,dex, 1.0$\sigma$);
\item the one massive S0 in SPARC, UGC 2487, would rotate $+0.14$\,dex faster than observed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Passive disks (lane CFG37).} Sixteen ATLAS$^{\rm 3D}$ early types with HI circular velocities at 8--28\,kpc (den Heijer et al.\ 2015 [dH15]) follow the law at $+0.026\pm0.085$\,dex (0.3$\sigma$). With $\log M_*=10.3$--$10.9$ their collapse masses are used up by the phantom ($f_{\rm ex}=0$ in all sixteen), so the rule predicts the same thing and passes too.

\textbf{Massive early types (lane CFG38).} The SLUGGS survey measured globular-cluster dispersion profiles of 19 early types out to 5--15 effective radii (Forbes et al.\ 2017 [F17]). The law leaves them $+0.080\pm0.024$\,dex short (3.3$\sigma$), worst in the most massive (M87 $+0.28$). With the red collapse masses the rule adds debris to the ten galaxies above $\log M_*\approx11.2$ and fits the sample at $+0.007\pm0.017$\,dex (0.4$\sigma$). M87 moves to $+0.08$, and the rise of the offset with mass disappears.

\textbf{The harness (lane CFG39).} The rule changes only systems with $f_{\rm ex}>0$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{SPARC:} the rms moves from 0.1003 to 0.1012\,dex; only UGC 2487 is touched.
\item \textbf{Cold budget:} it gains at most 0.007 of $\Omega$ and stays within $\Omega_c$.
\item \textbf{KiDS:} the isolated lenses [Bro21] carry no debris.
\item \textbf{X-COP and the Bullet:} they cannot be re-scored without circularity. The rule can only raise their dark mass.
\end{itemize}
The score therefore stays at 42/48, inferred rather than established: the lane's declared test failed on an artefact of its own threshold scan, which is recorded.

\section{What is not claimed}\label{sec:not}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The data do not favour the framework over $\Lambda$CDM.} $\Lambda$CDM on the same measured halos (stars plus the full halo, no phantom) sits at $-0.037\pm0.017$\,dex on the globular clusters (2.1$\sigma$; a post-hoc comparison). It has its own modelling freedom: contraction, anisotropy, the IMF.
\item \textbf{The rule is not a prediction of individual collapse masses.} It inherits the stellar-to-halo relation's systematics: stellar-mass conventions, and centrals versus satellites (M87 is a cluster central; NGC 4374, 4365 and 4649 are Virgo members).
\item \textbf{Groups and clusters are not scored.} Every collapse-mass estimate available here comes from their own mass.
\item \textbf{One galaxy disagrees.} UGC 2487 is one massive S0 with a measured rotation curve.
\end{itemize}

\section{Tests that decide it}\label{sec:tests2}

The rule makes a sharp prediction where it differs from the law. \textbf{Massive passive disks ($\log M_*>11.2$) with extended HI or tracer kinematics must rotate faster than the law at large radii, by the amount their colour's collapse mass implies. Star-forming disks of the same stellar mass must not.} That sample is small but observable. The rule also inherits the programme's standing tests: Gaia DR4 wide binaries (2 December 2026) and $a_0$ at $z\approx2.5$.
