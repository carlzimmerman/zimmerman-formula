TITLE
Crispy Fried Chicken Conjecture on Galaxy Dynamics

ABSTRACT (249 words; Papers max 250)
Galaxies spin as if they feel more pull than their visible matter can supply. The extra pull appears below one special acceleration, a0 ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². This number is close to ½ c √(Gρ_Λ), where ρ_Λ is the density of the cosmological constant (dark energy). We call the guess a0 = κ c √(Gρ_Λ), with κ a pure number, the Crispy Fried Chicken conjecture, and test it with every step of the algebra shown. First, what is κ? On 175 nearby galaxies (the SPARC sample) three methods give 0.45 ± 0.07, 0.58 ± 0.18 and 0.43 ± 0.08. All agree with 1/2, and with other simple numbers. The answer depends on how much mass each unit of starlight carries, so 1/2 is a fitted value, not a derived one. Second, did a0 change over cosmic time? If it is tied to dark energy, it did not. If it is tied to the expansion rate, it was 3.8 times larger at redshift z = 2.5. The standard dark-matter model (ΛCDM) predicts about 1.6 times. Working out a0 from one galaxy magnifies the error in its visible mass by 4 or more at the accelerations of today's distant galaxies, so they cannot decide. About eight magnified (lensed) discs at z ≃ 2.5 would give 20:1 odds if their mass errors were independent. If the errors share a common calibration error of 0.06 dex (15 per cent), 34 are needed, and above that the sample average alone can never reach 20:1.
