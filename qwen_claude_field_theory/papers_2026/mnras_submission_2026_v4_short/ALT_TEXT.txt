Figure 1
Two panels. Panel (a): the change of the acceleration scale a0 since today, in powers of ten, against redshift from 0 to 3.5. A thick horizontal line at zero is the prediction of a scale tied to the cosmological constant. A dashed curve, for a scale tied to the critical density (proportional to the expansion rate), rises to 0.58 at redshift 2.5 and about 0.75 at redshift 3.5. A dot-dashed curve, for dark-matter haloes of one trillion solar masses, rises to 0.33 at redshift 2.5. Four open circles at redshifts 1, 2, 2.5 and 3, for the lighter haloes of the discs proposed for the test, lie below it, at 0.06, 0.16, 0.22 and 0.27. Panel (b): the factors by which errors are multiplied when a0 is worked out from one galaxy, against the ratio of visible acceleration to a0 on a logarithmic axis from 0.005 to 16. A solid curve (errors in the observed acceleration) starts at 2 and a dashed curve (errors in the visible mass) at 1; both stay nearly flat below a ratio of 0.3, a region shaded green and labelled as the proposed gate, then rise steeply, the dashed curve passing 3.8 at a ratio of 2.4 and 8 near a ratio of 6.4. A grey band from 0.9 to 7.2 marks where the central 68 per cent of the RC100 galaxies lie.
