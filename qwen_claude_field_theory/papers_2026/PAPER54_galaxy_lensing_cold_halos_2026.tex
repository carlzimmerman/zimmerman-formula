{\Large\bfseries No Edge to the Halo: KiDS-1000 Galaxy Lensing Against\\ a Dark-Energy-Tied Gravity Framework's Cold Halos}\\[6pt]

\begin{abstract}\noindent
In a gravity framework where the galactic acceleration scale is set by the dark-energy density, $a_0=\tfrac12c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$ (the $\tfrac12$ is fitted), each galaxy's extra lensing mass is a collisionless ``cold'' component. That component settles onto the law $g_N=g^2/(g+a_0)$. We test four pre-registered statements about it against the KiDS-1000 lensing rotation curves of isolated galaxies (Brouwer et al.\ 2021; 4 stellar-mass bins, full covariance).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The edge is excluded.} A halo that stops where it has used up the galaxy's own cosmic share of cold energy ($5.364\,M_b$, at $5.84\,r_M$) is excluded: $\Delta\chi^2=+197$ within 0.31\,Mpc and $+753$ over all radii, on both $a_0$ footings.
\item \textbf{The framework's own infall repairs it.} The framework's collisionless infall fills onto the law out to 20--50\,$r_M$, and fits as well as an edgeless law ($\Delta\chi^2=+0.1$), provided each galaxy draws on $N\ge3$--4 times its own baryons.
\item \textbf{The supply needs no fit inside 0.31\,Mpc.} Taking $N=1/f_{\rm ret}$ from the baryon census used in the framework's simulations ($N\approx6$--10 at these masses) fits within 0.31\,Mpc with no fitted parameter ($\Delta\chi^2=-0.2$ and $-1.5$ for the two footings).
\item \textbf{Neighbours narrow the outer gap.} Beyond each catchment edge (0.32--0.45\,Mpc), adding a two-halo term from the framework's own billion-particle simulation narrows the outer deficit from $\Delta\chi^2=+149$ to $+11$, with an effective bias of 0.2--0.5.
\end{enumerate}
The absolute fit is poor for every model, including the plain law ($\chi^2\approx160$ on 52 degrees of freedom). The misfit sits in the most massive bins: the known massive and early-type lensing excess.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Settled profile.} For a galaxy of baryonic mass $M_b$, the settled cold mass is
$$M_c(r)=\tfrac12M_b\left(\sqrt{1+4r^2/r_M^2}-1\right),\qquad r_M=\sqrt{GM_b/a_0}.$$
Far out it behaves as an isothermal sphere with locked amplitude, $\Delta\Sigma\to\sqrt{GM_ba_0}/(4GR)$.
\item \textbf{Data.} The KiDS-1000 lensing rotation curves of isolated galaxies [1]. They span 0.035--2.6\,Mpc in four stellar-mass bins ($\log M_\star=8.5$--11), with the full $60\times60$ covariance and the multiplicative bias applied.
\item \textbf{Fitting.} Baryonic masses are profiled per bin, with the same freedom for every model.
\item \textbf{Ranges.} The primary range is $R\le0.31$\,Mpc, where the isolated sample is clean. All radii are secondary.
\item \textbf{Footings.} $a_0=9.36\times10^{-11}$ (canonical) and $1.13\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$ (alternative), never pooled.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Controls.} Every lane passed its reproduction checks. Each deliberately broken variant reproduced the failure it should, except two:
\begin{itemize}
\item In the two-halo lane, a $\sigma_8$ cross-check failed by 3.2\% (a 2\% tolerance). The amplitude is absorbed by the profiled bias.
\item The no-exclusion variant shifted the bias in only two of the four bins.
\end{itemize}

\section{Interpretation and limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Where the cold mass is.} The data rule out a cold halo confined to each galaxy's own cosmic share. They are consistent with cold energy gathered from a larger catchment by collisionless infall, which is the framework's own dynamics.
\item \textbf{The supply factor is an input.} $N$ comes from an observational baryon census. A framework-native derivation was attempted and failed: an energy-budget model retains almost all baryons.
\item \textbf{Inherited simulation inputs.} The two-halo term uses a simulation with inherited $\Lambda$CDM initial conditions. A native-input rerun is queued.
\item \textbf{The absolute fit is poor for all models.} The heavy bins dominate it: the inner points are over-predicted and the 0.2--1.4\,Mpc points under-predicted. This is the massive and early-type excess, which the framework does not yet explain.
\end{itemize}
