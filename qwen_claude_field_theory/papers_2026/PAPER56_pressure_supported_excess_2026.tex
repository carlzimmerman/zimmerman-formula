\begin{abstract}\noindent
We report two more days of pre-registered tests of a gravity framework whose galactic law is $g_N=g^2/(g+a_0)$ with $a_0=\tfrac12 c\sqrt{G\rho_{\rm DE}}$ (the $\tfrac12$ is fitted). Its extra gravitating mass is a collisionless ``cold energy'' component, not a particle. Every lane froze its criteria before computing and ran a mutation control. Post-hoc amendments are disclosed, and both $a_0$ footings ($9.36\times10^{-11}$ and $1.13\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$) are reported and never pooled.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Shear.} Rerun from the framework's own early-universe inputs, the structure stays CONSISTENT with raw KiDS-1000 shear on both footings against the plain-gravity control NCS0, which is matched in initial conditions only and has no gas expulsion ($\Delta\chi^2=-10.08$ canonical, $-9.39$ alternative; Correction v1.1). Plain gravity with the framework's census gas expulsion scores $-11.87$ against NCS0, and the framework scores $+1.78$ / $+2.47$ against it, within noise: KiDS cannot tell the capped cold sector from plain gravity plus gas expulsion, and the result shows that the capped cold sector does not overproduce structure, not a preference for the law (Correction v1.1). On the alternative footing DES Y3 is diagnostic and also CONSISTENT ($-2.15$), but plain gravity with the expulsion gives $-2.17$ (Correction v1.1). Shear $\Delta\chi^2$ sizes are set mainly by the lensing amplitude, and no arm fits well in absolute terms (Correction v1.1). The engine that produces the pass has no established physical parent (PAPER57); per CFG963 that is not load-bearing for the shear result (Correction v1.1).
\item \textbf{CMB lensing.} On raw Planck PR4 lensing ($L$ 8--400) the four runs cannot be told from their controls. They are CONSISTENT only under a post-hoc amendment, and the test has little power. A sealed forecast finds that S4-class lensing at $L\approx1000$--2000 could separate them; ACT-like data and Planck's multipole range cannot.
\item \textbf{The pressure-supported excess.} Every system with an excess over the law (dwarfs, spheroidal lenses, cluster cores) is pressure-supported, and no rotating system has one. But no single kinematic measure or local density sets the amounts, none of five candidate selectors fits all the facts, and the record's own cold-energy equations cannot select on kinematics at all. The cluster-core excess is not a baryon-census error, and it survives four small reframings. A stellar-IMF correction removes the red-lens excess if discs are not bottom-heavy; after it, one velocity-scale selector fits the galaxy-scale facts, conditionally, but not the clusters. The dwarf excess survives every reframing tried. A fossil $a_0$ helps only under the rival $a_0\propto H(z)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}

\section{The native-input shear reruns}

Two pre-registered addenda reran it from the framework's own early-universe inputs: the Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE values of $\omega_b$, $\omega_c$, $A_s$, $n_s$ and $\theta_*$, passed through CAMB in the era before the law switches on, with the framework's own turnaround and catchment. CFG654 A2 reran the canonical footing as NC1x; A3, frozen before its job started, reran the alternative footing as NC2x. The control, NCS0, is the same pipeline with every module off (plain Poisson gravity) on the same initial conditions; it has no $a_0$, so it serves both footings, but it is matched in initial conditions only: it applies no gas expulsion, while NC1x and NC2x include the census gas expulsion M3 (CFG963; Correction v1.1). The framework beats NCS0 by suppressing small-scale power, and the suppression is the gas expulsion's: plain gravity with the same expulsion reaches $\chi^2_{\rm min}=142.89$, as good a fit or slightly better (Correction v1.1).
\item \textbf{Inputs still shared with standard cosmology:} the Planck parameter column (from a base-$\Lambda$CDM chain), $H_0$ from $\theta_*$, massless neutrinos, the empirical retained-fraction census, the cooling inputs, and the survey nuisances and covariances.
\item \textbf{The engine has no established physical parent.} The companion note PAPER57 finds that the engine producing both passes has a phantom that is a global QUMOND field with a satellite external-field effect, applies the law only to large retained-baryon systems at this resolution, and relocates its source in a way that cannot conserve momentum, and that no tested parent theory contains it; the pass is a computational result of that engine. \section{The pressure-supported excess}
The previous note left two open excesses over the law at low acceleration: spheroidal (red) isolated lenses, and Milky Way and M31 satellite dwarfs. Cluster cores carry a third (about $\times1.8$ in the core, from earlier lanes). The lanes below treat the three together.

\item \textbf{The ordering is real.} At matched $g_{\rm bar}$, pressure-supported classes sit above rotation-supported ones. The ordering statistic is $T=+0.528$ with permutation $p=0.002$. Spheroidal lenses sit above disc-like lenses by $\Delta E=+0.29$ in natural-log units ($z=+3.5$). No rotating cell carries an excess.
\item \textbf{There is no single kinematic form.} A linear form $E=A f_P$ gives $\chi^2=89.4$ for 32 degrees of freedom on the canonical footing and 93.5 on the alternative ($p<10^{-6}$ on both), and a step at $v/\sigma=\sqrt3$ does no better. The dwarfs carry 3--5 times the spheroidal lenses' excess at the same $g_{\rm bar}$, and at $f_P\approx1$ the clusters' excess rises with $g_{\rm bar}$ while the dwarfs' falls.
\item \textbf{Class and fragility.} The class is SELECTOR CONTRADICTED on both footings, as frozen and under a post-hoc amendment (identical results). It is FRAGILE: doubling the per-cell systematic floor to 0.10\,dex turns the canonical footing SUPPORTED and the alternative NOT DIAGNOSTIC. Lens kinematics are a S\'ersic-index proxy, the dwarf $f_P$ is a class value, early types with measured dynamics are not tested, and globular clusters carry no class weight.
\item \textbf{Density does not order it either (CFG914).} At the same local density, dwarfs carry an excess where SPARC discs do not, and cluster cores carry one where blue lenses do not. Clusters rise and red lenses fall with density. Local density is excluded as the unifying cause on both footings and in all ten variants.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The record's equations cannot select on kinematics}
In the record's cold-energy system (CFG638) the baryons enter only through their density: in the potential, in the settling clock, in the law's phantom target and in the region indicators. No baryon velocity or stress appears. In the stationary state the cold energy settles onto the phantom target, which in spherical symmetry is fixed by the enclosed baryonic mass. A rotating system and a dispersion-supported one with the same enclosed baryonic mass therefore settle to the same $g(r)=g_{\rm law}$: the equations cannot order an excess by kinematic state, and they produce no excess over the law in equilibrium. CFG888 reached the same point from the action: the law's source is the baryons in every formulation in the record, and a cold-energy clump has no self-gravity in the canonical action. Any kinematic selector is therefore a new posit: a coupling to the baryons' stress (with a new velocity scale), a retention rule that depends on kinematic state, or a non-spherical geometric effect not yet calculated.

\subsection{The dwarf excess is not environmental}
The excess is not tied to gas lost by stripping (CFG860, NOT GAS on both footings): isolated gas-free and gas-bearing dwarfs carry it too. With gas counted, all 11 isolated Local Group dwarfs sit $+0.194\pm0.077$\,dex over the law and $-0.100\pm0.091$\,dex below the satellites (CFG874), UNDECIDED between a satellite property and a small-system property; the 11 are the whole supply. There is no significant trend with host distance (CFG875, slope $-0.190\pm0.150$\,dex per dex, UNDECIDED).

\subsection{Cluster cores are not a census error}
CFG922 re-checked the cluster-core excess ($A_{\rm MID}=1.91$ canonical, 1.75 alternative, for 318 eRASS1 clusters) against every baryon component on disk.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{What is needed.} To bring $A_{\rm MID}$ to 1 the law needs extra concentrated baryons inside 0.5\,Mpc equal to 2.24 (canonical) or 1.84 (alternative) times the hot gas there.
\item \textbf{What cannot supply it.} Stars would need 32.1 / 26.6 times the top of the measured stellar bracket. A hot-gas renormalisation would need 3.68 / 3.21, against an upper end of 1.50 measured from 68 cross-matched clusters. Radius convention, hydrostatic bias, clumping and Eddington bias cannot (three have the wrong sign), nor can cold atomic gas or a bottom-heavy IMF.
\item \textbf{All at once.} With every testable component at its upper end the core still sits at 1.49 / 1.37 ($Z=+5.2$ / $+4.3$). Any baryon fix that removes the core excess also breaks the outer agreement.
\item \textbf{Untestable.} Intracluster light and BCG molecular gas have no tables on disk, and either would need 22--27 times the stellar bracket.
\item \textbf{Class.} EXCESS STANDS on both footings and both kernels, as frozen and under a post-hoc census amendment that moved no class. The core excess is a property of the extra mass, not of the census.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Five candidate selectors all fail}
CFG921 graded five candidate new selectors on all the facts above, with a trials factor of 5. Four have one constant each, fixed once on the 83 dwarfs; the fifth is a geometric effect with no constant.
P1, a baryon-stress coupling, cannot even be calibrated: there is no equilibrium at the dwarf amplitude. P2, velocity-space capture, fails on clusters ($z=-7.3$ / $-6.2$) and red lenses ($-4.4$ / $-3.0$). P3, late binding plus escape, fails on clusters, red lenses and the dwarf size split. P4, phase-space-density capture, fails on clusters ($-7.3$ / $-6.2$); its canonical red-lens miss (3.8$\sigma$ at the least severe corner) does not reach the trials-factor bar. P5, the QUMOND geometry of spheres versus discs, has the right sign for spheres but is about 40 times too small. None survives on either footing. The reason is structural. Dwarfs ($\sigma\sim10$\,km/s) and red lenses ($\sigma\sim100$\,km/s) need a 3--5$\times$ suppression between them. Selectors with a velocity scale give $\gtrsim100\times$, and scale-free ones give $1.25\times$. The cluster excess rises with $g_{\rm bar}$, where every capture or latch posit gives zero.

\subsection{The dwarf route and its conditions}
The dwarf excess has been modelled as a retained cold-energy clump. Three lanes limit that picture, and mobility rules do not rescue it.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No universal clump velocity (CFG880).} Across 83 dwarfs the intrinsic scatter in the log of the clump velocity is 0.380 (95\%: 0.281--0.510, canonical; the alternative also gives 0.380), about $\times1.46$ per dwarf. NOT UNIVERSAL on both footings and in all six variants.
\item \textbf{The clump relations are not derived (CFG888).} The clump scale relations used in earlier fits do not follow from the action. A finite Newtonian clump needs a truncation that adds a parameter.
\item \textbf{The gate does not keep the clump (CFG889).} With orbits mixing, the clump erodes in all eight cells, with a gated half-life of 105--148\,Myr$/\alpha$. This lane is inconclusive as frozen, because one check failed.
\item \textbf{One local law (CFG913).} No single local mobility law, in any of 42 pre-declared forms, reconciles mergers, galaxies and retained clumps. A galaxy's dense inner region must stay mobile to finish settling.
\item \textbf{A history label (CFG915).} Making cold energy bound at formation immobile, and later arrivals mobile, only moves the obstruction to cluster outer profiles. It is CONTRADICTED under both label rules.
\item \textbf{Release (CFG916).} Adding release beyond the tidal radius is CONTRADICTED as posed. It is UNDERDETERMINED only with a new capped-capture rule.
\end{itemize}
What survives is one dwarf route. It needs cold energy bound at formation to be fully immobile, dwarfs to have lost a median $\times4.32$ of their baryons after formation, and binding to come before that loss. Three lanes test its conditions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Chemistry (CFG920): UNDERDETERMINED on both footings.} The sample's median required loss is 3.81 (canonical) or 3.18 (alternative) for the 20 dwarfs with metallicities. The chemistry holds this at a low iron yield and excludes it at a high one. Per dwarf (canonical, $1\sigma$): 1 compatible, 15 underdetermined, 4 contradicted. The metal-poor gas-free dSphs strain it unless the iron yield is low, winds are metal-enhanced, or binding is late.
\item \textbf{Clock (CFG923): UNDERDETERMINED overall, because the footings disagree.} The framework's own formation clock binds too early for most dwarfs that need late binding: the universal clock is CONTRADICTED on the canonical footing and UNDERDETERMINED on the alternative, and the orbital clock is CONTRADICTED on both. Late binding survives only as a new posit, a trigger 4.9--12.7\,Gyr after star formation begins in some dwarfs and not others. The hardest cases are isolated field dwarfs, so the trigger would have to be internal.
\item \textbf{Receiver (CFG918).} For Draco, Sextans, Fornax and Leo~I, the route needs somewhere to put the energy and angular momentum it sheds. A bound outer envelope is excluded for Sextans in one mass branch, and for Draco and Sextans when transfer starts at the catchment radius. With escape allowed, the capacity is there except in one indeterminate cell. Only an escaping receiver remains; this tests necessary feasibility, not formation.
\end{itemize}

\section{What this establishes, and what it does not}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{What holds, conditionally.} The shear pass survives the framework's own early-universe inputs on both footings (CONSISTENT; amplitude-driven; neither arm a good absolute fit). Against plain gravity with the same gas expulsion it is also CONSISTENT ($+1.78$ / $+2.47$), so KiDS cannot tell the capped cold sector from plain gravity plus gas expulsion; the pass shows only that the capped cold sector does not overproduce structure (CFG963; Correction v1.1). CMB lensing at $L\le400$ does not contradict it (post-hoc amendment; little power). CONSISTENT is a class, not a preference.
\item \textbf{What is not established.} A physical parent for the engine that produces the pass (PAPER57), and any CMB-lensing evidence for the framework; S4-class high-$L$ lensing is a sealed future test. Also not established: any shear preference for the law over plain gravity with gas expulsion (CFG963; Correction v1.1).
\item \textbf{What is open: the pressure-supported excess.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The empirical pattern is firm: pressure-supported systems carry an excess and rotating ones do not. The record's equations produce no excess in equilibrium.
  \item None of these sets the amounts: local density, one kinematic measure, five candidate selectors on all the facts, environment, the dispersion estimator, self-sourcing, an external field, the lensing estimator, or a fossil of the framework's own $a_0(z)$.
  \item The cluster-core excess is not a census error, and no small reframing removes it. The red-lens excess may be a stellar-IMF effect if discs are not bottom-heavy; after that correction one selector fits the galaxy-scale facts (conditional, post hoc), not the clusters.
  \item Nothing tested explains the dwarf excess. The one dwarf route left needs an immobile bound component, a median $\times4.3$ baryon loss, a late internal binding trigger and an escaping receiver. Each is a new posit or a strained condition.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{What is not derived.} $\kappa=\tfrac12$, the cold-to-baryon supply ratio and the retained fraction remain inputs.
\end{itemize}
