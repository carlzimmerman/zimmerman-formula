\begin{abstract}\noindent
This programme studies gravity in which the galactic acceleration scale is set by dark energy, $a_0=\kappa c\sqrt{G\rho_\Lambda}$ with $\kappa=\tfrac12$ fitted (equivalently $G\rho_\Lambda=4a_0^2$, $\Lambda=32\pi a_0^2/c^4$), and in which the gravitating mass that baryons do not supply is a collisionless \emph{cold dark energy}: a field component with $w\approx0$, paired with dark energy, not a particle. Following the owner's decision of 2026-10-25, this note presents the theory as the acceleration law plus a cold dark energy sector whose halo cores are set by $a_0$. Every result below comes from a lane that froze its criteria before computing and ran a mutation control, on both $a_0$ footings ($9.36\times10^{-11}$ and $1.13\times10^{-10}$\,m\,s$^{-2}$, never pooled; pairs are canonical / alternative).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The law.} At galaxy scale the law passes:
\begin{itemize}
\item the SPARC radial-acceleration relation (rms 0.054\,dex);
\item MeerKAT's local $a_0$;
\item isolated-disc weak lensing ($A=1.016\pm0.086$);
\item a screened Solar System.
\end{itemize}
Its $a_0$ tracks dark energy: the native-ruler BAO reconstruction gives $a_0(z)/a_0(0)$ in [0.76, 1.03] at $z=1$ (68\%).
\item \textbf{The cold sector, closed doors.} Twelve mechanisms that would add, heat, select or redistribute cold dark energy were tested and closed. These include hot-gas tracking (the measured sign is opposite), any dark-energy-keyed heater (a certified no-fold theorem), feedback, baryonic contraction, and member-halo nesting.
\item \textbf{The cold sector, the core law.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The anchor.} Halos cored at the surface density $\Sigma_{a_0}=a_0/(2\pi G)$ (107 / 129\,M$_\odot$\,pc$^{-2}$) pass SPARC. Their pull is certified never to exceed $\tfrac{9}{32}a_0$ at any radius.
\item \emph{The law.} With a self-coupled cold dark energy the core law is $\Sigma_0^2=9\sigma^{1+n}/(4\pi GKt)$ (Lean-certified). Anchoring $K$ by $\Sigma_{a_0}$ at the median galaxy and fixing the one exponent on SPARC alone ($q_f=0.892$ / 0.748) improves SPARC to rms 0.0605 / 0.0591.
\item \emph{Out of sample.} It predicts 14 of 15 cluster cells of 326 eRASS1 clusters that were never fitted. The heavy-core misfit falls from $\sum|Z|=17.61$ to 5.31 (canonical).
\item \emph{Physics reading.} The implied self-interaction falls with velocity, $n=0.784$ / 0.496.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Open.} The same law fails the faint dwarfs (48--65\% exceed their cap). Rate saturation, gravothermal collapse and a self-consistent halo dispersion are each ruled out as the fix. The dwarf excess behaves like a separate small-scale floor. The innermost cluster band is mildly over-predicted. No native CMB or growth computation exists yet for the self-coupled sector.
\item \textbf{Certified mathematics.} Sixteen theorems (Lean~4 + Mathlib, no \texttt{sorry}, standard axioms only, mutation copies fail to compile) carry the core-law chain from the Burkert mass to the dwarf tests.
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}

\section{The acceleration law}
The law is the simple MOND relation on baryons, $g_N=g^2/(g+a_0)$, with $a_0$ tied to the dark-energy density. In its own regime of isolated systems it passes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Rotation curves:} the SPARC radial-acceleration relation, rms 0.054\,dex on both footings (P4; EM1 reproduction 0.0541 / 0.0537).
\item \textbf{The local scale:} MeerKAT's $a_0$ sits $+0.032$\,dex from SPARC's with the same recipe (PAPER60).
\item \textbf{Isolated-disc lensing:} KiDS-1000 blue lenses on the law, $A=1.016\pm0.086$ (CFG829; reproduced in HG2 as $1.0165\pm0.0859$).
\item \textbf{The Solar System:} screened, Cassini passes (PAPER58).
\item \textbf{$a_0$ tracks dark energy.} With the native sound horizon, DESI BAO plus Pantheon+ reconstruct $\sqrt{X}=a_0(z)/a_0(0)$ in [0.76, 1.03], [0.71, 1.12] and [0.32, 1.21] at $z=1$, 1.5 and 2.33 (68\%). The rival $a_0\propto H(z)$ is far outside these intervals. A constant dark energy is not rejected (1.97$\sigma$, class CONSISTENT WITH CONSTANT; CFG689, 6c064b83a3). The decisive point is $z\approx2.5$ at 0.10\,dex.
\end{itemize}
The law does not, by itself, explain the cluster cores, the faint dwarfs or the growth of structure. Those need the cold sector.

\section{The core law}
\subsection{Cores at the $a_0$ surface density}
In route E the cold dark energy gravitates normally and forms cored (Burkert) halos. X38 (f72216ad98) set the core surface density to
\[
\rho_0r_0=\Sigma_{a_0}=\frac{a_0}{2\pi G}=107\ /\ 129\ \mathrm{M_\odot\,pc^{-2}},
\]
with no new constant.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{SPARC:} passes, rms 0.0795 / 0.0729 at median $\Upsilon$ 0.77 / 0.60.
\item \textbf{Cluster OUTER bands:} pass. Every earlier added-component model overshot them.
\item \textbf{Fails:} the cores of the heaviest, hottest clusters.
\end{itemize}
The anchor is comparable to the empirical constant core surface densities of disc galaxies (Donato et al.\ 2009); that is context, not a test. A fixed-$\Sigma$ Burkert halo's pull is $G M/r^2=\pi G\Sigma\,\mathrm{Bk}(x)/x^2$ with $x=r/r_0$, so over every core radius it is capped: certified $\le\tfrac{9}{32}a_0$, numerically $0.254484\,a_0$ (LN1). On X1's 89 dwarfs per footing this cap is CONSISTENT: 1.1\% exceed it at $z\ge2$, with median required fraction $S=0.53$ / 0.44 (CD1, 809e61b319).

\subsection{A self-coupled cold dark energy}
Let the cold dark energy interact with itself, with cross-section per mass $\propto v^{-n}$, and let the core form where rate $\times$ age $=1$ on an isothermal core. Then
\[
\Sigma_0^2=\frac{9\,\sigma^{1+n}}{4\pi G K t},\qquad \Sigma_0\propto\sigma^{q}t^{-1/2},\quad q=\frac{1+n}{2}
\]
(LN1, theorem T7). Fixing $K$ so that the median SPARC galaxy ($\sigma_{\rm ref}=70.50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, $V_{\rm last}/\sqrt2$) keeps $\Sigma_{a_0}$ makes $a_0$ set the coupling's normalisation:
\[
\Sigma_i=\Sigma_{a_0}\,(\sigma_i/\sigma_{\rm ref})^{q}.
\]
At the fixed candidate $q=\tfrac13$, SPARC improves on X38 (rms 0.0686 / 0.0636) and the clusters' failing $\sum|Z|$ falls from 17.61 to 11.40 with no new failure (SC1, d2f4a621d8; frozen class SC PASSES SPARC ONLY).

\subsection{Physics reading}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The self-interaction falls with velocity.} $q=(1+n)/2$ gives $n=0.784$ / 0.496 (LN2). The cross-section per mass falls with velocity, the conventional direction for velocity-dependent self-interacting dark sectors.
\item \textbf{What differs from such a sector.} It differs in two places: the normalisation is fixed by $a_0$, hence by $\Lambda$, and the component is a field paired with dark energy, not a particle species.
\item \textbf{It never reaches the fluid regime.} With the coupling anchored this way, the Knudsen number is certified to be
\[
\mathrm{Kn}=\frac{4}{3\sqrt\pi}\,\frac{2a_0t}{\sigma_{\rm ref}}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\rm ref}}\Big)^{q-1},
\]
which evaluates to 653--1158 (canonical) and 539--2048 (alternative) from 5 to 1000\,km\,s$^{-1}$. The sector is in the long-mean-free-path regime everywhere.
\end{itemize}

\section{The dwarfs: the open problem}
Below $\sigma\approx20$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ the core law gives cores too shallow for the faint dwarfs, which need 2--10 times more central pull. DW1 (279b4aa47c / 0dd2bffffa) tested three stated mechanisms:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{M1, rate saturation:} RULED OUT ($\mathrm{Kn}\gg1$).
\item \textbf{M2, gravothermal collapse:} RULED OUT. Under the core law $\Gamma t=1$ in every system, a certified structural statement.
\item \textbf{M3, the halo's own dispersion:} FAILS. The certified unique solve gives $\sigma_h$ only about twice the stellar $\sigma$; 64.0\% / 40.4\% of dwarfs still exceed the cap.
\end{itemize}
Independently:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The floor:} the dwarf extra behaves as a velocity floor of about 4--4.6\,km\,s$^{-1}$ (X24, 19d31a99d9).
\item \textbf{Its radial form:} $g\propto r^{-0.8}$ (X28, dad0c9f1fe).
\item \textbf{Its equation of state:} $\Gamma=0.88$ $[0.81,0.94]$, softer than isothermal, which no named equation of state supplies (DF1).
\end{itemize}
The dwarf excess needs physics that acts only at small scales; it is the second blank in the theory.
