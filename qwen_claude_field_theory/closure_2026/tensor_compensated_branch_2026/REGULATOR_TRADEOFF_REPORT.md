# Local-regulator tradeoff

Consider the linear equations generated by the current derivative multiplier
architecture,

\[
R+k^2\Lambda=0,\qquad k^2d+F(k^2)\Lambda=0,
\]

where (d=\Phi-\Psi) (up to a nonzero normalization) and (F) is any local
quadratic regulator kernel.  Symbolic elimination gives

\[
\Lambda=-R/k^2,\qquad d=F(k^2)R/k^4.
\]

Therefore a nonzero regulator produces slip for every generic (R\ne0\), and
the unregularized choice (F=0) keeps the (1/k^2) multiplier pole.  A
constant regulator is worse ((d\sim k^{-4})); higher-derivative local terms
still leave an infrared pole.  `regulator_tradeoff_gate.py` derives the five
identities without hard-coded ranks, PPN values, or DOF counts and exits 0.

This is a conditional no-go for local quadratic regularizations of the present
derivative tensor-compensator architecture.  Escaping it requires changing the
metric coupling itself, accepting slip, or introducing a genuinely nonlocal/
boundary prescription; none is yet a closed single-metric theory.
