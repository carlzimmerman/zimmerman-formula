## Route (i) — the AeST two-potential / pressure-vs-rotation lensing — CLOSED as an escape, but the physics is framework-FAVOURABLE
The most physical candidate: could a dispersion-supported (early) vs rotation-supported (late) system lens
DIFFERENTLY at fixed g_bar in AeST, even though the dynamical law is shared? **Verified from the AeST field
equations — NO.**
- **Verwayen, Skordis & Zlosnik 2024 (MNRAS 531 272, arXiv:2304.05134)**, the authoritative AeST quasistatic
  weak-field paper, states explicitly: *"the two standard weak-field metric potentials Φ and Ψ are equal. This
  ensures that the lensing mass is equal to the dynamical mass."* **No gravitational slip.** The weak-field
  equations (∇²Φ̃ + μ²Φ = 4πG_N ρ_b /(1+β₀), coupled to χ) depend **only on the baryonic density ρ_b(r)** — there is
  **no pressure or velocity-dispersion term**. Two systems with identical baryon distribution but different
  kinematic support produce the **identical** Φ=Ψ and the **identical** lensing.
- The Mistele AeST-lensing paper (arXiv:2301.03499) is consistent: matter couples through one metric with
  Φ=Φ̂+φ; lensing and dynamics use the **same** combined potential. No distinguished scalar lensing contribution.
- **CONSEQUENCE, both ways:** this CONFIRMS the load-bearing anchor — in AeST lensing mass = dynamical mass, so
  "lensing proves DM" reduces to the cluster-dynamical residual, NOT a separate proof. But it ALSO means the
  framework's OWN law **cannot** manufacture a morphology split: AeST is type-blind by construction. So route (i)
  gives the framework **no escape valve AND no new loss** — the split, IF intrinsic to the data, would falsify the
  law; the framework's prediction is therefore **one RAR for both types**, and the question becomes whether the
  data actually show a split or a systematic. That is routes (ii)–(iv).

## Route (ii) — EFE / environment — WRONG SIGN — CLOSED (and it would DEEPEN the puzzle)
Early types live in denser environments (higher g_ext). Direction check from the framework's own EFE: the EFE
**REDUCES** the inferred a_tot (suppresses the MOND boost) — Mistele's AeST paper states this directly ("the EFE
generally reduces the observationally inferred a_tot"). Higher g_ext → ETG g_obs pushed **DOWN**, below MOND. But
the observed ETG anomaly is an **EXCESS** (g_obs ABOVE MOND). **The dynamical EFE has the wrong sign to explain an
excess** — it would make the discrepancy worse, not better. Route (ii) is closed as a cure. (The denser-environment
effect that DOES help is not the EFE but the 2-halo lensing CONTAMINATION — an observational systematic — route iv.)

## Route (iii) — IMF / M*/L (heavier early-type stellar mass) — RIGHT SIGN, PARTIAL — LIVE-as-contributor
To erase the split you must INCREASE the inferred g_bar of ETGs (slide them right onto the relation): ETGs need

fix. IMF is a real partial contributor with the right sign, not a full cure.

## Route (iv) — the ISOLATION / selection systematic — THE LIVE RESOLUTION (published, by the RAR's own architects)
**This is the genuine live avenue, and it is strong.** **Mistele, McGaugh, Lelli, Schombert & Li 2024

> *"Contrary to previous studies, we find that early- and late-type galaxies lie on the same joint RAR when a
> sufficiently strict isolation criterion is adopted and their stellar and gas masses are estimated consistently."*

The mechanism (their Fig. 4, digitized here):
- ETG deviation from the RAR falls from **~7.5σ at R_isol=2.7 Mpc → ~0–1σ at R_isol=4.0 Mpc**. **LTGs are flat

- Physical cause: ETGs are **more clustered** (morphology–density relation), so Brouwer's 3 Mpc cut leaves the

  *"partly due to our somewhat larger baryonic masses for ETGs and partly … a stricter isolation criterion"*
  (route iv + route iii together). McGaugh's contemporaneous write-up: *"the distinction … goes away when
  R_isol = 4 Mpc/h70."*
