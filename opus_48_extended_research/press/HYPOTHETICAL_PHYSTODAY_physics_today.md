# The Acceleration That Dark Energy Built

**An independent researcher argues that MOND's mysterious a₀ is fixed by Λ. The functional form is over-determined and one knob away from zero parameters — but the value is posited, the lensing is unfinished, and the decisive test is hostage to DESI.**

There is a number that will not go away. In the rotation curves of galaxies, the Newtonian prediction and the observed kinematics begin to part company not at a fixed radius, not at a fixed mass, but below a fixed acceleration: a₀ ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². It is the scale Milgrom identified in 1983, the knee of the radial-acceleration relation, the coefficient buried inside the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation. And for fifty years it has carried an embarrassment of riches in the form of a numerical coincidence: a₀ sits within a factor of order unity of cH₀, and equally of c²√Λ — the acceleration scale you can build from the cosmological constant alone. Most workers have filed this under "suggestive, probably not fundamental." Carl Zimmerman, an independent researcher operating under the banner of Briar Creek Tech, has spent the past year arguing, in three openly posted papers, that it is causal.

His proposal — call it de Sitter–MOND — is compact enough to state in one line:

> a₀ = c² √(Λ/32π) = (c/2)√(G ρ_DE) = cH_Λ / Z,  with Z = √(32π/3) ≈ 5.789,

which evaluates to a₀ = 9.36 × 10⁻¹¹ m s⁻² when ρ_DE is the pure-Λ dark-energy density. The claim is not that this is a fit. It is that the MOND scale and the dark-energy scale are the same scale, and that the factor relating them is geometry.

## The mechanism: inertia, not a fifth force

What makes the proposal more than dimensional analysis is the mechanism it leans on, which is **modified inertia** of the de Sitter–Unruh type — a line that runs through Milgrom's 1999 modified-inertia program and the Deser–Levin treatment of detector response in de Sitter space. The physical picture is this. A universe with a positive cosmological constant has a de Sitter horizon, and that horizon carries an irreducible temperature floor, the Gibbons–Hawking temperature T_dS = ħH_Λ/2πk_B. An accelerated detector in such a spacetime does not see the flat-space Unruh spectrum; it sees a response governed by a combined scale that interpolates between the Unruh temperature ∝ a and the de Sitter floor ∝ H_Λ. The crossover happens when the proper acceleration drops to of order cH_Λ — that is, at a₀.

Zimmerman's reading is that this temperature floor manifests, for matter in near-free-fall, as extra resistance to being pushed: extra inertia. A star drifting in a galactic outskirt at 10⁻¹¹ m s⁻² is in precisely the regime where the de Sitter floor is no longer negligible against its own Unruh temperature, and the excess inertia, seen from outside, masquerades as excess gravity. There is no dark-matter particle. Crucially — and this is the structural payoff of doing it through *inertia* rather than through an added force — the effect switches off where accelerations are high. In the Solar System a₀/g ≈ 10⁻⁶, the de Sitter floor is utterly swamped, and the modification vanishes. The framework is **Cassini-safe by construction**, not by tuning, which is a genuine and underappreciated virtue of the modified-inertia route over fifth-force or extra-field implementations.

## Why the form is over-determined

The part of this work that deserves the most serious attention from theorists is not the coincidence itself but the claim that its **functional form is forced**. Several independent routes — horizon thermodynamics, a Friedmann-level energy argument, the CKN-type holographic bound, and a direct de Sitter–Unruh estimate — all return a₀ ∝ c²√Λ. The form, including its non-trivial half-integer power of π, is over-determined rather than chosen.

The constant deserves a second look, because its anatomy is transparent. Write the gravitational kernel as √(8π/3): the 8π is Einstein's, the 3 is the Friedmann factor in H² = (8πG/3)ρ. The half-integer power of π is therefore not an aesthetic flourish but an inheritance from the field equations and the FRW geometry. Folding in the dark-energy density ρ_Λ = Λc²/8πG and the modified-inertia coefficient gives a₀ = c²√(Λ/32π) directly. One does not get to choose the π; it is handed over by the same constants that appear in any Λ-cosmology.

## One knob, and a clean account of it

Here is the sharpest technical result of the program, and the one most worth scrutiny. The framework reduces to a **single free parameter**, κ = 1/2, and Zimmerman's one-parameter paper argues that this κ is not forceable by the usual structural constraints — ghost-freedom, unitarity, holographic entropy bounds, or a count of Standard-Model degrees of freedom — and is instead **pure geometry**.

The geometric identity he points to is genuinely clean. The coefficient 3/8π that sits at the heart of the kernel factors as

> 3/8π = (1/2) / (4π/3),

the ratio of the Schwarzschild factor 1/2 to the volume of the unit ball 4π/3. In the same breath the κ = 1/2 limit is identified with the **single-degree-of-freedom case of the Cohen–Kaplan–Nelson bound** — the UV–IR relation that ties an effective-field-theory cutoff to the size of the region through the threshold of black-hole formation. CKN with one degree of freedom is, in this reading, what fixes the relationship between the IR scale (the horizon) and the inertial response.

The honest accounting matters here, and Zimmerman makes it himself: this does **not** derive the *value* of a₀. κ = 1/2 is a geometric *posit*, defensible and economical but not a theorem. The theory is therefore **one-parameter, not zero-parameter**. What it buys, against the six-parameter ΛCDM concordance model plus an as-yet-undetected particle sector, is a striking economy: a single geometric knob standing in for the dark sector's phenomenology at galaxy scales. That is a real claim worth weighing — but it is a claim about *parameter count and structure*, not a derivation of nature's number, and the framework's own papers are careful to say so. Readers tempted to write "a₀ derived from first principles" should resist; the form is forced, the value is posited.

## The distinctive prediction: a₀ runs with dark energy

Where de Sitter–MOND parts company from ordinary MOND — and where it earns the right to be called a physical hypothesis rather than a relabeling — is its prediction that **a₀ is not constant in cosmic time**. If a₀ tracks the dark-energy density, then

> a₀(z) ∝ √ρ_DE(z),

and the moment dark energy is allowed to evolve, a₀ acquires a redshift dependence that ordinary MOND, with its fixed fundamental constant, flatly forbids. This is the framework's cleanest discriminator, and it is now testable rather than rhetorical.

Under the DESI DR2 preference for evolving dark energy (w₀ ≈ −0.75, wₐ ≈ −0.86), ρ_DE(z) is non-monotonic, and so is a₀(z): a bump of roughly +6% near z ≈ 0.4, in the neighborhood of the phantom-divide crossing, followed by a decline to about 0.74 of today's value by z ≈ 3. The observational handle is the **sign of the high-redshift baryonic Tully–Fisher offset**: distant galaxies should rotate a few percent slow — of order 7% at z ≈ 3 — and, tellingly, the √ρ_DE branch and a rival "rising-a₀" reading (which tracks cH(z) with the full matter term) diverge by a factor of about 7.5 at z = 3. These are not subtle variants of one another; they are opposite predictions, with flat-a₀ MOND sitting between them. DESI DR3 (around 2026–27) and high-redshift kinematics from ELT, JWST, and ALMA (roughly 2028 onward) are the instruments that will adjudicate.

There is also a second, entirely independent line of distinctive content. Because de Sitter–MOND singles out a preferred frame — it is, in the relevant sense, **Lorentz-violating** — it is itself a Standard-Model-Extension background. The same a₀ that bends rotation curves induces a computable, currently-bounded SME coefficient and a falsifiable theorem that the violation is **Lorentz-violating but CPT-preserving**. Whether or not the cosmological test lands, this furnishes a laboratory-scale, preferred-frame handle on the same physics — a welcome second axis, and one that situates the framework alongside Einstein-aether and khronometric theories rather than apart from them.

## The gaps, at full weight

A fair feature owes the reader the framework's liabilities as plainly as its appeals, and they are substantial.

It is **not a theory of everything**, and Zimmerman is the first to say so — "not a theory of everything yet, as frustrating as it may be" is his own framing. It is a theory of gravity and the dark sector, full stop. The **Standard Model is walled off**: the proton-to-electron mass ratio reduces to a free Yukawa and the strong coupling and is not derivable; the Koide relation is not derived; there are no particle masses, no gauge group. This is no worse than every other extant framework — none of them deliver these either — but it must not be smuggled in as a virtue it does not possess.

**Lensing is the genuine theoretical sore spot.** A no-go theorem established in this work forbids a covariant, Solar-System-safe MOND lensing within the framework's assumptions; the upshot is that the framework's light-bending remains an *un-derived free function*. The logical structure of the obstruction is worth stating, because it is not an accident of a particular Lagrangian: diffeomorphism invariance, a tensor-mode speed c_T = c (as the binary-neutron-star event GW170817 demands), and ghost-freedom together forbid a covariant action that produces MOND-strength deflection in galaxies while collapsing to general relativity in the Solar System. Something must give — a preferred frame, an extra field with its own free function, or a non-local kernel — and de Sitter–MOND's preferred-frame character is precisely what lets it evade the theorem, at the cost of leaving the deflection law unfixed. This is the same unfinished business that AeST (Skordis–Złośnik) and the broader relativistic-MOND program carry, and de Sitter–MOND inherits it rather than resolving it. Anyone who has followed the relativistic-MOND literature will recognize the shape of this gap immediately.

The galaxy-scale relations the framework reproduces — the radial-acceleration relation, the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation — are **shared with the entire MOND family** and are not unique to it. The genuinely framework-distinctive content reduces to two items: the a₀(z) evolution and the Solar-System Lorentz-violation dipole. And here is the most important caveat of all: the a₀(z) test is **non-diagnostic today** and is a **hostage** to DESI. The distinctive prediction lives or dies on whether dark energy actually evolves; prior-sensitivity reanalyses of the DESI signal have it dropping toward ∼1.3σ under some choices, and if the evolving-dark-energy preference reverts to a plain cosmological constant, de Sitter–MOND's distinctive content dissolves back into ordinary MOND.

Finally, **galaxy clusters** retain a residual mass discrepancy that this framework, like MOND generally, does not fully cure. The pattern is well known from forty years of MOND fits: applying the same a₀ that works in galaxies leaves clusters still short by a factor of order two in the inner regions, a residual that classical MOND papers over with a posited reservoir of unseen baryons or sterile neutrinos. De Sitter–MOND's density-dependent reading of the effective acceleration scale moves the residual in the right direction and to roughly the right order, but it does not close the gap — it understands the discrepancy rather than dissolving it. Clusters remain, as they have for the whole MOND family, the regime where the modified-dynamics program is weakest and where the lensing-mass offsets of merging systems such as the Bullet Cluster have long been read as a direct argument for collisionless dark matter. The framework's preferred-frame, SME route makes no claim to resolve those offsets either; it is a handle on a different observable, not a cluster answer.

And the meta-caveat: this is a single independent-researcher proposal, posted to Zenodo, **not peer-reviewed and not established**. Mainstream cosmology overwhelmingly favors particle dark matter and ΛCDM, on the strength of the CMB acoustic peaks, big-bang nucleosynthesis, structure formation, and the Bullet-Cluster-class lensing-mass offsets that MOND-family theories famously struggle with. Critics will reasonably note that a half-century of the a₀–Λ coincidence has invited many causal stories, and that none has yet survived contact with clusters and lensing.

## Why it is worth the column-inches anyway

What makes de Sitter–MOND worth a serious physicist's attention is not that it has solved anything — it has not — but that it is **brave, economical, and falsifiable** in a literature where those three rarely coincide. It takes the oldest coincidence in the modified-gravity playbook and commits to it physically, through a mechanism (de Sitter–Unruh modified inertia) with real antecedents in Milgrom and Deser–Levin; it is honest about its one knob and gives a clean geometric account of it; it is Cassini-safe by construction rather than by epicycle; and it stakes out a prediction — a₀ running as √ρ_DE — that the next half-decade of data can kill.

The right posture is neither dismissal nor advocacy. It is to watch the two tests that actually discriminate. One is cosmological and contingent: does dark energy evolve, and if so, does the baryonic Tully–Fisher zero-point drift with the sign and magnitude that √ρ_DE demands? The other is local and unconditional: does a Solar-System preferred-frame dipole appear at the SME coefficient the framework forces? Both are answerable this decade. That is more than can be said for most ideas about a₀ — and it is the most honest reason to keep this one on the table.
