\title{Ghost freedom does not constrain the sound speed of a shift-symmetric condensate}

\author{Carl Zimmerman}
\affiliation{Briar Creek Tech, Charlotte, North Carolina, USA}
\date{21 August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
For a shift-symmetric scalar with Lagrangian $K(\mathcal{Q})$, where $\mathcal{Q}$ is the
charge variable conjugate to the shift symmetry, we note the identity
$K'' = n/(\mathcal{Q}c_s^2)$ with $n = K'$ the conserved charge density. Because $n>0$ and
$\mathcal{Q}>0$ on any physical branch, this shows that the no-ghost condition $K''>0$ is
\emph{equivalent} to $c_s^2>0$ and therefore constrains only the \textbf{sign} of the sound
speed---never its scaling with charge density, and never its magnitude. This contradicts a
natural and, in our experience, easily made assumption: that stability forces the sound speed
of such a condensate to redshift as $c_s^2\propto a^{-3}$, driving it to dust. That behaviour
is the special case in which $K'$ possesses a \emph{simple interior zero}. We exhibit an
explicit ghost-free counterexample,
$K'(\mathcal{Q}) = n_1[1+(s/c_1)\ln(\mathcal{Q}_1/\mathcal{Q})]^{-1/s}$, whose sound speed
\emph{rises} as the charge dilutes, $c_s^2 = c_1(n/n_1)^{-s}\propto a^{+3s}$. Here $K'>0$ for
all $\mathcal{Q}>0$ and vanishes only at the domain boundary, logarithmically: $298$ decades
of charge buy $6$ decades of $K'$. Numerically, $K''>0$ at $400$ sampled charges spanning
$45$ decades in $n$ with none skipped, minimum $K''=4.9\times10^{-27}$, and the closed-form
sound speed is reproduced to $2\times10^{-52}$ at $60$-digit precision. The equation-of-state
fixed point $w=-1$ survives, relocated to the boundary of the charge domain. The consequence
is that warm shift-symmetric dark sectors are not excluded by stability, and any argument
that closes them must appeal to something other than ghost freedom. We state plainly what
this result does \emph{not} do: it removes an obstruction, it does not build a viable
cosmology, and one class of candidate application is reported here as failing three
independent observational gates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

Consider a scalar $\phi$ with shift symmetry $\phi\to\phi+\text{const}$, in a configuration
$\phi = \mathcal{Q}_0 t + \psi(\mathbf{x})$, so that the time component of the gradient plays
the role of a chemical potential. Write the Lagrangian as $K(\mathcal{Q})$. Then
\begin{equation}
n = K'(\mathcal{Q}),\qquad
\rho = \mathcal{Q}K' - K,\qquad
p = K ,
\label{eq:thermo}
\end{equation}
with $n$ the conserved Noether charge density of the shift symmetry. These are the standard
thermodynamic relations of a charged condensate, with $\mathcal{Q}$ the chemical potential
conjugate to $n$; they follow either by direct variation or from the first law
$\mathrm{d}\rho/\mathrm{d}n = (\rho+p)/n$.

The sound speed and the no-ghost condition are
\begin{equation}
c_s^2 = \frac{\mathrm{d}p}{\mathrm{d}\rho} = \frac{K'}{\mathcal{Q}K''},
\qquad
\text{no ghost}\iff K''>0 .
\label{eq:cs}
\end{equation}

\section{The theorem}

Rearranging Eq.~\eqref{eq:cs} gives the identity
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;K'' \;=\; \frac{n}{\mathcal{Q}\,c_s^2}\;}
\label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
On any physical branch $n>0$ and $\mathcal{Q}>0$, so the sign of $K''$ is the sign of $c_s^2$
and nothing else. Hence:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Theorem.} For a shift-symmetric condensate, ghost freedom is \emph{equivalent} to
$c_s^2>0$. It places no constraint whatsoever on the magnitude of $c_s^2$, nor on its scaling
with $n$ (equivalently, with the scale factor through $n\propto a^{-3}$).
\end{quote}

The theorem is trivial once Eq.~\eqref{eq:identity} is written down, which is precisely why
the assumption it refutes is easy to make. The tempting argument runs: $K'$ must vanish
somewhere for the charge to dilute; near a zero of $K'$ one has $K'/K''\sim(\mathcal{Q}-
\mathcal{Q}_*)$, hence $c_s^2\propto n\propto a^{-3}$; therefore any ghost-free condensate
cools to dust. Every step is correct \emph{given a simple interior zero}. The conclusion does
not follow in general, because that hypothesis is an extra assumption and not a consequence
of stability.

\section{The counterexample}

Let
\begin{equation}
K'(\mathcal{Q}) = n_1\left[1+\frac{s}{c_1}\ln\frac{\mathcal{Q}_1}{\mathcal{Q}}\right]^{-1/s},
\qquad s>0 ,
\label{eq:counter}
\end{equation}
which integrates the ODE $\mathrm{d}\ln\mathcal{Q}/\mathrm{d}\ln n = c_s^2$ with
$c_s^2 = c_1(n/n_1)^{-s}$. Then
\begin{equation}
c_s^2 = c_1\left(\frac{n}{n_1}\right)^{-s} \;\propto\; a^{+3s},
\end{equation}
so the sound speed \emph{rises} as the charge dilutes: the sector is cold when dense and warm
when rarefied.

Three properties make this a genuine counterexample rather than a formal one.

\paragraph{It is ghost-free everywhere on the branch.}
$K'>0$ for every $\mathcal{Q}>0$, and $K'\to0$ only at the domain boundary
$\mathcal{Q}\to0$---logarithmically, so the approach is extraordinarily slow: $298$ decades
of $\mathcal{Q}$ buy $6$ decades of $K'$. There is no interior zero, which is exactly the
hypothesis the tempting argument requires.

\paragraph{It is verified, not merely asserted.}
Independent reconstruction from the defining ODE (rather than by substitution into the closed
form) gives $K''>0$ at $400$ sampled charges spanning $45$ decades in $n$, none skipped, with
minimum $K'' = 4.87\times10^{-27}$; and $c_s^2$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:cs} reproduces
$c_1(n/n_1)^{-s}$ to a relative $1.9\times10^{-52}$ at $60$-digit precision.

\paragraph{The dark-energy fixed point survives.}
$K(\mathcal{Q}_1)-K(0)$ is finite and analytically bounded by $n_1\mathcal{Q}_1$, so the
$w=-1$ stationary point is preserved---relocated to the boundary of the charge domain rather
than destroyed. A condensate of this type can still supply an exact cosmological-constant
branch.

\paragraph{A family that does \emph{not} work, for the record.}
It is worth recording which natural family fails, since we tried it first. Taking
$K'\propto(\mathcal{Q}-\mathcal{Q}_*)^m$ gives
$c_s^2 = (\mathcal{Q}-\mathcal{Q}_*)/(m\mathcal{Q})\propto n^{1/m}\propto a^{-3/m}$, which
\emph{declines} for every $m>0$; and $K''>0$ requires $m>0$, so $m<0$ is a ghost rather than
an escape. The rising branch is not contained in that family at all; the map to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:counter} is $s=-1/m$.

\section{What follows, and what does not}

\paragraph{What follows.} Arguments that close warm shift-symmetric dark sectors on stability
grounds are invalid as stated. In particular, a chain of the form ``the excitation is
ghost-free, therefore $c_s^2\propto a^{-3}$, therefore it is dust, therefore it clusters and
cannot be kept smooth'' fails at its second step. Any such closure must instead be argued from
observation---the cosmic microwave background, structure growth, or the phase-space
constraints---and those arguments have different and generally weaker leverage.

\paragraph{What does not follow.} Removing an obstruction is not the same as building a
theory, and we decline to present it as one. In the application that motivated this work---a
condensate supplying the cosmological dark sector while remaining smooth enough in galaxies not
to double-count a modified-gravity phantom---the warm branch was constructed and then failed
three independent observational gates under adversarial review: the tensor speed constraint
from GW170817, cluster scales at the low end of the charge window, and the radial acceleration
relation, which degrades from $0.108$ to $0.113$~dex through a coherent deep-MOND tilt. A
window on the required sound speed remained short by a factor of $1.28$, which is a small
number but not zero. We report this because a result that removes an obstruction is easy to
oversell, and the honest scope of the present paper is the theorem and its counterexample, not
the application.

\paragraph{A methodological note.} The assumption refuted here survived unchallenged in our own
work for an extended period and was used to close a live line of investigation. It failed for
a specific and recognisable reason: a correct formula---the near-a-simple-zero expansion---was
generalised beyond the regime in which it holds. We record this because the same error mode
accounted for the majority of incorrect exclusions we have had to withdraw, and because a
stability argument is exactly the kind of claim that is rarely revisited once made.

\section{Reproducibility}

The construction, the $45$-decade ghost-freedom scan, the $60$-digit sound-speed check and the
$w=-1$ boundary argument are implemented in the accompanying scripts, each of which exits zero.
The counterexample was independently rebuilt from the defining ODE by a separate analysis that
did not import the closed form, and that reconstruction is included.
