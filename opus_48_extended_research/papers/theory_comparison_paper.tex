\title{Falsifiability and Testable Predictions:\\
the $a_0=c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/32\pi}$ framework among the major theories of physics}
\author{C.\ Zimmerman}
\date{June 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We place the emergent-gravity framework $a_0=c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/32\pi}=9.36\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ on the
same axes as the other leading theories---quantum field theory (the Standard Model), general relativity,
$\Lambda$CDM, cosmic inflation, standard MOND, Verlinde's emergent gravity, supersymmetry, string theory, and loop
quantum gravity---and ask two operational questions: how many concrete, dated, \emph{falsifiable} predictions does
each make, and how many are \emph{confirmed}? The honest answer, stated both ways: quantum field theory and general
relativity are the gold standard (richly predictive and confirmed to extraordinary precision); $\Lambda$CDM is the
confirmed concordance model carrying real tensions; the present framework and standard MOND are highly falsifiable
but only partially confirmed, sitting in the ``testable-not-yet-confirmed'' regime with conceded losses; and string
theory makes essentially zero unique near-term confirmable predictions, drawing the standard falsifiability
critique. The framework is decisively \emph{more falsifiable} than string theory---but that is a low bar, it is not
more predictive than the Standard Model, and its one genuinely distinctive prediction (a declining $a_0(z)$) is
currently unconfirmed and leaning unfavourable. We give the comparative scorecard, the falsifiability--confirmation
map, and the per-theory prediction lists.
\end{abstract}

\section{What ``testable'' means here}
A theory earns scientific standing by making predictions that could have come out otherwise and then survived the
test. We score each theory on three operational axes: (i) the number of \emph{concrete, falsifiable} predictions it
makes---predictions with a stated threshold that a measurement could violate; (ii) how many of those are
\emph{confirmed}; and (iii) its \emph{falsifiability}---whether a feasible experiment could, even in principle, kill
it. These axes are qualitative characterisations, not exact data, and we flag them as such; but the gross ordering
they produce is not in serious dispute. Crucially, ``more falsifiable than theory $X$'' is \emph{not} the same as
``more correct than $X$,'' and we keep the two separate throughout.

\section{Per-theory predictions}
\textbf{QFT / Standard Model (the ceiling).} The electron anomalous moment is verified to $\sim$0.13 parts per
trillion---the most precise prediction in science; plus the Higgs (predicted, found 2012), the $W/Z$ bosons,
neutrino oscillations, running couplings, CP violation, the Lamb shift, the Casimir effect, and a host of masses
and cross-sections. Nothing else listed approaches this; the framework does not claim to.

\textbf{General relativity.} Perihelion precession, light bending, gravitational redshift, the Shapiro delay,
frame-dragging, binary-pulsar decay, gravitational waves (2015), the black-hole shadow (2019)---all confirmed.

\textbf{$\Lambda$CDM.} The CMB acoustic-peak spectrum, big-bang nucleosynthesis, the baryon acoustic scale,
structure growth, lensing maps---broadly confirmed, but the dark-matter particle is undetected after forty years
and $\Lambda$'s value is unexplained, and it carries the $\sim$5.8$\sigma$ Hubble tension and a $\sim$3$\sigma$
preference for evolving dark energy.

\textbf{This framework.} $\sim$9 dated, falsifiable predictions: the declining $a_0(z)\!\propto\!\sqrt{\rho_{\rm
DE}}$ (DESI~DR3, high-$z$ kinematics); the high-$z$ baryonic Tully--Fisher offset; the wide-binary external-field
boost ($+6.6\%$ in velocity on the framework's own de Sitter--Unruh law, Gaia~DR4); the external field effect
itself; the lensing radial-acceleration relation; a Solar-System (Cassini) bound; a predicted $a_0$ bump near
$z\!\approx\!0.4$. The radial-acceleration relation and BTFR successes are \emph{shared with standard MOND}; the
distinctive content is the $a_0(z)$ evolution---currently leaning $\sim$1.5$\sigma$ unfavourable.

\textbf{Standard MOND.} The radial-acceleration relation (a real, tight success), the BTFR, the external field
effect, rotation-curve shapes---confirmed at galaxy scale; fails galaxy clusters ($\sim$2$\times$) and the CMB.

\textbf{Supersymmetry.} Superpartners, gauge-coupling unification, a light Higgs. Superpartners are \emph{not}
found at the LHC (gluinos excluded to $\sim$2.2~TeV); the one prediction that landed (a light Higgs) is shared with
the Standard Model.

\textbf{String theory.} Requires supersymmetry (absent) and extra dimensions (absent); ``predicts gravity'' is a
post-diction; the $\sim$$10^{500}$-vacua landscape yields no unique near-term confirmable test, which is the
substance of the long-standing falsifiability critique.

\textbf{Loop quantum gravity.} Discrete spacetime, a possible Planck-scale Lorentz violation (searched in
gamma-ray-burst timing, not seen), a big-bounce cosmology---no near-term confirmation.

\section{The honest verdict}
\textbf{Versus string theory.} The framework is decisively more falsifiable: $\sim$5--6 dated, telescope-named,
kill-thresholded near-term predictions against essentially zero unique confirmable string predictions. This is a
genuine virtue---risky, dated bets where the alternative makes none. \emph{Three guardrails, each true and each
stated:} (i) falsifiability is precisely the axis the framework wins on; (ii) that is a \emph{low bar}---beating
string theory on testability is not being established; (iii) more predictions $\neq$ more correct, and the framework
is \emph{not} more predictive than the Standard Model, whose electron $g\!-\!2$ stands at 0.13~ppt.

\textbf{Where the framework actually stands.} It is a falsifiable, internally consistent, $\Lambda$-anchored
modified-inertia realisation that reproduces MOND's $z\!=\!0$ galaxy record, is sign-aligned with DESI's evolving
dark energy (the one cosmological tension that points its way), and makes a sharp distinctive bet in $a_0(z)$. It
also carries real, conceded losses---chief among them an $a_0$-independent $8.6$--$9.2\sigma$ weak-lensing
morphology split that no choice of convention removes---and an underived coefficient $Z=\sqrt{32\pi/3}$. It does not
break $\Lambda$CDM, and $\Lambda$CDM does not falsify it; the verdict comes from the 2026--2032 forward tests.

\textbf{Bottom line.} On the one axis that most cleanly separates science from speculation---can a measurement kill
it?---the framework is among the strongest of the live alternatives and far ahead of string theory. On the axis
that separates a hypothesis from established physics---is it confirmed?---it sits, honestly, with MOND in the
testable-but-unproven quadrant, well below quantum field theory and general relativity. Both statements are true at
once, and keeping them distinct is the whole point.
