To the Editors, Physical Review D

**Context.** The acceleration scale a₀ that organizes galactic rotation curves has been known since
Milgrom (1983) to lie near cH₀, and the coincidence has attracted a series of proposed closed forms — a
derived 2cH_Λ from a de Sitter–Unruh argument (Milgrom 1999; also Pikhitsa 2010, Klinkhamer & Kopp 2011),
and an empirical cH_Λ/2π (Milgrom 2020). This manuscript records an exact expression in the dark-energy
density with a rational coefficient, and is careful to establish what that does and does not add to the
above.

**Key findings.** (i) a₀ = ½ c √(Gρ_Λ) = c²√(Λ/32π) = cH_Λ/√(32π/3); the three forms are algebraically
identical, and the reduction cancels every factor of π and the 3, which shows that the apparent structure
√(32π/3) is only a unit conversion and carries no content. (ii) On Planck 2018 inputs the expression gives
9.43 × 10⁻¹¹ m s⁻², which is 0.70% from the value obtained by fitting the 175 SPARC rotation curves at
Υ_[3.6] = 0.70 — a mass-to-light ratio inside the range expected for Spitzer 3.6 μm populations, so the
agreement does not require an implausible fit. (iii) The relation carries a falsifiable consequence
(§VI): if a₀ ∝ √ρ_Λ then a₀(z) is exactly constant for w = −1 and rises to a maximum before *declining*
for the DESI-preferred w₀ > −1, w_a < 0, in contrast to the monotonic rise predicted by a₀ ∝ cH(z). A
measured monotonic rise would exclude the relation.

**What I have not claimed, stated here because it bounds the paper.** The interpolating function I use is
*identical* to Eq. (9) of Milgrom (1999) — not a variant of it — and that same paper *derives*
a₀ = 2cH_Λ, a factor 11.58 larger. My contribution is therefore a renormalization of his coefficient to
match data, not a new derivation, and §II says so in those words. Further, the coefficient here is not
observationally distinguishable from Milgrom's cH_Λ/2π: the two differ by 7.9%, which is 0.018 dex in
g_obs against a fit scatter of 0.108 dex. I make no claim of improved fit; the contribution is one of
*form*. §V states that the coefficient ½ is fitted, not derived, and reports my own failed attempt to
force it. §VII hands over the outstanding tensions unprompted, including that the cluster-scale
discrepancy is 13% *worse* for this lower a₀ than for the conventional value, and that the exact
interpolation leaves a solar-system residual three orders above Earth–Mars ranging bounds.

**Reproducibility and disclosure.** Every numerical claim is produced by a public script that performs
internal consistency checks and exits with a nonzero status if any fails; the code is archived at
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21724575. §VIII discloses that portions of the analysis and drafting were
carried out with AI assistance: I directed the work, specified and reviewed every load-bearing
calculation, and take full responsibility including for any errors. No AI system is listed as an author.
Several intermediate claims produced during the work were found to be incorrect and were withdrawn before
submission.

**Submission history.** No version of this manuscript has previously been submitted to Physical Review D
or to any other Physical Review journal, nor to any other journal. It is not part of a joint submission
or a sequence of papers. A preprint is deposited at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21724575 (deposited
concurrently with this submission); it is not a journal publication. The manuscript is not posted to
arXiv at the time of submission; I will inform the editorial office if that changes.

# Notes for you — NOT part of the letter

## Why these four referees

All four know the a₀ literature cold, which is the point: **§II is the section that decides this paper.**
A referee who recognizes Milgrom 1999 Eq. (9) on sight will confirm you credited it correctly; a general
cosmologist might not realize you had done the honest thing, and could read §II as a weakness rather than
as due diligence.

- **McGaugh** — SPARC co-author, discovered the radial acceleration relation, and has written on the
  a₀–cosmology coincidence. The single best-matched referee for both the fit and the coincidence.
- **Famaey** — co-author of the standard MOND review; knows the coefficient literature in detail.
- **Lelli** — SPARC lead author; best placed to judge whether the Υ = 0.70 / 0.108 dex fit is done right.
- **Desmond** — works on RAR intrinsic scatter (your own budget discussion uses his σ_int = 0.034) and on
  MOND constraints. He will scrutinize the scatter claim, which is exactly where the paper's honesty
  lives, so if it survives him it survives.

## On Milgrom — read this before you decide

**I deliberately did not name him**, and you should know the reasoning so you can override it if you
disagree.

1. The paper is substantially about renormalizing *his* coefficient. Editors often treat the author of
   the work being modified as insufficiently independent.
2. **He was very likely among the ~22 researchers you emailed in June 2026** — the round that carried the
   TOE/Standard-Model claims you subsequently retracted publicly. If he recalls that, a referee request
   arriving from you with him named as your suggestion is an awkward combination. Only you know the exact
   list; check it.
3. **You do not need to name him, and it costs you nothing if the editor sends it to him anyway.** That is
   a real possibility and the manuscript is written to survive it: §II concedes, in advance and in his
   terms, every objection he would raise. That is the whole design.

If you *do* want to name him, the honest way is to disclose the prior contact in the same sentence — e.g.
"I have previously corresponded with Prof. Milgrom regarding related work." Undisclosed prior contact with
a suggested referee is the kind of thing that damages you if discovered later.

## The affiliation question

You are submitting from your own company rather than a university, and an editor will notice. This is
entirely normal — PRD publishes independent researchers — and the letter does not try to obscure it. Your
strongest counterweight is already in place: a DOI-archived, self-checking reproducibility trail. Very few
submissions of any provenance arrive with that. Lean on it rather than apologizing for the affiliation.

## One thing you may want to add

If you post to arXiv before the editor assigns referees, email the editorial office the arXiv ID. It costs
nothing and editors prefer being told.
