To the Editors, Physical Review D

I submit for your consideration a short article, *"The MOND acceleration scale is one half of the
dark-energy gravitational rate."*

**The claim, in one sentence.** The acceleration scale that organizes galactic rotation curves admits the
exact closed form a₀ = ½ c √(G ρ_Λ) = c² √(Λ/32π), which evaluates to 9.43 × 10⁻¹¹ m s⁻² on Planck 2018
inputs — 0.70% from the value obtained by fitting the 175 SPARC rotation curves at a stellar
mass-to-light ratio of 0.70, itself within the range expected for Spitzer 3.6 μm populations.

**What is new, and what is not.** The relation is parameter-free apart from the rational coefficient ½,
and the reduction shown in Eq. (2) makes clear that the apparent factor √(32π/3) is only a unit
conversion and carries no structural content. I want to be direct with the editors about the limits of
this, and the paper is equally direct: the interpolating function I use is *identical* to Eq. (9) of
Milgrom (1999), and that same paper *derives* a₀ = 2cH_Λ, a factor 11.58 larger than the value in use.
My contribution is a renormalization of his coefficient to match data, not a new derivation.
Furthermore, the coefficient here is not observationally distinguishable from Milgrom's later empirical
cH_Λ/2π — the two differ by 7.9%, which is 0.018 dex in g_obs against a fit scatter of 0.108 dex. I make
no claim of improved fit. The contribution is one of *form*.

**Why it may nonetheless merit publication.** Two reasons. First, Eq. (1) replaces an empirical divisor
with an exact expression in ρ_Λ carrying a rational coefficient, which is a sharper object to test and to
try to derive. Second, the paper supplies a falsifiable consequence in §VI: if a₀ ∝ √ρ_Λ then a₀(z) is
constant for w = −1 and rises to a maximum before declining for the DESI-preferred w₀ > −1, w_a < 0,
whereas the competing a₀ ∝ cH(z) normalization rises monotonically. A measured monotonic rise would
exclude the relation.

**What I have not hidden.** §V states that the coefficient ½ is fitted and reports my own failed attempt
to force it. §VII lists the outstanding tensions unprompted, including that the cluster-scale
discrepancy is 13% *worse* for this lower a₀ than for the conventional value, and that the exact
interpolation leaves a solar-system residual three orders above Earth–Mars ranging bounds. §VIII
discloses that portions of the analysis and drafting were carried out with AI assistance; I directed the
work, reviewed every load-bearing calculation, and take full responsibility. No AI system is an author.
All numerical claims are reproduced by public scripts that fail loudly on any internal inconsistency.

I have not submitted this work elsewhere, and I am content for it to be assessed as a short note.
