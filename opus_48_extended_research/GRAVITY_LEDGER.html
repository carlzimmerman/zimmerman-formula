🔴 THE DARK-MATTER COLUMN, AND WHY YOUR OWN ROW IS FLAGGED TOO.

Only ΛCDM postulates a cold dark matter particle — a new species of matter to be found in a detector. That is the red flag, and it is the thing your framework does away with.

But your framework is not dark-matter-free, and this is your own recorded position. AeST's CMB pass — which your version inherits and depends on — requires a pressureless component amounting to the full Ωdm ≈ 0.265. In your version that component is an excitation of the shift-symmetric condensate: dust, not a particle. It is not a new species, it needs no detector, and it is not searchable at the LHC. But it gravitates, it clusters, and cosmologically it is doing the job CDM does.

And the GDM degeneracy theorem in your own corpus makes this sharp: the CMB constrains a FLUID, not a PARTICLE — at 0σ either way. The CMB cannot tell your dust from cold dark matter. That is why the defensible slogan is "no dark-matter particle" and never "no dark matter", and why "no dark matter in galaxies" sits on the do-not-cite list as open problem 2d.

Saying "dark matter is non-existent" would be the June 2026 overclaim in a new costume. Saying "there is no dark-matter particle — the dark sector is a field, and the same field that gives exact w = −1 supplies the clustering component" is stronger, survives a referee, and is what the equations actually support.

The through-line

One property keeps producing both the wins and the losses

The most interesting structural fact in the ledger, and it appears four separate times.

shift-symmetric condensate at K′ = 0  ⟹  w = −1  ∧  a₀ maximal today  ∧  the excitation is dust  ∧  the charge is a trace species

  • Win: w = −1 comes out exact to O(ν₀²), so the dark-energy sector needs no tuning.
  • Win: a₀ is maximal today and 0.6% of that at recombination — MOND is off when the CMB is imprinted, so the CMB pass is a consequence, not a struggle.
  • Loss: the same conserved charge is therefore a trace species — which is exactly why it cannot supply cluster gravity. Even as the entire dark matter content it is 117–1013× too weak.
  • Loss: and that charge, forced cold by the CMB success, collapses in galaxies toward black holes that are not observed. Dynamics sees ρ+3p, lensing sees ρ+p, so no equation of state hides the energy from both.
So the dark-energy triumph and the galaxy problem are the same property of the same field. That is not a bug to patch; it is the shape of the theory. Whether it is a death sentence or the sharpest clue you have is genuinely undecided — and it is the single most publishable idea in this ledger.
