\begin{abstract}
The radial-acceleration relation of disc galaxies can be written as an exact identity, $E \equiv g_{\rm obs}^2 - g_{\rm bar}^2 = a_0\,g_{\rm bar}$: the `missing-gravity' excess is a straight line through the origin whose slope is the low-acceleration scale $a_0$ at every acceleration, following algebraically from the interpolation kernel $\nu(y)=\sqrt{1+1/y}$ of \citet{Milgrom1999}. This converts interpolation-function fitting into the measurement of a single slope. We measure that slope on the gas-dominated subsample of the SPARC database \citep{Lelli2016}, where the stellar mass-to-light ($\Upsilon_\star$) degeneracy is suppressed by ${\sim}71$ per cent, obtaining $\hat a_0 = (0.84$--$1.36)\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}$ -- the full estimator-and-cut envelope; $\pm16$ per cent is the $1\sigma$ systematic budget about the central value. Inverting through $\Lambda = 32\pi\,\hat a_0^2/c^4$ recovers the \textit{Planck} cosmological constant to within a factor of ${\sim}2$ (central estimates $1.08$--$2.03\times$ \textit{Planck}) from galactic kinematics that carry no cosmological input -- a sharpened, inversion-form statement of the known $a_0 \sim c\sqrt{\Lambda}$ coincidence. We map the dominant systematics with reproducible pipelines: clean tip-of-the-red-giant-branch distances collapse the estimator-choice systematic but are not the binding wall, and external $\Upsilon_\star$ priors tighten $a_0$ by 2--12 per cent yet leave the measurement systematics-limited even in the perfect-$\Upsilon_\star$ limit. The binding floor is gas-mass calibration, which does not average down: the measured box cannot separate the two candidate normalisations of the scale ($cH_\Lambda/Z = 9.36\times10^{-11}$ versus $cH_0/Z = 1.13\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}$, $Z=\sqrt{32\pi/3}$) at $2\sigma$. The order-of-magnitude inversion is robust; the ${\sim}21$ per cent normalisation distinction is data-blocked. The value of $a_0$ and the sign of the correction are posited inputs throughout, not derived.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The framework, the horizon scale, and the two footings}
\label{sec:footings}

Modified inertia (MI) proposes that the inertial response of a body to a force is altered at accelerations below a critical scale $a_0$, rather than the gravitational field being modified \citep{Milgrom1983,Milgrom1999}. In the de Sitter--Unruh realization considered here, an accelerated observer in a universe with a cosmological horizon experiences a floor in the vacuum's response set by the horizon temperature, and the transition scale is fixed to the horizon:
\begin{equation}
a_0 = \frac{cH_\Lambda}{Z} = \frac{c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/3}}{Z},\qquad Z=\sqrt{\tfrac{32\pi}{3}}=5.78881,
\label{eq:a0def}
\end{equation}
where $H_\Lambda\equiv c\sqrt{\Lambda/3}$ is the de Sitter (pure dark-energy) Hubble rate. With the \textit{Planck} 2018 value $\Lambda=1.089\times10^{-52}\ {\rm m^{-2}}$ \citep{Planck2018} this gives the \textbf{canonical footing}
\begin{equation}
a_0^{\rm canon}=c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/32\pi}=9.355\times10^{-11}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}
\label{eq:canon}
\end{equation}
(\textit{Planck}-anchored, $\pm1$ per cent).
A second, distinct \textbf{ALT footing} replaces the pure-$\Lambda$ density by the total present-day critical density, $a_0^{\rm ALT}=cH_0/Z=1.1305\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}$. A \emph{footing}, in the sense used throughout this paper, is a choice of which cosmological density the horizon term tracks -- pure dark energy ($\rho_{\rm DE}$, giving $cH_\Lambda$) versus the total present-day density (giving $cH_0$). It is a physics choice about the framework, not a fitting freedom. The two footings differ by $20.9$ per cent; deciding which one nature uses is one of the two questions this paper confronts, and \textbf{both footings are carried on every dimensional number below.} Nothing that follows derives the value of $a_0$ or the sign of the inertial correction $s=-1$: those remain postulates. What the framework ties to the cosmological horizon is the \emph{scale} of $a_0$, and it is that tie -- the $\Lambda\leftrightarrow a_0$ relation -- that is under measurement.

The proximity of $a_0$ to a cosmological acceleration is not new: the numerical coincidence $a_0\sim cH_0$ is Milgrom's own observation \citep{Milgrom1983}, and the specific proportionality of $a_0$ to the dark-energy density, $a_0\propto\sqrt{\rho_{\rm DE}}$, together with its redshift test, is due to \citet{Limbach2008}. The relativistic completion of the MOND scale as a covariant field theory is the domain of AeST \citep{Skordis2021}, which is modified \emph{gravity}, not the modified-inertia reading adopted here. This paper claims none of those; its contribution is the measurement framing developed below.

\subsection{What is established, and the question this paper answers}
\label{sec:question}

The \emph{forward} statement of the tie -- given $\Lambda$, predict $a_0$ -- was reported in \textit{The Zimmerman Theory of Gravity} and formalized in the MI field-theory results \citep{Zimmerman2026a}, with the lensing sector constrained separately \citep{Zimmerman2026b}. Independently, the standard MOND phenomenology on the SPARC rotation-curve database is well established: the radial-acceleration relation \citep[RAR;][]{McGaugh2016} exhibits a characteristic scale $g^\dagger=1.2\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}$, and the SPARC sample itself \citep{Lelli2016} provides the baryonic decompositions used here.

This paper asks the \textbf{inverse} question. If $a_0$ is genuinely a horizon-scale quantity, then galaxy rotation curves -- sensitive to $a_0$ in their low-acceleration outskirts -- should let one \emph{read the cosmological constant back out}, via $\Lambda=3Z^2 a_0^2/c^4$. Can they? And can the measured $a_0$ tell the two footings apart? The answer developed below is the crispest single statement of the result: \textbf{the order-of-magnitude inversion is real and striking (rotation curves recover $\Lambda$ to a factor ${\sim}2$ with no cosmological input on the galactic side), but the 21 per cent footing distinction is blocked by an irreducible systematic floor.} We report this as a \emph{measurement} -- striking but non-decisive -- and we are equally careful not to manufacture a canonical detection nor a deficit: the best current $a_0$ estimate is a box that straddles \emph{both} footings.

\subsection{Credit for the kernel}
\label{sec:credit}

The interpolation function underlying the identity of Section~\ref{sec:identity},
\begin{equation}
\nu(y)=\sqrt{1+1/y},\qquad y\equiv g_{\rm bar}/a_0,
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
is \textbf{not new to this framework}. It is exactly the kernel written by \citet[][Eq.~9]{Milgrom1999}, in the same functional form, in the original MOND context of \citet{Milgrom1983}. Milgrom's coefficient was $2cH_\Lambda$ rather than $cH_\Lambda/Z$. The identity of Section~\ref{sec:identity} follows from \emph{squaring} that kernel and is therefore a re-expression of a known law; what the present framework contributes, and what this paper measures, is (a) the exact-constancy fingerprint $\varepsilon=1$, (b) the specific $cH_\Lambda/Z$ coefficient, and (c) the $\Lambda$-inversion. We credit the kernel at the point of use and claim only the distinctive content.

\section{The $\protect\azero$-line identity}
\label{sec:identity}

\subsection{The exact identity}
\label{sec:exact}

The framework's interpolation (Milgrom's kernel, Section~\ref{sec:credit}) $g_{\rm obs}=\sqrt{g_{\rm bar}^2+a_0 g_{\rm bar}}$, i.e.\ $g_{\rm obs}=g_{\rm bar}\,\nu(y)$ with $\nu=\sqrt{1+1/y}$, squares to
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;E\equiv g_{\rm obs}^2-g_{\rm bar}^2 = a_0\,g_{\rm bar}\;}\qquad\text{exactly, at every }y.
\label{eq:a0line}
\end{equation}
The excess $E$ -- the squared difference between observed and baryonic gravity, i.e.\ the `missing gravity' in acceleration-squared units -- is a \textbf{straight line through the origin with slope $a_0$}, valid at all accelerations. There is no deep-MOND selection, no interpolation fit, no small- or large-$y$ approximation: the relation is algebraic and exact (verified symbolically in \texttt{identity\_uniqueness.py}, Appendix~\ref{sec:scripts}). This converts the usual task -- \emph{fit an interpolation function} -- into a simpler one: \emph{measure one slope}.

\subsection{Uniqueness, honestly characterized}
\label{sec:unique}

Demanding an exactly linear excess, $y^2(\nu^2-1)=\alpha y+\beta$ for all $y>0$, gives $\nu^2=1+\alpha/y+\beta/y^2$. The $\beta$ term produces a non-zero floor, $g_{\rm obs}^2\to\beta a_0^2$ as $g_{\rm bar}\to0$, so requiring $g_{\rm obs}\to0$ forces $\beta=0$; the surviving family $\nu=\sqrt{1+\alpha/y}$ is pure $a_0$-rescaling, and the standard deep-MOND normalization $g_{\rm obs}\to\sqrt{a_0 g_{\rm bar}}$ fixes $\alpha=1$. So $\nu=\sqrt{1+1/y}$ is the \textbf{unique interpolation with an exactly linear excess}, up to the definition of $a_0$ itself (verified in \texttt{identity\_uniqueness.py}; re-derived one generalization further in \texttt{verify\_independent.py}). We state this honestly: the claim is definitionally tight -- `excess exactly linear through the origin' \emph{is} the framework law restated. Its value is methodological, not a new physical fact by itself.

\subsection{The $\varepsilon=1$ fingerprint versus rival kernels: a future-data lever}
\label{sec:fingerprint}

Define the dimensionless excess ratio $\varepsilon(y)\equiv E/(a_0 g_{\rm bar})=y(\nu^2-1)$. For the framework, $\varepsilon\equiv1$ exactly at all $y$. Rival kernels do not share this (Table~\ref{tab:kernels}).

\begin{table}
\centering
\caption{The excess ratio $\varepsilon(y)\equiv E/(a_0 g_{\rm bar})$ for the framework kernel and two rivals.}
\label{tab:kernels}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2pt}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
$\nu(y)$ & $\varepsilon(y)$ & $y\to0$ & $y\to\infty$ \\
\hline
framework $\sqrt{1+1/y}$ & $1$ (exact constant) & $1$ & $1$ \\
RAR-fit $[1-e^{-\sqrt y}]^{-1}$ & $y(\nu^2-1)$ & $1$ & ${\sim}2y\,e^{-\sqrt y}\to0$ \\
simple $\tfrac12+\sqrt{\tfrac14+1/y}$ & $1+\tfrac y2(\sqrt{1+4/y}-1)$ & $1$ & $2$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The three agree in the deep regime ($\varepsilon\to1$ as $y\to0$) but diverge at high $y$. The \citet{McGaugh2016} RAR-fit excess \textbf{dies superexponentially} -- a death that survives any choice of $g^\dagger$. The simple kernel's excess \textbf{persists but at slope $2a_0$}, so $\varepsilon\to2$: tail persistence alone does \emph{not} separate the framework from the simple kernel; only the \emph{exact constancy} $\varepsilon\equiv1$ at \emph{all} $y$ does. This is the framework's genuine fingerprint. The separation from the RAR fit reaches a factor ${\sim}110$ at $y=100$ on a matched scale (a factor ${\sim}11$--$44$ at the rival's own preferred scale -- the tail \emph{death} is scale-robust, the exact multiplier is convention-cosmetic).

Two points on the logic. First, the \textbf{constancy} of $\varepsilon$ across $g_{\rm bar}$ is a scale-independent statement (it needs no value of $a_0$); the further claim that the constant \emph{equals} $a_0$ does need $a_0$. It is the constancy, not the value, that is the clean fingerprint. Second, the catch, quantified in Section~\ref{sec:measurement}, is that SPARC samples this fingerprint thinly: the median point sits at $y=0.31$, and only one point in the entire quality-cut sample exceeds $y=100$. The constancy test is in-principle orthogonal to the M/L degeneracy but, at SPARC's sampling, delivers less than $1\sigma$ of persistent-versus-dying discrimination today (a global framework-versus-RAR-fit shape comparison is a wash, $|\Delta\chi^2|\approx2$, with M/L profiled; \texttt{fire\_linearity.py}). \textbf{It is a future-data lever, not a present discriminator}, and it is not tested by the data used here.

\section{The gas-dominated measurement}
\label{sec:measurement}

\subsection{The gas cut and why it matters}
\label{sec:gascut}

The slope $a_0$ is estimated by iterated generalized least squares (GLS) through the origin with model-based errors,
\begin{equation}
\hat a_0=\frac{\sum_i w_i E_i g_i}{\sum_i w_i g_i^2},\qquad {\rm Var}_{\rm stat}=\frac{1}{\sum_i w_i g_i^2},
\label{eq:gls}
\end{equation}
on the SPARC point sample after quality cuts ($Q\le2$, inclination $\ge30\degr$, $e_V/V<10$ per cent; frozen read-only database, 175 galaxies). The controlling systematic on the full sample is the stellar mass-to-light ratio $\Upsilon$: swinging $\Upsilon_d$ over its full physical range $0.5\to0.8$ moves the full-sample $\hat a_0$ from $1.89\to1.10\times10^{-10}$, a 62 per cent swing that reproduces the known RAR $a_0$--$\Upsilon$ degeneracy essentially in full. On the full sample the $a_0$-line is an \emph{exact reframing} of the RAR, but it inherits that degeneracy completely and adds no information.

The genuinely new content comes from the \textbf{gas-dominated subsample} -- the points where the atomic-gas mass (measured from H\,{\sc i}, independent of any stellar M/L) dominates the baryonic gravity, so the dominant M/L nuisance is largely removed. Applying a point-level cut
\begin{equation}
V_{\rm gas}^2 > \Upsilon_d V_{\rm disk}^2 + \Upsilon_b V_{\rm bul}^2,\qquad \Upsilon_b=1.4\,\Upsilon_d,
\label{eq:gascutdef}
\end{equation}
isolates those points. At the fiducial $\Upsilon_d=0.7$ this retains 310 points in 49 galaxies (weighted stellar share $\langle\varphi\rangle=0.32$). On this subsample the absolute $\Upsilon$ swing in $\hat a_0$ collapses from $7.90\times10^{-11}$ (full sample) to $2.29\times10^{-11}$ (gas-dominated): \textbf{the gas cut removes ${\sim}71$ per cent of the absolute $a_0$--$\Upsilon$ degeneracy} (\texttt{fire\_slope.py}; equivalently, in fractional terms the swing drops from 62 to 19 per cent, a ${\sim}69$ per cent reduction; independently reproduced at 15--29 per cent residual swing in \texttt{verify\_independent.py}). This is the paper's single new measurement lever, and it is standard in spirit -- gas-rich galaxies as M/L-insensitive probes date to \citet{McGaugh2011}; `new' here means new relative to the framework's own previously established walls.

\subsection{The estimate and its honest box}
\label{sec:box}

The gas-dominated slope is
\begin{equation}
\hat a_0^{\rm gas}=1.181\times10^{-10}\ (\text{GLS})\ /\ 0.973\times10^{-10}\ (\text{median}),
\label{eq:slope}
\end{equation}
with independent-machinery estimators (own parser, own cuts, own estimators) landing at $0.92$--$0.97\times10^{-10}$ -- i.e.\ siding with the median variant. The GLS--median spread is the single largest line in the error budget and is a physics-free degree of freedom; it is charged as a systematic, not hidden. Combining all estimator and cut freedom gives an \textbf{honest all-choices box}
\begin{equation}
\hat a_0^{\rm gas}\in(0.84\text{--}1.36)\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}.
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
Two distinct widths are quoted throughout, and they are not the same number: the $(0.84$--$1.36)\times10^{-10}$ box is the \emph{full estimator-and-cut envelope}, while $\pm16$ per cent is the \emph{$1\sigma$ systematic budget about the GLS central} (Section~\ref{sec:budget}); the envelope is wider than the $1\sigma$ budget by construction.

Placed against the two footings at the gas slope's honest error, the GLS point estimate sits $+1.29\sigma$ above canonical and $+0.27\sigma$ above ALT; the median variant sits $+0.19\sigma$ above canonical and $-0.83\sigma$ below ALT. Because the independent estimators lean toward the median -- the \emph{better-supported} central -- and it lands \emph{closer} to canonical, \textbf{there is no usable footing lean in either direction}: the 21 per cent fork is not decided. The box straddles both footings.

One estimator bias runs in a known direction. The response $E=g_{\rm obs}^2-g_{\rm bar}^2$ carries an uncorrected positive bias: $\mathbb E[g_{\rm obs}^2]=g_{\rm obs,true}^2+{\rm Var}(g_{\rm obs})>g_{\rm obs,true}^2$, so $\mathbb E[\hat a_0]\approx a_0+\sum w\,g\,{\rm Var}(g_{\rm obs})/\sum w\,g^2$ -- a positive push of order (fractional velocity error)$^2\approx1$--$4$ per cent. (The committed \texttt{gls(biased=False)} weighting cures the \emph{weight}--noise correlation, $\mathbb E[w\epsilon]<0$; it does not debias the \emph{response}.) This residual is small and sub-dominant to the effect of Section~\ref{sec:decline}, but it pushes the central in exactly the `sits above both anchors' direction on which the mild disfavouring of canonical rests. Its sign therefore means the canonical footing is, if anything, treated \emph{conservatively} -- the true $a_0$ is slightly lower than the reported central, i.e.\ slightly closer to canonical, not further from it.

\subsection{The declining per-point $a_0$: an unmodelled $g_{\rm bar}$-correlated systematic}
\label{sec:decline}

A load-bearing caveat, exposed under adversarial verification: the per-point quantity $a_{0,\rm pt}=E/g_{\rm bar}$ \textbf{declines across the deep regime}. Binning the gas points by $g_{\rm bar}$ (TRGB set, $\Upsilon_d=0.7$) gives per-point $a_0$ of ${\sim}1.62\to1.24\to0.62\times10^{-10}$ from the deepest to the highest $g_{\rm bar}$ tercile -- a factor ${\sim}2.6$ decline, entirely within $y\in[0.009,0.17]$.

It is important to name this correctly, because it is easy to mislabel. The framework's own fingerprint (Section~\ref{sec:fingerprint}) is $\varepsilon\equiv1$ \emph{exactly at all $y$}, which is precisely the statement $E/g_{\rm bar}={\rm const}=a_0$ with \textbf{zero shape to leak}: under the framework there is nothing about the kernel that could make $a_{0,\rm pt}$ decline. Nor do the rivals explain it: at the sampled deep $y\in[0.009,0.17]$, the RAR-fit and simple kernels both predict $\varepsilon$ \emph{rising} with $g_{\rm bar}$ ($\approx1.13\to1.32$ and $\approx1.13\to1.34$, respectively) -- the \emph{opposite sign} to the observed decline. The decline therefore matches \emph{no} candidate kernel in sign or magnitude. It is not kernel shape; it is \textbf{an unmodelled $g_{\rm bar}$-correlated systematic}, most plausibly residual stellar M/L acting through the $\varphi$--$g_{\rm bar}$ correlation at the fixed fiducial $\Upsilon_d=0.7$ (raising or lowering $\Upsilon$ tilts the trend -- a testable signature of M/L, not of the interpolation). Two consequences follow. First, a genuinely declining $a_{0,\rm pt}$ is a \emph{mild violation} of the framework's own $\varepsilon\equiv1$ fingerprint, not a benign feature of it; we book it as a systematic within the estimator-choice line $s_{\rm Est}$, not as a measured $\varepsilon\ne1$ (which the data are too shallow in $y$ to establish). Second, because the cause is a fixable estimator/M-L bias rather than irreducible kernel curvature, the estimator-weighted central is a \emph{regime-weighted slope} rather than a clean single $a_0$: median/GLS weight the deep regime ($\to1.3\times10^{-10}$), while a weight-free $g_{\rm bar}^2$-OLS weights the high-$g_{\rm bar}$ tail ($\to0.89\times10^{-10}$, near canonical -- though that weighting is wrong for the strongly heteroscedastic $E$). A future $E$-debiased or forward-modelled estimator, or points reaching $y\sim1$, could recover a cleaner single $a_0$. The estimator-choice systematic $s_{\rm Est}=|{\rm GLS}-{\rm median}|/2\approx1.04\times10^{-11}$ books this spread, and on the full-gas sample it alone exceeds the footing-splitting threshold. The honest consequence is that the measured $a_0$ is intrinsically a \textbf{box straddling both footings}, not a single-footing detection -- and the low $g_{\rm bar}^2$-OLS value is likewise \emph{not} a canonical detection.

\section{The systematic map: distance and mass-to-light}
\label{sec:systematics}

Three independent levers could in principle split the footings on current SPARC -- the radial-acceleration relation itself, distance quality, and stellar M/L. The first is convention-compatible and non-diagnostic of $a_0$'s exact value (Section~\ref{sec:gascut}); the second and third are mapped and spent below (Sections~\ref{sec:trgb} and \ref{sec:mllever}). All three fail on the same wall, which is why the fork is BIG-SPARC-gated (Section~\ref{sec:fork}). Each lever below carries a dedicated, committed, exit-0 pipeline and an adversarial verification pass. The threshold that matters throughout is the one needed to split the two footings at $2\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Delta&=|a_0^{\rm ALT}-a_0^{\rm canon}|=1.951\times10^{-11}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}},\\
\sigma_{\rm tot}&\le|\Delta|/2=9.753\times10^{-12}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The TRGB distance lever}
\label{sec:trgb}

Distances enter the excess through $g_{\rm obs}\propto1/D$ (while, exactly, $g_{\rm bar}\propto D^0$ for gas \emph{and} stars, since baryonic surface density is distance-independent -- verified symbolically). SPARC's distance-method flag $f_D$ isolates galaxies with tip-of-the-red-giant-branch (TRGB) or Cepheid distances ($f_D\in\{2,3\}$, distance error ${\sim}5$ per cent) from the Hubble-flow majority ($f_D=1$, ${\sim}25$ per cent). Restricting the gas-dominated subsample to $f_D\in\{2,3\}$ retains 18--20 galaxies (147--191 points), roughly half the gas sample and point-balanced against the Hubble-flow set -- so this is a \emph{budget} test, not a sample-size test (\texttt{scout\_split.py}). Crucially, the TRGB and Hubble-flow subsamples occupy the \emph{same} acceleration window ($y$-medians 0.037 versus 0.041): the identity $E=a_0 g_{\rm bar}$ holds at every $g_{\rm bar}$ regardless of a galaxy's mass or distance, so the two probe the same segment of the $a_0$-line.

The lever works mechanically (\texttt{est\_gls.py}, \texttt{est\_robust.py}; verified in \texttt{verify\_trgb.py}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The distance systematic is cut ${\sim}2$--$3.5\times$} ($6.5\to2.8$ per cent fractional at $\Upsilon_d=0.7$).
\item \textbf{The estimator-choice systematic collapses}: on the clean-distance set the median and GLS estimators agree to ${\sim}0.2\sigma$ (median $1.273$, GLS $1.333\times10^{-10}$ at $\Upsilon_d=0.7$).
\item \textbf{A Hubble-flow artifact is caught}: the banked low full-gas median ($0.973\times10^{-10}$) was largely a Hubble-flow-distance artifact; the clean-distance central moves \emph{up} coherently across median, GLS, and bootstrap to ${\sim}1.27$--$1.35\times10^{-10}$ (Hubble-flow-only median $0.805\times10^{-10}$). This is robust (galaxy-level bootstrap $1.329$ [16--84 per cent: $1.173$, $1.486$] $\times10^{-10}$; maximum leave-one-out shift $<10$ per cent) and is a distance-\emph{scale} effect, not a $g_{\rm bar}$-segment selection artifact (range-matching trims zero points).
\end{enumerate}

But the upward move is itself estimator-weighting-dependent (the unmodelled $g_{\rm bar}$-correlated systematic of Section~\ref{sec:decline}: the weight-free OLS gives $0.89\times10^{-10}$), so it is a regime-weighted slope of uncertain origin, not a canonical detection nor a manufactured deficit. And, decisively, \textbf{distance is not the binding wall}: once gas-dominated, the total error is floored by the M/L and gas-calibration systematics \emph{above} the $|\Delta|/2$ target on every prior convention. The clean-distance central at ${\sim}1.3\times10^{-10}$ sits above \emph{both} anchors, leaning mildly \emph{against} canonical ($-2.75\sigma$) rather than detecting it, with ALT weakly favoured ($-1.28\sigma$) but not confirmed -- and, per Section~\ref{sec:decline}, that very overshoot is partly the unmodelled systematic rather than a clean signal, so it is not read as a real move against canonical. The footing separation is $\le1.3$ bans (log-flat), $<1$ ban under an adversarial informed prior -- below the 2-ban line on every convention. \textbf{Verdict: the TRGB lever tightens the method but is non-diagnostic of the footing; the distance flag is spent.}

\subsection{The mass-to-light lever}
\label{sec:mllever}

The residual $\Upsilon$ uncertainty ($\sigma_{\ln\Upsilon}=0.0999$ dex) enters the current budget as \emph{one global, fully coherent} number, so it does not average down with galaxy count -- it is a floor. The lever (\texttt{setup\_mlpriors.py}, \texttt{est\_indep.py}, \texttt{est\_marg.py}, \texttt{est\_wall.py}; verified) decomposes it into a \textbf{coherent SPS/IMF zero-point floor} (irreducible; external colours cannot touch it) plus a \textbf{per-galaxy reducible part} (which external $[3.6]$+colour SPS priors shrink, and which averages ${\sim}1/\sqrt N$). Two calibration-preserving literature decompositions (quadrature $\approx$ the banked 0.0999 dex; \citealt{Schombert2019,Meidt2014,McGaughSchombert2014,BelldeJong2001}) are used, since SPARC ships one fixed $\Upsilon$ per galaxy and no local colour vector:
\begin{itemize}
\item balanced: coherent 0.060 dex $+$ per-galaxy residual 0.040 dex;
\item NIR-realistic: coherent 0.075 dex $+$ per-galaxy residual 0.035 dex.
\end{itemize}

Three findings, each verified both ways:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The real gain is recognizing the split, not the external prior.} Because at $[3.6]$ the M/L is nearly constant, the per-galaxy reducible signal is intrinsically small; the coherent line drops from $9.57\times10^{-12}$ to ${\sim}5.8\times10^{-12}$ (balanced, $\Upsilon_d=0.7$) mostly by acknowledging that part of the M/L error already averaged over ${\sim}49$ galaxies, not by new colour data.
\item \textbf{The coherent $\Upsilon$ zero-point is only \emph{partially} degenerate with $a_0$.} Treating it as a proper Bayesian nuisance template ($C=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_i^2)+s^2 UU^\top$, Sherman--Morrison marginalization) gives correlation $\rho(a_0,\alpha)=-0.65$ to $-0.81$, \emph{never} $\to1$, because the stellar share $\varphi$ genuinely varies with $g_{\rm bar}$ so the template $U$ is not proportional to $g$. The data therefore self-calibrate the nuisance, and marginalization \textbf{tightens} $a_0$ by 2--12 per cent (marg/quad $=0.88$--$0.98$): the quadrature budget was mildly conservative, not anti-conservative.
\item \textbf{Even perfect M/L is insufficient.} Zeroing the $\Upsilon$ systematic \emph{entirely} still leaves the best-case total at ${\sim}12.96\times10^{-12}$ (TRGB, $\Upsilon_d=0.7$; ${\sim}1.94\sigma$ footing separation) -- above $9.753\times10^{-12}$ and under $2\sigma$ everywhere. Beating $\Upsilon$ is \emph{necessary but not sufficient}.
\end{enumerate}

The reason is that \textbf{the true wall after $\Upsilon$ is gas-mass calibration} ($s_G$, coherent $\sigma_{\ln G}=0.10$). It does \emph{not} average down and does \emph{not} self-calibrate, because on gas-dominated points $1-\varphi\approx1$, so the gas template is nearly proportional to $g$. The coherent floor, $\mathrm{hypot}(5.75,\,8.63)\times10^{-12}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{hypot}(s_{U,\rm coh},\,s_G)=1.037\times10^{-11}>|\Delta|/2
\label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
\textbf{exceeds the footing-splitting threshold at any $N$} at $\sigma_{\ln G}=0.10$ (coherent terms do not average down). An external swap to colour-based M/L \citep{McGaughSchombert2014} trades the SPS coherent zero-point for a comparable colour-M/L/dust/age--metallicity zero-point (${\sim}0.10$--$0.15$ dex) with no net gain (a swept coherent floor never crosses the threshold). \textbf{Verdict: the M/L lever tightens $a_0$ but is non-diagnostic of the footing; the wall shifts to gas-mass calibration.}

\section{The $\Lambda$-inversion}
\label{sec:inversion}

The horizon tie inverts algebraically to the cosmological constant:
\begin{equation}
a_0=\frac{c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/3}}{Z}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\boxed{\;\Lambda=\frac{3Z^2\hat a_0^2}{c^4}\;}
\label{eq:inversion}
\end{equation}
(sympy-inverted, \texttt{fire\_lambda.py}; re-derived by hand in verification; note $3Z^2=32\pi$, so equivalently $\Lambda=32\pi\hat a_0^2/c^4$).

\textbf{What is and is not tested by this inversion -- the load-bearing point.} Because the canonical $a_0^{\rm canon}\equiv c^2\sqrt{\Lambda_{\rm Planck}/32\pi}$ is \emph{defined} from \textit{Planck}'s $\Lambda$, the predicted ratio is \emph{identically}
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Lambda_{\rm pred}}{\Lambda_{\rm Planck}}=\left(\frac{\hat a_0}{a_0^{\rm canon}}\right)^2\quad\text{exactly.}
\label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
The inversion is therefore a \emph{single-number test} -- does the rotation-measured $a_0$ equal the horizon value $cH_\Lambda/Z$? -- squared and re-expressed, not many independent decades of confirmation. The `factor ${\sim}2$ in $\Lambda$' is the `factor ${\sim}1.4$ in $a_0$' squared. What \emph{is} genuinely independent and non-trivial is that the rotation-curve $\hat a_0$ uses \emph{zero} cosmological input (only rotation velocities $V$, radii $R$, and baryonic decompositions), so its agreement with a horizon-derived acceleration is a real galactic-versus-cosmological coincidence -- the classic Milgrom $a_0\sim cH$ coincidence, here sharpened. What is \emph{not} tested is (a) the normalization $Z=\sqrt{32\pi/3}$, which appears on \emph{both} sides of the inversion and is assumed throughout, never checked against data; and (b) the `large exponent' of $\Lambda$ (${\sim}10^{-52}\ {\rm m^{-2}}$ in SI units), which is a unit-dependent dimensional statement, not a measured dynamic range. We flag both so the coincidence is not over-read.

The clean $\Lambda$ statement is on the canonical (pure-$\Lambda$) footing; the ALT footing ties $a_0$ to $H_0$ (matching the GLS slope at $+0.27\sigma$) and does not itself produce a `$\Lambda$', so the inversion column is canonical-only by construction.

The headline is the coincidence itself: \textbf{rotation curves of gas-rich dwarfs recover the cosmological constant to a factor ${\sim}1.1$--$2.0$ of the value measured from the cosmic microwave background -- two determinations separated by the span from galactic to cosmological scales, with no cosmological input on the galactic side.} This is what a horizon-scale $a_0=cH_\Lambda/Z$ predicts, and it is the same information as the previously reported $a_0\sim cH_\Lambda/Z$ coincidence, reframed as an inversion -- now with a sharper falsification target: a future gas-dominated slope at $3\times10^{-10}$ would break it outright. Two honest hedges. First, the \emph{better-supported} estimators (median and independent machinery) land \emph{closer} to \textit{Planck} ($+0.24\sigma$), so the inversion is if anything cleaner than the GLS headline. Second, the \emph{cleaner the distances, the more the central overshoots Planck}: the TRGB-tightened central sits $2.03\times$ high ($+2.76\sigma$), further above rather than onto \textit{Planck}, so the sharpest current data mildly \emph{disfavour} the canonical footing rather than confirm it -- and, per Section~\ref{sec:decline}, that overshoot is itself partly the unmodelled $g_{\rm bar}$-correlated systematic, so its interpretation is uncertain and it is not read as a real deficit either.

\textbf{The Occam comparison.} Contrasting the zero-parameter horizon prediction $M_0=\{a_0\equiv cH_\Lambda/Z,\ \text{no free parameters},\ \pm1\ \text{per cent anchor}\}$ against a free-$a_0$ model $M_1$ with a log-flat prior of width $W$:
\begin{equation}
B_{01}=\underbrace{\frac{W}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,s}}_{\text{Occam}}\;\underbrace{e^{-t^2/2}}_{\text{fit penalty}},\qquad t=\frac{\ln a_0^*-\ln\hat a_0}{\sqrt{s^2+s_{\rm anchor}^2}},\quad s=0.161.
\label{eq:occam}
\end{equation}
On the default two-decade prior: $B_{01}=+0.60$ bans (canonical) / $+1.04$ bans (ALT), with a prior/estimator envelope $+0.30$ to $+1.38$ (canonical) / $+0.57$ to $+1.34$ (ALT). On Jeffreys' scale this is `substantial' -- \textbf{positive but modest, explicitly not decisive}. It formalizes \emph{predicted-not-fitted} (that $a_0$ was fixed from $c$, $H_\Lambda$, $Z$ before the fit), not new data, and it is prior-convention-fragile: a literature-informed prior conditioned on the MOND scale can drive the canonical bans toward ${\sim}0$ (though never negative). Two-sided, the lever is also a genuine falsification exposure: if a future clean measurement holds the GLS central ($1.18\times10^{-10}$) at $3\times$ smaller error, the canonical footing would move to $-2.45$ bans -- which is precisely what makes the measurement worth sharpening.

\section{The footing fork and honest scope}
\label{sec:fork}

The two footings are 20.9 per cent apart:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
a_0^{\rm canon}&=cH_\Lambda/Z=9.355\times10^{-11}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}\ (\rho_{\rm DE}),\\
a_0^{\rm ALT}&=cH_0/Z=1.1305\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}\ (\rho_{\rm total}).
\end{split}
\label{eq:forkdef}
\end{equation}
The measured $a_0$ is a box $(0.84$--$1.36)\times10^{-10}$ that \textbf{straddles both footings}. On the estimator-weighted central the canonical footing is mildly \emph{disfavoured} (the central sits above it, ${\sim}{-3}\sigma$ high-side across configurations), while ALT is \emph{not confirmed} (${\sim}{-1.4}\sigma$). We manufacture neither a canonical detection nor a deficit: the honest reading is a straddle, and the mild high-side lean is partly the response bias (Section~\ref{sec:box}) and the unmodelled $g_{\rm bar}$-correlated systematic (Section~\ref{sec:decline}), both of which push the central up.

The fork is \textbf{not decidable from current SPARC by any systematic lever}. The RAR is convention-compatible and non-diagnostic of $a_0$'s exact value; the TRGB distance lever is spent (Section~\ref{sec:trgb}); the M/L lever is spent, including its perfect-M/L limit (Section~\ref{sec:mllever}). The binding wall is the coherent gas-calibration $+$ SPS floor $+$ estimator/regime-slope spread, and $\mathrm{hypot}(s_{U,\rm coh},s_G)=1.037\times10^{-11}$ exceeds $|\Delta|/2=9.753\times10^{-12}$ at \emph{any} $N$ at the current gas-calibration precision.

Deciding the fork therefore requires \emph{all} of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{BIG-SPARC-scale statistics} -- a clean-distance gas-dwarf sample of $N\sim300$--$670$ (the what-it-takes map: at $\sigma_{\ln G}=0.08$, $N\sim666$; at 0.06, $N\sim273$), to pull the statistical floor down and force the estimator-choice systematic into the open;
\item \textbf{an independent gas-mass calibration} cutting $\sigma_{\ln G}$ from 0.10 to $\lesssim0.08$ (interferometric H\,{\sc i} $+$ CO, He/metal corrections) -- since at $\sigma_{\ln G}=0.10$ no finite $N$ suffices;
\item \textbf{points reaching $y\sim1$} to break the regime-slope magnitude systematic of Section~\ref{sec:decline} (and, as a bonus, to make the $\varepsilon=1$ constancy fingerprint of Section~\ref{sec:fingerprint} testable).
\end{enumerate}

What is robust today is the \textbf{order-of-magnitude inversion}: $\Lambda$ recovered from dwarf rotation to within a factor ${\sim}2$ of \textit{Planck}, with no cosmological input on the galactic side. What is blocked is the \textbf{21 per cent which-dark-energy-quantity distinction} -- canonical $\rho_{\rm DE}$ versus total $\rho_{\rm total}$. The first is a striking measurement; the second is data-gated, and we state it as such.

A footing-fork subtlety worth flagging for future epochs: the canonical footing predicts the gas slope tracks $\sqrt{\rho_\Lambda}$ (constant in redshift), while the ALT footing tracks $cH(z)E(z)$ (rising). A multi-epoch $a_0(z)$ measurement (e.g.\ via DESI-scale samples) would turn the 21 per cent degeneracy into a slope-versus-redshift discriminator -- the one route that does not run into the static systematic floor mapped here.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The result of this paper is a \textbf{measurement}, and it should be read as one. The $a_0$-line identity converts the framework's rotation-curve law into a single measurable slope; the gas-dominated cut on SPARC removes ${\sim}71$ per cent of the stellar M/L degeneracy that otherwise dominates such measurements; and inverting the slope recovers the cosmological constant to a factor ${\sim}2$ of the \textit{Planck} value, from galactic-scale kinematics that carry no cosmological input. That coincidence -- that the outskirts of nearby gas-rich dwarfs encode, to within a factor of two, the same $\Lambda$ that governs the accelerating expansion of the universe -- is the striking positive of the framework's horizon-scale $a_0=cH_\Lambda/Z$ hypothesis, and it is stated here without overclaim. Its honest weight is exactly that of the classic Milgrom $a_0\sim cH$ coincidence and of the $a_0\propto\sqrt{\rho_{\rm DE}}$ proposal of \citet{Limbach2008}, sharpened into an inversion and a falsifiable target -- no more, because the inversion is one comparison squared, and it assumes rather than tests the normalization $Z$.

What the measurement does \emph{not} do is equally important. It does not distinguish the two dark-energy footings: the measured $a_0$ is a box straddling both, and every systematic lever available on current SPARC (the RAR, distance, M/L) has been mapped and spent without crossing the $2\sigma$ splitting threshold. The binding wall is gas-mass calibration together with an irreducible coherent SPS floor and an unmodelled $g_{\rm bar}$-correlated systematic that pulls the inferred magnitude down across the deep regime -- a mild tension with the framework's own $\varepsilon\equiv1$ fingerprint, not a benign feature of it. None of these is reducible by more distance or M/L work, and even a perfect M/L measurement leaves the footing separation below $2\sigma$. The identity is a re-expression of a kernel due to \citet{Milgrom1999}; the distinctive empirical content is the exact-constancy fingerprint (data-starved today), the $cH_\Lambda/Z$ coefficient, and the inversion. And the value of $a_0$ and the sign $s=-1$ are postulates: what is tied to the horizon is the \emph{scale}.

Read as a statement about method, `$a_0$-from-rotation $=$ the dark-energy density scale' is the sharpest available single-number route from galaxy rotation to the cosmological constant, and this paper strengthens the \emph{method} (the gas cut, the distance and M/L maps, the inversion) while being explicit that it does not yet strengthen the \emph{claim} to a footing-level detection. A genuinely striking numerical coincidence has been sharpened into a falsifiable target, and the two-sided Occam lever means the framework now carries real falsification exposure on the canonical footing -- which is what makes the next generation of data (BIG-SPARC, an independent gas-mass calibration, and $y\sim1$ points) worth acquiring. An in-principle modified-gravity-impossible discriminator between the modified-inertia and modified-gravity readings -- the relational velocity-dispersion spread -- is developed separately \citep{Zimmerman2026c}, but is not yet detectable with existing data.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\textbf{Central claim.} Gas-rich dwarf-galaxy rotation curves invert, through the fixed constant $Z=\sqrt{32\pi/3}$, to the cosmological constant measured by \textit{Planck} to within a factor of ${\sim}2$ -- a striking coincidence with no cosmological input on the galactic side -- but the measured $a_0=(0.84$--$1.36)\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}$ is a systematics-limited box that straddles both the canonical ($cH_\Lambda/Z=9.36\times10^{-11}$) and ALT ($cH_0/Z=1.13\times10^{-10}$) footings; the ${\sim}21$ per cent distinction between which dark-energy quantity the horizon term tracks is blocked by a gas-mass-calibration floor and cannot be decided on current SPARC.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The identity.} Squaring the framework's interpolation gives $E\equiv g_{\rm obs}^2-g_{\rm bar}^2=a_0 g_{\rm bar}$, exact at every acceleration -- the missing-gravity excess is a straight line through the origin with slope $a_0$. The kernel is Milgrom's (1999); the distinctive content is the exact constancy $\varepsilon=1$, the $cH_\Lambda/Z$ coefficient, and the inversion.
\item \textbf{The measurement.} On the gas-dominated SPARC subsample the slope is $\hat a_0=(0.84$--$1.36)\times10^{-10}\ {\rm m\,s^{-2}}$ (the full estimator-and-cut envelope; $\pm16$ per cent $1\sigma$ systematic budget; no usable footing lean), with ${\sim}71$ per cent of the absolute M/L degeneracy removed and an unmodelled $g_{\rm bar}$-correlated systematic (a mild tension with $\varepsilon\equiv1$) carried throughout.
\item \textbf{The systematic floor.} The TRGB distance lever collapses the estimator-choice systematic and catches a Hubble-flow artifact but is not the binding wall; the M/L lever tightens $a_0$ by 2--12 per cent through partial self-calibration ($\rho\approx-0.7$ to $-0.8$) yet is insufficient even in the perfect-M/L limit. The true wall is gas-mass calibration: $\mathrm{hypot}(s_{U,\rm coh},s_G)=1.037\times10^{-11}$ exceeds $|\Delta|/2=9.753\times10^{-12}$ at any $N$.
\item \textbf{The inversion.} $\Lambda=3Z^2\hat a_0^2/c^4$ recovers the cosmological constant to a factor ${\sim}2$ of \textit{Planck} (full-gas GLS $1.59\times$, $+1.45\sigma$; median/independent $1.08\times$, $+0.24\sigma$; TRGB $2.03\times$, $+2.76\sigma$ -- overshooting) -- the striking positive, favoured by $+0.60$ bans (canonical) / $+1.04$ bans (ALT) on a log-flat Occam comparison, substantial but not decisive. It is one comparison, $\hat a_0$ versus $cH_\Lambda/Z$, squared and re-expressed, and it assumes the normalization $Z$.
\item \textbf{The scope.} The order-of-magnitude inversion is robust; the 21 per cent canonical-versus-ALT footing distinction is data-blocked, requiring BIG-SPARC-scale clean-distance samples, a sharper gas-mass calibration ($\sigma_{\ln G}\lesssim0.08$), and $y\sim1$ points. The value of $a_0$ and the sign $s=-1$ remain postulates; what is tied to the horizon is the scale.
\end{enumerate}
