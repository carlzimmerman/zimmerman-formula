To the Editors, Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society

Dear Editors,

I submit for consideration in MNRAS a manuscript titled "Reading the
cosmological constant from gas-rich dwarf rotation curves: the a0-line and
its systematic floor."

The single result. The MOND low-acceleration excess can be written as an
exact identity, E = g_obs^2 - g_bar^2 = a0 * g_bar -- a straight line through
the origin whose slope is a0 at every acceleration. Fitting that slope on the
gas-dominated subsample of SPARC (Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 2016), where the
stellar mass-to-light degeneracy is suppressed by roughly 70 per cent, gives
a0 = (0.84-1.36) x 10^-10 m s^-2 -- the full estimator-and-cut envelope, with
a +/-16 per cent 1-sigma systematic budget about the central value. Inverting
through Lambda = 32*pi*a0^2/c^4 recovers the Planck cosmological constant to
within a factor of ~2 -- from galactic kinematics that use no cosmological
input. That is the paper's headline, and it is stated as a measurement, not a
confirmation.

Why it is honest. The measured a0 is a systematics-limited box, not a point.
It straddles the two candidate normalisations of the acceleration scale
(c*H_Lambda/Z = 9.36 x 10^-11 and c*H_0/Z = 1.13 x 10^-10 m s^-2), and the
paper demonstrates -- with committed, reproducible pipelines -- that no lever
available on current SPARC (distance quality, mass-to-light priors, or the
RAR itself) can separate them: the binding wall is gas-mass calibration. I
report the factor-~2 inversion as striking but non-decisive, and I am equally
careful not to manufacture a detection or a deficit. The interpolation kernel
nu(y) = sqrt(1 + 1/y) is Milgrom's (1999), credited at the point of use; the
a0 ~ sqrt(rho_DE) coincidence predates this work (Limbach, Psaltis & Ozel
2008) and is credited; the value of a0 and the sign of the inertial
correction are treated throughout as posited inputs, not derived quantities.

Why MNRAS. This is a galaxy-dynamics measurement paper built on the SPARC
database, the radial-acceleration relation, and standard stellar-population
and distance systematics -- core observational-astrophysics territory for
MNRAS, and the venue where much of the SPARC/RAR systematics literature has
appeared. The contribution is methodological and empirical: (i) recasting the
RAR as a single measurable slope via the exact a0-line identity; (ii) using
the gas-rich cut to suppress the mass-to-light degeneracy; and (iii)
inverting to Lambda with a fully mapped systematic budget. All load-bearing
numbers reproduce from committed, exit-0 scripts in the public repository
(self-verifying); the a0-line identity and slope are additionally implemented
in the a0kit package (Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21478981; ASCL submission
under editor review).

Scope and honesty statement. I am an independent researcher without academic
affiliation, and I have tried to calibrate the claims accordingly: bold where
the data earn it (the order-of-magnitude inversion with zero cosmological
input), bounded where they do not (the ~21 per cent normalisation
distinction, which is data-blocked), and falsifiable throughout (a future
gas-dominated slope near 3 x 10^-10 m s^-2 would break the inversion
outright). The manuscript makes no claim to derive a0, to derive Lambda, or
to resolve the MOND-versus-dark-matter question.

I confirm the work is original and not under consideration elsewhere. The
underlying manuscript is archived on Zenodo as a preprint (DOI
10.5281/zenodo.21419735); an arXiv posting (astro-ph.GA) is planned [arXiv ID: __________]. I have no competing interests and no funding to declare. I would welcome
referees with expertise in rotation curves and MOND phenomenology, and
