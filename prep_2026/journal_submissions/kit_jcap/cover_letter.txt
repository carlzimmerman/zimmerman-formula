To the Editors of the Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics

Dear Editors,

I submit for your consideration "A parameter-free, pre-registered test that
the galaxy acceleration scale tracks the dark-energy density:
a0(z)/a0(0) = sqrt(rho_DE(z)/rho_DE,0)".

The one result. In the de Sitter-Unruh modified-inertia framework the
low-acceleration scale is sourced by the cosmological horizon, a0 = cH_L/Z.
Promoting this relation adiabatically as the horizon evolves yields the
displayed law (the promotion is an adiabatic-horizon ansatz, itself posited).
The observable is the ratio R = a0(z)/a0(0), which is independent of the
framework's internal normalization Z and of the absolute value of a0 - both
cancel identically - so R depends only on the measured dark-energy history
w(z). R tracks sqrt(rho_DE(z)): under the DESI-preferred (w0 > -1, wa < 0)
histories it declines at high redshift, it reduces to R = 1 if w = -1, and a
measured rise registers as a strained verdict in the frozen protocol. This
turns the "why is a0 ~ c*sqrt(rho_Lambda)" coincidence into a quantitative,
falsifiable, redshift-dependent test.

Why it fits JCAP. The claim connects a galactic-dynamics scale to the
dark-energy equation of state, is confronted against DESI DR2 w0-wa
constraints with standard CPL machinery, and is pre-registered - estimator,
thresholds, and data hierarchy SHA-256-hashed and Zenodo-timestamped - against
the Rubin/LSST calibrated supernova sample (~2027+). It is a falsifiable
program with a pre-committed decision procedure, not a discovery announcement.

Honest scope, stated up front. (1) The cosmology-side gate inherits w(z): it
is a consistency condition, not independent evidence, and if w = -1 the
distinctive prediction dissolves into constant-a0 MOND. (2) The
a0 ~ sqrt(rho_DE) coincidence is not original to me - Limbach, Psaltis & Ozel
(2008) noted and tested it; my addition is fixing the coupling to rho_DE via
the adiabatic-horizon ansatz plus the committed cross-scale test. (3) The
value of a0, the normalization Z, and the sign are posited, not derived; the
kernel nu = sqrt(1+1/y) is Milgrom's (1999). (4) The current verdict is
UNDECIDED (DESI DR2: R = 0.775 [0.68, 0.88], a 2.0-sigma hint below the
committed 3-sigma bar). (5) The galaxy-side lane is underpowered and
M_bar-dominated; the manuscript's central methodological finding is that its
dominant M_bar systematic is common-mode, so the BTFR test never reaches
3 sigma at any sample size above a ~0.08-dex correlated floor - the decisive
lever is common-mode M_bar calibration to <~0.05 dex, and the
degeneracy-breaking observable is the rotation-curve/RAR transition.

I am an independent researcher without academic affiliation. Every
quantitative figure in the manuscript is produced by a committed, runnable,
exit-0 script in a self-verifying public repository (the a0kit toolkit,
Zenodo software DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21478981), and every claim is bounded both
ways. I publicly retracted earlier over-broad "theory of everything" claims in
2026; my position here is strictly the a0(z) reframing and its test. What I
ask JCAP to referee is not "is the framework correct" but "is this a
well-posed, honestly bounded, pre-registered cosmological test worth having on
record before the Rubin sample lands."

The work is not under consideration elsewhere. The Zenodo deposits it
consolidates (DOIs 10.5281/zenodo.20737162, 21440407, 21478568) are my own
timestamped preprints, released for reproducibility; two companion
manuscripts (a field-theory paper and a SPARC measurement paper) treat
logically distinct results and are cited, not consolidated. I have no
competing interests and no funding to report.
