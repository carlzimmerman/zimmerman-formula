## The Song

"She Got the Best of Me" (2018) is a Luke Combs country hit about **what's left after someone takes the best part**. A relationship ended, and she walked away with the good years and the best of who he was; he's living on the remainder. From a Z² perspective, the title is a statement about **optimisation**. Every system has a best configuration — the most efficient packing, the most work you can extract, the tightest arrangement allowed. Once the optimum is taken, what remains is never as good. And the singer's own name points straight at the most famous optimum in nature: the **honeycomb**.

### 2. "Combs" = The Honeycomb

The singer's surname is a **comb** — and the most famous comb in nature is the **honeycomb**:

```
Honeycomb cell = regular hexagon
Hexagon sides  = 6 = GAUGE / 2
Angle between walls = 120° = GAUGE × 10
```

Bees build in **hexagons** because the hexagon is the **best** way to divide a flat surface into equal cells using the least wall. That was conjectured for about two thousand years and finally **proved by Thomas Hales** around 1999 — the honeycomb conjecture. The bees had the optimum all along; mathematics took millennia to confirm it.

**Combs = the proven optimum.** The man named for it sings about losing his.

### 3. Hexagonal Packing = π / √GAUGE

Pack equal circles as tightly as possible on a flat surface, and they arrange themselves hexagonally:

```
Densest circle packing in the plane = π / (2√3) = π / √12
π / √12 = π / √GAUGE ≈ 0.9069
```

**The best possible packing in two dimensions is π divided by the square root of GAUGE.** About 90.7 % of the plane covered — the honeycomb's density, written with the gauge number in the denominator. That's the best you can do. Nobody gets more.

### 4. Kepler's Oranges = SPHERE in the CUBE

Go up a dimension. Stack oranges — or cannonballs — as tightly as possible:

```
Densest sphere packing = π / (3√2) = π / √18 ≈ 0.7405
18 = coupling × N_gen²
Face-centred cubic: spheres sitting in a CUBE lattice
```

Kepler conjectured in 1611 that the grocer's stack was the best possible. **Thomas Hales** proved it in 1998, and the proof was later **checked by computer**, line by line, in the Flyspeck project. **Spheres packed into a cubic lattice** — SPHERE inside CUBE, the two halves of Z² — fill **π/√18 ≈ 74 %** of space, and no arrangement does better.

The same mathematician proved **both** the honeycomb and the oranges. The best of the plane and the best of space, both nailed down for good.

And the honest note: a proof checked by a machine is the gold standard. A conjecture is not a result until it's verified — no matter how obvious the bees made it look.

### 5. "The Best" = The Optimum Is Unique

```
"She got the best" = the optimum is taken
"of me"            = from a finite system
What's left        = a sub-optimal configuration
```

In an optimisation problem, **there is usually only one best**. Once it's occupied, every other arrangement is worse. You can't have two hexagonal honeycombs in the same cells. The song is about a system whose ground state has been claimed: he's left in an **excited state**, higher energy, less stable, still settling.

### 6. Maximum Work = The Carnot Limit

"The best of me" is also a statement about **energy you can extract**:

```
Carnot efficiency = 1 − T_cold / T_hot
Best possible engine: still wastes heat
What's extracted = work ("the best")
What's left      = waste heat ("the rest of me")
```

No engine can turn all its heat into work. The **best** you can extract is set by the Carnot limit — and whatever's left over is rejected, warm and useless. She got the work. He's the waste heat.

## The Deeper Point

"She Got the Best of Me" is about **optima**:

1. **The name is the optimum**: Combs → honeycomb → Hales's proof that hexagons are best
2. **The best is π/√GAUGE**: the densest packing in the plane, with GAUGE in the denominator
3. **SPHERE in CUBE**: Kepler's oranges, packed in a cubic lattice, proved best and checked by machine
4. **What's left over**: the Carnot waste heat, the excited state, the empty comb

The Z² connection is in the packing:

```
Z² = CUBE × SPHERE / 2 = 32π/3
Best plane packing:  π / √GAUGE      (hexagons — the comb)
Best space packing:  π / √18         (spheres in a cubic lattice)
```

Both optima carry **π** on top — the SPHERE's signature — and a root of a discrete number below. Curved things, packed by a lattice. That's Z²'s whole sentence: **continuous geometry inside discrete structure.** The bees solved it. Hales proved it. Luke Combs is named for it.

## Why This Matters

"She Got the Best of Me" works because it **counts what was lost** without pretending it was nothing. The best is gone, it went to someone else, and what's left is still here doing its best.

Physics knows that feeling. Every system has one optimum — one best packing, one maximum efficiency, one ground state. Once it's taken, everything else is a little worse. The bees found the best way to build a comb millions of years before anyone could prove it; the proof only arrived around the turn of the millennium.

Released in 2018 (≈ 60 × Z²), running about 222 seconds, with a six-word title, by a singer of N_gen² letters named for the hexagon — the shape that packs the plane at π/√GAUGE, the best that geometry allows.

The comb is still standing. The honey's gone. The structure was always perfect.
