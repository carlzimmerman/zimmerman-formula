## A Universe Out of Balance

In March 2025, the DESI Collaboration released its first-year results—the most precise measurements of cosmic structure from galaxy clustering ever made. The results were stunning, but not in the way cosmologists hoped.

DESI's "full-shape" analysis, which extracts cosmological parameters from the detailed pattern of galaxy clustering, found a matter density parameter Ω_m = 0.291 ± 0.009. This disagrees with the Planck satellite's measurement of Ω_m = 0.315 ± 0.007 at the 2.8σ level.

This isn't a one-off fluctuation. DESI's baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements give Ω_m ≈ 0.295. The Pantheon+ supernova sample gives similar results. A pattern is emerging: **late-time probes of the universe prefer lower matter density than the cosmic microwave background (CMB)**.

Sound familiar? It should. This is the same pattern seen in the Hubble tension, where late-time measurements of the expansion rate (H₀ ≈ 73 km/s/Mpc) disagree with CMB-inferred values (H₀ ≈ 67 km/s/Mpc).

We may be witnessing a second crack in the standard ΛCDM model.
