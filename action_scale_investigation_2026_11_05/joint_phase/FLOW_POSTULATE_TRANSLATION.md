## A concrete illustrative action

In c=hbar=1 units, signature -+++, write a complex field as f exp(i theta),
with the following normalization:

    S=integral sqrt(-g)[M_Pl^2 R/2
         -(partial f)^2/2-f^2(partial theta)^2/2
         -V0-lambda(f^2-v0^2)^2/4]+S_b[g,matter].

lambda,v0,V0 are positive. This standard broken-U(1) example is chosen
to test the distinction between a background and vortices, not to assert
that it is the user's theory or to select its parameters. Matter is
minimally coupled; no extra MOND force has been inserted.

The field equations are

    box f-f(partial theta)^2-lambda f(f^2-v0^2)=0,
    nabla_mu(f^2 partial^mu theta)=0.

The homogeneous constant configuration f=v0, theta=constant has
T_mu_nu=-V0 g_mu_nu: rho_E=V0 and pressure=-V0. Thus a field background
can have vacuum-like stress even though its excitations can carry gradients
and motion. This is different from the low-pressure massive excitations
called 'cold energy' in the existing carrier inventory. Low temperature
alone does not determine the sign or magnitude of pressure.

## Eddy-like structure and what it does not establish

For a straight static vortex in a locally flat cylindrical patch, take
theta=n phi with integer n and f=f(r). The field equation becomes

    f''+f'/r-n^2 f/r^2-lambda f(f^2-v0^2)=0,
    f(0)=0 for nonzero n, f(r)->v0 far from the core.

The winding condition is integral grad(theta).dl=2pi n. The characteristic
radial mass is m_f^2=2lambda v0^2. Far from the core the leading gradient
energy density is n^2 v0^2/(2r^2), and the energy per unit length has an
infrared logarithm pi n^2 v0^2 log(R/r_core). This is an idealized line
defect requiring finite-size/boundary treatment, not a spherical galaxy.
The field amplitude is depleted at the center. Additional matter clumping
does not follow from the winding boundary condition.

A nonrelativistic condensate interpretation can relate velocity to a phase
gradient, but it requires a finite-density state and its normalization.
The static vacuum vortex above does not by itself define a timelike fluid
four-velocity, and no formula identifying star velocity with phase velocity
has been assumed. Stars respond to the metric and any separately derived
matter coupling. Consequently vortex existence is not a derivation of
g=sqrt(a0 g_b) or V_flat^4=G M_b a0.

V0 disappears from the fixed-metric vortex profile equation, while it
sets the homogeneous vacuum energy. Changing V0 therefore leaves that
local profile equation unchanged. Gravity can reintroduce background
dependence, so this is not a full backreacting independence theorem.
Nevertheless, the action supplies no identity tying V0 to lambda,v0 or
the circulation integer. An initial 'kick' also leaves its amplitude,
angular momentum and spectrum as state data unless a production mechanism
is specified. No coefficient1/2 has been derived.

## Literature boundary and next useful question

The general background-plus-vortices idea has precedents.
[Huang, Xiong and Zhao,1304.1595v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.1595v2)
describe a vacuum scalar/superfluid picture with dark energy, density
variations and galactic vortices. This is an abstract-level relevance
check, not verification or adoption of their cosmological/galaxy claims.
[Mauland and Elgaroy,2109.01011v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01011v3)
investigate vortices in a specific superfluid-dark-matter model and report
significant limits on their galactic impact and the attempted vortex
solution. That does not refute every fluid picture; it shows why a vortex
must be connected explicitly to the force on visible matter. Source
metadata and abstracts checked5 November2026; no novelty claim is made.
