# A charged radial principal-symbol test

Partial result for the single-clock density candidate, not a coefficient
derivation or a complete stability verdict. Self-review only.

## Static ansatz and omitted equations

In unitary gauge phi=q t with constant V, the projected polarization action
depends on N,h_ij,P^i and their spatial derivatives, not the ADM shift.
X=q^2/(2N^2), A_i=D_i log N and D.P are intrinsic to the same leaves.
At a static zero-shift background K_ij=0, the Einstein momentum constraint
vanishes as well. Thus no missing radial momentum source is generated by
this ansatz. For a smooth covariant solution with all remaining metric and
P equations satisfied, the diffeomorphism identity then recovers the scalar
equation since grad phi is timelike. In spherical areal gauge the angular
metric equation follows from the radial identity and the N,B,p equations.
This is an analytic symmetry/constraint argument, not a numerical check of
an independently coded full covariant stress tensor. It assumes the spatial
P constraint and its multiplier are eliminated consistently.

## Radial kinetic coefficient

Use ds^2=-N^2dt^2+B^2(dr+beta dt)^2+r^2dOmega^2. The extrinsic-curvature
eigenvalues are

    k_r=(Bdot/B-beta'-beta B'/B)/N, k_t=-beta/(Nr).

After integrating beta beta' by parts, the quadratic velocity action around
the static branch is

    Ltime=2M^2 r beta deltaBdot/N
              -M^2 r (B'+B N'/N) beta^2/N.

Eliminating beta gives a Hessian

    Mt=2M^2 r/[N(B'+B N'/N)].

The static field equations give the exact useful identity

    B'+B N'/N = B r/(2M^2)
       [B^2(2X+N W_N)+B d/K+gamma d^2/K^2],
    d=p'+2p/r.

For X>0,p>=0,d>=0 this is positive. More general branches require checking
its actual sign. Lapse and polarization have no velocity terms on this
background and do not change Mt when their quadratic spatial constraints
are eliminated. This is a radial unitary-gauge statement, not a general DOF
count. Boundary terms must vanish for the perturbation class used.

## Frozen short-wave radial symbol

Set s=p/(a0-p), h=s+s/(1+s)-2log(1+s), rho=X+V and

    T=1+b h/(2K)-b X s^3/[2K rho(1+s)],
    z=gamma d/(K B),
    D0=2X T+(1+z)^2/gamma,
    Num=2X+N W_N+d/(K B)+gamma d^2/(K^2 B^2).

The lapse-lapse Hessian of the radial action is 2B r^2 X T/N. The
polarization derivative Hessian is -gamma N r^2/(K^2 B). The N,p spatial
antisymmetric mixing is -r^2/K-gamma r^2d/(K^2B), and the B,N mixing
is -2M^2r/B^2. Eliminating the high-k polarization constraint adds a
positive constant to the effective lapse coefficient:

    Aeff = B r^2 D0/N.

For a generic Hermitian three-field matrix with all finite lower-order
entries retained, the remaining B coefficient divided by k^2 tends to
-(2M^2r/B^2)^2/Aeff. Combining it with Mt and converting coordinate
speed to local radial speed gives

    c_radial^2 = Num/D0.

Background derivative terms do not alter this leading result: L_N,N'=0,
so the effective lapse diagonal has no missing derivative correction;
derivatives of the N,p mixing coefficient enter at order k^0 rather than
its order-k leading mixing; derivatives of the p spatial Hessian enter at
order k rather than k^2. Lower-order terms may still change finite-k
stability. The asymptotic limit also requires wave numbers where the EFT
remains valid; this calculation supplies no ultraviolet cutoff.

The useful exact speed comparison is

    D0-Num=(1+z)/gamma - b X^2 s^3/[K rho(1+s)].

It shows why a negative two-clock T by itself no longer proves a ghost
for this single-clock screened candidate. It does NOT make stability
automatic on every background: D0,Num,Mt and the cutoff still matter.

## Four frozen finite-shell profiles

All four converged twice with maximum scaled profile difference2.27e-7
(required1e-5). Sampled 0<p/a0<1 and positive N,B hold. On their804 saved
profile points, Mt and D0 are positive and 0<c_radial^2<1. The ranges are:

| b | Lstar | minimum c_radial^2 | maximum c_radial^2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| .1 | 1 | 5.00e-10 | 8.99e-6 |
| .1 | 3 | 4.50e-9 | 2.91e-5 |
| .3 | 1 | 5.00e-10 | 8.27e-6 |
| .3 | 3 | 4.50e-9 | 1.98e-5 |

For b=.1,Lstar=3 the independent-clock diagnostic T reaches-7.92 while
the single-clock radial principal coefficients remain positive. This is a
specific illustration of why the earlier two-clock obstruction cannot be
transferred. It is not an empirical success or a full-theory counterexample
to every MOND stability objection. Small speeds also leave a strong-coupling
question; positivity is insufficient to settle it.

## Remaining discriminator

The finite-frequency radial operator including background gradients and
boundary conditions has not been graded. Derive its variational Hessian,
keeping lapse and polarization as constraints, and test a controlled radial
box before angular modes or physical background matching. No value of b
has been selected: these successes occurred at two stipulated b values.
