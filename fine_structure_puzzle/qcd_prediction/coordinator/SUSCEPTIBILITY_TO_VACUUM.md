# Why topological susceptibility alone cannot determine the cosmological coefficient

FSP-003, parent FSP-002. Coordinator analytical reduction. No new executed experiment or novelty claim.

Let f(H,theta,alpha) be a fully specified Euclidean vacuum functional density with all renormalized input data fixed. Suppose its small-H expansion contains

f(H,theta,alpha)-f(0,theta,alpha)
=H A_f(theta,alpha)+o(H),

with enough regularity to commute theta derivatives and the limit. Topological susceptibility is chi_f=partial_theta^2 f at theta zero. Its linear-H response then gives partial_theta^2 A_f(0,alpha), not A_f(0,alpha).

Even complete knowledge of the theta dependence of this response only determines A_f(theta,alpha) up to a theta-independent function B(alpha), after CP fixes the first derivative at zero. Periodicity does not determine B. The flat subtraction removes the H-independent vacuum constant; it does not remove H B(alpha). This is an information deficiency in the proposed inference, not a claim that a specified microscopic QCD theory may choose its answer arbitrarily or that H B is an ordinary local gravitational counterterm.

For the round-sphere stress prescription in METRIC_RESPONSE.md, apply the metric operator before taking the coefficient: A_rho=(3/4)A_f when the linear asymptotic exists. The same distinction persists. The electromagnetic insertion measuring chi involves a connected Q_top^2/current-current correlator; the vacuum coefficient involves a geometric vacuum/current-current insertion. They are different observables.

The leading small-quark-mass potential -m Sigma cos(theta) ties one theta-dependent contribution to a condensate. It does not prove that every geometric contribution, including its theta-independent part, has that form. Thus the calculated pion-loop response of chi or m Sigma is a real partial contribution, but replacing the full c_H by it requires an additional established geometry matching relation.

The controlled compact-Maxwell calculations give an explicit related check: nonzero infinite-area susceptibility coexists with zero linear-H metric coefficient in the chosen boundary-free two-dimensional model. Nonzero topology or susceptibility alone therefore does not force that finite-curvature law. This is a scoped counterexample to sufficiency, not a universal no-go for QCD.
