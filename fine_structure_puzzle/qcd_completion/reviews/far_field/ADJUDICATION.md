## Explicit common-PV calculation

Let `p_n=2pi n/L+h`, `m_n=|p_n|`, and `m_M,n=sqrt(p_n^2+M^2)`. Use exactly the common subtraction generated by multiplying the complete vector-minus-ghost proper-time kernel by `1-exp(-s M^2)`. This is equivalent, in the derivative sector, to subtracting the same charged vector and ghost operators with `+M^2` in each operator.

For the regulator determinant alone, the IBP orbital magnetic coefficient is `1/(24pi m_M)`, but the orbital electric coefficient is `p_n^2/(24pi m_M^3)`. The reason is elementary: the mass-potential gradient is `partial_i m_M=(p_n/m_M)partial_i h`. The spin coefficient is `-1/(2pi m_M)` for either field. Hence

`K_B(M)=-11/(24pi) sum_n [1/|p_n|-1/m_M,n]`,

`K_E,IBP(M)=K_B(M)+1/(24pi) sum_n M^2/(p_n^2+M^2)^(3/2)`.

The second sum is convergent for each M. As ML tends to infinity it is a Riemann sum with mesh 2pi/(ML):

`sum_n M^2/(p_n^2+M^2)^(3/2) -> L/pi`,

because `int_-infinity^infinity du/(1+u^2)^(3/2)=2`. Thus the finite bulk result in this regulator and representative is

`K_E,IBP-K_B=L/(24pi^2)`.

It is independent of nu within the gapped chamber. The magnetic coefficient has the standard expression

`K_B(M)=-11L/(48pi^2)[2log(ML/(4pi))-psi(nu)-psi(1-nu)]+o(1)`.

An independent dimensional-regularization derivation agrees. With d=3-2epsilon spatial dimensions, let

`C_d=(4pi)^(-d/2) Gamma(2-d/2) |p_n|^(d-4)`.

The B and E coefficients of one charged pair are, respectively,

`[(d-1)/6-4] C_d`,

`[(d-1)(4-d)/6-4] C_d`.

Their difference is `(d-1)(3-d) C_d/6`. The circle sum of C_d has pole `L/(16pi^2 epsilon)`. The product has finite limit `L/(24pi^2)`. Taking d=3 separately before the divergent sum removes this product. This explains exactly which operation the harmonic-first result does differently.

This calculation is sufficient to reject an unqualified identification of the harmonic-first off-shell E coefficient with the common-PV IBP coefficient by a common gauge-coupling shift alone. It is not a claim that a frequency-first prescription cannot be defined or used.
