# Kernel and rank: independent raw derivation

This is a hand derivation by the kernel/rank subagent, begun before reading the angular operator or asymptotic calculation. It is independent of the angular derivation for the Hessian factorization and explicit collective-coordinate modes. It is not an independent invention of the Wronskian rank argument: the coordinator supplied that candidate after the factorization/source work, and this agent then audited it. The weighted-adjoint and cokernel bridge below was developed here, with the coordinator checking the weight identifications.

## 1. Cartesian factorization and modes

Use a positive invariant inner product on the real adjoint bundle, anti-self-adjoint covariant derivatives, \(D_4=\operatorname{ad}\Phi\), and a smooth charge-one solution of \(B_i=D_i\Phi\) on all of \(\mathbb R^3\). For a static fluctuation \(a=(a_i,a_4)\), define

\[
C(a)=D_i a_i+D_4a_4,\qquad
L_i(a)=\epsilon_{ijk}D_j a_k-D_i a_4+D_4a_i,
\qquad T a=(L_i(a),C(a)).
\]

Expanding covariant commutators, with \(F_{i4}=B_i\), gives

\[
T^*T=-D_\lambda D_\lambda\delta_{\mu\nu}
       -2\operatorname{ad}F_{\mu\nu}=H,
\qquad TT^*=-D_\lambda D_\lambda\,1_4.
\tag{K1}
\]

For example, the spatial block uses
\((\operatorname{curl}_D)^2_{ij}=D_jD_i-\delta_{ij}D_kD_k\) and
\([\operatorname{curl}_D,D_4]_{ij}=-\operatorname{ad}F_{ij}\).
The remaining \([D_j,D_i]\) supplies the other curvature term. The mixed block is \(-2\operatorname{ad}B_i\). The partner curvature cancels by the same Bogomolny identity.

On the smooth complete-space Friedrichs realization, the quadratic form is \(\|Ta\|^2\). Hence \(\ker_{L^2}H=\ker_{L^2}T\); this is an operator-domain statement, not an inference from the classical moduli count alone. There is no origin extension parameter because the Cartesian coefficients are smooth and the origin is included.

Explicit real zero modes are

\[
a^{(j)}_\mu=F_{j\mu}\quad(j=1,2,3),\qquad
a^{(\chi)}=(D_i\Phi/v,0).
\tag{K2}
\]

The first three follow from translations plus the compensating gauge transformation; the fourth is generated by the gauge parameter \(\Phi/v\). Direct substitution in \(C,L\), using the field equations and Bianchi identity, gives zero. They are smooth at the origin and \(O(r^{-2})\) at infinity, hence square-integrable. The translation modes form a real grand-spin-one representation; the phase is grand spin zero. Translation has a nonzero neutral \(r^{-2}\) physical tail, or \(r^{-1}\) reduced radial tail. Varying the asymptotic mass instead produces a nondecaying Higgs perturbation and is outside this domain.

The partner has no ordinary \(L^2\) kernel: integration with radial cutoffs gives \(D_i\psi=D_4\psi=0\), and a parallel section has constant norm and cannot lie in \(L^2(\mathbb R^3)\) unless zero. Exhaustion of (K2) still needs an index with the correct neutral threshold domain.

## 2. Explicit weighted bridge to a primary Fredholm index

Let \(x\) be smooth and positive in the interior and equal \(1/r\) near infinity. Split the adjoint bundle near infinity into the neutral line \(V_0=\mathbb R\Phi\) and its charged orthogonal complement \(V_1\). For the exact BPS solution the off-diagonal covariant derivatives of this split are exponentially decreasing, since \(D_i n=O(K/r)\), \(K=r/\sinh r\), in dimensionless units.

The honest hybrid domain at Sobolev order zero is

\[
X=\mathcal H^{0,1,0,1}
 \simeq H_b^1(V_0)\oplus xH_{\rm sc}^1(V_1),
\qquad Y=xL^2(V_0\oplus V_1),
\tag{K3}
\]

with the ordinary interior \(H^1\) domain. This is the hybrid-space definition in Kottke's general primary paper, Definition 2.3 and Theorem 2.4, not a literal use of the abbreviated derivative norm in the monopole paper. Here \(H_b^1\) controls \(r\partial_r\) and angular derivatives in the physical \(L^2\) measure, while \(H_{\rm sc}^1\) controls ordinary derivatives. The general theorem explicitly permits order \(k=0\).

Kottke's monopole Theorem 4.7, specialized to \(\partial X=S^2\), \(k=1\), \(\gamma=0\), gives Fredholm index four for the weighted Hodge operator. The allowed framed interval is \(-1/2<\gamma<1/2\), since the first positive scalar spherical eigenvalue is two.

There is a necessary target identification before comparing that operator with \(T\). Write \(D_1\) for the infinitesimal gauge action, \(D_2=L\), and \(D_1^*\) for its formal unweighted adjoint (its sign relative to \(C\) is immaterial to the index or factorization). At \(\gamma=0\), the three Hilbert-space weight matrices are

\[
W_0=\operatorname{diag}(x^2,x^{-2}),\quad
W_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,x^{-2}),\quad
W_2=x^{-2}1.
\]

These correspond to the gauge input \(L^{-1,1}\), middle space \(L^{0,1}\), and deformation output \(L^{1,1}\). The genuine Hilbert adjoint is

\[
D_1^\dagger=W_0^{-1}D_1^*W_1.
\]

Its gauge output lies in \(L^{-1,1}\). The isometry
\(R=\operatorname{diag}(x^2,1):L^{-1,1}\to L^{1,1}\)
converts that output to the common target \(Y\). Consequently the index-four Hodge operator, after this target isometry, has gauge row

\[
RD_1^\dagger=\rho D_1^*\rho^{-1},\qquad
\rho=\operatorname{diag}(1,x^2).
\tag{K4}
\]

Thus its difference from the ordinary Coulomb row is exactly
\(\rho[D_1^*,\rho^{-1}]\). The prefactor \(\rho\) is essential. The displayed expansion in equation (2.15) of the monopole source suppresses this prefactor, and the common-target rescaling must also be made explicit; the printed commutator expression alone is not a valid reason to assert compactness.

For the charged diagonal row the difference in (K4) is multiplication by \(O(x)\); the neutral diagonal row is unchanged. Off-diagonal terms are exponentially small times fixed powers of \(r\). These maps are compact \(X\to Y\): conjugating the charged source and target by \(x^{-1}\) reduces the diagonal assertion to multiplication by a function tending to zero from \(H^1\) to \(L^2\), and local Rellich compactness handles bounded sets. The neutral off-diagonal terms have extra decay sufficient to compensate its \(b\)-derivatives and weight. Therefore the actual Coulomb/Bogomolny operator \(T:X\to Y\) is Fredholm with index four.

Its cokernel vanishes without assuming an unweighted Fredholm property. If \(\psi\in Y\) annihilates its range, let \(\chi=x^{-2}\psi\). The weighted pairing implies \(T^*\chi=0\) distributionally, and \(x\chi\in L^2\). Interior ellipticity gives a smooth \(\chi\). By (K1), \(-D_\lambda D_\lambda\chi=0\). If \(\eta_R\) is one inside radius \(R\), zero outside \(2R\), and \(|d\eta_R|\le c/R\), then

\[
\int\eta_R^2\bigl(|D_i\chi|^2+|D_4\chi|^2\bigr)
\le 4\int |d\eta_R|^2|\chi|^2
\le c'\int_{R<r<2R}|\chi/r|^2\longrightarrow0.
\tag{K5}
\]

A parallel \(\chi\) has constant norm, while \(\chi/r\in L^2\) on \(\mathbb R^3\) forces that constant to vanish. Hence \(\operatorname{coker}T=0\), and \(\dim_{\mathbb R}\ker_XT=4\).

Finally the kernels on \(X\) and physical \(L^2\) agree. Inclusion \(X\subset L^2\) is immediate. For the converse, split an \(L^2\) solution of \(Ta=0\) near infinity. The charged operator has a positive mass bounded away from zero and curvature \(O(r^{-2})\). Apply its exterior coercive estimate to \(r\eta_Ra_1\): the commutator with \(r\eta_R\) is bounded by \(c|a_1|\), and coupling to \(a_0\) is exponential. Uniform bounds followed by \(R\to\infty\) give \(ra_1\in H^1\). The neutral diagonal is the free flat Dirac-type operator \(T_0\). The weighted derivative estimate

\[
\|r\nabla a_0\|_{L^2}\le
c\bigl(\|a_0\|_{L^2}+\|rT_0a_0\|_{L^2}
             +\|a_0\|_{H^1(\text{fixed annulus})}\bigr)
\]

follows by applying the ordinary Dirac estimate to Cartesian coordinate multiples of cutoff \(a_0\); their commutators are bounded. Its right side is finite because \(T_0a_0\) is exponentially decreasing coupling to \(a_1\). This gives \(a_0\in H_b^1\). All origin regularity follows from interior ellipticity. This proves the converse inclusion and exhausts (K2).

The word "framed" is important: quotienting only transformations approaching the identity keeps the global phase. Quotienting the asymptotic stabilizer \(U(1)\) removes it and yields the unframed dimension three. A framed Dirichlet ghost domain does not remove the phase; its parameter \(\Phi/v\) does not belong to that ghost domain. Either convention leaves precisely the translation triplet in the \(J=1\) Hessian kernel.

## 3. Audited Wronskian rank argument

This section audits the coordinator's candidate, using the angular agent's ghost construction. It assumes the exact outer mode decomposition for the ten-channel radial operator: six charged growing/decaying pairs, one neutral \(C=0\) pair \(r,1\), two neutral \(C=2\) pairs \(r^2,r^{-1}\), and one neutral \(C=6\) pair \(r^3,r^{-2}\). The reduced norm is \(\int|u|^2dr\). The decomposition is a statement about exact solutions with these asymptotics; merely setting \(K=0\) in the differential expression is insufficient.

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the twenty-dimensional zero-energy solution space with nondegenerate Wronskian form, \(\mathcal L\) its ten-dimensional smooth-origin subspace, and \(\mathcal S\) the ten-dimensional subspace with no growing coefficient (six charged decays, the \(C=0\) constant, and the three other neutral decays). Both \(\mathcal L\) and \(\mathcal S\) are Lagrangian. The first claim follows by evaluating Wronskians at the smooth origin; the second follows by evaluating at infinity on the listed slow modes. For the two five-channel invariant blocks the corresponding dimensions are five and five.

The only obstruction to identifying \(\mathcal L\cap\mathcal S\) with the \(L^2\) kernel is the neutral \(C=0\) constant. It is excluded by a regular gauge gradient \(g_0=P\varphi_N\), with

\[
\varphi_N=n_a(n\cdot p)[r+A/r^2]
           +O(\operatorname{poly}(r)e^{-r}),
\]
\[
(g_0)_i=n_a\{p_i+A r^{-3}[p_i-3n_i(n\cdot p)]\}
           +O(\operatorname{poly}(r)e^{-r}),\qquad
(g_0)_4=O(\operatorname{poly}(r)e^{-r}).
\tag{K6}
\]

Here \(P\varphi=(D_i\varphi,[\Phi,\varphi])\). Existence does not require the unknown coefficient \(A\): the regular two-channel ghost space has dimension two, one charged growing coefficient and one neutral growing coefficient. If its charged-growing row vanished identically, a nonzero regular combination could cancel the neutral growing coefficient and become an \(L^2\) ghost zero mode, contradicting positivity of \(M=P^*P\). Therefore that row has rank one. Its one-dimensional kernel must have nonzero neutral growing coefficient, for otherwise it too would be an \(L^2\) ghost zero mode. Normalize it to one. The ghost neutral outer equation has reduced solutions \(r^2,r^{-1}\), giving (K6). Charged decay and all differentiated remainders follow from the outer equations. This argument uses the exact two-channel decomposition but not a fit or guessed scattering coefficient.

The reduced field \(g_0\) has a nonzero \(C=0\) growing \(r\) term, a possible \(C=6\) decreasing \(r^{-2}\) term, and no other neutral growing component. Since \(g_0\in\mathcal L\), the equality \(W(g_0,s)=0\) for \(s\in\mathcal L\cap\mathcal S\) forces the coefficient of its \(C=0\) constant to vanish. The other pairings vanish at infinity. Hence

\[
\mathcal L\cap\mathcal S=\ker_{L^2}H_{J=1}
                         =\mathbb R\,t
\tag{K7}
\]

per real magnetic component, where \(t\) is the translation. The full growing-coefficient map \(A:\mathcal L\to\mathcal V/\mathcal S\) therefore has rank nine.

Its unique linear relation is the functional \([v]\mapsto W(t,v)\). It is well-defined since \(t\in\mathcal S\), and it annihilates \(\mathcal L\) since \(t\in\mathcal L\). The nonzero neutral \(C=2\) decreasing tail of the translation makes the coefficient of at least one neutral \(C=2\) growing row nonzero in this functional. Consequently the relation cannot be supported on the six charged growing rows alone. Those rows have rank six. On their common kernel the remaining neutral growing map has rank three: its domain has dimension four and its kernel is the one-dimensional translation.

More specifically, the five-channel block without translation has growing rank five, charged rank three, and neutral residual rank two. The translation block has growing rank four, charged rank three, and neutral residual rank one. In the latter, after charged growth is canceled, the neutral growing direction is orthogonal to the translation's neutral decreasing tail, exactly the selection used in the angular asymptotic calculation.

These ranks close the connection-rank conditions. They do not by themselves prove a \(1/r\) determinant coefficient. That still requires the exact outer basis and control of its charged-induced neutral tails, along with the stated mixed wall normalization. In particular, exponentially small off-diagonal coefficients multiplied by charged growing solutions need not produce exponentially small neutral components. The angular lane must justify the required remainder order rather than infer it from small coefficients alone.

## Verdict and scope

The exact whole-space \(L^2\) kernel statement is proved by the primary weighted index plus the explicit internal bridges above. For charge one it is four real dimensions before quotienting the asymptotic phase, and the \(J=1\) kernel consists exactly of translations, one radial channel per magnetic component. The supplied rank implication is proved under the exact outer decompositions stated in section 3; it does not assume that the non-\(L^2\) neutral constant is an eigenvector in the physical kernel.

No finite-wall eigenvalue has been removed, no numerical fit was used, and no physical QCD stress coefficient or fine-structure prediction follows here.
