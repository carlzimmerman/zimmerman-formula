## Normalization fixes the relevant derivative

For a finite positive regulated measure, let Z(j,theta)=integral exp[-S0-j S+i theta Q]. Here S is a real integrated scalar insertion and Q a real integrated pseudoscalar, with <Q>=0 at theta=0. Direct differentiation gives

    partial_j log Z=-<S>,
    partial_j^2 log Z=Var(S),
    -partial_theta^2 log Z=Var(Q).

Thus the response generated by log Z contains the connected scalar fluctuation. A raw <S^2> contains the additional <S>^2 term. Source-dependent definitions add explicit derivative contacts; the same distinction remains.     Var(S) >= Var(Q)-<S>^2,

and does NOT alone prove Var(S)>=Var(Q). A pointwise two-density version likewise loses the disconnected scalar contribution upon subtraction.
