## Claim card and scope

Pure QCD has strictly positive equal up/down masses, m_u=m_d>0. Let S be the
constant vector flavor rotation exchanging u and d, and let Q be any diagonal
rational charge matrix, with Q'=SQS^{-1}. All other flavor data are held fixed
or transformed covariantly. The photon coupling is written sqrt(u) A_mu J_Q^mu,
so u=e^2. We compare an unconditioned vacuum functional and, separately, a
proton-conditioned, consistently Born-subtracted two-current amplitude.
No full nonperturbative value of the vacuum or hadron response is assumed.

## Independent exact comparison

The QCD action, fermion measure and metric coupling are invariant under the
nonanomalous constant vector transformation psi -> S psi. The source insertion
obeys J_Q[S psi]=J_{S^{-1}QS}[psi]. For an involutive flavor exchange this is Q'.
Changing fermion integration variables therefore gives Z[g,A;Q]=Z[g,A;Q'] at
all backgrounds and regulator parameters for a flavor-covariant regulator.
Consequently W[g,u;Q]=W[g,u;Q'] after integrating the entire photon field with
the same gauge fixing, measure, boundary conditions and photon regulator.
Differentiation in u and g preserves the equality, including seagulls,
coincident metric-current terms, normalization terms and all matching contacts.
One must compare the same complete functional, not one selected graph. Matching
and local counterterms preserve this identity when the renormalization scheme
and matched coefficients are transformed with Q. It is not a theorem for two
models with independently reassigned finite coefficients or flavor-breaking
mass inputs.

For nucleon conditioning the same change of variables also rotates the external
state/interpolator: S exchanges p and n. Thus T_p(Q')=T_n(Q), and T_n(Q')=T_p(Q),
with spin/isospin conventions rotated consistently. Their elastic poles,
residues, charge/contact pieces and form factors transform the same way. If Born
subtraction is defined covariantly, T_p^NB(Q')=T_n^NB(Q), not generally T_p^NB(Q).
For physical up/down charges, the Q' proton has the physical neutron charge and
vice versa. Hence the p-minus-n NB tensor is odd under Q<->Q', while their sum
is even. Equality of unconditioned vacuum functionals does not imply equality
of the same externally selected proton tensor.

The electromagnetic Ward relation constrains longitudinal terms together with
its required equal-time/contact contributions. Diffeomorphism Ward relations
likewise include variations of currents, external conditions and measures.
Neither fixes the remaining transverse NB scalar functions or sets p-minus-n
polarizabilities to zero. A complete photon integration/matching map applied to
the two rotated state sectors is covariant, but cannot silently replace the
neutron sector by the proton sector. An isolated transverse example need not
alone establish a value of a full metric response; all same-order terms and
contacts must be included before making that stronger claim.

## Soft-photon contraction from the effective action (pre-source derivation)

Use conventional rest-frame polarizabilities with Delta H=-2*pi*(alpha E_M^2+
beta B_M^2), equivalently Delta L_M=+2*pi*(alpha E_M^2+beta B_M^2) for a static
normalized heavy particle. Wick rotation t=-i*tau and A_0^M=i*A_4^E gives
E_M=i E_E, B_M=B_E, and L_E=-L_M|Wick. Therefore
Delta L_E=+2*pi*alpha E_E^2-2*pi*beta B_E^2. The relative minus sign is essential:
Euclidean electric and magnetic squares cannot both be assigned the Minkowski
Hamiltonian sign.

For an SO(4)-invariant transverse photon contraction at a single point, the six
independent components F_ab^2 have equal expectation. Sum_i F_4i^2=E_E^2 has
three components; sum_{i<j}F_ij^2=B_E^2 also has three. Therefore an invariant
contraction of the static polarizability action is proportional to alpha-beta,
with the common normalization determined by propagator/action conventions.
In Feynman gauge <A_a A_b>=delta_ab D(k), E_E^2 contracts to
(3 k_4^2+|k|^2)D(k), B_E^2 to 2|k|^2 D(k); SO(4) averaging gives 3 k^2 D(k)/2
for each. Thus the action coefficient before the radial integral is
3*pi*(alpha-beta)*integral d^4k/(2*pi)^4 k^2 D(k).
This is a local soft expansion for a massive target after complete elastic/Born
subtraction; it is not itself a complete all-momentum Cottingham formula.
For D(k)=1/(k^2+lambda^2), the contracted massless-to-massive difference is
k^2/(k^2+lambda^2)-1=-lambda^2/(k^2+lambda^2). Analytic pieces depend on matching;
nonanalytic lambda terms can be isolated only after the tensor is specified.
The soft expansion requires k,lambda << m_pi at fixed m_pi>0. Taking m_pi to zero
first destroys that uniform expansion. A chiral logarithm in a soft coefficient
need not authorize exchange of these limits.
