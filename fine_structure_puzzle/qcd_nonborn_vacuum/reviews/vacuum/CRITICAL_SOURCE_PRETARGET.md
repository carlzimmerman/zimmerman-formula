## First-principles meaning of scalar zero-momentum response

At a positive finite regulator with a linear real scalar source lambda, -W_lambdalambda/V = Var(S_lambda)/V. More generally it is [Var(S_lambda)-E(S_lambdalambda)]/V. A finite scalar low-energy identity proportional to a one-point condensate cannot literally equal an infinite-volume unconnected scalar integral when the scalar VEV is nonzero: the unconnected integral contains V times the squared condensate. ## The exact algebraic implication and its limitation

The Euclidean Hodge decomposition gives s(x)>=|q(x)| (up to the same positive normalization) configuration by configuration. It implies S>=|Q| for integrated fields, hence E(S^2)>=E(Q^2) for a positive measure. It does not imply Var(S)>=Var(Q). At theta=0 with parity E(Q)=0, centering gives only

Var(S) >= Var(Q) - E(S)^2.

Dividing by V yields a negative extensive subtraction, not the desired useful bound. An explicit two-state parity-invariant positive law with equal weights has S=1 in both states, Q=+1 or -1. It satisfies S>=|Q| and saturates the uncentered square bound, but Var(S)=0<1=Var(Q). At a single point this is realized algebraically by equal-norm selfdual and anti-selfdual two-forms. This counterexample invalidates the inference from pointwise Hodge positivity plus measure positivity alone; it is NOT a counterexample to QCD or to the possibility of an additional QCD-specific theorem.
