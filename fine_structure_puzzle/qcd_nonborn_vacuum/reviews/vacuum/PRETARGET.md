## Exact finite-regulator claim card

Let the normalized probability measure be proportional to exp(-S(t,u)); any measure/Jacobian dependence is absorbed into S in a common integration parametrization. Set W=-log Z, V=Omega exp(4t), H=exp(-t), and rho=W_t/(4V). All t derivatives hold the renormalized physical parameters, including u, fixed; u derivatives follow a specified path holding the other renormalized physical parameters fixed. Assume differentiation under the regulated integral, finite moments, and a positive measure for covariance bounds. Without positivity the derivative identities can hold but Cauchy--Schwarz for a probability law is unavailable.

Write X=S_t, Y=S_u, X_t=S_tt, X_u=S_tu. Then

W_t=E[X], W_tt=E[S_tt]-Var(X),

partial_t rho=(W_tt-4W_t)/(4V).

The contact-complete response that extracts a possible coefficient A in rho(H,u)=rho_0(u)+A(u)H+remainder is

M(t,u)=-exp(t) partial_t rho=exp(t)/(4V) [4W_t-W_tt]
       =exp(t)/(4V) { E[4S_t-S_tt]+Var(S_t) }.

The explicit S_tt term is the seagull/contact generated by the nonlinear Weyl path. The 4S_t term is generated by the volume normalization. Neither can be discarded merely because a separated-point scalar correlator is positive. A field-independent cosmological counterterm W=V C(u) cancels exactly in M. For rho=AH, integrating W_t=4V AH gives W=(4/3)Omega A exp(3t)+t-independent term, and M=A identically. This checks the factor 4 and sign.

This is a global-Weyl response, not automatically an integrated local trace two-point function without contact terms. Passing to the renormalized continuum theory requires the same action/measure/counterterm prescription for every derivative. Positivity of a regulator covariance does not make a subtracted renormalized susceptibility positive.

## u derivative and covariance bound

For any insertion F, partial_u E[F]=E[F_u]-Cov(F,Y). Define K=4S_t-S_tt, Z=S_tu, Q=K+(X-E[X])^2. Direct differentiation gives

partial_u M=exp(t)/(4V) { E[4S_tu-S_ttu]+2Cov(S_t,S_tu)-Cov(Q,S_u) }

=exp(t)/(4V) {4E[S_tu]-E[S_ttu]-4Cov(S_t,S_u)
              +Cov(S_tt,S_u)+2Cov(S_t,S_tu)-kappa_3(S_t,S_t,S_u)}.

The centered Q formula includes the derivative of the normalization; it is not obtained by differentiating an unnormalized integral. Cauchy--Schwarz yields the finite-regulator bound

|partial_u M| <= exp(t)/(4V) [ |E[4S_tu-S_ttu]|
                    +2 sqrt(Var(S_t) Var(S_tu))
                    +sqrt(Var(Q) Var(S_u)) ].

Every explicit mixed derivative/contact must be supplied by the same fixed-physics path; omitting it is an extra assumption. Even a valid bound at every finite t gives no finite bound for A_u absent suitable large-t control.

## Limits and geometry

M -> A requires the remainder to satisfy exp(t) partial_t remainder ->0. The bare asymptotic remainder=o(H) alone is insufficient, as oscillatory differentiable remainders can have much larger derivatives. Passing partial_u through the H->0 limit additionally needs local uniformity of the response derivative (or another valid interchange theorem), and existence of the corresponding renormalized limits. To claim |A_u| bounded, the displayed right side needs a uniform finite limit/limsup and the derivative interchange.

A flat finite bag and a smooth compact S4 have different boundaries, spectra and response operators. A flat bag Casimir term proportional to 1/L does not establish an S4 rho proportional to H. A source at finite real acceleration and finite temperature likewise needs an independently proved map to this compact Euclidean fixed-physics H path. Local curved counterterms and their contacts must be accounted for before comparing susceptibilities.
