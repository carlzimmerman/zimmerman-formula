# MV28: a microscopic stiffness is calculable; the simple halo portal fails

The explicit four-superfield model and its matching assumptions are in
MODEL.md. Its known metastable radial vacuum was reproduced in all nine
predeclared r,y cases. The actual spectrum determines a stiffness ratio
as a function of couplings, rather than leaving a freely specified B in
the scalar potential:

    B_curv/V_model=lambda^2 x0^2 c''(x0)
                       /[9(1+lambda^2 c(x0))].

Here V_model is the specified sector's leading flat one-loop result in the
weak-gravity approximation. It is not a calculation of the observed full
cosmological vacuum, an exact supergravity loop result, or a universal
constant. The assumed absence of W0 and unknown Kahler operators remain
substantive matching restrictions. The relative-motion portal is also
assumed, not generated by this superpotential.

All candidate minima have strictly positive transverse tree spectra.
Changing lambda at fixed r,y changes the ratio approximately as lambda^2;
the 27 predeclared coupling cases therefore do not select a number.
There is no fit to the old acceleration target.

In eight cases a pressure-sign boundary occurs before 0.98 times the exact
transverse bound. At that boundary the scalar energy curvature remains
positive: W_ss=5U_s>0. This is a compressional instability of the proposed
matter closure, not a tachyon already present in the vacuum spectrum.
In the ninth case no pressure-zero was found before that cutoff; the
transverse branch ends at its stated tree bound. Neither outcome provides
an unrestricted dense logarithmic core. No nonlinear halo formation or
metastable lifetime was calculated.

## Verification and scope

The run completed with 16 named algebraic/numerical checks. The published
r=4,y=0.2 benchmark near x=1.3 was reproduced at 1.327263. Spectral Hessians
agree with 65-digit eigenvalue finite differences to at most 1.52e-11
relatively. The pressure derivative cross-check differs by at most 3.06e-8
in the specified scaling. Four small-y origin-mass controls agree with the
source expansion within 0.036%. The full six-scalar matrix and scale-change
checks pass. These are finite, floating-point diagnostics; the scalar
potential and trace identities are separately derived in MODEL.md.

One author performed the derivation and an adversarial self-check of scalar
counts, field normalization, the total scalar pressure, and gravity's
Kahler/W0 dependence. There was no independent-agent review. The invariant
criterion is U_ss>3U_s on a radially stable positive-source branch; a
loop-generated shallow potential need not satisfy it as particles compress.

The constructive result is a calculable radiative stiffness and a specific
failure of the simplest portal. A continuation should produce a steeper
potential microscopically while preserving a calculable positive energy,
and derive the portal rather than adjusting it to repair this table.
