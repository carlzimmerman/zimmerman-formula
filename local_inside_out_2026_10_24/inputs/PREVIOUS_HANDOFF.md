## Next physical obligation, not another generic scan

All these constructions use one global phase for the entire cold reservoir.
An actual formation mechanism must specify spatially local work transfer and
conversion, including the matter that remains outside the forming disk. A
plausible continuation is inside-out conversion during baryonic angular
momentum transport, starting before the free reservoir is tightly bound.
This is untested, not a successful mechanism. In spherical geometry external
free shells do not contribute to the inner force, but that observation alone
does not supply the required density profile or local conversion dynamics.

Write the local action or transport energy BEFORE a simulation. Include
the parent's spatial phase stiffness rather than copying the global-phase
result into local cells. Derive the moving-interface work, angular momentum
flux, receiver and backreaction. The 30-kpc stiffness previously used for
protection may itself block a conversion front. Test that cost first. Do not
assign phase by galaxy type, impose the RAR density, or discard outer free
mass from the source when it enters the tested radius.

Missing microscopic interaction, inertia, viscosity and noise normalization
remain named inputs. More adjustable coefficients would establish only a
phenomenological feasibility model. A new local interaction is a substantive
construction, not an earned consequence of the existing data.
