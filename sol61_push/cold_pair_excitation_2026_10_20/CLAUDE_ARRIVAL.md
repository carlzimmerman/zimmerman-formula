## What “supply explains” means here

The aperture prediction is

```
f = min[1, M_supply / ((nu-1) M_b)],
g_pred = g_N [1+f(nu-1)].
```

For the tidal dwarfs the primary f=0 comes from S4/no-capture plus the
assumed host-tethered catchment. It is not calculated by a microscopic
scattering or transport experiment. For DF2/DF4, inherited CFG657 capture
at alpha_P=0 gives f=0.0005888/0.0016161 canonical and
0.0004133/0.0010816 alternate. The data are statistically compatible with
those nearly Newtonian predictions: TDGs have mean Newtonian residual
0.05463 +/- 0.11439 dex; DF2/DF4 have about +0.021 +/- 0.539 ln.

The operational rule uses formation labels and was developed with these
objects in view. The README explicitly says that it is label-driven and
not an out-of-sample confirmation. EXPLAINS is the declared statistical
class, not proof of a physical zero capture cross-section. Generous
TDG-reservoir corners change the class, and two inherited DF capture
fallback corners restore enough supply to change it; FRAGILE matters.
“Only zero supply works” should be read as a comparison within these
tested route/parameter choices, not a universal no-go for all physical
partial-capture histories.

The clock `f(t)=1-exp(-t/tau)` at tau=3.47 Gyr fills the old DF objects to
about 0.908 and 0.954 and fails for them on both footings. Young TDGs fit
only near the slow/young end. The EFE-free TDG lower clock limits are
2.04174/2.29087 Gyr using their -1-sigma ages, leaving the declared window
open; nominal-age limits 3.80189/4.26580 exceed 3.47. These are conditional
clock fits, not measured settling times of the new scalar fields.

## Implication for microscopic pair work and the four dwarfs

A successful cold-fragment pair interaction would establish a channel for
objects already made of those fields. It would not automatically imply
that a baryonic debris potential captures ambient cold matter efficiently.
Conversely, the S4 debris label supplies no dynamical rule for deciding
whether our fields create or capture a fragment. The model must distinguish
seed availability, incoming phase-space density, relative velocity, phase,
impact parameter and available dissipation through actual dynamics.

The present CFG936 evidence supplies no conserved E/Q/J transfer rate,
outer receiver, energy source, collision amplitude, scalar mass/coupling
normalization or four-dwarf mass inventory. It therefore adds a useful
population-level constraint: rapid concentration in the target dwarfs must
not be silently generalized into universal filling of old debris systems.
It does not establish the needed selectivity between Draco/Sextans and
Fornax/Leo I, which are not its test sample.
