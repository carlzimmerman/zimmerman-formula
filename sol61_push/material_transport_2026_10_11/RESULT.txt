1. The field correction need not imply negative cold mass

In the engine's source units let c_s=1.5 Om(1-FB)/a and let the base cold
source be c_s(1+delta_p)>=0. Write its proposed material replacement as

 c_eff=c_s(1+delta_p)+e_capped-q c_s(1+delta_p) I_shell.

The positive addition e_capped is nonnegative, the shell flag is 0 or 1,
and the applied catchment factor obeys 0<=q<=1. Thus c_eff>=0 pointwise
in exact arithmetic, assuming the deposited base density is nonnegative.
The negative COMPENSATION is a removal from existing mass, not automatically
a negative physical mass population. This distinction prevents a false no-go.

For a catchment with available source C>0 and requested source E, the ideal
cap adds min(E,C) and removes the same amount. The code's max(C,1e-30)
floor and float32 arithmetic mean exact zero-sum balance is not a universal

was separately reported. This proof is not a new cellwise numerical audit.

Therefore an instantaneous nonnegative, approximately mass-balanced density
map is plausible from the implemented algebra. Positivity does not establish
that particles can realize it causally with the required velocities and energy.

2. The least kinetic current for a specified density HISTORY

In physical coordinates on a fixed domain, suppose rho(x,t)>0 and dot rho
are prescribed, with no normal flux at the boundary and int dot rho=0.
Any material current j must satisfy dot rho+div j=0. Its bulk kinetic energy
is K_bulk=1/2 int |j|^2/rho. Random velocity dispersion can add energy but
cannot reduce this bulk lower bound.

Minimize K_bulk over currents subject to continuity. Varying
1/2 int |j|^2/rho + int lambda(div j+dot rho) yields

 j_min=rho grad lambda,
 -div(rho grad lambda)=dot rho,
 K_min=1/2 int rho |grad lambda|^2.                    (1)

This is an instantaneous conditional kinematic construction. For any other
admissible current j=j_min+w, div w=0 with zero boundary flux. The cross term
int grad lambda dot w vanishes, so

 K_bulk[j]=K_min+1/2 int |w|^2/rho >=K_min.            (2)

Equivalently every smooth test function psi supplies the lower bound

 K_bulk >= int psi dot rho - 1/2 int rho |grad psi|^2.

These formulas give a way to test the transport cost of a proposed field
history without positing a dissipative mobility. They require the HISTORY,
not merely the final compensated density. Different interpolation times give
different currents/costs; this does not remove the missing physical clock.

If rho vanishes, the elliptic problem can be degenerate. Disconnected supports
may impose separate mass-compatibility conditions and infinite-cost barriers.
No general existence theorem for the capped simulation density is asserted.
At moving catchment boundaries, the missing boundary flux must be retained.
Cosmological use also requires scale-factor conventions: for comoving mass
density mu=a^3 rho and comoving flux j_x, the peculiar bulk energy is
a^2/2 int |j_x|^2/mu. A snapshot difference is not itself a physical velocity.

3. What a momentum closure still owes

Continuity plus (1) does not derive the force or pressure that produces j_min.
For u=j/rho, a Newtonian kinetic completion must also satisfy

 rho[partial_t u+(u dot grad)u]
       =-rho grad Phi-div Pi+f_exchange,

where Pi is the velocity-dispersion stress and f_exchange has a receiver
reaction and energy balance. Many choices give the same density/current;
the minimum-current prescription does not select one, conserve each particle's
angular momentum in arbitrary geometry, or prove stability of the final halo.
As dot rho tends to zero, (1) gives zero bulk current, NOT zero velocity
dispersion. Confusing those would repeat the earlier unsupported-halo failure.

Suppose a cold-density-only potential energy U[rho] yields the force potential
Phi[rho]=delta U/delta rho. In a finite cell model its Jacobian must be
symmetric on allowed fixed-mass variations: h^T D Phi k=k^T D Phi h.
This is the equality of mixed second variations, a necessary condition for
a twice differentiable energy functional on the smooth branch considered.

Let T(rho) implement the unsaturated deficit fill plus proportional shell draw:
the target fixes each core cell's output mass, and all removed shell mass is
distributed in proportion to its existing shell masses. The force prescription
under test is Phi=K T(rho), with symmetric Newtonian kernel K. Symmetry of K
does not ensure symmetry of K DT.

Exact four-cell counterexample (units G=1): place cell centres at x=0,1,3,4.
Cells 1,2 form the core and 3,4 the shell; their radii from x=.5 are .5,.5,
2.5,3.5. Take input masses (1,1,2,2) and target core masses (1.5,1.5).
The deficit is 1, shell supply 4, so the cap is inactive and shell retention
is b=3/4. In a neighborhood that keeps the same branch,

 T_core=(1.5,1.5),
 T_shell=rho_shell [1-(3-rho_1-rho_2)/(rho_3+rho_4)].

Choose the fixed-total-mass perturbations
 h=(1,-1,0,0), k=(0,0,1,-1).
Both keep every density positive for sufficiently small amplitudes, and
 DT h=0, DT k=(3/4)k.

For the cross-cell Newtonian entries K_ij=-1/|x_i-x_j|,
 h^T K k=-1/3+1/4+1/2-1/3=1/12.
No diagonal self-energy entry is used in this calculation. Hence

 h^T D Phi k=1/16,   k^T D Phi h=0.

The fixed-target redistribution plus the unmodified Newtonian force of the
redistributed density is therefore NOT the derivative of a density-only
energy in this example. A sufficiently small loop in these two density
directions has nonzero work to leading order, with magnitude coefficient
1/16 times its oriented area. This is a local branch result, not a statement
about global catchment switching or a measured simulation energy drift.

The example is nonspherical within a round catchment. Restricting every
perturbation to exactly spherical shells can remove this particular test by
the shell theorem. The example must not be used to claim all spherical
implementations fail. A three-dimensional completion needs its own admissible
perturbation space specified and audited.

5a. Why 'use the gravitational energy of T(rho)' changes the force

One can define W_T=1/2 T(rho)^T K T(rho), but its derivative is

 grad_rho W_T=(DT)^T K T(rho),

not K T(rho). Fixed external baryon terms acquire the same chain-rule factor
when written as functions of T. Omitting that factor is not a variational
derivation. Including it changes the prescribed response and requires checking
the acceleration law again. In this example W_T is invariant in direction h,
although the originally prescribed Phi need not give zero h-work.

5b. Scope and implication for the dynamical law

This closes a tempting shortcut: a mass-balanced positive redistribution does
not automatically supply a conservative cold-density action. It does NOT
rule out a theory with independent transported density, a receiver, additional
state variables, memory, or a genuinely dissipative law. Those are precisely
the missing dynamics; their equations and energy transfer must be supplied.
Nor does it prove that the actual complete CFG596 mapping is nonvariational:
its other density dependencies were deliberately held fixed in this test.

The section-5 obstruction is not repaired merely by naming an auxiliary field.
Suppose a smooth conservative energy U(rho,z) contains auxiliary coordinates
z. On a local single-valued equilibrium branch U_z(rho,z_*(rho))=0, assume
U_zz invertible. Eliminate z by defining U_eff(rho)=U(rho,z_*(rho)).

The chain rule and differentiated equilibrium condition give

 grad U_eff=U_rho,
 dz_*/d rho=-U_zz^(-1) U_zrho,
 Hess U_eff=U_rhorho-U_rhoz U_zz^(-1) U_zrho.

This matrix is symmetric because U is a scalar twice differentiable energy.
The conclusion also holds for an indefinite invertible U_zz, although that
does not establish physical stability. Thus neither a stable auxiliary
minimum nor an unstable but regular stationary elimination can reproduce the
section-5 asymmetric response while leaving its prescribed force unchanged.

This applies on the allowed fixed-mass tangent space. Density-independent
linear constraints can be eliminated before taking the same derivatives.
State-dependent constraints require their reaction forces; discarding those
is not a counterexample. Singular branches, hysteresis and unconverged or
driven auxiliary states are outside the smooth stationary-elimination argument.
They need explicit dynamics and cannot be described as an equilibrated
instantaneous conservative replacement.

Example of the tempting but incomplete construction: introducing z=T(rho)
as a constrained gravitating density while taking Kz as the force on rho
omits the constraint reaction. Proper variation yields the chain-rule terms
identified in section 5a. The target's appearance in a constraint is allowed;
discarding its variational consequences is not.

Consequence: for the fixed-target redistribution tested in section 5, the
completion must either change the effective force or retain genuinely
nonequilibrium/state-dependent dynamics rather than treating all added fields
as equilibrated algebraic auxiliaries. Finite memory or dissipation is not
automatically sufficient; either must be specified and shown to produce the
required response with its energy and momentum reactions.

This does not extend the four-cell result to the full CFG596 mapping or rule
out all actions. It sharpens the admissible completion of the tested branch.
No coefficient, receiver, reservoir profile, or observational fit is selected.
