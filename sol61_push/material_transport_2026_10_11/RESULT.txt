1. The field correction need not imply negative cold mass

In the engine's source units let c_s=1.5 Om(1-FB)/a and let the base cold
source be c_s(1+delta_p)>=0. Write its proposed material replacement as

 c_eff=c_s(1+delta_p)+e_capped-q c_s(1+delta_p) I_shell.

The positive addition e_capped is nonnegative, the shell flag is 0 or 1,
and the applied catchment factor obeys 0<=q<=1. Thus c_eff>=0 pointwise
in exact arithmetic, assuming the deposited base density is nonnegative.
The negative COMPENSATION is a removal from existing mass, not automatically
a negative physical mass population. This distinction prevents a false no-go.

For a catchment with available source C>0 and requested source E, the ideal
cap adds min(E,C) and removes the same amount. The code's max(C,1e-30)
floor and float32 arithmetic mean exact zero-sum balance is not a universal

was separately reported. This proof is not a new cellwise numerical audit.

Therefore an instantaneous nonnegative, approximately mass-balanced density
map is plausible from the implemented algebra. Positivity does not establish
that particles can realize it causally with the required velocities and energy.

2. The least kinetic current for a specified density HISTORY

In physical coordinates on a fixed domain, suppose rho(x,t)>0 and dot rho
are prescribed, with no normal flux at the boundary and int dot rho=0.
Any material current j must satisfy dot rho+div j=0. Its bulk kinetic energy
is K_bulk=1/2 int |j|^2/rho. Random velocity dispersion can add energy but
cannot reduce this bulk lower bound.

Minimize K_bulk over currents subject to continuity. Varying
1/2 int |j|^2/rho + int lambda(div j+dot rho) yields

 j_min=rho grad lambda,
 -div(rho grad lambda)=dot rho,
 K_min=1/2 int rho |grad lambda|^2.                    (1)

This is an instantaneous conditional kinematic construction. For any other
admissible current j=j_min+w, div w=0 with zero boundary flux. The cross term
int grad lambda dot w vanishes, so

 K_bulk[j]=K_min+1/2 int |w|^2/rho >=K_min.            (2)

Equivalently every smooth test function psi supplies the lower bound

 K_bulk >= int psi dot rho - 1/2 int rho |grad psi|^2.

These formulas give a way to test the transport cost of a proposed field
history without positing a dissipative mobility. They require the HISTORY,
not merely the final compensated density. Different interpolation times give
different currents/costs; this does not remove the missing physical clock.

If rho vanishes, the elliptic problem can be degenerate. Disconnected supports
may impose separate mass-compatibility conditions and infinite-cost barriers.
No general existence theorem for the capped simulation density is asserted.
At moving catchment boundaries, the missing boundary flux must be retained.
Cosmological use also requires scale-factor conventions: for comoving mass
density mu=a^3 rho and comoving flux j_x, the peculiar bulk energy is
a^2/2 int |j_x|^2/mu. A snapshot difference is not itself a physical velocity.

3. What a momentum closure still owes

Continuity plus (1) does not derive the force or pressure that produces j_min.
For u=j/rho, a Newtonian kinetic completion must also satisfy

 rho[partial_t u+(u dot grad)u]
       =-rho grad Phi-div Pi+f_exchange,

where Pi is the velocity-dispersion stress and f_exchange has a receiver
reaction and energy balance. Many choices give the same density/current;
the minimum-current prescription does not select one, conserve each particle's
angular momentum in arbitrary geometry, or prove stability of the final halo.
As dot rho tends to zero, (1) gives zero bulk current, NOT zero velocity
dispersion. Confusing those would repeat the earlier unsupported-halo failure.
