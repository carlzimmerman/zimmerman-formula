1. Claim card and assumptions

Action in a prescribed flat rest frame, in units c=hbar=1:
 L = K chi_dot^2/2 - 2 kappa |grad chi|^3/(3 mu^2)
     - (Delta chi)^2/(2 S^2) - alpha rho chi/M - lambda mu^4.
K,kappa,mu,S,alpha,M are positive. rho is a fixed, time-independent source.
There are two time derivatives and four spatial derivatives. No dynamical
metric, clock field, matter backreaction, or quantum UV completion is assumed.
The force dictionary is u=alpha chi'/M, G=1/(8 pi M^2).
The normalization a0=alpha^3 mu^2/(kappa M) follows only in the leading
large-radius scalar-force regime. It is not a derivation of the full MOND
interpolation or of the observed lensing potential.

Dependency chain:
declared action -> variation and boundary terms -> finite-source equations
-> source solutions and fluctuation operator -> quantum matching -> physical
gravity/acceleration dictionary -> absolute coefficient selection.
The first four links are addressed below under explicit restrictions. The
quantum matching test produces additional operators. The last two links are
not established. No earlier step is evidence that they automatically follow.

2. Variational derivation and finite-domain result

On a ball B_R, fix chi=0 at the boundary. The static energy is
 E[chi]=integral_B [2 kappa |grad chi|^3/(3 mu^2)
                    +(Delta chi)^2/(2 S^2)+alpha rho chi/M].
Its first variation gives
 div[(2 kappa/mu^2)|grad chi| grad chi]-Delta^2 chi/S^2=alpha rho/M.
Since variations vanish at the boundary but their normal derivatives are
free, the natural additional boundary condition is Delta chi=0 there.
This is different from clamping both chi and its normal derivative.

Analytic finite-domain argument, not Lean-formalized:
Assume rho belongs to L^2(B_R). Use the reflexive graph space
 V={chi in H_0^1(B_R): grad chi in L^3, Delta chi in L^2}.
The source term is bounded by a constant times ||grad chi||_3 using the
Poincare inequality and finite volume. The cubic and quadratic positive
terms therefore make E coercive in the graph norm. For a minimizing
sequence, weak subsequences of chi, grad chi, and Delta chi remain compatible
as distributional derivatives. Norm powers are weakly lower semicontinuous,
and the linear source term is weakly continuous. Thus a minimizer exists.
Strict convexity of |grad chi|^3 implies uniqueness: equality for two minimizers
would force identical gradients, and the zero boundary trace removes their
constant difference. Rotational symmetry of rho and the ball then makes the
unique minimizer radial. This proves a weak finite-ball result, not a global
isolated solution on all of space. Interpreting the natural boundary condition
pointwise requires sufficient regularity; the variational statement itself
does not assume that extra regularity.

At a static solution with b=grad chi, the quadratic energy for a perturbation
eta with zero boundary trace is
 delta^2 E = integral [ (2 kappa/mu^2)(|b||grad eta|^2
                         +(b dot grad eta)^2/|b|)
                        +(Delta eta)^2/S^2 ].
At b=0 the first bracket is its continuous zero limit. Every term is
nonnegative. The Laplacian term can vanish for a zero-trace perturbation only
when eta=0. With K>0 this gives positive linear-mode energy for this fixed-frame,
fixed-source finite-domain problem. It is not a proof of nonlinear global
well-posedness, gravitational stability, or stability of a dynamical rest frame.

3. Source computation, including the interior

Let m_s be the asymptotic mass of a smooth Plummer source, C^2=alpha m_s mu^2/
(8 pi kappa M), and ell=mu^2/(2 kappa S^2 C). Set x=r/ell and
chi'=(C/ell)h(x). The enclosed mass fraction is
 m(x)=x^3/(x^2+d^2)^(3/2), where d is the source radius in units ell.
The source is restricted to the finite computational ball; its enclosed mass
at the outer boundary is m(L), not exactly one. Integration of the source
equation, with a regular origin, gives
 x^2 h|h|-x^2[h''+2h'/x-2h/x^2]=m(x).

The final numerical variable is f=x h. This is order one in the exterior,
which prevents an absolute solver tolerance from underweighting the far field.
The equation and natural outer condition are
 f''=(f|f|-m(x))/x+2f/x^2,  f(L)+L f'(L)=0.
Regularity implies h=b x-x^3/(10d^3)+b^2 x^4/18+3x^5/(56d^5)+... .
The computation starts at x0=1e-4 d with this regular Taylor boundary condition;
one concentrated-source control reduces x0 by a factor ten. chi itself is
recovered by integration with chi(L)=0; the gradient equation fixes its force.

There are 16 passing Plummer-source solves:
- d=0.2, 1, 5 and L=50, 200, 800: nine base cases.
- Two mesh/tolerance refinements, two alternative outer boundaries, two
  initial-guess changes, and one origin-cutoff reduction.
Base solver tolerance is 3e-9 with 8001 uniform mesh points plus a logarithmic
mesh. Refinements use 1e-9 and 16001 uniform points. No 1e-10 success is claimed.
The alternative outer condition matches the formal asymptotic value
 f(L)=1-1/L-(1/2+3d^2/4)/L^2.

Independent flux residuals use derivatives of the returned interpolant,
not substitution of the defining ODE for its second derivative. On 5000
logarithmic test points plus the source radius, the maximum absolute flux
error across all cases is 2.58936e-8, normalized to asymptotic source mass one.
The largest sampled profile change under mesh/tolerance refinement is
2.675e-13; initial-guess changes give 1.342e-10; the origin-cutoff control gives
1.122e-14. These differences are diagnostics, not rigorous error bounds.

For d=1, L=200, force divided by its leading C/r value is:
 x=5:  0.7365650159
 x=10: 0.8819965606
 x=20: 0.9461599695
 x=30: 0.9650936231
 x=40: 0.9741475226
The natural L=50 boundary shifts the x=40 ratio to 0.9637147765: a visible
boundary effect. Increasing L from 200 to 800 leaves these sampled interior
ratios unchanged to about 8e-12 for d=1. Thus the large-radius trend survives
source matching and a distant boundary; the asymptotic force is not exact at
finite radius. This is a dimensionless model test, not a fit to galaxy data.

Earlier uniform-density runs passed a looser flux bound but higher-precision
attempts encountered convergence and scaling problems. The final quantitative

preflights have not been silently substituted for audited evidence.

4. Quantum matching test: explicit new terms

Consider a locally constant gradient v=|grad chi|>0, still in the fixed frame.
The Gaussian scalar frequencies are
 omega^2=[p^4/S^2 + a(c) v p^2]/K,
 a(c)=2 kappa(1+c^2)/mu^2, c=cos(angle to the background gradient).
Define a sharp spatial momentum shell k_-<p<k_+ within the assumed EFT range.
The one-loop zero-point energy density is
 V1(v)=1/2 integral_shell d^3p/(2 pi)^3 omega(p,v).
This is a stated regulator and quantum approximation, not a physical
renormalization prescription fixed by the model.

For S^2 a(c) v << k_-^2 uniformly in angle,
 V1(v)-V1(0)=A v+B v^2+O(v^3),
 A=kappa S(k_+^3-k_-^3)/(9 pi^2 sqrt(K) mu^2)>0,
 B=-7 kappa^2 S^3(k_+-k_-)/(30 pi^2 sqrt(K) mu^4)<0.
The source-free baseline itself is
 V1(0)=(k_+^5-k_-^5)/(20 pi^2 S sqrt(K)).

Derivation: with b=S^2 a(c)v, the radial integral has primitive
 I(p,b)=(p^2+b)^(5/2)/5-b(p^2+b)^(3/2)/3.
Multiply I(k_+,b)-I(k_-,b) by 1/(4 pi^2 S sqrt(K)) and average over c.
Its linear and quadratic b coefficients are (k_+^3-k_-^3)/6 and
-(k_+-k_-)/8. The angular averages are
 <a>=8 kappa/(3mu^2), <a^2>=112 kappa^2/(15mu^4), giving A and B above.

Five symbolic identities verify these steps. Independent nested numerical
quadrature at 50 decimal digits uses k_-=1/16, k_+=1/8 and K=kappa=mu=S=1.
For v=1e-6,1e-7,1e-8, it approaches A=1.9239591078e-5 and
B=-0.001477600595, with a remainder scaling as v^3. These quadratures verify
the integral, not perturbative viability of that unit-parameter model.

Control of the quantum approximation is separate. At fixed K, extrapolating
arbitrarily close to v=0 need not be justified. The leading shell variance is
<|grad delta chi|^2>=S(k_+^3-k_-^3)/(12 pi^2 sqrt(K)). A semiclassical
background expansion requires its square root to be small compared with v.
For illustration, K=1e32 and the same other parameters give an RMS gradient
3.80e-11, below the tested v range, while the largest S^2 a v/k_-^2 is 0.001024.
The determinant coefficients scale by 1/sqrt(K). This illustrates compatible
small-parameter conditions; it is not a calculation of all higher loops or a
physical measurement of K. No claim of exact quantum stability is made.

The resulting |grad chi| and |grad chi|^2 terms are absent from the proposed
tree action and allowed by its stated symmetries. Their nonzero shell
contributions demonstrate a need for additional counterterms/matching data.
A subtraction can change their renormalized coefficients. Consequently these
numbers do NOT predict an unavoidable physical instability, a universal
acceleration cutoff, or regulator-independent observables. They DO invalidate
the claim that the tree action is protected without further work. The signs
of individual loop terms cannot settle stability of the renormalized theory.
The finite-momentum background expansion is not a proof about the exact
v=0 quantum effective action.

5. Absolute normalization and the original target

Even the classical model has Lambda/a0^2=lambda kappa^2/alpha^6 if lambda mu^4
is assumed to be the total vacuum energy. A constant shift of lambda changes
this ratio without changing the finite-source equation or its Hessian. The
one-loop baseline is cutoff dependent and needs vacuum matching; it does not
supply a preferred physical lambda. A full preferred-frame completion can
also change the relation between energy density and curvature.
There is therefore no derivation of Lambda/a0^2=32 pi or of a0^4 Vol(S4)=3/128.
Replacing this missing relation with a chosen subtraction would be a fit.
