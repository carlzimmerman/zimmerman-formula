1. Restore the omitted spatial degrees of freedom

Use flat spacetime, units c=1, r>0, and the SAME shift-symmetric kinetic action:

 L = (rdot^2 - |grad r|^2)/2
     + r^2 (thetadot^2 - |grad theta|^2)/2 - U(r).

The phase equation and conserved shift current are

 d_t(r^2 thetadot) - div(r^2 grad theta) = 0,
 j^0 = r^2 thetadot,  j = -r^2 grad theta.

Conservation of the integral of j^0 is NOT conservation of phase energy.
Define

 e_theta = r^2 (thetadot^2 + |grad theta|^2)/2,
 F_theta = -r^2 thetadot grad theta.

Product differentiation followed by the phase equation gives EXACTLY

 d_t e_theta + div F_theta
       = r rdot (|grad theta|^2 - thetadot^2) = Q_theta.

This has both signs and a spatial energy flux, with no explicit shift breaking.
The radial equation is

 rddot - Laplacian r + U'(r)
       = r (thetadot^2 - |grad theta|^2).

With e_r=(rdot^2+|grad r|^2)/2+U and F_r=-rdot grad r,
d_t e_r+div F_r=-Q_theta. Therefore total local energy is conserved.
There is no phenomenological alpha in these identities. They do not select
U, its scales, the initial state, a halo target, or a coarse-grained rate.

2. Why K2's conserved homogeneous momentum does not remove wave exchange

For a homogeneous radial background r(t), a nonzero spatial Fourier mode
theta_k obeys

 d_t(r^2 thetadot_k) + r^2 k^2 theta_k = 0.

Thus its canonical momentum p_k=r^2 thetadot_k is NOT constant when k!=0.
Its spatially integrated shift charge can vanish throughout while its energy
changes. Setting chi_k=r theta_k gives

 chiddot_k + [k^2 - rddot/r] chi_k = 0.

This exhibits the missing parametric channel without adding a phase potential.
The k=0 homogeneous sector instead recovers the exact K2 identity.

3. Exact finite-mode counterexample to 'shift symmetry forbids secular exchange'

Prescribe positive r(t) alternating r1 and r2. Each plateau lasts pi/(2k),
k>0. At a jump theta_k and p_k are continuous, as follows by integrating the
phase equation. For constant radius r_i, one quarter-period maps (theta,p) by

 A_i = [[0, 1/(r_i^2 k)], [-r_i^2 k, 0]].

One cycle, returning the background to r1, has

 A_2 A_1 = diag(-r1^2/r2^2, -r2^2/r1^2).

For r2!=r1, an eigenvalue has absolute value greater than one. An initial
state along that eigendirection gains energy each cycle, measured at the same
background radius. For r2=2r1 and theta_initial=0, p grows by -4 per cycle
and its phase-mode energy grows by 16 per cycle. Shift symmetry is exact.
The global charge of a nonzero Fourier mode on a periodic box is zero.

This is an externally pumped linear subsystem, not a finite-energy autonomous
halo solution. The pump supplies its energy. Narrow smooth transitions give
nearby transfer matrices because the first-order system for (theta,p) has
bounded coefficients when r is bounded away from zero. The strict instability
therefore is not contingent on literal discontinuities. An autonomous radial
sector must include backreaction; indefinite exponential growth is not claimed.

In the actual smooth radial model U=(omega^2/2)(r-f)^2, theta=constant and
r=f[1+epsilon cos(omega t)] are a background solution for |epsilon|<1.
Linear phase perturbations obey the preceding chi equation, whose small-
epsilon frequency squared is k^2+epsilon omega^2 cos(omega t)+O(epsilon^2).
This identifies a resonance candidate near 2k=omega; no quantitative rate
or full nonlinear solution is asserted from this expansion in this record.
