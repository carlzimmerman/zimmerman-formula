LOCAL CRITICAL-BRANCH EXISTENCE — CONDITIONAL MATHEMATICAL PROOF
Applies only to the explicit C2 constitutive law and Einstein comparison in
DERIVATION.txt. This does not prove those physical postulates.

Claim. There is epsilon*>0 such that, for 0<epsilon<epsilon*, the
dimensionless equations in DERIVATION.txt have a regular-center solution
with a free critical edge Y, u>0 on [0,Y), u(Y)=u_y(Y)=0 and B>=1/2.
The parameters (uc,Y) are unique in a neighborhood of their weak-limit pair.
They supply the stated static weak phase/metric solution by the vacuum join.
There is no global uniqueness claim or explicit bound on epsilon*.

1. Regular center and smooth parameter dependence.

Write w(y)=y^3 v(y). With rho_bar(u)=1+2u+3epsilon u^2/2,
 p_bar(u)=-1+epsilon u^2/2,
 v_u(s)=int_0^1 t^2 rho_bar(u(st))dt.
Then the regular equations are equivalent to the Volterra fixed point
 u(y)=uc-int_0^y (1+epsilon u(s))s[v_u(s)+p_bar(u(s))]/
                    [2(1-epsilon s^2 v_u(s)/2)] ds.
Choose a bounded C0 ball about the constant uc0 function, a small neighborhood
of uc0, and |epsilon| small. On a sufficiently short fixed [0,L], all
denominators are >=1/2. Polynomial coefficients and their derivatives are
uniformly bounded there. The integral factor s gives an O(L^2) displacement
from uc and an O(L^2) uniform Lipschitz constant for the map. Taking L small
makes it preserve the ball and contract strictly, uniformly in the parameters.

The map is smooth on that denominator-bounded Banach neighborhood. Since
||DT||<1, I-DT has inverse sum_(j>=0)(DT)^j. The Banach implicit function
theorem (or smooth contraction dependence) gives smooth dependence of the
unique fixed point on (epsilon,uc). Differentiating the Volterra expression
in y gives u_y=O(y), v bounded, w=O(y^3), and the center series stated in the
derivation. Evaluation at L>0 supplies smoothly dependent initial data for
the ordinary nonsingular ODE. Standard ODE dependence extends through a
fixed interval containing Y0, since the epsilon=0 solution is bounded there
and B=1. Shrinking the parameter neighborhood ensures continuation and a
denominator bounded away from zero. Algebraic extension across u=0 is used
only to define the endpoint map, not as an admitted physical exterior.

2. Nondegenerate endpoint map.

At epsilon=0,
 u(y;uc)=1+(uc-1)sin(y)/y,
 w=y^3-2y^2u_y.
Let Y0 be the first negative sinc minimum, with tanY0=Y0 and
Y0 in (pi,3pi/2), and uc0=1-1/sincY0. Its endpoint has u=u_y=0.
The weak ODE u_yy+2u_y/y+u=1 gives u_yy(Y0)=1.
For F(epsilon,uc,Y)=(u(Y),u_y(Y)),
 D_(uc,Y)F(0,uc0,Y0)=diag(sincY0,1).
Because sincY0<0, this matrix is invertible. The ordinary parameter IFT gives
a smooth nearby critical branch (uc(epsilon),Y(epsilon)), locally unique.
On B>0, the exact u_y equation shows F=0 is equivalent to u(Y)=0,w(Y)=Y^3.

3. Positivity and metric admission.

The weak solution is positive before its first minimum, because sinc(y) is
strictly decreasing between 0 and Y0. It therefore has a positive margin on
any closed subinterval separated from Y0. Smooth dependence preserves this
compact interior margin. Closeness alone is insufficient at the double zero.
At every nearby critical endpoint, differentiating the exact ODE gives
 u_yy(Y)=1/[1-epsilon Y^2/2]>0.
Smooth C2 dependence on the fixed endpoint neighborhood makes u_yy>0 on a
uniform neighborhood of that endpoint. Since u(Y)=u_y(Y)=0, Taylor's integral
formula gives u(y)>0 immediately inside Y. This overlaps the compact interior
argument and proves u>0 throughout [0,Y).

w/y=y^2 v extends continuously to zero at the center and is uniformly bounded
on the continuation interval. Shrinking epsilon* ensures B=1-epsilon w/(2y)
>=1/2 there, also BR=1-epsilon Y^2/2>0. Thus N=(1+epsilon u)^(-1/2) is real,
positive, and the interior has PX>0 and positive local time kinetic coefficient.

4. Exterior and weak join.

Mass at the edge fixes the exterior B and N shown in DERIVATION.txt. In the
outer static patch B decreases from its positive maximum at R. Taking the
square root of psi'^2 there yields real psi', continuous to zero at the edge,
and choosing psi(R)=0 matches theta. Because u=u_y=0 at the edge, rho,p,J
match with their first derivatives to the exact vacuum. The Einstein equations
hold on each side; continuity of metric first derivatives avoids a delta
curvature shell. J^r is identically zero, so its distributional divergence
has no delta source. This supplies a C1 phase / weak field solution.

It does NOT supply a globally C2 phase: the outside psi'' limit is generally
nonzero while it vanishes inside. Nor does it certify a nondegenerate vacuum
principal symbol or any perturbative/UV/horizon stability. The weak join and
the degenerate effective field theory's physical admission are separate claims.
