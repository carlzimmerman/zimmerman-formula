# Moving gravitational boundary limitation

The lens box in physical coordinates has radius/halfwidth a(t)B. Even when the material shell mass near that boundary is negligible, the cold gravitational field is nonzero there. A moving zero-Dirichlet potential boundary can exchange gravitational field energy and change the potential reference. The material boundary-mass gate alone is therefore not an energy-boundary-work certificate.

The actual lens outputs report raw K+W drift, without a separately measured moving gravitational boundary-work ledger. Do not attribute all of that drift solely to spatial truncation, cooling or a receiver. The free Gaussian benchmark has gravity disabled, so this caveat does not explain away its discretization-dependent energy error. All actual lens runs already fail the amplitude-phase gate; no accepted physical remnant was inferred.

A physical energy flux test must include the gravitational stress/energy terms and the moving-boundary contribution, or use a converged fixed/infinite domain. The subsequent HJ packet uses the original fixed box and isolates carrier-gauge kinetic defects separately; its reported numerical gauge jumps are not this moving-boundary work.
