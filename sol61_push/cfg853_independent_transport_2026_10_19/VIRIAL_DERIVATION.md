# A two-tracer mass floor for cold-core proposals

## Exact mathematical statement

Let two nonrotating spherical, stationary collisionless tracer populations
have three-dimensional Plummer tracer-weight density profiles with scales
`0 < a1 < a2` and total weights `L_i`. Let `S_i` denote the **whole-population LOS
second moment with exactly that same weight**. Number weighting uses `L_i=N_i`;
luminosity weighting uses the luminosity density. A number-density fit can be
used with a luminosity-weighted moment only if the mean tracer luminosity has
no radial dependence. The common spherical gravitating mass is a nonnegative finite
radial measure `dM(s)` of total mass `M`. Tracers may have different anisotropies.
Assume the Jeans equation holds, the integrals exist, and the radial-pressure
boundary term vanishes. All gravitating mass, including stars and any cold
population, is included in `dM`. The force in this theorem is Newtonian and is
generated by that mass. A separate nongravitational force or non-Newtonian
acceleration is outside its hypotheses.

The projected scalar virial identity and interchange of nonnegative integrals
give, for either population,

```
S_i = (4 pi G / (3 L_i)) integral_0^infinity nu_i(r) M(<r) r dr
    = G/(3 a_i) integral_0^infinity [1 + (s/a_i)^2]^(-3/2) dM(s).
```

For completeness the first equality follows by multiplying spherical Jeans by
`4 pi r^3`, integrating, and averaging one Cartesian velocity component over
solid angle. The factor `3-2 beta` from total kinetic energy cancels in this
average; isotropy is not required. For Plummer
`nu_i=3 L_i/(4 pi a_i^3) [1+r^2/a_i^2]^(-5/2)`, and the remaining radial integral
is `a_i^2/3 [1+s^2/a_i^2]^(-3/2)`.

Define `y_i=3 a_i S_i/G`, `h=a2/a1>1`, and `R=y2/y1`, with `y1>0`.
Each tracer separately requires `M >= y_i`. Both together require `1<=R<h^3`
for a finite profile with nonzero moments. At `R=1`, all contributing mass
must be at the origin. At `R>=h^3` no finite nonzero radial mass measure fits.
For `1<R<h^3`, the stronger sharp bound is

```
M >= M_min = y1 * [(h^2-1)/(h^2/R^(2/3)-1)]^(3/2).
```

Proof: put `u=[1+(s/a1)^2]^(-3/2)`. The second tracer weight equals

```
g(u) = h^3 u / [1+(h^2-1)u^(2/3)]^(3/2),  0<=u<=1.
g'(u) = h^3 [1+(h^2-1)u^(2/3)]^(-5/2).
g''(u) = -(5/3) h^3 (h^2-1) u^(-1/3)
          * [1+(h^2-1)u^(2/3)]^(-7/2) < 0   (0<u<=1).
```

The continuous concave extension has `g(0)=0`. Jensen with probability
`dM/M` gives `y2/M <= g(y1/M)`. Dividing by `y1/M`, solving this inequality,
and using positivity gives the bound. Equality is attained by a thin spherical
shell at the unique radius determined by `u=y1/M_min`. This sharp measure bound
is a necessary condition only: the shell need not admit positive stationary
tracer distribution functions. Requiring smooth physical mass profiles or
distribution-function consistency cannot weaken it. A specified stellar
distribution consumes part of the allowed mass and generally strengthens it.

## Compactness restriction, without choosing a mass profile

If the **entire gravitating mass** is inside `b`, the ratio `R` is an average
of the increasing weight ratio `g(u(s))/u(s)`, with positive weights
`u(s)dM(s)`. Consequently

```
R <= [(1+b^2/a1^2)/(1+b^2/a2^2)]^(3/2).
```

Thus the shell radius that saturates the mass floor is also a lower bound on
the outer support radius of any fitting mass profile. This statement concerns
all gravitating mass; an extra extended envelope changes the support premise
and must be included in the stock and both tracer moments. It is not a bound
on a core's scale when unlimited external mass is allowed.

## Application contract, not an observational certificate

`source_extraction.json` records Table 2 of Tolstoy et al. (2025), arXiv
2504.02787v1. A spherical reinterpretation takes `a=D tan(Rh)` and
`S=sigma^2`, with optional common radius factor `c_geom`. The table actually
fits elliptical number-density profiles and Gaussian widths in a selected sample.
A consistent number-weighted Jeans moment or a justified conversion to
luminosity weighting is needed. Selection,
ellipticity, radial variation, population membership and non-equilibrium must
be forward modelled before its numbers certify the theorem's premises. The
two widths are not assumed to be newly binary-cleaned moments.

The conditional finite-stock comparison additionally uses the old Sextans
`Upsilon_V=2`, luminosity, catchment retention `f_ret=.1` and cold/baryon ratio
`5.364`. Then **total** available gravitating mass, even placing all remaining
stars and all cold at favorable radii, is
`M_stock=(1+5.364/f_ret) Mstar`. This is deliberately more generous than the
remaining-free-stock budget; no retained draw is subtracted twice. All stocks
and retention limits here are model inputs, not new measured baryon fractions.

The one-tracer outer bound and the sharp two-tracer bound are algebraic. The
checker verifies their arithmetic, recovers an independent thin-shell fixture,
and checks the lower bound against a discretized positive-mass linear program.
The linear program supplies a numerical cross-check, not the proof.
