Result
In the declared power-law scalar family, the logarithmic force profile's
long-shell instability disappears at n=3/2, precisely where a cubic spatial
gradient energy supports the logarithmic profile without a tuned potential.
That same exponent gives square-root source-mass scaling. This connects the
previous unstable canonical construction to the known AQUAL-type force law.
It is a conditional stability/exponent relation, not a new MOND theory or a
selection of32pi. Stability permits all n>=3/2; the potential-free logarithmic
condition is needed to single out n=3/2 within this family.

The cost is explicit: the simple Lorentz-invariant kinetic continuation has
radial characteristic speed squared2n-1, equal to2 at the boundary. It also
has vanishing quadratic kinetic coefficient as the background gradient goes
to zero. A full gravity/matter/UV completion has not been established.

1. Declared model and conventions
Flat Minkowski metric (-+++), X=(partial chi)^2/2, static background X=Y>0,
 L=-H(X)-U(chi), H(X)=kappa X^n/n, kappa>0, n>1/2.
The fractional power is used only on the spacelike X>0 branch. No smooth
continuation to timelike X or across X=0 is assumed.
Use a prescribed logarithmic profile chi=chi0+C log(r/r0), C>0, so
 Y=C^2/(2r^2), H_Y=kappa Y^(n-1)>0.
U is chosen along the profile to solve the source-free radial equation.
This step imposes the desired force shape rather than deriving that shape
for arbitrary n. A linear matter coupling gives acceleration q chi'/M.
No metric or matter perturbations are included in the stability result here.

2. Potential required by the logarithmic profile
The radial equation gives
 U_chi=(1/r^2)(r^2 H_Y chi')'=C(3-2n)H_Y/r^2,
 U_chichi=-2n(3-2n)H_Y/r^2.
The radial gradient coefficient is H_Y+2Y H_YY=(2n-1)H_Y>0.
The time kinetic coefficient on the static spacelike background is H_Y.
Thus for n<3/2 the required potential has negative second derivative;
for n=3/2 it is constant on the sampled interval; for n>3/2 it is convex.

3. Exact finite-shell trial form and threshold
For radial Dirichlet perturbations eta on r1<r<r2, the quadratic energy is,
up to a positive normalization,
 Q=integral r^2[(2n-1)H_Y eta'^2+U_chichi eta^2]dr.
Let t=log(r/r1), L=log(r2/r1), and choose
 eta=(r/r1)^(n-3/2) sin(pi t/L).
Dividing by the positive overall factor from kappa,C,r1 yields
 Q_norm=L/2[(2n-1)(pi/L)^2+B(n)],
 B(n)=(2n-1)(n-3/2)^2-2n(3-2n)
     =(2n-3)(4n^2+3)/4.
For 1/2<n<3/2, B<0: a negative trial form occurs whenever
 L>2pi sqrt[(2n-1)/((3-2n)(4n^2+3))].
At n=1 this reduces exactly to2pi/sqrt7, the previous canonical result.
For n>=3/2, U_chichi>=0 and every gradient coefficient is positive, so the
full scalar quadratic energy on any finite Dirichlet shell is nonnegative.
Angular harmonics add a nonnegative angular-gradient term and do not spoil
this conclusion. This last statement follows from the full quadratic energy,
not merely from one successful trial function.
Therefore n=3/2 is the boundary for absence of this instability on shells of
arbitrarily large logarithmic span within the stated family. For n below the
boundary, a finite short shell may still be stable: do not confuse a long-shell
obstruction with a claim of instability at every radial range.

4. Why the boundary also has the required mass scaling
At n=3/2, H=(2kappa/3)Y^(3/2), so spatial gradient energy is proportional to
|grad chi|^3. The logarithmic profile has constant flux
 r^2 H_Y chi'=kappa C^2/sqrt2,
and U_chi=0. A point-source flux q M_b/(4pi M) fixes
 C^2=sqrt2 q M_b/(4pi kappa M).
Thus the magnitude of the scalar acceleration is
 g=q C/(M r),
 g^2=G M_b a0/r^2,
 a0=2sqrt2 q^3/(kappa M), with the reference G=1/(8pi M^2).
This is a spherical static force normalization, not a fit to galaxy data or
an established identification of M with a measured gravitational constant
in a completed theory. A tensor contribution would also need to be included
when the scalar force does not dominate. No high-acceleration interpolation,
finite source matching, lensing, or cosmology is supplied here.

There is a second simple way to see the exponent restriction: without U_chi,
constant flux implies r^2 chi'^(2n-1)=constant. Requiring chi' proportional to
1/r forces2n-1=2, hence n=3/2. The stability calculation supplies additional
information: this potential-free force law sits at the boundary where the
negative-potential-curvature shell instability disappears.

5. Remaining cone, vacuum and normalization issues
The local principal coefficients of the Lorentz-invariant continuation give
 c_radial^2=(H_Y+2YH_YY)/H_Y=2n-1,
 c_angular^2=1.
Thus the stability boundary has c_radial=sqrt2 relative to the metric light
cone. In this narrow family, imposing both absence of the arbitrarily-long-
shell instability and c_radial<=1 is impossible. This is a comparison of
characteristic cones on the declared background, not a blanket assertion
about causality of every nonlinear scalar theory or a UV completion.
