The U(1)-invariant density modulation is \(\delta V=\epsilon s|\Psi|^2\), with radial shape

\[

\]

For each grid/profile its reduced force \(F_1=-\sqrt2\epsilon rfS\) is normalized to \(\Delta r\sum_i|F_i|^2=1\). The required \(\epsilon\), response norm, and profile record are preserved. Thus the table compares unit canonical force norms; it does not assert a universal physical \(\epsilon\), merger displacement, or excitation energy. Linearity permits an arbitrarily small physical amplitude, with flux proportional to its square. The \(\ell=2,m=2\) angular flux corresponds to chirality imposed by the external modulation.

In the outgoing solve, the fluctuation Dirichlet endpoint is replaced by the exact free circular-channel ghost ratio
\(c_j(L)/c_j(L-\Delta r)\), using reduced outgoing Hankel functions for open channels and real decaying functions for closed channels. The physical minus sideband remains conjugated. The background itself retains the supplied variational Dirichlet endpoint. Endpoint interaction corrections are bounded in the results and tested by moving \(L\).

The discrete link flux is checked against the independently evaluated source work:

\[
 \dot K_{\rm edge}=-\frac{2\Omega}{\Delta r}
   \Im(y_N^\dagger y_{N+1}),\qquad
 \dot K_{\rm work}=2\Omega\Im\!\left(\Delta r\sum_i y_i^\dagger F_i\right),

\]

The continuum coefficient flux \(\dot K_{\rm cont}=\sum_j2\Omega p_j|A_j|^2\) is reported separately. It differs from the exact finite-difference link flux by a discretization factor; for a free \(\ell=0\) wave that factor is \(\sin(p\Delta r)/(p\Delta r)\). They were not silently identified.

## Charge, angular momentum, and the next natural-source test

The records include signed laboratory \(\dot E,\dot Q,\dot J_z\) for each channel. Here all open charged waves have positive \(\dot Q\). Because the full density modulation is U(1)-invariant, a sustained physical realization must compensate that radiation with background/remnant charge loss at second order. Holding the linear background fixed does not evolve that compensation. The wave's total laboratory energy includes the \(\omega\dot Q\) term; it is not all excess cooling energy.

Likewise, the imposed \(m=2\) modulation supplies angular momentum. The identity \(\dot J_z=(m/\Omega)\dot K\) checks its wave normalization; it does not show that an initially unforced fragment or merger creates the required chiral excitation. Fragment-centred intrinsic angular momentum still differs from galaxy-centred orbital transport.

The next discriminating calculation should replace the arbitrary drive by the quadratic source generated by a normalized localized fragment mode. A second harmonic above the first vacuum threshold can radiate even when its fundamental is linearly trapped. Its source coefficient, angular selection rule, mode-energy normalization, background-charge compensation, and outgoing power must all be retained. Such a calculation would measure a small-amplitude nonlinear radiation coefficient, still without supplying a merger's mode amplitude or the four-dwarf transport budgets.
