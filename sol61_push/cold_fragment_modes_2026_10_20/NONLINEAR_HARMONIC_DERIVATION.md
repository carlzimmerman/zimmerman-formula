# Quadratic self-forcing and second-harmonic radiation

20 October 2026. Independent analytic derivation for the explicitly changed common matter action. The reviewed bytes and source revision are pinned in `sources_nonlinear.json`. No outgoing solver, spectral solve, simulation, or merger experiment was run for this audit.

**Result.** The proposed five quadratic nonlinearities and the angular projection coefficient are correct. The proposed source and mode-energy normalization are consistent in the explicit no-half complex-amplitude convention below. Positive outgoing power implies a **negative** derivative of the retained mode energy. The angular-momentum sign must match the chosen temporal and azimuthal conventions. This is a same-action channel for loss of an excited fragment's internal energy and spin, conditional on nonzero computed overlap and outgoing flux; it does not supply galactic orbital transport or a four-dwarf formation history.

## 1. Canonical fields and the cubic potential

Write

    Psi=e^(i omega t)(F+u+i v)/sqrt(2),
    X=e^(2i omega t)(B+s+i t_X)/sqrt(2),
    chi=h+xi,
    F=sqrt(2)f, B=sqrt(2)b.

The five real perturbations have canonical kinetic terms. The interaction potential is

    V=m^2|Psi|^2+MX^2|X|^2
        +g(X*Psi^2+X Psi*^2)+lambda|Psi|^4
        +a chi|Psi|^2+U(chi),
    U(chi)=M^2 chi^2/2-M chi^3/8+chi^4/4.

Terms from the rotating kinetic energy are at most quadratic in these perturbations. Thus the quadratic nonlinear force comes entirely from the cubic potential

    V3=lambda F u(u^2+v^2)
        +(g/sqrt(2))[s(u^2-v^2)+2 t_X u v]
        +(a/2)xi(u^2+v^2)+(U'''(h)/6)xi^3,
    U'''(h)=-3M/4+6h.

Differentiating V3 gives, in canonical field names,

    N_u=lambda F(3u^2+v^2)+sqrt(2)g(su+t_X v)+a xi u,
    N_v=2lambda Fuv+sqrt(2)g(-sv+t_X u)+a xi v,
    N_s=(g/sqrt(2))(u^2-v^2),
    N_t=sqrt(2)g u v,
    N_chi=(U'''(h)/2)xi^2+(a/2)(u^2+v^2).

All five supplied formulas therefore have the correct factors and signs. For the existing spatial/gyro matrix order `Z=(u,s,xi,v,t_X)`, assemble the nonlinear vector as **`(N_u,N_s,N_chi,N_v,N_t)`**. No extra background B term occurs at cubic order: B enters the linear spatial Hessian.

The full equations have the form

    z_tt+G z_t+H z+N2(z)+N3(z)=0,

where N2 is the homogeneous quadratic polynomial above. At the order considered, N3 contributes to higher-order corrections and the fundamental frequency shift, not to the leading second-harmonic source.

## 2. Complex amplitude, angular projection, and radial source

Fix unit-normalized complex spherical harmonics, integral |Y_lm|^2 dOmega=1, with Y_lm proportional to exp(i m phi). Choose the **no-half** real-field convention

    z1(t,r,angles)=A[Z(r)Y_22 exp(i Omega t)/r
                         +Z(r)*Y_22* exp(-i Omega t)/r].

Here A is real and slowly varying in a later multiscale approximation. The complex modal vector Z contains the relative phases of all five canonical fields. N2 is evaluated on its complex argument by polynomial continuation, with **no complex conjugation** inside the second-harmonic polynomial. For example, `u^2` becomes `Z_u^2/r^2`, not `|Z_u|^2/r^2`. Conjugated products belong to the static self-mixing sector.

The exact highest-weight identity is

    Y_22^2=C22 Y_44, C22=sqrt(5/(14 pi)).

It follows from the explicit normalized harmonics, or by taking the ratio of their sin^4(theta) exp(4i phi) coefficients. Since N2 is quadratic,

    [N2(z1)]_(2Omega,44)=A^2 C22 N2(Z/r)Y_44 exp(2i Omega t).

For the reduced radial second-order field w2=r z2, its positive-frequency coefficient obeys

    [W_ell4-(2Omega)^2 I+i(2Omega)G]w2=F2,
    F2=-C22 r N2(Z/r).

The actual driven coefficient is A^2 F2. The minus sign comes from moving the nonlinear force to the right-hand side. This source is generated by the action and parent eigenmode, rather than by an independently imposed density drive.

The same real first-order perturbation also generates a zero-frequency source from Y_22 Y_22* in ell=0,2,4. It produces static background deformation and charge/frequency corrections. These terms do not constitute leading outgoing radiation, but they cannot be omitted from a claim of a complete second-order solution or an exact fixed-total-charge family. A real standing quadrupole or an arbitrary mixture of m and -m is a different angular source and generally has different spin and radiation weights.

If instead one writes `z1=A Re[Z Y exp(i Omega t)/r]`, the factors change: the quadratic mode energy is one quarter of the no-half expression, and a second-harmonic real-part phasor has source `-A^2 C22 r N2(Z/r)/2`. Mixing these conventions produces a factor-of-four error in the radiation-power coefficient. The numerical eigenvector alone does not select a real-field amplitude convention.

## 3. Conserved quadratic energy and normalization

Let W be the real self-adjoint reduced radial spatial operator in the chosen ell sector. The linear equation is z_tt+G z_t+Wz=0, with G real and skew. Its conserved quadratic energy is the second variation of K=E-omega Q:

    K2=(||z_t||^2+<z,Wz>)/2.

In the no-half convention and for m=2, angular integration eliminates the terms containing Y_22^2. Consequently

    K2=A^2 kappa,
    kappa=integral[Omega^2 Z†Z+Z†WZ]dr.

On the existing uniform reduced grid this is

    kappa=dr[Omega^2 Z†Z+Z†WZ].

There is no extra 4pi factor when integral |Y|^2=1. The background stationary charge integration still contains 4pi because its background is an unnormalized angular constant. These two normalizations must not be mixed.

For a positive-energy mode, replace Z by Z/sqrt(kappa). Then the parent mode's leading K energy is A^2. This normalization defines a perturbative coefficient; it does not assert that arbitrarily large amplitudes remain linear. A tiny real part of a finite numerical eigenvalue must be convergence-assessed before replacing its time dependence by a pure oscillator. A positive computed kappa is finite evidence for the selected mode, not a theorem about every perturbation.

## 4. Angular momentum and the chirality sign

The quadratic rotating-frame Lagrangian can be written

    L2=||z_t||^2/2+(z_t^T Gz)/2-<z,Wz>/2,
    pi=z_t+Gz/2.

Using the standard active-rotation/physical stress-tensor convention,

    J_z=-integral pi^T partial_phi z d^3x.

For the temporal and harmonic convention `exp(+i Omega t)Y_lm`, the eigenvalue equation gives

    (W-Omega^2 I+i Omega G)Z=0,
    kappa=2Omega^2 integral Z†Z dr-i Omega integral Z†GZ dr,
    J_z=-(m/Omega)K2.

Thus `J_z=+m K2/Omega` requires the opposite temporal convention `exp(-i Omega t)` or harmonic index `-m` with `exp(+i Omega t)`. Equivalently one can explicitly define m as the physical chirality, opposite to the index of Y_lm in the positive-time convention. The magnitude relation is always

    |J_z|/K2=|m|/Omega

for the single chiral mode. A signless or magnitude ledger is adequate for capacity checks, but an actual vector-conservation ledger must state its orientation convention. Do not silently reverse the m sign only in the radiation flux.

The generated chiral second harmonic has frequency 2Omega and angular index 4. Its ratio is 4/(2Omega)=2/Omega, equal in magnitude to that of the parent m=2 mode. This is consistent with removal of the mode's internal angular momentum through its own second harmonic. It is **intrinsic spin about the fragment centre**, not the centre-of-mass orbital angular momentum of fragments around a dwarf or of the dwarf around its host. A mechanism coupling those orbital degrees of freedom to this internal mode is an additional obligation.

## 5. Leading damping law and separate E/Q ledgers

Suppose an outgoing calculation with the K2=1 modal shape and its exact quadratic source returns a positive outgoing rotating-frame power gamma. At amplitude A, the second-harmonic response is proportional to A^2 and its power is proportional to A^4. In a weak-amplitude, slowly evolving, isolated system with this channel dominant,

    dK_mode/dt=-gamma K_mode^2+higher-order terms,
    K_mode(t)=K0/[1+gamma K0 t],
    A(t)=sqrt(K0)/sqrt(1+gamma K0 t).

The **outgoing** power is `+gamma K_mode^2`; the retained mode-energy derivative has the minus sign. If an overlap cancels, gamma can be zero despite an open channel. Finite nonzero flux must be shown with residual, work/flux and box/grid checks; kinematics alone does not establish damping.

The emitted invariant is K_out=E_out-omega Q_out. For the open positive-charge Psi sideband at lab frequency nu=omega+2Omega,

    E_out/Q_out=nu,
    K_out/Q_out=2Omega,
    Q_out=K_out/(2Omega),
    E_out=[1+omega/(2Omega)]K_out.

These are flux ratios in the matching no-half convention; signs for other sidebands need their signed physical frequency and charge. The background loses that emitted charge and changes its stationary energy by dE_bg approximately omega dQ_bg. Therefore E_out may exceed the excitation's initial K budget without supplying free laboratory energy: the background charge/energy ledger pays the difference. Background shift, nonlinear frequency shift, static self-mixing, and other harmonics enter a controlled multiscale development. The fixed-omega leading damping law is not an exact all-amplitude evolution or a proof of indefinite charge loss.

    P4(z)=1-10i/z-45/z^2+105i/z^3+105/z^4.

For a closed decaying channel exp(-kappa r), the corresponding real polynomial is

    D4(z)=1+10/z+45/z^2+105/z^3+105/z^4.

An open/closed-channel boundary check at ell=0 or2 alone does not verify this ell=4 boundary.
