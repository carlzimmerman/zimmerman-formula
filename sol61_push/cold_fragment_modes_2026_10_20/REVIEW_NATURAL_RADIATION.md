## Source and normalization reconstruction

In canonical fields \(\Psi=e^{i\omega t}(F+u+iv)/\sqrt2\), \(X=e^{2i\omega t}(B+s+it_X)/\sqrt2\), with \(F=\sqrt2f\) and \(B=\sqrt2b\), the cubic potential is

\[
 V_3=\lambda F u(u^2+v^2)
  +\frac g{\sqrt2}\left[s(u^2-v^2)+2t_Xuv\right]
  +\frac a2\xi(u^2+v^2)+\frac{U'''(h)}6\xi^3,
 \quad U'''(h)=-\frac{3M}{4}+6h.
\]

Independent differentiation of the **full** canonical potential, followed by extraction of the quadratic gradient coefficient, reproduces all five nonlinearities in `natural_mode_source_v2.py`. Evaluating that independently reconstructed gradient on each cached complex mode reproduces every cached reduced source to relative error below \(2.1\times10^{-16}\).

The code correctly uses polynomial products such as \(u^2\), not \(|u|^2\), for the positive second harmonic. The exact identity

\[
 Y_{22}^{2}=\sqrt{\frac5{14\pi}}Y_{44}
\]

then gives \(F_2=-\sqrt{5/(14\pi)}\,rN_2(Z/r)\). No additional half belongs in this source under the stated phasor-plus-conjugate convention. The coefficient is generated by the parent mode and action; the outgoing solver's use of an inhomogeneous frequency equation does not make it an externally imposed density drive.

For each cached mode, a separate matrix-free finite-difference reconstruction gives

\[
 \kappa=\Delta r\left[\Omega^2Z^\dagger Z+Z^\dagger WZ\right]=1

within \(7.1\times10^{-14}\). There is no extra \(4\pi\): the perturbation harmonic has unit angular norm. The canonical Noether formula in the chosen chirality convention is

\[
 J_2=\Delta r\left[2m\Omega Z^\dagger Z-imZ^\dagger GZ\right]

It agrees with the physical circular-sideband calculation. The second harmonic has \(m_h=4\), \(\Omega_h=2\Omega\), hence the same \(J/K=2/\Omega\); there is no extra factor two in the loss law. ## Charge, DC deformation, and the correct changing-background energy

Let \(\mathcal Q\) be the charge of the **whole retained bound object**, including its mode and induced static deformation, and let \(E_s(\mathcal Q)\) denote the selected smooth stationary branch with \(E_s'(\mathcal Q)=\omega(\mathcal Q)\). Define

\[
 K_{\rm exc}=E_{\rm bound}-E_s(\mathcal Q).
\]

Stationarity gives \(K_{\rm exc}=A^2+O(A^4)\). With no external source, conservation yields

\[
 \dot K_{\rm exc}=-\left[\dot E_{\rm out}
                 -\omega(\mathcal Q)\dot Q_{\rm out}\right]
       =-\gamma A^4+O(A^6).
\]

Therefore \(\dot K_{\rm exc}=-\gamma K_{\rm exc}^2+O(K_{\rm exc}^3)\), and the same leading coefficient applies to the quadratic mode-energy coordinate. This is the appropriate conservation bridge. Differentiating the unsubtracted \(E_{\rm bound}-\omega(t)\mathcal Q\) would add \(-\mathcal Q\dot\omega\); it is not the mode-energy derivative.

The emitted laboratory energy exceeds the emitted excitation energy because positive charge leaves the stationary reservoir. The **whole** bound object must lose charge at \(-\gamma K_{\rm mode}^2/(2\Omega)\). A fixed-charge remnant cannot be assumed simultaneously.

Separately, the raw quadratic charge of the first-order mode, before its DC correction, is about 14.2840 and 20.3054 per unit mode \(K\), respectively. Those values differ from the charge radiated per unit lost \(K\). If
\(\mathcal Q=Q_b+q_2K_{\rm mode}+O(K_{\rm mode}^2)\), the stationary-background allocation obeys

\[
 \dot Q_b=\gamma K_{\rm mode}^2
                \left(q_2-\frac1{2\Omega}\right)+O(K_{\rm mode}^3).
\]

Here \(q_2\) includes the chosen static correction and background/charge convention. It is not automatically the raw mode-charge number. This allocation is not mass creation, a galactic mass-concentration result, or a substitute for total-charge conservation.

The same quadratic nonlinearity produces zero-frequency \(m=0\), \(\ell=0,2,4\) sources. They do not radiate at this order or change the leading positive \(2\Omega\) source, but their solvability, gauge condition, fixed-total-charge adjustment, and induced frequency shift have not been solved. A regular DC correction changes \(K_{\rm exc}\) at order \(A^4\), affecting the slowly varying loss law only at higher order; a resonance or singular correction would invalidate that simple argument.

## Remaining restriction and next check

The coefficient supports a candidate weak-amplitude internal energy/spin relaxation channel in this alternative action. To use the scalar single-mode equation over a finite amplitude range, one still needs regular DC/fundamental corrections, exclusion of competing resonant transfer, and control of the nonlinear frequency shift. The \(Q=80{,}000\) fundamental is only about 0.002745 below the first vacuum threshold. A finite-amplitude frequency shift that opens fundamental radiation would change the leading channel and invalidate an extrapolation of this second-harmonic-only law.

The listed \(K_0=1\) half-lives, about \(2.63\times10^5\) and \(5.96\times10^6\) inverse-mass time units, are **formal values of the leading equation**. A small damping-to-frequency ratio alone does not establish that \(K_0=1\) stays perturbative or below all shifted thresholds. The cheapest next check is the charge/gauge-fixed DC response and the parent mode's leading nonlinear frequency shift; a controlled weak-mode evolution with energy, charge and intrinsic-spin flux would then test the actual decay law.

No statement here supplies a merger's excitation amplitude or chirality, the fraction of a binding gap coupled to this mode, laboratory unit conversion, intact daughter escape, external receiver dynamics, galactic orbital torque, or selective formation of Draco/Sextans without excess Fornax/Leo I mass.
