1. Precisely declared action and conventions

Use signature (-+++), R_deS=12H^2, M the reduced Planck mass, and
tau=-log(phi/F), phi>0. Set X=(partial tau)^2. Study
L = M^2 R/2 - (partial phi)^2/2 - U0 exp(phi^2/F^2)
    - beta [tau E4 + 4 G^{mn} partial_m tau partial_n tau
             - 4 X box(tau) + 2 X^2] + beta tau C^2.
Here G^{mn} is the Einstein tensor. beta>0. This includes the complete
displayed WZ functional but is not claimed to be a complete microscopic EFT:
nonminimal two-derivative terms and independent invariant operators can occur.

Source: Komargodski-Schwimmer, On Renormalization Group Flows
in Four Dimensions, arXiv:1107.3987v2, Eq. (2.13), with Appendix A conventions:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.3987
The source uses (+---). Under a constant metric sign reversal with the same
Riemann convention, E4, C^2, G^{mn}, X box(tau), and X^2 are unchanged.
Thus its displayed WZ coefficients transfer to the convention above.
Its a includes the loop factor: beta=Delta a/(16 pi^2) in the central-charge
normalization used in the preceding report. Matching requires the full IR
spectrum; values assigned below are conditional examples, not established flows.

2. The cancellation survives, but the kinetic term changes

At constant phi all the additional derivative terms and their first variations
vanish. The exact constant-scalar Einstein branch from the parent survives.
On de Sitter, G^{mn}=-3H^2 g^{mn}. Consequently the derivative term is
  -4 beta G^{mn} partial_m tau partial_n tau
   = +12 beta H^2 (partial delta_phi)^2/phi_v^2
at quadratic order. Relative to the healthy -1/2 (partial delta_phi)^2,
this reduces the scalar kinetic coefficient by 24 beta H^2/phi_v^2.
The X box(tau) and X^2 terms begin at cubic and quartic order respectively.
The Weyl term vanishes throughout the homogeneous conformally flat sector.

It is essential to include the metric constraint, not stop at the fixed-metric
kinetic coefficient. In spatially flat gauge let lapse=1+n and v=delta_phi_dot.
After integration by parts of the Euler term, the terms needed to quadratic
order, divided by the background scale factor cubed, are
 -3 M^2 H^2/(1+n) + v^2/[2(1+n)] - (1+n)U(phi)
 -8 beta v H^3/[phi(1+n)^3]
 -12 beta H^2 v^2/[phi^2(1+n)^3].
The exact symbolic Hessian and elimination of n give
 D = 1 - 24 beta H^2/phi_v^2
       + 96 beta^2 H^4/(M^2 phi_v^2),
 N = U'' + 24 beta H^4/phi_v^2
       - 384 beta^2 H^6/(M^2 phi_v^2).
The reduced quadratic action is a^3[D delta_phi_dot^2-N delta_phi^2]/2,
up to a total derivative. The mixed term was integrated with the a^3 weight.
The homogeneous equation is D(delta_phi_ddot+3H delta_phi_dot)+N delta_phi=0.
check.py derives both coefficients from this lapse-dependent action; it does
not merely substitute into the previous scalar equation.

3. New necessary health condition

Write epsilon=F^2/M^2 and z=phi_v^2/F^2. The constant-scalar branch still has
H^2=M^2 z/(4 beta), U=3M^4 z/(4 beta). Hence
 D=(epsilon-6+6z)/epsilon,
 (N/D)/H^2=12(1-z)/(epsilon-6+6z).
For 0<z<1 the restoring numerator is positive. Positive homogeneous kinetic
energy requires epsilon>6(1-z). Equality is a degenerate quadratic kinetic
term and cannot be treated as an ordinary healthy endpoint.
When D<0, positive N does not save the theory: the kinetic sign is wrong and
N/D is negative. The stable result from the truncated action cannot simply
be inherited after anomaly completion. These tests are necessary only;
inhomogeneous scalar/tensor modes and the higher-derivative cutoff remain open.

4. A sharper physical implication at the proposed coefficient

The declared minimal action retains Lambda/phi_v^2=3/(4 beta epsilon).
Impose beta=Delta a/(16 pi^2) and the target 32 pi. Then
 epsilon=3 pi/(8 Delta a),
 z>1-pi/(16 Delta a).
Let q=phi_v^2/M^2=epsilon z. The same health test implies
 q>epsilon-epsilon^2/6.

Conditional examples:
Delta a=1/4: epsilon=4.712389, z>0.214602, q>1.011287.
Delta a=3/4: epsilon=1.570796, z>0.738201, q>1.159563.
Thus these examples require a Planck-scale canonical scalar vacuum, incompatible
with identifying phi_v directly with a cosmologically tiny acceleration in
natural units. This is not an exclusion of all Delta a: the bound depends on
Delta a and on the chosen minimal action and dictionary.
A strictly positive limiting kinetic coefficient as z tends to zero requires
Delta a<pi/16. Equality has a vanishing limiting coefficient, although finite
positive z can still have D>0. For finite z use the exact inequality above.

This is the useful design clue: a conformal order parameter and an observable
acceleration scale need not be the same variable. A replacement dictionary
must come from a specified matter/force sector; multiplying by a freely chosen
tiny constant would merely reintroduce the unexplained normalization.

5. Two adversarial controls

The modified model has healthy homogeneous examples. Choose epsilon=12 and
z=1/2, with arbitrary beta>0 and tune U0 to the branch equation. Then D=3/4,
(N/D)/H^2=2/3, but Lambda/phi_v^2=1/(16 beta) varies continuously.
Health alone therefore does not select the desired coefficient.

Adding a nonminimal term -xi phi^2 R/2 also matters even at the vacuum level.
Put B=M^2-xi phi^2 and require B>0. Constant fields obey
U=3BH^2, U'=-12 xi phi H^2+24 beta H^4/phi.
With the same exponential potential these imply
Lambda/phi^2 = (3/(4 beta))[M^2/F^2 + xi(2-z)].
Thus the old amplitude-independent ratio is changed for xi nonzero. The
conformal value xi=1/6 cannot be inserted while retaining the old formula.
No general stability calculation for this nonminimal extension is claimed.
