## Independent bound

In the stated cold, static, spherical, negligible-stiffness R2 equilibrium, put `b=g_b+g_f`, with aligned enclosed source accelerations and `g_f>=0`. The tether equation gives `R(u_t)=b`, so total acceleration is `g=b+u_t=g_law(b)`. Define the inverse law

`H(g)=g^2/(g+a0)`.

For g>0, `H'(g)=g(g+2a0)/(g+a0)^2>0`. Therefore if `g0=g_law(g_b)>0` and `g<=(1+epsilon)g0`,

`g_f <= H((1+epsilon)g0)-H(g0)`.

Writing `x=g0/a0>0` gives exactly

`g_f/g_b <= epsilon [1+(1+epsilon)/(1+(1+epsilon)x)]`.

For epsilon>0 this upper bound lies strictly between epsilon and `epsilon(2+epsilon)`, with the Newtonian and deep limits attained asymptotically. At epsilon=0 it is zero. Thus the source's illustrative1e-6 model-equation tolerance permits at most roughly1e-6..2e-6 of the enclosed baryonic source in additional free gravity at the tested radius. This is **not** a measured error bar, empirical exclusion significance or allowed dispersion error. Illustrative5%/10% tolerances are illustrative choices only.

The result is profile-independent only within this pointwise enclosed-source geometry. In a spherical system g_f/g_b is the enclosed free-to-baryonic mass ratio at that radius, not the total cosmic or catchment ratio. Free mass outside the radius need not exert a spherical force there. Non-spherical/external fields, environmental-frame effects, or the finite-stiffness term `gamma rho_t'` can change the equation and require a new bound. No stellar Jeans/aperture inference is supplied.
