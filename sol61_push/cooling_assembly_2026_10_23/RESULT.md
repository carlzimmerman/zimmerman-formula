# Formation times and available matter: result

The new discriminator is that cosmological size scaling removes the earlier fixed-size mass advantage in the dynamical clock. It leaves a density/epoch threshold and a conditional coherence-dependent efficiency peak, not a predicted core mass or population. The tested weak, periodically breathing clouds radiate much too slowly to shed an order-Newtonian-binding-energy budget within the available cosmic time. This does not exclude nonlinear compact formation, but it does not supply the requested mechanism.

## Quantitative result

The illustrative clock uses H0=67.4 km/s/Mpc and Omega_m=.315, the base-LambdaCDM values in [Planck 2018 VI, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06209v4). They are reference inputs, not parameters inferred for this theory. The Gaussian-size convention, harmonic driving and all validity restrictions are in CONTRACT.md and DERIVATION.md.

The inherited phase gap corresponds to 16.564 Gyr^-1. If the driving frequency equals Omega_dyn, the radiation channel is open for z>11.234, corresponding to ages less than .403 Gyr in the reference clock. For twice that frequency it is open for z>6.698, or ages less than .806 Gyr. These are early-time windows for characteristic virial clouds, not universal formation redshifts. Late dense substructures and high-frequency transients can still radiate.

No sampled weak case approaches a cooling time comparable to the age. The selected weak-case masses at z20 and z30 sit at the validity boundary of the grid, not at demonstrated physical optima. Other epoch/frequency pairs have no propagating fundamental in this approximation. The times use a continuously maintained 10% radius oscillation and Newtonian Gaussian binding-energy proxy. They are not actual formation times. The scan is not a theorem over all masses, densities, spectra or nonlinear trajectories.

The unfiltered curves do contain efficiency minima, shifting with epoch and mode frequency; some lie outside the weak-field assumptions. None is evidence for a remnant attractor. More accurate integration resolves the small-cloud numerical error without changing this conclusion. See cooling.png.

## The old cooling result remains real but finite

The previous fully spatial simulations measured loss fractions .000401, .001666 and .006026 for initial masses 1,2,4 times the canonical 1.095e7 solar masses during .217 Gyr at fixed compact initial geometry. Extending the heavy case to .724 Gyr raised the fraction only to .007516. These are fractions of initial finite-box total energy, not isolated Newtonian binding energy. The late export rate decayed; extrapolating the initial burst as a permanent cooling rate would manufacture a mass threshold unsupported by the simulations.

## Matter supply does not close the population problem

Available matter fixes a mass moment: integral M n(M,t) dM = F(t) rho_c,0. It does not fix the formed fraction F, number of cores, mass distribution, dwarf central occupation or cluster radial profile. For the illustrative Omega_c=.265, rho_c,0=3.341e10 solar masses/Mpc^3. At F=.1, masses 10^6, 1.095e7 and 10^8 solar masses give respectively about 3341, 305 and 33.4 cores/Mpc^3, all with the same matter budget. These are explicit degeneracies, not population predictions.

A formation criterion and source supply spectrum must be derived before adding assembly histories. Importing a standard halo mass function would add untested collapse and initial-spectrum assumptions to this modified force law.

The inherited EdS diagnostic grants complete instantaneous conversion to ideal pressureless cores at z=6.70,11.23,20,30 or100. It retains 95.566–95.606% of the dust amplitude at k=1/Mpc, and about99.950% at k=.1/Mpc. This is a mathematical diagnostic, not an observational exclusion or a joint CMB/large-scale-structure fit. The EdS diagnostic and LambdaCDM age clock are different imposed backgrounds. Early diffuse-phase growth loss survives later conversion even under this favorable assumption.

## Changed research decision

Do not pursue a population prescription by extrapolating the fixed-size M^4 emission law over a cosmological age. The next meaningful discriminator is whether the action produces substantially denser, nonlinear precursors early enough, with a resolved binding/fragmentation criterion and a self-consistent primordial spectrum. Those precursors must be formed dynamically rather than inserted as initial compact seeds. Their mass distribution and baryonic transport then need direct validation. Repeating isolated late-time cooling experiments cannot answer that question.

This closes the bounded cooling-clock and matter-budget test, not the larger theory. No successful simultaneous dwarf, rotation-curve, cluster and growth model has been demonstrated.
