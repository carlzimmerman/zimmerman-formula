# Forward halo feedback test: target not recovered

12 October 2026. Follow-up to GRAVITATIONAL_POWER_SOURCE.md. The proposed gravitational transfer channel has now been placed into a finite forward halo calculation. The tested gas cycles do not produce the target. In the refined runs they remove energy on net, despite positive-work portions of each cycle. This does not exclude feedback generally; it refutes treating the earlier equilibrium oscillator gain as an automatic heating prescription during collapse.

## Evolution and controls

Use the frozen CFG626 Milky Way canonical scales and supplied cold mass from the previous halo experiment. Initial cold particles occupy the same type of extended uniform sphere with radius 2.43039 target radii and Gaussian one-dimensional dispersion 0.05 velocity units. Target radius is 514.318 kpc and velocity unit is 165.240 km/s. Self-gravity uses the previous softened spherical shell Hamiltonian. All particles are retained, including those outside the target radius.

Baryons now have an analytic Hernquist potential, with fixed mass Mb and base scale a0=2.5 kpc. A gas fraction f_g is redistributed with scale a(t), while the rest retains scale a0:

    Phi_b(r,t)=-Mb[(1-f_g)/(r+a0)+f_g/(r+a(t))].

This differs from the previous softened baryon force. Static controls here use this same new potential; comparisons to the old runs must not be treated as isolated feedback effects. The gravitational force and potential are a consistent analytic pair.

The gas scale expands from a0 to 8a0 and returns, preserving total baryon mass. Four cycles start at code time 3.6, spaced by 0.8. Each active cycle lasts 0.62. Rapid expansion lasts 0.02 (about 61 Myr), with return over 0.60; slow control has 0.31 for each leg. Both use cosine ramps and end with the identical original baryon profile. “Slow” is relative to the rapid prescription, not a guarantee of adiabaticity for every orbit. A timing variant starts at code time 1.0. The baseline gas fraction is 0.5; a 0.9 variant brackets a stronger perturbation.

These histories are prescribed, not generated by stellar feedback. No star-formation history, gas energy equation, or source-budget viability is asserted. This is a mechanism discrimination test before attempting that costly closure. There is no thermostat, target force, target dispersion, or nongravitational collision term. Omitting self-scattering isolates the gravitational channel; it does not establish the combined revised particle model.

## Work accounting

Each step changes the baryon potential to its midpoint value at fixed particle positions, evolves the particles with kick-drift-kick under that midpoint potential, then changes to the endpoint potential at fixed positions. Both fixed-position potential changes are added to driver work W. The diagnostic is |E(t)-E(0)-W(t)|/|E(0)|, where E includes cold kinetic energy, cold self-energy counted once, and cold–baryon potential energy. Positive-work sums also record gross transfers; they are not net heat or a proved minimum stellar budget because negative work can return energy to the driver.

All eight runs satisfy the declared 2% numerical work-balance threshold. Refined errors are much smaller (0.058–0.163%). Their final baryon potentials are identical, so the net energy differences are not potential-zero artifacts. This checks numerical accounting for a prescribed external driver, not total conservation for a simulated gas+stars system.

The refined rapid case loses about 6.50% of its initial binding-energy magnitude to the prescribed driver; its maximum work-accounting error is about 0.127%. The corresponding slow case loses about 8.25%, with 0.058% accounting error. Thus the sign in those runs is larger than the measured integration discrepancy. The rapid cycle removes less energy than the slow cycle, but neither provides net positive energy. The gas-rich and early coarse cases have weaker net work relative to numerical uncertainty, so their signs should not be overinterpreted.

## What the result changes

A stationary phase-uniform oscillator and a collapsing halo sample the same driver differently. The exact energy exchange is weighted by the evolving density, integral rho_c partial_t Phi_b d³x. Return of gas when more cold mass occupies the centre can outweigh the earlier positive work. “Rapid outflow heats the halo” therefore needs its phase-distribution and dynamical-state qualifications.

The target discrepancy persists by tens of percent. Small differences between gas histories do not establish convergence toward the target or an attractor. The finite tests supply no mechanism selecting the acceleration scale from vacuum density. Stellar feedback remains a possible ingredient, but these particular prescribed cycles are not the missing solution.

## Provenance and next discriminating step

feedback_halo.py, feedback_halo_contract.json, and feedback_halo_run/ contain scripts, inputs, snapshots, numerical checks and the validated manifest. Original results remain intact.

A meaningful continuation must change a tested assumption: form a halo first and determine its actual orbital state, then evolve an energy-budgeted gas component with feedback tied to a stated stellar population, rather than selecting pulses to improve target agreement. That work remains open; it has not been completed here. Even success would still require checking mass scaling, initial-condition dependence, and the vacuum normalization. No universal no-go conclusion follows from these eight finite runs.
