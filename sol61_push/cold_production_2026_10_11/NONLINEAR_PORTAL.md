# Nonlinear vacuum pressure without heating the dense source

Status: a candidate classical interaction passes analytic positivity,
compressibility, homogeneous-source and initial conversion-band checks.
It supplies more than a constant mass shift. Halo support parameters are
chosen, not predicted; no production yield or self-consistent halo has
been recalculated for this action.

## The density hierarchy excludes a simple quadratic choice

Retain the charge-aligned invariant S=|phi|^2+4|chi|^2 and add U(S) to
the previous positive charged scalar potential. It can be implemented by
one four-form in a fixed-flux sector with

    Z(S)=[1+U(S)/epsilon_v]^-1,
    V_4=epsilon_v+U(S),    epsilon_v=q^2/2.

To define an action independently of its flux sector, specify a fixed
nonnegative dimensionless function h(S), Z=1/(1+h), then U=epsilon_v h.
The formulas below describe one sector; coefficients must not be retuned
as q varies while pretending to keep the same action.

At leading coherent NR order S=rho/(2m^2), with rho the conserved leading
rest-energy density in natural units. For a local interaction energy U,

    P_int=S U'(S)-U(S),
    c_int^2/c^2=S U''(S)/(2m^2).

This is a coherent local mean-field relation. An incoherent multimode
ensemble requires averaging the interaction and its correlations; these
formulas are not an established kinetic closure for that ensemble.

For U=B S^2, achieving c_int=200 km/s at the illustrative halo density
1e-21 kg/m^3 requires B=2.06518e-49. The original source density is about
2.25402e12 times the halo density. On the original source amplitude,
U is 501,585 times the original source energy. Extrapolating an NR sound
speed into this regime would be invalid, so use the full relativistic
homogeneous circular-source equation instead.

With phi=0, chi=A exp(-i Omega t), S=4A^2 and bare source mass M,

    Omega^2=M^2+4U'(S),
    E=2M^2 A^2+S U'(S)+U(S),
    P=S U'(S)-U(S),    Q=4 Omega A^2.

For the quadratic U this gives P/E=0.3333331 at that amplitude. The source
is radiation-like, despite having zero spatial momentum. The previously
cold source and production history cannot be transferred to that choice.
This rejects this quadratic example with that amplitude, not every possible
source preparation or nonlinear interaction.

## A convex interaction that becomes linear at high density

Consider instead

    U(S)=A_* [sqrt(S^2+S_0^2)-S_0],    A_*>0, S_0>0.

It corresponds to h(S)=eta_v[sqrt(1+(S/S_0)^2)-1], with
eta_v=A_* S_0/epsilon_v fixed as part of the action. Its exact properties are

    0<=U(S)<=A_* S,
    U'(S)=A_* S/sqrt(S^2+S_0^2),
    U''(S)=A_* S_0^2/(S^2+S_0^2)^(3/2)>0,
    P_int=A_* S_0[1-1/sqrt(1+(S/S_0)^2)].

The interaction is approximately quadratic at small S and linear at large
S. Its pressure saturates at A_* S_0 rather than growing as density squared.
The compressibility remains positive for finite S. Positivity of U leaves
the original classical fixed-background bound E_material>=m|Q| intact.
This is not a proof of self-gravitating stability or quantum naturalness.

Put x=S/S_0. Then

    c_int^2/c^2=(A_*/(2m^2)) x/(1+x^2)^(3/2).

The sound speed peaks at x=1/sqrt(2) and falls at high density. It is not
constant over a halo. Choose S_0=rho_halo/(2m^2) and
A_*=4 sqrt(2) m^2 (200 km/s / c)^2. At x=1 this supplies the chosen
200-km/s mean-field speed. Numerically,

    S_0=2.155065262e18 eV^2,
    A_*=2.517639679e-30 eV^2,
    A_*/m^2=2.517639679e-6.

For the same original source amplitude, now using its self-consistent
circular frequency, the exact source formulas give

    Omega/M=1.00000125882,
    P/E=5.58477e-19,
    (E-mQ)/E=1.75882e-6.

Thus this specific interaction can provide the illustrative halo pressure
scale without destroying this source's low-pressure, small-excess-energy
condition. The scalar energy here includes U and excludes only the constant
vacuum background. The chosen amplitude has not been regenerated by the
previous loading-clock calculation; that preparation would need rechecking.

## The charge alignment preserves the initial conversion band

For any differentiable U(S), linearizing daughters about phi=0 gives the
diagonal mass shift U'(S), while the circular source frequency shifts by
4U'(S). Hence

    Omega^2/4-[m^2+U'(S)]=(M^2-4m^2)/4=Delta/4.

The earlier linear pair-instability condition retains its center:

    |p^2-Delta/4|<2 |g A|.

The source background has constant S and the conversion term oscillates
at Omega, so the usual rotating-frame linear calculation applies. U''
enters nonlinear perturbations and source fluctuations, not this linear
phi diagonal term at phi=0. Growth rates depend on Omega; preserving the
band does not preserve a nonlinear conversion yield. Neither the previous
Gaussian yield nor the spatial simulations are results for this new action.
The 1:4 alignment is declared, not proven radiatively protected.

## What remains missing from a halo explanation

The extra pressure is real in the declared classical action, but S_0 was
chosen from an illustrative halo density and A_* from a desired support
speed. That parameter choice is not selection of the repo's density law.
For fixed h, the pressure strength scales with epsilon_v; obtaining the
specific a0 proportional to sqrt(rho_DE) still requires a derived solution,
including baryons, transport and boundary conditions.

There is a useful restriction on any attempt to use this as the sole,
universal barotropic support. In a self-gravity-dominated flat-rotation
region with rho=V^2/(4 pi G r^2), hydrostatic balance requires

    dP/dr=-rho V^2/r,  d rho/dr=-2rho/r,
    therefore dP/d rho=V^2/2.

Two galaxies with different flat speeds and overlapping density ranges
cannot both satisfy that relation using the same density-only P(rho).
This scoped argument assumes flat curves, negligible baryonic force in
the region, isotropic barotropic support and equilibrium. Kinetic stress,
anisotropy, varying state variables or non-equilibrium flow can evade it;
none has yet been solved for the nonlinear portal. The pressure alone
therefore cannot be advertised as the universal baryonic scaling mechanism.
