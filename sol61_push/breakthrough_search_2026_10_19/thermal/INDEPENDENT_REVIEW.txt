1. Gibbs expectation and susceptibility: PASS
The energy levels are d(Delta +/- R), R=sqrt(Delta^2+E^2).
The free-energy derivative gives each site's expectation d E tanh(R/t)/R,
t=kBT/d. Multiplying by the number density w/d gives exactly the stated
p_T integral. Ground-state integration follows from the elementary change
R=sqrt(Delta^2+E^2):
 integral_E^infinity dR/(a^2+R^2-E^2)^(3/2)=1/[a(a+E)],
with E=a supplied by the continuous limit. Hence p_0=(a/K)E/(a+E).
At positive T, w/Delta is integrable at both endpoints and dominates the
E-derivative locally; the derivative-at-zero and change of variables are valid.
K chi_T=integral(1+x^2)^(-3/2)tanh(x/theta)dx follows with theta=t/a.

2. Deficit, strict signs, monotonicity, low-T coefficient: PASS
The weight integrates to one. For T>0, 0<tanh(x/theta)<1 at x>0,
so 0<delta_T<1. Increasing T strictly decreases the integrand for chi_T,
giving strict increase of delta_T. The scaled formula bounds its denominator

High-T limit delta_T->1 also follows by dominated convergence in x.

The source has the exact split

5. Retarded leaf and temperature/absorption ratio: PASS
At E=0 each transition frequency is omega=2dDelta/hbar and the transition
source element is d. The positive-frequency Kubo delta function, multiplied
by w/d and its frequency Jacobian, gives
 K Im chi=(pi/2)x/(1+x^2)^(3/2), x=omega/(2da/hbar).
Therefore tau0=pi hbar/(4da), and eliminating da from the low-T deficit
gives delta_T/(T tau0)->(4log2/pi)kB/hbar. Units are inverse-temperature
times inverse-time on both sides. At T>0 the transition population difference
is tanh(hbar omega/(2kBT)), so Im chi becomes quadratic near omega=0 and
its strict low-frequency slope is zero. The noncommuting limits and the
distinction from a bath's relaxation time are correctly stated. Variable
d(Delta) is correctly identified as extra freedom preserving the ground law.

6. General spectral mass-temperature bound: PASS under its separate premises
I also checked dynamic_response/DERIVATION.txt section 8, equations (19)-(20),
because this is the coordinator's proposed broader thermal result.
Let dmu=(2/(pi chi0))chi''_0(omega)domega/omega be a probability measure,
chi0=1/K, with E_mu[omega^2]<=K rho_eff. For independent symmetric TLS
with purely off-diagonal zero-field source, fixed gaps/inventory, and Gibbs
populations, chi_T/chi0=E_mu[tanh(hbar omega/(2kBT))].
The function F(z)=tanh(c sqrt(z)), c=hbar/(2kBT)>0, is increasing and
concave on z>=0; its printed second derivative has the correct two negative
terms for z>0, while F itself extends continuously to F(0)=0. Jensen followed by
the moment bound proves
 delta_T>=1-tanh[hbar sqrt(Krho_eff)/(2kBT)].
For 0<epsilon<1, delta_T<=epsilon therefore requires
 T<=hbar sqrt(Krho_eff)/[2kB artanh(1-epsilon)].
The bound is sharp as a statement about probability measures with a fixed
second moment: a point measure and saturated moment attain equality.

identity. Those are named assumptions, not covered cases. The finite-inertia
mass source/sum-rule premise is conditional on its declared canonical source
operator and must not be inferred from formal gravitational labels alone.
