Finite oscillator strength, critical gravitational response, and temperature
Checkpoint BZS-2026-10-19-A. Raw coordinator proof for independent audit.

Claim card
Take a stable passive zero-temperature material response to H(E)=H0-E D,
with susceptibility per volume chi_R, an unsubtracted static dispersion
relation without an instantaneous contact term, no zero-frequency atom/pole,
and finite positive spectral moments. Assume the operator domains permit
the stated Lehmann and double-commutator identities. Let K=4 pi G.
Assume chi_0=1/K and

M1=(2/pi) integral_0^infinity omega Im chi_R(omega) domega <=rho_D.

Then the fraction of zero-T static susceptibility carried by frequencies
omega>=Omega satisfies

eta_fast(Omega)<=min(1,K rho_D/Omega^2).                              (A)

If, in addition, this response is a fixed inventory of independent symmetric
two-level systems, with each zero-field source off-diagonal in its own
energy basis and each site in the Gibbs state at the same T>0, then

chi_T/chi_0 <= tanh[hbar sqrt(K rho_D)/(2 k_B T)],
delta_T=1-K chi_T >=1-tanh[hbar sqrt(K rho_D)/(2 k_B T)].             (B)

Thus delta_T<=epsilon, 0<epsilon<1, necessarily requires

T <= hbar sqrt(K rho_D)/[2 k_B artanh(1-epsilon)].                   (C)

rho_D is an OSCILLATOR-STRENGTH DENSITY. It equals gravitating carrier
mass density only under a separately justified universal-mass displacement
source dictionary. It is not inferred from the static MOND law itself.

1. Sum-rule premise from canonical finite inertia
For H0=sum_i p_i^2/(2m_i)+V({x_i}), m_i>0, and D=sum_i q_i x_i along
one probe direction, canonical commutators give

[D,[H0,D]]=hbar^2 sum_i q_i^2/m_i.

For a stationary ground state and volume V the Lehmann representation is

Im chi_R(omega)=(pi/(V hbar)) sum_n |D_0n|^2 delta(omega-omega_n),
omega_n=(E_n-E_0)/hbar>0.

Consequently chi_0=(2/(V hbar))sum |D_0n|^2/omega_n and
M1=<[D,[H0,D]]>/(V hbar^2)=sum_i q_i^2/(V m_i).

An admitted two-level subspace consumes at most this oscillator budget;
other transitions also consume positive weight. For q_i=m_i, rho_D is the
participating mass density. For nonuniversal force charges it is generally
not the mass density. Count moving supports and conversion/source products.
Do not treat an external rigid anchor as a free astrophysical component.
An isolated composite with universal gravity has no internal uniform mass
dipole about its COM, as established in the separate original-action audit.
This theorem does not undo that source obstruction or construct an anchor.

2. Proof of the frequency-fraction bound
The positive normalized static susceptibility measure is

dP(omega)=[2 Im chi_R(omega)/(pi chi_0 omega)] domega.

Its total mass is one by the static dispersion relation, and
E_P[omega^2]=M1/chi_0<=K rho_D. Integrating omega^2>=Omega^2 over
omega>=Omega proves (A). A spectrum whose entire susceptibility lies
above Omega therefore requires Omega^2<=K rho_D. This is a moment bound,
not a theorem that halo equilibration takes a particular number of years.
Bath damping, collective modes, self-consistent fields and finite k remain
to be derived before interpreting a finite-frequency gravitational response.

3. Proof of the finite-temperature bound
A symmetric zero-field TLS with splitting hbar omega and off-diagonal
source has static susceptibility reduced from its zero-T value by
tanh[hbar omega/(2k_B T)]. There is no diagonal population term in this
specified zero-field source. With fixed site gaps, dipoles and inventory,

chi_T/chi_0=E_P[tanh(c omega)], c=hbar/(2k_B T)>0.

As a function of z=omega^2, f(z)=tanh(c sqrt(z)) is increasing and concave.
Writing y=c sqrt(z), its second derivative for z>0 is

f''(z)=-c sech^2(y)/(4 z^(3/2))
        -c^2 sech^2(y)tanh(y)/(2z)<0.

Jensen, the moment budget and monotonicity yield
E_P[f(omega^2)]<=f(E_P[omega^2])<=f(K rho_D), proving (B).
Monotonic inversion of tanh gives (C). Equality can occur for a single
frequency saturating the budget. The rational ensemble is broader, so
the bound need not be saturated.

The independent symmetric TLS premise matters: a biased or asymmetric TLS
can have a diagonal population susceptibility; interacting or multilevel
matter, temperature-dependent inventory, non-Gibbs populations and new source
couplings need not obey (B). Finite background gravity changes the basis.
Neither the CMB temperature nor a de Sitter detector temperature has been
identified with this material's internal Gibbs temperature.

4. Concrete numerical illustration, not an observed bound
Choose rho_D=10^-21 kg/m^3 ONLY as a reference input. With G=6.67430e-11,
hbar=1.054571817e-34 J s, k_B=1.380649e-23 J/K,
sqrt(4 pi G rho_D) is about 9.16e-16 /s. For epsilon=.01, (C) requires
T below about 1.32e-27 K. This concerns one-percent retention of critical
zero-field susceptibility, NOT one-percent accuracy of all galaxy forces.
A kinetic rest-mass medium called 'cold' has not thereby been shown to meet
this internal-state temperature requirement. Conversely an isolated or
prepared nonthermal ground-state sector is outside the Gibbs assumption.

5. Provenance, overlap and significance
The spectral representation, oscillator sum rule, and Jensen/Markov bounds
are established mathematical tools. Dipolar MOND's gravitational timescale
sqrt(4 pi G rho) is already discussed by Blanchet and Le Tiec (2008),
Appendix A, arXiv:0804.3518v2. The finite-frequency fraction and TLS thermal
bound here are applications of those tools to the programme's critical
susceptibility; no global novelty or Nobel-level conclusion is claimed.
The repository's closure_particle_bridge_2026_10_16/PASSIVE_RESPONSE_AUDIT.txt
already proves an inertial spectral-weight/finite-time response bound for
positive reciprocal oscillator systems. Thus the general slow-response
mechanism is not a new local discovery either. The finite-T TLS inequality
is the additional specialization explored here.

This sharpens a model-building decision: finite-inertia mass dipoles cannot
simultaneously supply a critical response made entirely of arbitrarily fast
excitations and an ordinary-temperature Gibbs TLS population at low density.
One must compute the actual state/source/inertia, or add and justify other
microphysics. No kappa, abundance, new particle or observed anomaly is derived.
