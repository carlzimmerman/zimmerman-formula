DERIVATION AND CLAIM CARDS

Conventions: K=4 pi G>0, q=grad Phi is OUTWARD for a positive spherical mass; physical acceleration is -q. P is the frozen action's vector, u=K|P|, 0<=u<a0. W(0)=0. All density signs below use Laplacian Phi/K. gamma=K Lstar^2/2>0. These are the 14 October canonical action's conventions.

A. EXACT ACTION TO FINITE-STIFFNESS RESPONSE

The static field energy density is
 E=|q-KP|^2/(2K)+W(u)+(gamma/2)|grad P|^2,
 K W'(u)=u^2/(a0-u).
Write d=q-KP, n=P/|P|. Euler equations of the stated Lagrangian are
 div d=K rho_b,
 d=K W'(u)n-gamma Delta P.
Therefore
 Delta Phi=K(rho_b+div P).
This exact identity is the action-to-Poisson-source dictionary; it is not the assumption that div P is nonnegative. Literal S3 separately says div P=rho_c-rho_bar.

Take a constant external solution q_e=g_e zhat and P_e=(u_e/K) zhat. Put x=g_e/a0; u_e=a0 x/(1+x). The local constitutive derivative of K W'(u)n has eigenvalues A_perp=x and A_parallel=x(2+x), with respect to u-vector=KP. In Fourier space, linearization of the full action gives
 delta P_i=delta q_i/[K(1+A_i+ell^2 k^2)], ell^2=gamma/K,
 delta d_i=m_i(k) delta q_i,
 m_i(k)=(A_i+ell^2 k^2)/(1+A_i+ell^2 k^2).
Hence
 delta Phi_hat=-K rho_hat/[sum_i k_i^2 m_i(k)].
This matches the frozen CFG770 response formula by direct differentiation. At k->0,
 m_perp=mu_e=x/(1+x),
 m_parallel=mu_e Gamma,
 Gamma=1+L_e=2-mu_e,
 L_e=1/(1+x).
For a general local AQUAL response, the same definitions are mu_e=mu(g_e) and L_e=g_e mu'(g_e)/mu_e, with mu_e>0, Gamma>0. Our sign test additionally requires L_e>0.

At distances large compared with the response length, the leading monopole is thus the known Green function
 phi=-Gm/[mu_e sqrt(Gamma R^2+z^2)], R^2=X^2+Y^2.
Finite stiffness changes the Fourier denominator first at order k^4; its first noncontact far-field potential correction to this linear centered monopole is of degree r^-3, and its density correction of degree r^-5. For very small A_i one needs r>>ell/sqrt(min A_i), not merely r>>ell. A centered finite source also has source-size corrections. This is a linear-response asymptotic expansion; no claim of a global nonlinear remainder bound is made.

B. DENSITY, FINITE-HOST CANCELLATION AND THE POSITIVITY TEST

Directly differentiating the full three-dimensional Green function, outside the compact baryons, gives
 delta rho_c,s=Delta phi/K
 =m(Gamma-1)/(4 pi mu_e) * [2z^2-Gamma R^2]/[Gamma R^2+z^2]^(5/2).
On the equator z=0,R=r>0,
 g_s=Gm/[mu_e sqrt(Gamma) r^2],
 delta rho_c,s=-g_s L_e/[K Gamma r].
It is strictly negative if L_e>0. Its zero-density cone obeys tan^2(theta)=2/Gamma, another known/formal consequence already in the repo's September field-orbit report.

Now let the external acceleration be supplied by a spherical baryonic host, whose source is interior to radius D. In the local, long-wavelength constitutive regime,
 D^2 mu(g_e) g_e=G M_H,
 g_e'=-2g_e/(Gamma D).
Thus its baryon-free local inferred cold density is
 rho_c,H=(g_e'+2g_e/D)/K=2g_e L_e/(K Gamma D).
Substitution yields
 -delta rho_c,s/rho_c,H=(g_s/g_e) D/(2r).
This cancellation is independent of the interpolation function. It assumes the same local response function in both configurations, not object-wise retuned response parameters. For the canonical simple mu, all its coefficients are explicitly fixed above.

Let epsilon=g_s/g_e and delta=r/D. External-field dominance means epsilon<<1; slow host variation means delta<<1; dominance of the negative perturbation over the host's positive density means delta<<epsilon/2. The host's radial tidal-gradient magnitude is T_H=2g_e/(Gamma D), so the leading negativity criterion can equivalently be written g_s>Gamma T_H r. It therefore occurs where satellite self-gravity is already larger than the host's tidal differential acceleration by a factor Gamma. This is not a theorem about a rotating-frame Jacobi radius, orbital stability, stellar distribution functions or lensing.

If the cold background is rho_bar plus another component rho_other, positivity requires
 rho_bar+rho_other+rho_c,H+delta rho_c,s >=0.
The negative perturbation alone is not a negative total density theorem. At fixed independently specified positive background, the leading necessary background floor is
 rho_bar+rho_other >= (epsilon-2r/D) g_e L_e/(K Gamma r).
Finite-host/source/nonlinear corrections must be included before applying the floor quantitatively.

Exactly proved statement: the frozen full action's long-wavelength UNIFORM-BACKGROUND linearization has a negative material-density perturbation when its Poisson source is identified literally with div P. With zero material baseline this rules out a positive material interpretation in that model problem. Conditional statement: a finite positive-host / cosmological-baseline solution must become negative in the ordered regime after a sufficiently strong signed gap is proven to dominate all errors. The present calculation supplies the gap formula but not that nonlinear estimate.

C. EXACT ISOLATED SCALAR VIRIAL WITH STIFFNESS

This branch uses a different configuration: smooth isolated baryons of total mass M, no external field, no pressure surface term, and a regular solution with q=A rhat/r+o(r^-1), P=A rhat/(Kr)+o(r^-1), A=sqrt(GMa0). Required derivative falloffs and integrability of every displayed bulk term are hypotheses. Point sources or source self-energy excisions require extra inner-boundary terms and are not covered.

Define the static canonical translation tensor
 T_ij=d_j q_i/K+gamma (partial_j P_a)(partial_i P_a)-delta_ij E.
Its divergence on the Euler equations is +rho_b q_i. It need not be symmetric; a translation tensor is sufficient for its trace identity. The scalar gravitational virial is V_b=-integral rho_b x.q d^3x. Integrating div(x_i T_ij),
 V_b=integral trace(T) d^3x -lim_R surface_R x_i T_ij n_j dS.
The leading far-field radial traction has value 2A^3/(3Ka0 R^3), so the outer surface is
 B=(2/3)sqrt(Ga0) M^(3/2).
Before using the P equation, trace(T)=q.d/K-3|d|^2/(2K)-3W-(gamma/2)|grad P|^2. Insert q=d+KP and integrate P.d=uW'-gamma P.Delta P. The latter integrates to uW'+gamma|grad P|^2 when the additional P normal-derivative surface integral vanishes. Therefore

 V_b=-B+integral[-|d|^2/(2K)+uW'-3W+(gamma/2)|grad P|^2]d^3x.

Signs checked against the gamma=0 exact cubic deep-MOND limit: the integrand vanishes at leading deep order and V_b=-B. The positive gradient term reduces the magnitude of a negative virial if other terms were held fixed; they are not independent because the solution changes with gamma. Thus no monotonic-force or universal-dispersion theorem follows from its sign alone.

For the canonical saturation potential,
 W=[-a0^2 log(1-u/a0)-a0u-u^2/2]/K.
The local term uW'-3W is positive for 0<u<a0, whereas -|d|^2/(2K) is negative. Together with the positive stiffness term, the full defect has no sign supplied by this identity. In equilibrium, 2T_b=-V_b only if ordinary baryonic inertia and the relevant isolated equilibrium/pressure assumptions hold. We do not insert unproven equilibrium or cold-sector kinetic assumptions.

In a reduced exactly cubic deep-MOND plus gradient model with P=q/K at the retained order, the dilation defect is +(gamma/(2K^2)) integral |Hess Phi|^2. Subject to decaying derivative boundary terms, integral |Hess Phi|^2=integral (Delta Phi)^2, making a density-squared link possible. But replacing the exact frozen fields by that reduced model without a controlled approximation changes the claim. This attractive observable route remains conditional and was not promoted.
