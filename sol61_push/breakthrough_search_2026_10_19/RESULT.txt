Main finding
The strongest lead is a precise link between the required critical
gravitational susceptibility, finite oscillator strength, and the internal
quantum state of a proposed dipole medium. It is a conditional consistency
theorem, not a first-principles derivation of the galaxy law, a new verified
particle, or an established Nobel-level result.

Let K=4 pi G, chi_0=1/K, and rho_D be the finite oscillator-strength density
of a passive source with a valid positive spectral representation. Then

  fraction of static response from omega>=Omega <= K rho_D/Omega^2.

If the material is a fixed independent symmetric two-level ensemble in a
Gibbs state, the same spectral measure gives

  chi_T/chi_0 <= tanh[hbar sqrt(K rho_D)/(2 k_B T)].

For universal-mass displacement coordinates, rho_D equals the participating
mass density. This identification requires an actual source/inertia proof;
it does not hold for an unspecified spin, portal charge or scalar operator.
With rho_D=10^-21 kg/m^3 as a REFERENCE INPUT, retaining 99 percent of the
zero-field critical susceptibility requires T<=1.32e-27 K. This is an
internal Gibbs-state temperature, not a measured halo temperature or a
prediction that every cold medium must be that cold. Finite field, diagonal
population response, interactions, nonthermal states and changing inventory
are outside the thermal theorem. See SPECTRAL_BOUND.txt for the exact proof.

Why this is useful
The programme's ordinary positive-mass particles already lack an isolated
internal uniform-gravity dipole. Even a separately postulated supported
dipole ensemble needs an explicit quantum state, finite oscillator budget,
and reciprocal support/source bookkeeping. Calling its rest-mass stress
'cold' does not establish any of those ingredients. The new constraint
turns that missing state into an inequality that a proposed realization
can be checked against.
