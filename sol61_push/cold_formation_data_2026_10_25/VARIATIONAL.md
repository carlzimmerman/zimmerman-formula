# Small core versus whole-cloud collapse: derived diagnostic, not formation

For an isolated Gaussian core rho=M exp(-r^2/R^2)/(pi^(3/2) R^3), the same Hamiltonian gives

    K = 3 eta^2 M/(4 R^2),
    W = -G M^2/(sqrt(2 pi) R),
    R_stationary = (3 sqrt(2 pi)/2) eta^2/(G M).

The stationary point is a minimum within the ONE Gaussian scale family. It is not the exact ground state, a nonlinear stability theorem, a core-selection law, or a solution embedded in a massive envelope/baryons. The two terms are independently controlled by test_gaussian. R is density width; Gaussian projected half-light/mass radius is sqrt(log2) R only if that hypothetical core profile traces the chosen projected scale. The observed stellar radius does NOT measure cold core radius.

At eta=1500pc km/s a Gaussian core whose *hypothetical projected mass half radius* equals Draco's observed stellar193.22pc would have total8.48 million Msun and about2.47 million inside the sphere193.22pc. This is near the few-million *cut-shape* contribution that CCT018 allowed, but it is not an independent cold-radius measurement or a data fit; the full envelope also contributes central gravity. By contrast, placing the entire521.75 million supply in a Gaussian variational minimum gives width3.77pc. This explains why the all-supply collapse branch has a severe overconcentration channel.

The useful next obligation is dynamical selection of a small condensed fraction while preserving a supported outer field. One cannot assign the small core mass, keep the rest of the mass in a planted halo, and call that formation. The surrounding envelope changes both the stationary mode and the energy budget. A common eta alone does not select the four condensed fractions or solve energy/spin disposal.
