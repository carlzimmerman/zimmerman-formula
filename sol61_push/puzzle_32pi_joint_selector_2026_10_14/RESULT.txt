Target and distinction from previous work
Test an action in which a positive scalar scale and de Sitter curvature are
both varied. A successful selection must predict Lambda/a0^2=32 pi, or
equivalently a0^4 Vol(S4)=3/128, without inserting that number in a coupling.
The previous reciprocal-potential/GB construction left a neutral vacuum
family. The construction here has a restoring homogeneous mode on a lower
branch and fixes a ratio independent of its potential's overall amplitude.
It still leaves a free dimensionless coupling, and is not a MOND theory.

1. Declared action and constrained stability calculation
Use c=hbar=1, signature -+++, M^2=M_Pl^2>0 and a canonical scalar phi>0:
 S=integral sqrt(-g)[M^2 R/2 -(partial phi)^2/2 -U(phi)+f(phi) E4].
The proposed identification a0=phi_vac is an explicit hypothesis. No
covariant polarization/source sector deriving this identification is supplied.
Take a logarithmic curvature coupling f=beta log(phi/F), beta,F>0.
This is a chosen classical function, not an anomaly-matched quantum action.

On a constant-scalar, expanding de Sitter solution,
 3 M^2 H^2=U, U'=24 H^4 f'.
Varying the metric constraint along with the scalar equation gives
 D delta_phi_ddot+3 H D delta_phi_dot+N delta_phi=0,
 D=1+96 H^4 f'^2/M^2,
 N=U''-24 H^4 f''-384 H^6 f'^2/M^2.
The last term is crucial: freezing the curvature misses a possible tachyon.
It follows by substituting
 delta_H=(4 H^3 f'/M^2)delta_phi-(4 H^2 f'/M^2)delta_phi_dot
into the linearized scalar equation. This derivation is checked symbolically.

For any positive U, define p=d log U/d log phi and p_dot=d p/d log phi.
At a stationary point for the logarithmic f,
 N=(U/phi^2)(p_dot-p^2).
For U=lambda phi^p with constant nonzero p, N=-p^2 U/phi^2<0.
Thus a pure-power potential does not supply a homogeneous stable positive
vacuum in this class. This closes that candidate, not all scalar potentials.

2. A potential that supplies a restoring branch
Choose U=U0 exp(phi^2/F^2), U0>0. Let
 z=phi_vac^2/F^2, epsilon=F^2/M^2, eta=4 beta U0/(3 M^4).
The two vacuum equations reduce to
 z exp(-z)=eta, H^2=M^2 z/(4 beta).
For 0<eta<1/e, there are two positive roots, one below 1 and one above 1.
The function z exp(-z) increases up to 1 and decreases thereafter, so this
root count follows analytically, not from a scan. At eta=1/e the roots merge.

At either root,
 D=1+6z/epsilon,
 N=4U(1-z)/F^2,
 m_eff^2/H^2=12(1-z)/(epsilon+6z).
Therefore 0<z<1 has D>0 and positive restoring mass. Both characteristic
exponents of the homogeneous equation have negative real part. The z>1
root has a tachyonic growing mode; z=1 is marginal in this linear test.
This is a coupled homogeneous result, not a global or sourced stability proof.

The curvature ratio on either stationary branch is
 R=Lambda/phi_vac^2=3M^2/(4 beta F^2)=3/(4 gamma),
 gamma=beta F^2/M^2.
U0 changes which phi_vac and H are selected, but cancels from this ratio.
Unlike the earlier neutral family at fixed action, the lower root is isolated
and restoring for fixed parameters. This is a genuine dynamical locking of
two scales under the stated action and identification.

3. Does stability choose the required value? No
The desired coefficient is equivalent to
 gamma=3/(128 pi).
The associated dimensionless round-sphere volume is
 phi_vac^4 Vol(S4)=(128/3) pi^2 gamma^2,
which equals 3/128 only at that chosen gamma.

There is an exact stable counterfamily: fix M,F and any z in (0,1), choose
ANY gamma>0, and set
 beta=gamma M^2/F^2,
 U0=[3M^4/(4 beta)] z exp(-z).
Every member satisfies the stationary equations and positive homogeneous
mass test. Gamma=3/(128 pi) gives R=32 pi; gamma=3/(64 pi) gives R=16 pi.
The dimensionless trajectory equations below do not depend on beta at fixed
z and epsilon. Consequently the sampled stability behavior does not separate
those two couplings either. This is not evidence that all gamma give viable
galactic or quantum theories; those additional obligations remain untested.

The exponential was engineered by solving the desired amplitude-insensitive
structure: for log f and a constant desired R, the vacuum equations require
 d log U/d phi=(8 beta R/(3M^2)) phi.
Integration produces an exponential in phi^2. Its exponent therefore encodes
the free ratio; its mathematical success is not independent microphysics.

4. Nonlinear homogeneous tests and propagation diagnostics
Normalize y=phi/phi_vac, h=H/H_vac, tau=H_vac t, u=dy/dtau. The exact
homogeneous equations are solved as a 2x2 linear system for u',h':
 [epsilon, -6h^2/y; 2z h^2/y, 2+4z h u/y] [u';h'] =
 [-3epsilon h u-6y exp(z(y^2-1))+6h^4/y;
  -epsilon z u^2+2z h^3 u/y+2z h^2 u^2/y^2].
Initial data u=0 and h=exp[z(y^2-1)/2] satisfy the Friedmann constraint.
This normalization avoids losing the small-z branch to underflow.

The run uses epsilon=1, z in {10^-12,.1,.5,.9,1.5}, y initially .98 or1.02,
and tau<=60. All eight lower-branch trials move toward y=h=1. At z=.9 the
slow restoring mode is not fully relaxed: final y=.9996273 or1.0005452.
The smaller z cases reach equilibrium to numerical precision. On z=1.5,
the downward perturbation approaches the other stable branch (y~.645915),
and the upward perturbation hits the declared y=3 bound at tau~15.23448.
This is growth, not a claim of a singularity after the integration stop.

At 5001 samples per trajectory the normalized tensor kinetic QT, tensor
gradient FT, scalar kinetic Ksbar and scalar speed squared remain positive.
Across the lower-branch samples the smallest scalar speed squared is about
.97705 and the smallest tensor gradient coefficient about .97060. These are
bounded sample statements. The code also stops if a coefficient crosses zero.
No matter fluid, galaxy, compact source, or primordial cosmology is included.

The initial run at rtol=10^-9 failed its fixed absolute Friedmann residual
threshold 10^-6 on the runaway branch (residual 1.76e-5 while H grew to32).
That failure and its inputs are preserved. Tightening to rtol=10^-11, without
changing the threshold, passes all ten cases (largest residual1.12e-7).
Two cases were refined to3e-12: the stable near-threshold and runaway final
values agree closely; the runaway residual falls to3.16e-8. No tolerance
failure was discarded or relabeled as a physical instability.

5. Two further selection/protection tests
Additive vacuum shifts are not canceled. Replacing U by Uexp+Delta, at a
new positive stationary point define t=Delta/Uexp. The scalar and metric
equations imply exactly
 Lambda/phi^2=[3M^2/(4 beta F^2)]/(1+t).
A positive offset changes the ratio, provided the new stationary solution
exists. Independence of multiplicative U0 is not independence of additive
vacuum energy. Importing sequestering would change these same metric equations
and must be rederived, not combined with this result as a separate guarantee.

Topological quantization does not fix beta here. Phi>0 is a noncompact
canonical scalar. Declaring log(phi/F) periodic would identify phi with a
rescaled value, but both its canonical kinetic term and U0 exp(phi^2/F^2)
fail that identification. Thus the written action has no such gauge
periodicity. Adding a compact scalar, a symmetry or an anomaly-matched action
would be a changed theory. Even an independently quantized beta would still
need to fix F^2/M^2 and the operational conversion between phi and a0.

The local-source check also remains mandatory: a vacuum constant phi solves
U'=24H^4 f', whereas a Schwarzschild-de Sitter exterior has an additional
positive Weyl contribution to E4. Its scalar residual is -beta Weyl^2/phi,
which is nonzero. The homogeneous vacuum value cannot simply be held fixed
inside a galaxy while claiming the exact old response law. Screening, force
normalization and inhomogeneous perturbations are unresolved obligations.
