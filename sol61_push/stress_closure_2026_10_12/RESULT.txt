Exact equilibrium versus dynamical support: continuation of CFG660
2026-10-12. Base commit 53e9e6e56. Analytical result; self-review only.
Sources: CFG660 README and its stated spherical equilibrium equations.
No numerical experiment or new observational verdict. No novelty claim.
Original goal, a complete physical theory: NOT ACHIEVED.

Question
Can a local two-variable stress Pi(rho_c,g) add ordinary compressive
support while retaining the exact acceleration law for arbitrary smooth
spherical baryon profiles? CFG660 leaves this question open.

Answer under the following precise assumptions: NO.
The equilibrium requirement forces the two stress eigenvalues back to
CFG660's density-independent stress on an open set of admissible states.
This is not a no-go result for gradient, thermal, elastic or rate-dependent
constitutive laws, or for approximate agreement on a restricted population.

Assumptions and notation
------------------------
r>0; inward acceleration magnitude g>0; baryonic acceleration b=B(g),
where B is a smooth local inverse of the prescribed monotone law g=F(b).
Let C=4*pi*G and A(g)=1-B_g. Restrict to an interval where A is nonzero.
Real cold mass supplies the acceleration difference through Poisson:

 C rho = (g-b)' + 2(g-b)/r = A g' + 2(g-B)/r.                 (1)

The cold medium has independent density rho and radial/tangential stresses
P(rho,g), Q(rho,g), smooth on the open state domain, with no explicit r,
gradients, baryonic density, entropy or other state variables. Equilibrium:

 dP/dr + 2(P-Q)/r = -rho*g.                                 (2)

Require (2) for every locally admissible smooth spherical baryon profile
on the branch. Densities rho>0 and rho_b=(B_g g'+2B/r)/C>0 are strict
inequalities, so admissible local derivatives can be varied in an open
neighbourhood. This is a local theorem, not a claim about every global halo.

Proof
-----
1. Differentiate (1). The coefficient of g'' in rho' is A/C.
   Equation (2) contains g'' only through P_rho*rho'. Its other terms
   depend on g,g',r and rho. The baryon density depends on g' but not g'',
   so g'' can vary freely locally while the strict density conditions hold.
   Therefore P_rho*A/C=0, hence P_rho=0 on the accessible open set.
   Write P=p(g) there. This already excludes an ordinary positive radial
   acoustic modulus dP/d rho at fixed g in this two-variable class.

2. Substitute g'=[C rho-2(g-B)/r]/A into (2):

 rho*(C p_g/A+g)
   + (2/r)*[p-Q-p_g*(g-B)/A] = 0.                           (3)

   At fixed g and rho>0, vary r in a small interval and choose g' by (1).
   Positivity remains true locally. The constant and 1/r coefficients
   in (3) must separately vanish. Thus

 p_g = -g*A/C = g*(B_g-1)/C,
 Q = p + g*(g-B)/C.                                        (4)

   Q is therefore independent of rho as well. The only residual freedom
   is a common additive constant in p and Q, which creates no force in
   the bulk and no compressive modulus. Boundary stress fixes that constant.

3. Formula (4) is exactly CFG660's stress, since B_g=1/F'(B(g)).
   The hypothesised density dependence provides no new radial support.

Checks by direct algebra (no computer algebra run)
-------------------------------------------------
Substituting (4) into (2) gives
 -g*A*g'/C - 2g*(g-B)/(C*r) = -rho*g by (1).
Deep branch B=g^2/a0 gives
 p=-g^2/(2C)+2g^3/(3C*a0)+constant,
 Q-p=g^2/C-g^3/(C*a0), reproducing CFG660's deep expressions.
Dimensions: g^2/G is pressure; C*rho has acceleration/length units.
The proof does not cover A=0, rho=0, isolated admissible states, or an
explicit coordinate dependence. Adding such cases silently would be invalid.

A different continuation survives: stress that vanishes at rest
--------------------------------------------------------------
Do not spend another run merely fitting P(rho,g) within the excluded class.
A rate-dependent correction can vanish in every static equilibrium while
altering perturbations. For example a viscous force with longitudinal
kinematic coefficient nu_v>0 adds -nu_v*k^2*u to a local velocity mode.
This is a conditional constitutive proposal, not a derived material law.

For a frozen-coefficient mode of the CFG660 type, write the undamped
dispersion in growth-rate notation as lambda^2=J+i*K*k, with real J,K
(the sign of K changes with wave direction). With this damping:

 lambda^2 + nu_v*k^2*lambda - J - i*K*k = 0.                (5)

For fixed nu_v>0 and |k| tending to infinity, its roots satisfy
 lambda_fast ~ -nu_v*k^2,
 lambda_slow = i*K/(nu_v*k) + J/(nu_v*k^2) + O(k^-4).

Unlike the undamped square-root growth, neither root has an unbounded
positive real part at high k. This is a local dispersion check only:
finite-wavelength Jeans growth may remain, and it is not a proof of
nonlinear/global well-posedness, halo formation or relativistic causality.
At k=0 the roots are finite; finite k has finite polynomial coefficients.
The limit nu_v->0 is singular; an arbitrarily small coefficient cannot
be called irrelevant to resolution or relaxation times.

Energy and remaining obligations
-------------------------------
For a Newtonian viscous stress tau=2*eta*D_dev+zeta*(div u)*I,
eta,zeta>=0, force +div tau removes mechanical energy at rate
 Q_heat=2*eta*D_dev:D_dev+zeta*(div u)^2 per volume in a closed domain.
Its opposite must appear as internal/receiver energy. This identity
does not supply a conserved energy for CFG660's field-aligned equilibrium
stress; that underlying reciprocal action/energy remains to be derived.
If accumulated heat changes the equilibrium stress, include that change.
An unmodelled fixed-temperature bath is not an allowed closure.

Next discriminating work: derive a positive dissipative response and its
receiver from the existing cold-sector degrees of freedom, with a rate
fixed by their dynamics. First establish a common energy functional for
the conservative stress; then derive transport, rather than inserting a
free viscosity. This route is OPEN but unproved. No viscosity coefficient,
stable endpoint, mass abundance or kappa=1/2 has been derived here.

Meaning
-------
The exact static relation strongly constrains constitutive freedom. A
successful dynamical completion can contain physics invisible at static
equilibrium, so matching the relation alone cannot identify that physics.
This result narrows the next calculation; it does not identify the true
nature of cold energy. CFG660's particle-realizability and instability
claims retain their stated scopes; no universal exclusion is inferred.
