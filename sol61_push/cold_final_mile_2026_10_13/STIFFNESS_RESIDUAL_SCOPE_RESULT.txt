Exact statistic being tested
Set x=r/rM, ell=Lstar/rM and U=u/a0. The zero-gradient point-baryon profile is
 U0(x)=2/[1+sqrt(1+4x^2)], with U0^2/(1-U0)=1/x^2.
For P=P(r) rhat, the radial vector Laplacian contains
 U0''+2U0'/x-2U0/x^2, including its angular-basis contribution.
From gamma=K Lstar^2/2, the relative magnitude of the OMITTED static term,
evaluated on this trial profile, is
 R(x,ell)=(ell^2/2)|U0''+2U0'/x-2U0/x^2|/(1/x^2)
         =ell^2 (1+8x^2)/(1+4x^2)^(3/2).
The displayed expression is exact; the vector Laplacian is negative for x>0.
Its derivative is
 dR/dx=4ell^2 x(1-8x^2)/(1+4x^2)^(5/2).
Thus its global maximum is at x=1/sqrt8:
 R_max=(4sqrt6/9)ell^2=1.0886621...ell^2,
with R->ell^2 at the origin and R~ell^2/x at large radius.
A sufficient ALL-RADII condition for this STATISTIC to be below1% is
 ell<sqrt[.01/(4sqrt6/9)] (about0.09584).
This is a residual criterion, not a field-solution error estimate.

Smallest missing implication
A force-equation residual evaluated on a zero-gradient trial solution does
not by itself bound the distance to the finite-gradient solution. One needs
a specified source/core/outer boundary problem and an inverse/comparison
estimate for its nonlinear operator. A static residual bound also does not
bound the perturbation operator or exclude stiffness effects on modes: those
require variation of the same occupied action and physical boundaries.
This leaves AG2's full-medium spectrum obligation open. The core/supply/
metric issues in CE053/059 are not supplied by algebraic n=1 uniqueness.
Likewise no data comparison between n=1 and n=2 is resolved by this exact
law-form derivation. The source itself records that empirical counterweight.
