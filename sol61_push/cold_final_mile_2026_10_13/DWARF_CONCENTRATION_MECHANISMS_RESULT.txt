Outcome
A candidate route survives: energy redistribution/self-interaction can put
some satellite halos in a compact phase and others in an expanded phase.
It is conditional on microphysics, initial halos, orbits, tidal survival and
settled-phase response. Stellar-density attraction alone, a common inner
fraction, or removal of outer shells alone does not supply this mechanism.
There is no new completed physical model or eleven-dwarf prediction here.

1. Stellar contraction ordering is a discriminator, not a universal no-go
From the same LVD stellar inputs, Mb/rh^3 for Draco, UMi, Fornax, LeoI is
.0837,.0381,.1155,.7602 Msun/pc^3. Fornax and LeoI also have larger Mb/rh^2
and Mb/rh than Draco and UMi. A selector that makes the required enclosed
cold fraction a monotone increasing function solely of these current stellar
proxies has the wrong ordering. This does not rule out all baryonic contraction:
different initial halo profiles, histories, aperture ratios or feedback can
change the comparison. These must be independent inputs, not inferred by
fitting the missing mass in each galaxy.

2. Torque alone is not enough: a residence-time benchmark
Consider a bound test particle in a FIXED Kepler potential, apocentre ra,
pericentre rp=q ra, and aperture R=x ra. For q<x<1,
 e=(1-q)/(1+q), u=acos[(1+q-2x)/(1-q)],
 time_fraction(<R)=(u-e sin u)/pi.
It is zero for x<=q and one for x>=1. Differentiate with respect to q at fixed
ra and x. All remaining factors are positive, so the derivative's sign is
sign[x-(x+2)q]. The maximum over all bound q is at q*=x/(x+2). Hence
 Fmax(x)=[acos(1-x-x^2)-sqrt(1-(1-x-x^2)^2)/(1+x)]/pi.
For apocentres >=10R, the fraction is at most Fmax(.1)=.01876169063.
Thus low angular momentum can allow central passages but cannot make an
extended orbital stock spend half its time inside R. Under this Kepler
benchmark at least48.66% of Draco's original assigned cold stock must have
ra<10r12 (UMi55.14%, CVnI60.85%, Sextans98.95%) to reach the prior targets.
This mixture bound uses compact orbits' maximum contribution1 and the outer
stock's contributionFmax(.1). Antlia's formal bound>1 signals the pre-existing
fixed-budget failure, not a meaningful probability.
At fixed orbital energy the semimajor axis a is fixed and a<=ra<=2a. Starting
at ra=10R, arbitrary torques at the SAME Kepler energy still leave ra>=5R;
the residence envelope there is only.05280145129. Shrinking the energy scale
requires energy redistribution or a changing potential, not just changing j.
The source MethodA has a time-dependent self-gravitating potential, unlike this
benchmark; these are NOT bounds on every orbit in the actual extended halo.
Its imposed turnaround j prescription is not a proof of small final apoapses.

Removing cold shells outside the stellar aperture changes the outer stock,
not its already enclosed mass in a spherical instantaneous truncation. Tidal
stripping needs an induced dynamical response to create higher inner mass.
It cannot be used as a bookkeeping operation that concentrates a fixed stock.

4. Tidal-loss budget must accompany any collapse claim
For a FIXED original assigned cold stock C0=53.64Mb based on these current
stellar inputs, with no replenishment, survival fraction s must satisfy
 s >= Mcold(<r12)/C0 = eta.
Thus maximum cold loss is50.38% Draco,44.02% UMi,38.42% CVnI and1.03% Sextans
at the central inputs. An80% loss history would fail those fixed budgets.
If such a history is proposed, required original-retention values would be
f<=.04031/.03573/.03248/.02021 respectively, instead of the source's.10.
This is a sensitivity/new-catchment requirement, not an authorized change of
frozen inputs. Large uncertainties, stellar stripping, replenishment or a
different initial catchment change the premise and must be tracked explicitly.
A high-density collapse argument without bound-mass survival is incomplete.

5. Avoiding overboost requires controlling the settled component too
Under CE-FM-077's fully settled retained-star baseline, nonnegative extra free
mass is possible only if the retained contribution is multiplied by lambda
<=.7660798 Fornax and<=.9170868 LeoI (canonical): at least23.39% and8.29%
suppression before any extra central reservoir is added. Alt thresholds are
.6910708 and.8240777. These are central-value necessary conditions under the
chosen Wolf/stellar model, not observational exclusions.
Keeping only the FREE stock diffuse leaves the original settled overshoot.
A self-interacting collapsed/expanded phase must therefore specify whether it
also redistributes the settled stock or whether finite stiffness suppresses
its response. The latter must come from one coupled action/bookkeeping model;
one cannot retain the unsuppressed law and remove its assigned mass separately.

A conditional screening benchmark is executable, but not a derived coupling:
if CFG691's S(2 rM r12/L^2) were to multiply the retained draw, the required
canonical L thresholds are294.693pc Fornax and64.013pc LeoI; alt341.744pc and
96.624pc. A common L>=these thresholds could remove the baseline overshoot
in that PROXY dictionary while real cold mass supplies the dense dwarfs.
The multiplication is an explicit unproved bridge. These are not fitted
physical L estimates or a new frozen verdict. The old baryons-only dwarf
upper bound is not automatically transferable to a model with real cold mass.
The next field calculation must check this dictionary and avoid counting the
screened-away draw plus unchanged free stock as additional mass: every mass
transfer must conserve the original stock.
