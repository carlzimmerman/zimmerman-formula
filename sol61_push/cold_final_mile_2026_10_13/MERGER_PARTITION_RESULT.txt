New structural result: nonnegative enclosed excess is insufficient to define
a nonnegative free carrier density. CFG726 defines E(r)=max(C(r)-T(r),0),
where C is summed progenitor enclosed mass and T the remnant tether law,
and calls F=max_r E(r) the total free mass. A real positive species requires
E'(r)=4 pi r^2 rho_free>=0 and E(infinity)=F. Neither follows from E>=0.
This is the same positivity issue previously identified in CE-FM-016/017,
now specialized to the merger-memory proposal.

Take N equal concentric progenitors, deep-law coefficient A>0, conserved
supply S>0 and remnant supply cap S. Then
 C(r)=min(sqrt(N) A r,S), T(r)=min(A r,S).
For N>1, progenitor edge rp=S/(sqrt(N) A), remnant edge rr=S/A.
For r<rp, E=(sqrt(N)-1) A r rises. At rp it reaches
 F=S(1-1/sqrt(N)), exactly the declared closed-form maximum.
But for rp<r<rr, E=S-A r and E'=-A<0. At rr, E=0.
Thus this excess cannot be the enclosed mass of a separate everywhere
positive free species while retaining T as the full remnant tether.
Example N=4,A=1,S=12: E peaks at 6 at r=6, then falls to zero at r=12.
Holding that putative F=6 at large radius alongside T=12 instead produces
18 units from the conserved supply 12. The total max(C,T)=C is itself a
positive cumulative mass in this example; its label as T plus positive free
mass fails. It may still be a phenomenological total lensing profile.
The derivative issue is not an artifact of the deep approximation: whenever
C has saturated, T is increasing and C>T on an interval, E'=-T'<0 there.
