Load-bearing assumption audit:
Collisionless Hamiltonian transport preserves f along trajectories; it does
not generally preserve Q=rho/sigma^3 or make local Q equal global f_max.
Donor rho and sigma alone do not bound fine-grained f_max. Exact mathematical
counterexample: two centered isotropic Maxwellians, cold density fraction e=.01,
sigma_cold=.001 sigma_host and warm sigma^2=(1-e*.001^2)/(1-e)*sigma_host^2.
Total density and second moment equal a single Maxwellian's, but peak f is
10,000,000.975 times higher. As the narrow width tends to0, peak f is unbounded.
This is an underdetermination counterexample, NOT a predicted cold stream,
physical production route, mass budget, capture probability or fitted rescue.

A useful corrected CONDITIONAL bound:
Assume a smooth isotropic Maxwellian donor with known bound
f_max = rho_h / (2 pi sigma_h^2)^(3/2) for ALL relevant source trajectories.
For a captured distribution 0<=f<=f_max and 3D central second moment3 sigma_c^2,
fill the lowest-velocity states at f_max to minimize that moment for fixed rho.
Let a velocity ball have radius v0 and equal mass rho=(4pi/3)f_max v0^3.
Since (v^2-v0^2)(f-f_ball)>=0 inside and outside the ball, integrating gives
int v^2 f >= int v^2 f_ball = (3/5)rho v0^2. Thus v0^2<=5 sigma_c^2 and
rho <= (4pi/3) 5^(3/2) f_max sigma_c^3
     = 2.9735401936 rho_h (sigma_c/sigma_h)^3.
This allows ANY captured velocity shape with that trace of its covariance;
Gaussianity is assumed for the donor, not the captured distribution.
