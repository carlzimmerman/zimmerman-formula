Constructive kinematic reduction (isolated deep law, fixed flat speed V):
Let canonical enclosed carrier M0=A r, A>0; beta=V/(2 pi k)>0 and z=beta t/r.
A radial orbital clock gives
 M(r,t)=A r (1-exp(-z)).
Then M_r=A D(z), D=1-(1+z)exp(-z). Since D(0)=0 and D'=z exp(-z)>0,
0<D<1 for z>0. Thus rho=M_r/(4 pi r^2) is positive. M(0,t)=0 and
 M(infinity,t)=A beta t.
Unlike uniform f(t) times an infinite deep-law tail, this clock yields finite
mass at every finite age. For large r, rho~A(beta t)^2/(8 pi r^4).
For fixed r and old t, M approaches A r. This is a useful finite formation
front with a supplied clock, not a solved carrier trajectory. The deep law
is not valid arbitrarily near the centre; a real core must be matched.

Exact production/transport obligations:
 M_t=A beta exp(-z), M_rt=A beta^2 t exp(-z)/r^2.
If formation is at rest, spherical continuity requires the positive source
 S_rho=M_rt/(4 pi r^2)=A beta^2 t exp(-z)/(4 pi r^4).
Integrating over all space gives total production A beta. Consequently a
finite reservoir S cannot sustain this profile longer than t=S/(A beta).
Production also owes rest-energy c^2 A beta, momentum exchange and field/
binding changes. The source is a derived requirement of the supplied clock,
not a microscopic production law. The limit near t=0 is nonuniform at the
origin; a physical initial core and reservoir regulate it.

If instead one tries to obtain the SAME M purely by transporting a conserved
species, with zero source and no point flux through r=0, continuity fixes
 v=-M_t/M_r=-beta exp(-z)/D(z).
At large r for fixed t, v~-2r^2/(beta t^2), unbounded. This infinite-domain
transport-only realization cannot be a timelike finite-speed carrier; it
also has incoming flux A beta at infinity. A finite outer cutoff, nonzero
source, nonzero central boundary flux or a changed profile is a different
continuation, not excluded by this comparison. Do not present this as a
universal no-go for adiabatic assembly or for finite halos.
