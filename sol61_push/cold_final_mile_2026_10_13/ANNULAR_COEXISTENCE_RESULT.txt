Construction (G=a0=Mb=1)
Choose A>=sqrt2 and finite annulus 0<r_in<=r<=r_out. Give total cold enclosed
mass Mc=A r; set
  Mt=r^2(1+A r)/(r^2+1+A r),
  Mf=r[(A^2-1)r+A]/(r^2+A r+1).
These sum to Mc, and their positive inner values specify the required core
matching. Both densities are positive because
  Mt'=[A r^4+2r(1+A r)^2]/(r^2+1+A r)^2>0,
  Mf'=(1+A r)[(A^2-2)r+A]/(r^2+A r+1)^2>0.
Their ordinary mass counted once gives g=(1+A r)/r^2. With u=Mt/r^2,
0<u<1 and the exact full residual is
  R(u)=u^2/(1-u)=(1+Mf)/r^2.
Thus the tethered population can be at rest and force free on this annulus,
including the free population's gravity. No negative-density repair is needed.

Free orbital support
For every r in the annulus, place free mass Mf'(r)dr on randomly oriented
circular orbits with uniformly distributed orbital phases. Their speed is
  v_t^2=r g=A+1/r, angular momentum squared L^2=r+A r^2.
The ensemble is spherical, has no mean streaming and is stationary in the
specified spherical total potential. It is a measure-valued phase-space state;
smooth spatial density does not make the velocity distribution smooth.
This construction is a coupled static feasibility witness: its free mass is
exactly the mass used in the tether equation, rather than an independently
added baryon-only law profile. Orbital acceleration cancels exactly.
At fixed L and frozen total potential, linear radial frequency is
  omega_r^2=(1+2A r)/r^3>0.
This does not prove coupled Vlasov/tether or nonspherical stability. Perturbing
both gravitating populations changes the potential and requires its own audit.

What this teaches about amplitude

amplitude is sqrt(1+Mf(infinity)), so it reflects the baryon PLUS free mass.
That is a diagnostic limit, not a claim of finite total cold mass: Mt grows
linearly without bound under this extension. Use a finite annulus as claimed.
