Exact finite-stock implication for a proposed rescue
For an isolated nonnegative lensing mass component with finite total mass,
 Sigma(R)>=0 and M_cyl(<R)<=M_total. Hence
 DeltaSigma(R)=M_cyl/(piR^2)-Sigma(R)<=M_total/(piR^2).
This holds regardless of the radial profile; for a stack it applies with
the SAME positive lensing weights and weighted total mass. The field-to-lensing
mass identification and an isolated component are explicit premises.
With baryonic mass Mb and a total available cold inventory <=sMb,
 DeltaSigma(R)<=(1+s)Mb/(piR^2).
Moving that same inventory from settled material into free debris cannot
exceed this ceiling. Adding debris on top of a settled profile that already
uses all sMb double counts unless additional counted stock or baryon history
is specified. Imported debris, depleted/ejected baryons, a different available
inventory, environment, or changed metric/stress lensing are different
physical assumptions requiring predictions, not algebraic exceptions.

A pointwise diagnostic is M_total>=piR^2 DeltaSigma. At the .2233Mpc row
(the outermost supplied row below.3Mpc), nominal data imply minimum total
masses in bins1–4 approximately
 [5.5374e11,1.7684e12,1.1668e12,2.7387e12] Msun.
Using the reference mean baryonic masses above and s=5.364 gives nominal
minimum/reference-stock ratios [6.3034,7.4790,3.0428,4.7186].
These are DATA-LEVEL DIAGNOSTICS, not simultaneous confidence bounds or a
new measured cold-mass claim. The JSON includes diagonal-error lower-point
diagnostics, but correlations, lens weighting, mass scatter, circumgalactic
gas and environmental contributions must be treated before statistical or
population attribution. In particular the paper's reference means need not
be exactly the same weighting as the lensing stack. This exposes the budget
needed under stated assumptions, not an unqualified universal exclusion.
