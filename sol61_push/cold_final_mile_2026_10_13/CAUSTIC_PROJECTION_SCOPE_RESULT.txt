RADIUS ALONE DOES NOT FALSIFY A LENSING REMEDY
For spherical scalar lensing,
 DeltaSigma(R)=M_cyl(<R)/(pi R^2)-Sigma(R).
A shell of mass M at a<R has Sigma(R)=0 and M_cyl(<R)=M, hence
 DeltaSigma_shell(R)=M/(pi R^2)>0.
This remains EXACT for any finite-width positive shell fully inside R.
Thus 'the 3D caustic is inside the MID band' does NOT imply 'it cannot
contribute to the MID-band lensing signal.' Enclosed mass lenses there.
The nonlocal kernel was already derived in CE064; this checkpoint applies
it to the newly reported caustic-radius argument.

Use the actual source's8 logarithmic band edges .15 to3Mpc. MID indices3–5
span 0.46129–1.41861Mpc (exact values in run results), with centers
.55628/.80895/1.17638Mpc. The source nominal .48–1.24 interval is not exactly
those bin edges; both reported caustics nevertheless lie below MID here.
For a finite layer centered at .17 OR .43Mpc with5% fractional half-width,
all its mass is interior to each MID center. Per10^13Msun its ESD is
 10.2865 /4.8642 /2.3001 Msun/pc^2.
The two caustic radii give the same exterior tail for equal enclosed mass.
A mass-conserving GEOMETRIC countercontrol moves that mass from a positive
layer at1.9–2.1Mpc into either inner layer. The MID ESD increases by
 10.2949 /4.8838 /2.3529 Msun/pc^2.
No extra stock is added. This is a counterexample to a radius-only geometric
inference, NOT an admitted formation mechanism or an empirical halo fit.
The10^13Msun normalization is illustrative, not an inferred caustic mass.

Inside a shell its ESD is negative, and near/beyond it can strongly affect
INNER bins as well. Therefore changing the distribution may worsen other
points. Actual caustic mass, width, background profile and matched baryonic
response decide that question; a radius and steepest log slope do not.
The reported half-mass radius near2Mpc also warns that a sharp inner feature
need not contain much mass. No sufficient mass or full simultaneous repair
is demonstrated by these countercontrols.
