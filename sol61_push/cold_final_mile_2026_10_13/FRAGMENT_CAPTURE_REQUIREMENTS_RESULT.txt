inside kR where R=4a/3 and k=5,10,20. Initial enclosed fraction F0=k^-3.
Assume a static density rho=3Mres/[4pi(kR)^3] over10Gyr, cold velocity dispersion
like stellar observed sigma, RMS relative speed v=sqrt(6)sigma. A successful
collision deposits one affected mass element inside R with probability1:
F(T)=F0+(1-F0)[1-exp(-tau)], tau=rho*(sigma_capture/m)*v*T.
Targets are the CE084/077 remaining-reservoir fractions required in the aperture;

2. Arbitrary common velocity kernel cannot fix the same model
For independent centered isotropic Maxwellians with one-component dispersion s,
relative-speed density is p(v|s)=v^2 exp[-v^2/(4s^2)]/(2sqrt(pi)s^3).
For sd<sf, p(v|sd)/p(v|sf) <= (sf/sd)^3 POINTWISE. Therefore for ANY shared
nonnegative velocity-only capture kernel sigma_cap(v)*v with finite integrals,
Gamma_d/Gamma_f <= (rho_d/rho_f)*(sf/sd)^3.
This analytic inequality is uniform in velocity; the sampled speed checks only
illustrate it and do not establish its universal quantifier.
Here sf=Fornax12.1km/s; sd=Draco9.55 or Sextans7.9km/s. At all common k,
Gamma_Draco/Gamma_Fornax <=2.65369 and Gamma_Sextans/Gamma_Fornax <=.270043.
At k=5 target optical depths instead require122.092 and870.680, missing by
46.01 and3224.23 factors respectively. At k=10 the factors are19.61 and1361.17;
at k=20,18.32 and1269.97. Thus even a narrow resonance in a SHARED velocity-only
capture kernel cannot supply the desired rate ratios in these Maxwellian,
common-geometry/duration reservoirs. Satisfying Fornax is already necessary
before adding LeoI constraints. No inference is made for different cold velocity
DFs, potential-dependent capture, fragment mass/phase distributions, early dense
assembly or time-dependent densities. Those hypotheses change the actual test.

3. Energy and angular momentum requirements
Fixed-Kepler circular contraction from kR to R, with benchmark GM/R=3sigma_obs^2:
Delta e=(3/2)sigma_obs^2(1-1/k); j_final/j_initial=1/sqrt(k).
At k=5 Draco must export1.09443e8J/kg (=1.21772e-9 rest energy), Sextans7.4892e7
J/kg (=8.33286e-10 rest energy), and55.28% of initial specific angular momentum.
These are conditional orbital benchmarks, not measured halo binding energies:
an extended evolving halo is not a point potential. Smallness relative to rest
mass does NOT mean smallness relative to orbital kinetic/binding energy.

A fusion event with positive rest-binding release B obeys K_final=K_initial+B-Eesc
if other storage/work channels are absent. It cools orbital motion only if
Eesc>B. Simply radiating the released binding energy does not cool the orbit.
Momentum is separate: a perfectly inelastic equal-mass merger with a stationary
bound target has center-of-mass speed v/2. With v^2=v_infinity^2+v_escape^2,
it is bound only if v_infinity<sqrt(3)*v_escape. This is an ideal local kinematic
example, not a halo capture prediction. Internal dissipation does not by itself
remove the pair's center-of-mass motion; torques/angular momentum export also
need explicit accounting.

For equal fragments at RMS relative speeds sqrt(6)sigma, pair orbital kinetic
energy/(combined rest energy)=v_rel^2/(8c^2): Draco7.61e-10, Sextans5.21e-10.
Existing ARGON11 R1 GLOBAL fractional H drift1.55e-7 is ~204/298 times those
fractions. This is a diagnostic scale comparison, NOT a rigorous numerical
error floor for a different matched-pair computation. It means existing global
conservation/coarsening output does not establish dwarf-speed orbital cooling.
A dedicated paired-baseline/flux calculation needs convergence below its actual
orbital energy difference, consistent local Hamiltonian and separation of rest
binding, center-of-mass kinetic energy, internal excitation and outgoing flux.
The snapshot-gradient energy mismatch from CE083 must not be reused as radiation.
