1. Inertia is fixed by the carrier current
For radial enclosed physical carrier mass m(r,t), continuity gives
  rho=m_r/(4pi r^2), j=-m_t/(4pi r^2), v=j/rho=-m_t/m_r.
Ordinary nonrelativistic rest-mass kinetic energy therefore is
  K=integral m_t^2/(2m_r)dr.
This is a consequence of the specified physical current, not an independently
chosen constant I times Pdot squared. The timelike-current domain of the
relativistic comparison further requires |m_t|<c m_r where rho>0.
This does not repair CE-FM-013's freely varied reference-current obstruction;
the current identifies kinematics/inertia, not its full covariant dynamics.

About an at-rest positive-density interior equilibrium m0, put eta=m-m0.
To quadratic order,
  K2=1/2 integral eta_t^2/m0' dr;
  E2=1/2 integral R'(u0)eta^2/r^2 dr
      +beta/2 integral[r^2((eta/r^2)')^2+2(eta/r^2)^2]dr,
  u0=m0/r^2, R'=u0(2-u0)/(1-u0)^2, beta=gamma/(4piG).
Units G=a0=1 in this displayed effective radial energy. The inertia and
stiffness quadratic forms are positive in the interior. In a locally frozen
short-wavelength patch, the principal dispersion is
  omega^2=omega0^2+c_s^2 k^2,
  omega0^2=m0' R'(u0)/r^2, c_s^2=beta m0'/r^2.
Background-gradient terms are not included in this frozen dispersion. It is
not a full global mode spectrum, stability theorem, radiation power or rate.

2. Finite carrier support is not independent-P vacuum propagation
In vacuum m_r=0. An actual current with no density cannot carry finite j:
timelike current and finite kinetic energy force m_t=0. Thus a changing
radial carrier-displacement field cannot transmit this carrier wave through
the empty exterior while its mass remains fixed. The static tail m=S has
u=S/r^2 and can store gradient energy; that does not supply a vacuum wave
inertia or an outgoing carrier-radiation channel.
Moving support, expelled particles, singular mass surfaces, a nonzero ambient
carrier bath or genuinely independent field degrees of freedom are different
possibilities. This statement concerns linear finite-current vacuum, not a
universal no-export theorem. Actual natural interface conditions must be
derived from a reciprocal parent rather than imposing a reflecting boundary.

3. Alternative: energy can leave with escaping carrier mass
For zero stiffness and a frozen external point mass Q, let total tethered
supply be S and Re=sqrt(S+S^2/Q). A small test shell outside the ground-state
edge sees inward effective force Q/r^2-R(S/r^2). Its escape work is
  B(S,Q)=integral_Re^infinity[Q/r^2-S^2/(r^2(r^2-S))]dr
        =(Q+S)/Re-sqrt(S) atanh(sqrt(S)/Re)>0.
The integral uses the actual outward tether; ordinary Newtonian gravity
alone would overestimate this conditional binding. In the deep large-S/Q
regime, B~(2/3)Q^(3/2)/S. This is a test-shell work in a frozen background,
not a formula for a finite ejection event with unchanging fields.

Differentiate the minimized effective energy with respect to supply. Interior
Euler derivative vanishes, the cap region has dm_star/dS1, and the boundary
term vanishes because the capped/unconstrained energies agree atRe:
  dE_min/dS=integral_Re^infinity[R(S/r^2)-Q/r^2]dr=-B(S,Q).
For a FINITE loss d retaining an admissible matched core, the exact change
between the two equilibrium minima is therefore
  E_min(S-d)-E_min(S)=integral_(S-d)^S B(s,Q)ds>0.
With initial excess Eexc, final excess Erem and escaping kinetic energy
Kinf (all measured relative to the corresponding equilibrium), conservation
requires Eexc=integral_(S-d)^S B ds+Erem+Kinf in this fixed-external-source
model. Losing mass changes the minimum and COSTS binding energy; it is not
automatically removal of excess by the escaped packet's initial kinetic energy.
