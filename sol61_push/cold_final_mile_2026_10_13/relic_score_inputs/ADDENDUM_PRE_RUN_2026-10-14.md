# CFG777 pre-run addendum (2026-10-14, after CFG776's D1 tables, before any CFG777 run)

CFG777's rule assumes the relic is frozen (f = 1) in clusters. The justification given was that cluster clocks are ≲ 30–40 t0,
earlier than CFG747/776's onset of expulsion.

**CFG776's own D1 tables contradict that.**
- By t = 50 t0 the relic is already strongly redistributed, at every peak factor. For p = 1.5 the relic fraction is
  +0.23 / −0.03 / −0.90 / −0.84 / +0.79 at r = 2 / 5 / 8 / 11 / 25 r_M.
- By t = 100 the inner halo (≤ 8 r_M) is back on the law.
- The expulsion overshoots into a deficit that persists to 3000 t0.

So f = 1 is not supported by the framework's own dynamics, even for clusters.

**What this changes.** CFG777 still runs as frozen when T1x finishes; it is cheap and runs overnight. Its result is reported
only as a **phenomenological upper bound**: "a static extra tethered phantom of the peak condensed mass". It is NOT a test of
the framework's dynamics. A PASS would not rescue H-FR; a FAIL closes the static reading too.

**Lesson recorded (CFG776).** Within the record's collisionless tether dynamics (CFG747), cold energy in excess of the law is
expelled within ~50–100 t0 and overshoots. Any persistent core excess must therefore live in a component that does not feel
the cubic push (an untethered component, as in H-HT), in uncounted baryons, or in a change to the law. It cannot be surplus
tethered cold energy.
