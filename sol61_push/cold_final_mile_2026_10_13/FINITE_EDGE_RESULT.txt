2. Effective energy, assumptions and theorem
In G=a0=1 units take the conditional effective radial energy, after exact
tethered-self-gravity quadratic cancellation,
  E[m]=integral_0^infinity [r^2 w(m/r^2)-Q(r)m/r^2]dr,
  w(u)=-u^2/2-u-log(1-u), 0<=u<1,
  Q(r)=Mb(r)+Mf(r), prescribed nonnegative nondecreasing external mass.
The external gravitational energy is integral Phi_ext dm. Integrating by parts
with Phi_ext(infinity)=0 gives -integral Q m/r^2 dr; endpoint constants do not
affect variations. This counts the tethered mass once, uses residual w rather
than full W, and keeps free gravity in Q. Q's own distribution and orbital
energy are held fixed in this variation, not claimed to be selected by it.
For a rigorous conditional statement, require admissible finite-energy m with
fixed total S, m'>=0, 0<=m<=S, u<1; Q has finite positive total. On a matched
annulus the prescribed inner mass is the unconstrained root at its inner end.
The point-source center can instead be treated as a formal limit; no physical
regularity or quantum admissibility at a point mass is asserted.

For each r define the positive unconstrained root
  m0^2=Q(r)[r^2-m0],
  m0=[sqrt(Q^2+4Q r^2)-Q]/2.
Where Q>0 it satisfies 0<m0<r^2 and
  m0'=[Q'(r)(r^2-m0)+2rQ]/[2m0+Q]>=0.
Its outer growth is unbounded for finite positive Q(infinity). Thus
  m_star(r)=min(m0(r),S)
is admissible, reaches a finite edge Re, and has exactly the supplied total.
At fixed r the integrand e_r(m) is strictly convex for m>0:
  de_r/dm=R(m/r^2)-Q/r^2,
  d2e_r/dm2=R'(m/r^2)/r^2,
  R(u)=u^2/(1-u), R'(u)=u(2-u)/(1-u)^2>0.
Below the edge, derivative at m_star is zero. Above the edge, m_star=S<m0,
so derivative is negative and every admissible test m obeys m-S<=0.
Consequently de_r(m_star)(m-m_star)>=0 everywhere. Convexity gives
  e_r(m)-e_r(m_star)>=de_r(m_star)(m-m_star)>=0.
Integrate to prove E[m]>=E[m_star]. Strict convexity gives uniqueness up to
sets of zero radial measure within this admissible class. This proof does not
come from optimizer success and does not assume global unconstrained P EL.

For external point mass Q the edge is exactly
  Re^2=S+S^2/Q in these units,
  Re^2=(G/a0)(S+S^2/Q) when S,Q denote dimensional masses.
Q includes free cold mass when it lies wholly inside the edge. For distributed

gravity is allowed. Q=Mb alone recovers the previous conditional finite-supply
radius. It is not a new observation, new mass origin or selected cosmic supply.

Beyond Re the virtual test-carrier acceleration -Q/r^2+R(S/r^2) is inward.
The support ends without a reflecting wall or negative-density compensating

3. Existing stiffness term, finite numerical evidence
With beta=gamma/(4piG), the radial vector gradient contribution, in the same
normalization, is
  E_beta=E+beta/2 integral[r^2(u')^2+2u^2]dr, u=m/r^2.
Varying in a region with positive carrier density yields
  R(u)-Q/r^2-beta[u''+2u'/r-2u/r^2]=0.
Mass positivity and supply constraints remain at vacuum/support boundaries;
they cannot be dropped by solving this differential equation everywhere.
The gradient term is a positive quadratic and preserves convexity in m.

stiffness energy beyond Rout is beta S^2/Rout^3, a fixed endpoint constant.
This identifies the existing field stiffness term in the conditional radial
carrier energy; it does not derive the microscopic current/reference parent.
