The new information is constructive: CE-FM-037's positive orbital mass front
can be generated locally by two successive conversions with equal prescribed
rates. It need not be interpreted as production of rest mass from nothing.
This changes the next question to physical state identity and reciprocal
conversion dynamics, without answering either by introducing state labels.

At each radius let a fixed available density rho0=A/(4 pi r^2) be divided into
reservoir R, intermediate I and cold C. Take gamma=beta/r with beta>0 and
 Rdot=-gamma R, Idot=gamma(R-I), Cdot=gamma I,
initially (R,I,C)=(rho0,0,0). Define z=gamma t. Then
 R/rho0=exp(-z), I/rho0=z exp(-z),
 C/rho0=1-(1+z)exp(-z).
All fractions are nonnegative and sum to one. The cold fraction rises because
its z derivative is z exp(-z)>0. Differentiation verifies all three equations.
Integrating the cold density gives exactly
 Mc(r,t)=A r[1-exp(-beta t/r)],
because d/dr of the right side is A[1-(1+z)exp(-z)] and its inner limit is zero.
Thus Claude's exponential ENCLOSED clock admits a delayed, two-stage LOCAL
clock. It is not the one-stage local density reaction Cdot=gamma(rho0-C).
Equal rates are an additional premise: unequal rates give
 C/rho0=1-[gamma2 exp(-gamma1 t)-gamma1 exp(-gamma2 t)]/(gamma2-gamma1).
The limit gamma2->gamma1 recovers the stated two-stage expression. Neither
an orbital dimensional scale nor this algebra enforces that equality.

Supply ledger and physical caveats:
Truncate rho0 at radius Rmax. Its total mass is S=A Rmax, independent of time;
inside the cutoff the same cold profile holds. Total cold mass becomes
 Mc(t)=S[1-exp(-beta t/Rmax)],
with rate A beta exp(-beta t/Rmax), tending to S rather than consuming an
unbounded external supply. Reservoir and intermediate hold precisely the
remaining S-Mc. They must contribute their actual stress/energy and, if
massive, gravity BEFORE conversion. A state relabel cannot make their mass
invisible or automatically induce the intended tether force. This repairs
only the supply accounting, not formation from baryons or vacuum.
The ideal r=0 density and gamma are singular, and an abrupt exterior boundary
is imposed, not dynamically derived. A finite core changes the exact enclosed
exponential. For any finite integrable rho0 and bounded nonnegative gamma(r),
the same local solutions still give nonnegative populations and conserved
supply, but require computing the new enclosed profile before scoring data.

Why the local/enclosed distinction matters:
A one-stage local rule instead gives dMc/dr=A[1-exp(-beta t/r)]. At large r
this is A beta t/r+O(r^-2), causing logarithmically divergent cold mass for
any t>0 on an untruncated deep profile. The two-stage cold radial density is
A(beta t)^2/(2 r^2)+O(r^-3), so its cold total is finite. However its available
reservoir has infinite mass and its intermediate radial density ~A beta t/r
has infinite total on that infinite domain. A finite cutoff is therefore
needed for the entire population ledger even though the cold front is finite.
These are asymptotic integral arguments, not finite-grid conclusions.
