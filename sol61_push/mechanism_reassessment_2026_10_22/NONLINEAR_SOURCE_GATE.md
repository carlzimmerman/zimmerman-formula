# The large-source instability need not immediately empty the reservoir

This is an algebraic follow-up to the quantum-source estimate, not a completed nonlinear formation calculation. It uses the same carrier potential and keeps the two distinct claims separate: dilute decay is invalid for the estimated inflationary displacement, but that does not mean the first instability destroys most of the source energy.

At fixed A=|X|, choose the Phi phase minimizing the cubic term. With y=|Phi|²,

    V(A,y)=M² A²+(m²-2gA)y+lambda y².

For A<=A_c=m²/(2g), the minimum is y=0. For A>A_c,

    y_min=(2gA-m²)/(2 lambda),
    V_min(A)=M² A²-(2gA-m²)²/(4 lambda).

The maximum potential-energy release in a relaxation at fixed X, relative to the initially empty-Phi source potential, is therefore

    Delta V/(M² A²)
       = [g²/(lambda M²)](1-A_c/A)²
       < Delta/[M²(1+r)]
       = u_*²/[(1+u_*²)(1+r)],

where lambda=(1+r)g²/Delta. For the existing u_*=0.02, r=2 benchmark the upper value is approximately 1.3328e-4. Thus even a strongly tachyonic daughter Hessian releases less than 0.014% of the source's potential in this particular fixed-source minimization. The source energy scale and the daughter instability scale are different quantities.

The remaining quadratic coefficient in V_min(A) is

    M²-g²/lambda = 4 m²[1+u_*² r/(1+r)] > 4m².

This combination is already identified in ../nonthermal_state_2026_10_19/RESULT.txt as four times the coherent-branch chemical-potential cap and as the tree-level scattering-zero invariant. The present use connects that existing combination to the potential valley of a large neutral source; it is not a newly discovered coefficient or a proof of novelty.

Minimizing the potential does not eliminate the Phi kinetic energy, its gradients or the induced kinetic term along the valley. The displayed coefficient must not be promoted to an exact oscillation frequency. Repeated source oscillations, parametric amplification, neutral annihilation, expansion, and backreaction can transfer far more energy than a single fixed-X relaxation. A pre-existing inflationary Phi population can also block or modify the instability. This is not a coldness theorem.

The useful next calculation is now specific: initialize both fields from their quantum inflationary fluctuations, evolve their coupled expansion-dependent dynamics and fluctuation modes, and measure how the reservoir drains, whether opposite charges annihilate, and the final physical momentum distribution. A credible source mechanism must pass that calculation and the abundance test together. Treating the large source as an already cold dilute gas would skip precisely the unresolved step.

Coldness at galaxy formation need not mean coldness at the instant of conversion. For decoupled particles produced during an entropy-conserving radiation history,

    (p/m)_later = (p/m)_prod (T_later/T_prod)
                              [g*s_later/g*s_prod]^(1/3).

Early nonlinear heating is therefore not by itself a rejection of the source route. The relevant tests are surviving abundance and the integrated free-streaming/perturbation evolution of the actual spectrum, including subsequent interactions. A small late velocity alone is also insufficient to replace that integrated test. The previous near-threshold decay construction is one way to obtain cold material, not a necessary condition for every viable primordial source.
