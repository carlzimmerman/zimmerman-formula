# What would have to happen physically

This is a hypothetical particle mechanism, not an observed property of cold
energy. It requires adding a new interaction to the existing project model.

1. The cold component would contain a particle chi that carries hidden
   baryon number—the conserved bookkeeping quantity that a neutron can inherit.
2. A neutron portal would let chi transfer that quantity into an ordinary
   neutron. A possible microscopic parent uses heavy quark-coupled mediators.
3. The dark charge has to go somewhere. There are three explicit options:

| Input | Output | What enables it |
|---|---|---|
| chi + oppositely charged dark partner | neutron + photon | Opposite dark charges cancel; excess rest energy leaves as light |
| chi + energetic photon | neutron + charged dark remnant | Photon supplies enough energy; remnant retains dark charge |
| chi + proton | deuteron + charged dark remnant | Nuclear binding makes the final state energetically cheaper |

In one illustrative mass assignment, chi is 0.5 MeV lighter than a neutron
and the dark scalar is 1 MeV. A free chi cannot decay through these channels
in vacuum. A photon above 1.501198 MeV can activate neutron production. A
proton target can instead capture the new neutron into deuterium, releasing
0.724566 MeV. Or an opposite-charge dark partner permits a neutron plus a
0.499867-MeV photon, with no dark remnant in that reaction.

These numbers follow from assigned particle masses; they are not measured
dark-particle properties. The conversion rates depend on a new portal coupling.
Our illustrative heavy mediators give extremely small elementary rates; the
nuclear capture rate has not been matched. This is no practical converter.

The major unresolved question is whether the project's cold component really
contains such particles, charges and hidden baryon number. The earlier neutral
curvature field has not been connected to them. An initially symmetric dark
population would also make equal baryon/antibaryon contributions.
