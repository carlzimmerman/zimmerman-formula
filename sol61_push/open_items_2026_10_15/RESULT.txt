B. Ellipticals and clusters: a necessary transfer equation
Sources: THEORY_CANONICAL_2026-10-14.md S2-S4; THEORY_PUSH_2026-10-14/B_condensed_rule.md
and C_early_types.md; sol61_push/cold_baryon_selection_2026_10_14/RESULT.txt.
Use a local nonexpanding patch, mass-transfer rate S>=0, and mass currents:
  partial_t rho_t + div j_t = -S,
  partial_t rho_f + div j_f = +S.
Total mass is conserved. But the old identities
  div P_t = rho_t - rhobar_t,     partial_t P_t = -j_t
cannot both survive with fixed rhobar_t and nonzero S. Differentiating gives
  div(partial_t P_t + j_t) = -S - partial_t rhobar_t.
Thus a completed model needs J_conversion in
  partial_t P_t = -j_t + J_conversion,
  div J_conversion = -S - partial_t rhobar_t.
On a periodic box, compatibility requires partial_t rhobar_t=-<S>; then the
source is -(S-<S>). With fixed reference density and a closed boundary,
positive net conversion is incompatible with zero boundary flux. This does
not prohibit two populations; it identifies the missing transfer structure.
A longitudinal Poisson solution can enforce this identity, but does not by
itself provide a causal, reciprocal action or energy balance.

Conversion also changes the polarization energy. At fixed a0, gamma, and
spatial boundaries, its contribution includes
  dE_pol/dt|conversion = integral (delta E_pol/delta P_t).J_conversion dV.
The gravitational field and particle terms must be varied consistently too.
An instantaneous relabeling that deletes tether energy without accounting
for it is not an energy-conserving derivation. A kinetic implementation needs
mass-, momentum- and total-energy-consistent conversion operators, not only
a positive-energy flag.

Second obligation: detachment and retention differ. In a chosen common frame,
with potential zero at escape, the desired particles must obey simultaneously
  v^2/2 + Phi_t > 0,   v^2/2 + Phi_total < 0.
The allowed velocity window exists only where Phi_total<Phi_t, and its
occupied fraction depends on the distribution function. For a truncated
flat-speed halo of edge Re and speed V, Phi_total=-V^2[1+ln(Re/r)] inside,
including its exterior point-mass contribution. Retention requires
  v^2 < 2 V^2[1+ln(Re/r)].
This demonstrates a possible window in a prescribed potential, NOT a retained
fraction for a live merger. The potential itself changes during conversion.
A claim that all freed material stays is not implied by E_t>0.

Third obligation: equilibrium is not selection. For isotropic constant
sigma_f in a flat-speed region, the Jeans equation gives
  rho_f proportional to r^(-V_c^2/sigma_f^2).
The r^-2 profile requires sigma_f^2=V_c^2/2; its normalization and cutoff are
still independent data. Calling the profile isothermal does not derive these.
This matters to both core problems: adequate total supply does not locate mass
inside the measured apertures or prevent excessive mass farther out.

  W_C(u)=a^2/K [C-u/a-ln(1-u/a)].

  g=K W'_C=au/(a-u),   g_N=g-u=g^2/(a+g).

D. The 5.4: conserved-ratio freedom and the needed production equation
For separately conserved pressureless baryons and cold mass in FLRW,
  dot rho_b+3H rho_b=0,   dot rho_c+3H rho_c=0,
so R=rho_c/rho_b is an arbitrary integration constant. The Friedmann equation
can constrain total density but does not split two fluids with identical
background dilution. This establishes non-selection in that class only.
For baryons conserved and cold-sector production/exchange Q,
  dot rho_c+3H rho_c=Q,  dot R=Q/rho_b,
  R(t)=R(t_i)+integral[Q/rho_b]dt.
Thus selection needs both initial conditions and a physically normalized Q,
or an attractor demonstrably erasing initial dependence. Writing
Q=Gamma rho_b(R_star-R) gives an attractor but merely inserts R_star=5.364.
For baryon-to-cold conversion instead, dot rho_b+3H rho_b=-Q and the ratio
obeys dot R=Q(1+R)/rho_b; the previous formula must not be reused. For
cold-to-DE conversion Q<0, the present ratio is history-dependent, not a
universal consequence of the local galaxy law. No microscopic abundance
calculation selecting 5.364 was supplied by the inspected canonical model.
