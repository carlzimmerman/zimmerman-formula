1. Exact orbital invariant, including self-gravity

Use mass phase-space density f, static baryon potential Phi_b, isolated
Newtonian gravity Delta Phi_f=4 pi G int f d^3v, and no boundary flux.

 H_c[f]=int f (v^2/2+Phi_b) d^3x d^3v
                       +1/2 int rho_f Phi_f d^3x,
 C[f]=int C_0(f) d^3x d^3v,
 A[f]=H_c[f]+C[f]-mu int f d^3x d^3v.

Vlasov-Poisson preserves H_c, every sufficiently regular finite C, and mass.
Therefore dA/dt=0 under the FULL isolated orbital flow, even away from the
target. The spatially local density-deficit functional lacks this property.
Here mu is a mass-constraint multiplier, not a new force coefficient.

Suppose a self-consistent target has f_*=F(E_*), E_*=v^2/2+Phi_b+Phi_*, with
F strictly decreasing on its occupied energy range. Define on its image

 C_0'(f)=mu-F^{-1}(f),   C_0''(f)=-1/F'(F^{-1}(f))>0.

Then delta A/delta f=E_f+C_0'(f)-mu vanishes at f_* on its positive support.
The vacuum region also needs the appropriate nonnegativity/KKT inequality;
the displayed interior equation alone does not certify a constrained minimum.
In spherical dynamics one can conditionally use C_0(f,L), because each
particle's angular momentum is conserved. That extension is not a Casimir
for arbitrary nonspherical dynamics. The required inverse is at fixed L.

This inverse construction encodes a chosen distribution. It neither derives
the distribution from density alone nor makes all systems share one C_0.
A finite nonnegative orbit-weight solution does not prove F_E<0 or supply a
smooth inverse. The current CFG626 existence checks cannot substitute for it.

2. Two traps: stability and GENERIC degeneracy

For equal-mass perturbations supported where the interior target relation
holds, integration by parts gives exactly

 A[f]-A[f_*]
 =int [C_0(f)-C_0(f_*)-C_0'(f_*)(f-f_*)] d^3x d^3v
             -1/(8 pi G) int |grad(Phi_f-Phi_*)|^2 d^3x.

The first term is nonnegative if C_0 is convex. The gravitational term is
NEGATIVE. A coercivity/minimization argument on the admitted perturbations
is still required; convex C_0 alone is not a proof of halo stability.
New support outside the occupied target requires the additional KKT terms.

Moreover, dA/dt=0 along its Hamiltonian flow is weaker than L delta A=0.
For the Vlasov Poisson operator L, L delta C=0 and L delta mass=0, but

 L delta A = L delta H_c,

which is generally the nonzero orbital evolution vector. Thus choosing
S=-A/Theta does NOT satisfy the GENERIC entropy degeneracy L delta S=0.
Do not promote this repair to a proof that CFG542's full GENERIC gap is closed.
With reversible energy exchange to a wave receiver even the separate H_c
conservation used in section 1 need not persist.

3. A cheap admission test at a free outer edge

Consider an isotropic target with only energies E<E_cut and a free outer
radius R where Phi_tot(R)=E_cut. Let Psi=E_cut-Phi_tot(r)>0 inside. Then

 rho(r)=4 pi sqrt(2) int_0^Psi F(E_cut-epsilon)
                                      sqrt(Psi-epsilon) d epsilon.

For a nonnegative decreasing F(E), choose any occupied reference E_ref<E_cut
where F(E_ref) is finite. Near the edge, E_ref<E_cut-Psi, so
 F(E_cut-epsilon)<=F(E_ref) throughout the integral. Hence

 0<=rho(r)<= (8 pi sqrt(2)/3) F(E_ref) Psi^(3/2) -> 0.

Therefore this regular, decreasing, isotropic free-edge class cannot represent
a density with a finite nonzero inward limit followed by a sharp jump to zero.
This is an exact restricted obstruction, not a failure of all orbit-supported
targets. A reflecting outer wall, anisotropic angular-momentum-dependent
cutoffs, or distribution-valued populations fall outside this proof. No wall
may be silently interpreted as physical confinement. A merely narrow smooth
edge also is not an exact density jump.

The current record describes sharp supply-limited profiles. Before importing
an isotropic energy-Casimir selector, the actual continuum target and boundary
condition must pass this test. CFG626's finite approximately isotropic support
does not itself certify these continuum conditions. No new finite edge test
was run here and no CFG626 result is relabelled.

6a. Exact density integral and a nonempty singular edge family

Let R be a free outer radius, L=|x cross v|, and
 V_eff(r,L)=Phi(r)+L^2/(2r^2), E_R(L)=V_eff(R,L).
Restrict L to a compact interval I strictly below L_c=sqrt(R^3 Phi'(R)),
with positive turning-point slope a(L)=Phi'(R)-L^2/R^3. Assume smooth Phi
and nondegenerate inner turning points so the radial periods stay finite.
Then, writing x=R-r,

 delta(r,L)=E_R(L)-V_eff(r,L)=a(L)x+O(x^2)>0.

For an ordinary anisotropic distribution supported at E<E_R(L), its density is

 rho(r)=(4 pi/r^2) int L dL int_[V_eff(r,L)]^[E_R(L)]
                            f(E,L) / sqrt(2[E-V_eff(r,L)]) dE.

Take, locally near the upper-energy boundary on I,

 f(E,L)=A(L)[E_R(L)-E]^(-p),  A(L)>=0, p<1.

For a pure power on that interval the inner integral is exactly

 (A(L)/sqrt(2)) delta^(1/2-p) Beta(1-p,1/2).

Consequences for this family, with regular L integration:
 p<1/2: the density contribution vanishes at R;
 p=1/2: a finite, nonzero edge density is possible;
 1/2<p<1: the density contribution diverges at R.

At p=1/2 specifically,

 rho(R-)=(2 sqrt(2) pi^2/R^2) int_I L A(L) dL.

This is a local edge construction, not a reconstruction of the entire law's
density or a proof that the computed CFG620 weights converge to this family.
It proves that a finite-density jump need not require a physical outer wall
or a delta-function distribution. The energy singularity is integrable.

The velocity-moment check is equally explicit. At fixed L, the ratio of the
radial-pressure integral to the density integral is

 <v_r^2>_L=delta/(3/2-p).

At p=1/2 it is delta, so radial pressure vanishes linearly at the edge while
tangential speeds tend to L/R. This is compatible with strong tangential
support there. It is not an isotropic population at the boundary.

6b. Why this does not rescue the proposed convex selector

For p=1/2 with A(L)>0,

 partial_E f=+(A(L)/2)[E_R(L)-E]^(-3/2)>0.

At fixed L, the inverse construction of section 1 would therefore give
C_0,ff=-1/(partial_E f)<0 on that branch. The proposed CONVEX energy-Casimir
selector is unavailable for this edge family. This sign does not prove a
physical instability; it invalidates that sufficient variational route.

It also does not rule out maximum entropy at fixed spatial-bin masses:
with finite radial period and phase measure 8 pi^2 L T_r dE dL,
the singular contribution behaves as int_0^eta q^(-1/2)|log q| dq, which is
finite. Therefore finite discrete entropy or finite orbit mass cannot certify
bounded continuum f. The variational constraint in CFG620 is not the same
as minimizing energy plus a convex Casimir.

7. FOLLOW-UP: LOCAL DRIFT-DIFFUSION LAW REQUIRED BY THAT EDGE

Base 369eef6dff631fb239a679512009c550797c7e9d. Previous turn: progress.
This section derives an orbit-averaged local kinetic compatibility condition.
It does not identify a microscopic noise source or a complete physical law.

Hold the spherical potential, outer radius and angular momentum L fixed.
Set q=E_R(L)-E>=0. The density with respect to dq dL is not f itself:

 n(q,L)=8 pi^2 L T_r(E_R-q,L) f(E_R-q,L).

For the edge family in section 6,
n_*(q,L)=K(L) T_r(q,L) q^(-1/2). Assume T_r positive and continuously
differentiable near q=0 on a nondegenerate L interval. This regularity is an
additional condition, not a claim covering nearly circular or separatrix orbits.

Take an Ito diffusion at fixed L,
 dq=A(q,L) dt+sqrt(2D(q,L)) dW,
 partial_t n=-partial_q J,  J=A n-partial_q(D n).

Zero stationary flux requires EXACTLY, wherever n_*>0,

 A=D'+D partial_q log n_*
  =D'-D/(2q)+D partial_q log T_r.                     (7.1)

Consequences:
 - If D tends to D0>0 and D' remains finite, A must diverge as -D0/(2q).
   A bounded drift and nonzero edge diffusion cannot maintain this edge.
 - If D=d(L) q, d(L)>0, then
   A=d(L)/2+d(L)q partial_q log T_r,
   which is finite at the edge. Degenerate diffusion avoids a divergent drift.
 - More generally D=d q^s gives the leading pair
   A=d(s-1/2)q^(s-1), subject to the stated smooth period assumption.
   These are compatibility relations, not a derivation of d or s.

7a. An explicit regular edge process, with its limits

For constant T_r the choice D=d q, A=d/2 has the local equation

 dq=(d/2)dt+sqrt(2d q)dW.                            (7.2)

It has a nonnegative realization: let u be ordinary Brownian motion scaled
as du=sqrt(d/2)dB and put q=u^2. Ito's rule gives
dq=(d/2)dt+sqrt(2d)u dB; changing the Brownian sign on u<0 gives (7.2).
Thus the q=0 edge can be attained and left without extending to q<0.
This specifies the nonabsorbing squared-Brownian extension. Absorption at
zero would be a different boundary model and would not give the same law.

For an illustrative FINITE interval only, reflecting u at +/-sqrt(Q) yields
a uniform stationary u density and hence n_*(q)=1/(2 sqrt(Qq)), 0<q<Q.
The q process inherits a reflection term at Q. Without this upper confinement,
q^(-1/2) is not normalizable on the half-line and is not a global equilibrium.
Neither Q nor the reflecting upper boundary has been derived for a halo.
This finite-interval example demonstrates mathematical realizability only.

The factor 1/2 in (7.2) comes from the Ito transformation and the prescribed
edge exponent. It is NOT a derivation of the formula's kappa=1/2.

For general smooth T_r and a specified normalizable n_* on a finite domain,
(7.1) rewrites the current as

 J=-D n_* partial_q(n/n_*).

Under boundary conditions that remove the entropy boundary term,

 d/dt int n log(n/n_*) dq
       =-int D n [partial_q log(n/n_*)]^2 dq <=0.

The vanishing of boundary terms needs checking for the chosen singular
domain; it is not implied by merely writing a formal zero-flux condition.
The reflected-u example supplies one concrete boundary realization.

7b. What remains physical rather than algebraic

Equation (7.2) is a candidate LOCAL edge dynamics, not a parameter-free halo
law. Choosing q=u^2 manufactures the desired Jacobian unless u has an
independent physical identification and dynamics. A coordinate substitution
does not produce microscopic fluctuations or explain their strength.

At fixed potential and L, dE=-dq. Away from boundary reflection the mean
orbital energy change from (7.2) is -d/2 per unit time in specific-energy
units. The variance coefficient is 2d q. That energy must be balanced by a
physical receiver. A stochastic equation alone supplies no such receiver,
rest-energy accounting, angular torque model or equilibrium import capacity.
In a forming halo E_R and Phi change; dropping their time derivatives would
invalidate this energy ledger. Fixed L also cannot repair a mismatched total
angular-momentum distribution in the initial material cohort.

All positive rescalings of d leave the stationary exponent unchanged while
changing the time scale. The global density and distribution across L are
also unselected. Those facts prevent reporting this as the requested law.

Analytic controls: constant D and A=0 give n_*=constant (not the required
edge); D=dq and A=0 give n_* proportional to 1/q (nonnormalizable at zero).
The d/2 drift is essential for the integrable exponent within this class.
