DYNAMICAL MEDIUM TEST: A FINITE KINETIC-STABILITY WINDOW

Parent: puzzle_32pi_density_micro_2026_10_14/RESULT.txt.

Result: allowing the proposed stabilizing medium to fluctuate gives a stronger
condition than holding it fixed. The explicit coupled model below has positive
quadratic energy only in a finite gradient interval. Both small-gradient and
large-gradient limits fail for fixed finite coefficients. A finite window can
be useful for a halo, but it does not establish a complete theory or select
32 pi. This is a phenomenological scalar-elastic-medium test, not a derived
microscopic normal fluid and not a novelty claim.

1. Fully declared toy action

Use a phase theta and a displacement vector xi for a dynamical elastic medium.
In a reference rest frame let
 X=theta_dot-|grad theta|^2/(2m),
 Y=theta_dot+xi_dot dot grad theta,
 L=P_-(X)+A Y^2/2+rho |xi_dot|^2/2
       -rho c_N^2 sum_ij (partial_i xi_j)^2/2 - q rho_b theta - E0,
 P_-(X)=2 X sqrt(-X)/(3sqrt(g)), X<0.
m,g,A,rho,c_N,q are positive. rho is a medium inertia coefficient, rho_b the
external baryonic mass density. This is an explicitly chosen nonrelativistic
elastic action. It is not asserted to have full Galilean invariance, the
thermodynamics of a Landau two-fluid system, or a covariant gravitational
completion. In particular P_- is the phenomenological branch whose simple
one-density equilibrium derivation failed in the parent.

Choose a constant-gradient static background theta=v x, xi=0, v>0. Then
X0=-v^2/(2m), Y0=0. This is a local vacuum patch, not a global source solution.
Put b=sqrt(m/(2g)), B=-P_-''=b/v, n=P_-'=b v/m.
For perturbations pi=delta theta and eta=delta xi,
 L2= -B(pi_dot-v partial_x pi/m)^2/2 - n|grad pi|^2/(2m)
     +A(pi_dot+v eta_dot_x)^2/2+rho |eta_dot|^2/2
     -rho c_N^2 sum_ij(partial_i eta_j)^2/2.
The source is linear and contributes no quadratic coefficients in this local
test. The Hessian of this displayed L2 is checked directly by check.py.

2. Coupled kinetic matrix

The two velocities that mix have the kinetic matrix
 K=[[A-B, A v], [A v, rho+A v^2]],
 det K=(A-B)rho-A B v^2.
The two perpendicular displacement velocities have coefficient rho>0.
Freezing the medium would test only A>B and miss the second condition.
With the medium dynamical, positive definiteness requires
 A>B and rho>A B v^2/(A-B).
Equivalently, since B=b/v,
 v^2-(rho/b)v+rho/A<0.
This determinant inequality itself implies A>B for positive parameters.
There is a nonempty interval precisely when rho>4b^2/A, and its endpoints are
 v_-=(rho/(2b))[1-sqrt(1-4b^2/(A rho))],
 v_+=(rho/(2b))[1+sqrt(1-4b^2/(A rho))].
Only v_-<v<v_+ has positive kinetic energy. The endpoints have a zero kinetic
eigenvalue and are not ordinary healthy limits. As v tends to zero, B=b/v
diverges; any finite A fails. As v grows without bound, the mixing eventually
makes det K negative. This conclusion assumes constant finite A,rho,b.
State-dependent coefficients or additional operators can change it, but must
be specified and tested rather than assumed to repair it.

3. Spatial energy and coupled characteristics

The phase spatial stiffness is b v/m^2 transverse to the background and
2b v/m^2 parallel to it, both positive. The medium stiffness is rho c_N^2.
The remaining mixed term is +(b/m) pi_dot partial_x pi. Its contribution
cancels from the energy expressed in velocities, leaving
 E2=velocity^T K velocity/2 + positive spatial-gradient energy,
plus the uncoupled medium polarizations. In canonical momenta the same energy
is a positive completed square if K is positive. Thus the allowed interval
has positive local quadratic energy in all directions in this model.
This is not a global nonlinear stability result: arbitrary gradients can
leave the interval, and gravity and a cosmological background are absent.

For a wave making angle cosine c with the background gradient, the coupled
two-by-two characteristic equation is
 det[K omega^2-2 C omega k-G k^2]=0,
 C=diag(b c/m,0),
 G=diag(b v(1+c^2)/m^2, rho c_N^2).
The two other medium polarizations have speeds +/-c_N. Numerical values below
use m=1 and a corresponding fixed normalization of displacement coordinates;
kinetic eigenvalue magnitudes depend on field normalization, their signs do not.

4. Consequences for a source and the coefficient puzzle

On a static xi=0 branch, Y=0, so the A and rho terms do not change the phase
source equation. Its outward current is J=b v^2/m^2. For a spherical baryonic
source of mass M_b, J=q M_b/(4 pi r^2), hence v=C/r with
 C^2=q M_b m^2/(4 pi b).
The phase acceleration is u=q v and satisfies u^2=a0 G M_b/r^2 with
 a0=q^3 m^2/(4 pi b G).
This static relation is local to the assumed regime and negligible gravitational
backreaction. It does not demonstrate a global halo solution or lensing.

The coupled kinetic interval maps to
 C/v_+ < r < C/v_-.
For universal coefficients its boundaries scale as sqrt(M_b), and its radius
ratio v_+/v_- is independent of source mass. This is a conditional diagnostic
of this model, not a new observed relation or an established halo boundary.
The theory must transition or change before its kinetic matrix becomes singular.
For fixed coefficients it cannot describe the exact r->infinity deep-force
limit as an everywhere healthy coupled phase.

Changing E0 changes none of the current, kinetic matrix, or interval. If it
were the entire cosmological vacuum energy, Lambda=E0/M_Pl^2 with
M_Pl^2=1/(8 pi G), while a0=2q^3 m^2 M_Pl^2/b. Therefore
 Lambda/a0^2=E0 b^2/(4 q^6 m^4 M_Pl^6).
The normal-medium stability conditions do not determine E0 or this ratio.
The target 32 pi remains an independent condition, not a prediction.

5. Evidence and open obligations

Ten exact symbolic checks cover the Hessian, gradient and mixed coefficients,
determinant, interval roots and limiting signs. Six times 101 directional
mode calculations and two out-of-window controls are recorded. Three Lean
statements certify the determinant identity, the stronger kinetic requirement,
and exclusion of sufficiently low gradients. They do not formalize the field
variation, full spectrum or a microscopic completion. This is self-review.

This test advances the finite-temperature escape by keeping the stabilizing
component dynamical. It shows that merely adding a positive time kinetic term
is insufficient. A credible continuation requires a microscopic joint state
that predicts the medium coefficients, the transition at the interval edges,
and the total stress/vacuum offset. A physically meaningful result would also
need gravity, lensing, and the medium's full conservation laws. This toy action
has not supplied those ingredients, and no breakthrough claim is warranted.
