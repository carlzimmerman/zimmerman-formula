DECISIVE RECONSTRUCTION

1. Stationarity and the precise minimum being tested

Write f=sqrt(n), Phi=f exp(-i omega t), X=-h exp(-2i omega t), h>0.
For L=|dPhi|^2+|dX|^2-V, the homogeneous rotating-frame functional is

 U_omega(f,h)=V-omega^2(f^2+4h^2)
             =-delta f^2+D h^2-2g h f^2+lambda f^4,
 delta=omega^2-m^2, D=M^2-4omega^2.

Its two nonzero-amplitude equations are
 D h=g f^2,
 delta=2lambda f^2-2g h.
These give exactly h=g n/D and n=delta D/[2(lambda D-g^2)].

For arbitrary phases, put |Phi|=f, |X|=h. Conversion energy is
2g h f^2 cos(2 theta_Phi-theta_X). For g>0 its minimum has relative
phase pi modulo 2pi. At that minimum the relative-phase curvature is
2g h n>0. The common U(1) phase is a symmetry zero mode, not a negative
mode or a strictly stabilized absolute phase.

At the positive-amplitude stationary point, the Hessian in (f,h) is

 K = [ 8lambda n       -4g sqrt(n) ]
     [ -4g sqrt(n)      2D         ],
 det K=16n(lambda D-g^2).

It is positive definite precisely when D>0 and A=lambda D-g^2>0.
Since lambda>0, A>0 already implies D>0. With n>0, stationarity then
requires delta>0. On the selected omega>0 branch this is exactly

 m<omega<sqrt(M^2-g^2/lambda)/2.

The upper endpoint exceeds m because lambda Delta-g^2=lambda_rep Delta>0.
Writing eta=r/(1+r) gives g^2/lambda=Delta/(1+r), hence
omega_cap^2=m^2+Delta eta/4=m^2(1+u_*^2 eta).
At the cap, D->g^2/lambda>0 and delta->Delta eta/4>0, while A->0+;
both n and h diverge. No finite-density configuration reaches the cap.
At the other endpoint n,h->0. The endpoint n=0 is not an interior minimum
with positive amplitudes. This validates the strict open interval rather
than any finite-density decay or instability interpretation of its cap.

2. Current, stress and thermodynamic derivatives

The sign convention giving positive q for exp(-i omega t) is

 j^mu=i(Phi* d^mu Phi-Phi d^mu Phi*)
       +2i(X* d^mu X-X d^mu X*).

The X harmonic has frequency 2omega and charge weight 2, so
j^0=2omega n+8omega h^2=2omega(n+4h^2). There is no missing factor of 2.
For homogeneous fields the canonical stress gives

 epsilon=omega^2 n+4omega^2 h^2+V,
 p=omega^2 n+4omega^2 h^2-V.

Substitute Dh=gn and delta=2n(lambda-g^2/D). Then

 p=delta n-Dh^2+2gnh-lambda n^2
  =delta n/2,
 epsilon+p=2omega^2(n+4h^2)=omega q.

Equivalently p=-U_omega on its stationary branch. Differentiating this
last expression along the stationary point leaves only its explicit
omega derivative, giving dp/domega=2omega n+8omega h^2=q.
Consequently epsilon'=q+omega q'-p'=omega q'.

The derivative signs need an actual check; they do not follow merely from
the two numerator factors in n, since D decreases. With A=lambda D-g^2,
direct differentiation gives

 dn/domega=omega[D A+4g^2 delta]/A^2>0,
 dh/domega=g omega[lambda Delta-g^2]/A^2>0.

Thus q'=2(n+4h^2)+2omega(n'+8hh')>0, epsilon'>0 and

 dp/(epsilon+p)=domega/omega,
 c_s^2=q/(omega q')>0.

The same formulas also give c_s^2<1 in this homogeneous leading-derivative
EOS, because omega q'=q+2omega^2(n'+8hh')>q. This observation does not
replace a full two-field fluctuation calculation. Pressure and epsilon
vanish at the zero-density endpoint omega=m. For every positive-density
branch state, p>0, q>0, epsilon>0 and epsilon+p>0.

3. Fresh derivation of the critical TOV inequality

This step was reconstructed without the supplemental TOV script or its
verdict. Let mu(r)=4pi integral_0^r epsilon(s)s^2 ds, mu(R)=M_g.
For nonincreasing epsilon,

 mu(r)>=4pi epsilon(r) r^3/3,
 d[mu(r)/r^3]/dr=[4pi epsilon(r)r^3-3mu(r)]/r^4<=0.

It follows for 0<r<=R that mu(r)/r^3>=M_g/R^3, or
mu(r)>=M_g r^3/R^3. This is the required direction; reversing it would
invalidate the compactness argument. Regularity controls the r=0 limit.

The EOS gives H(p)=integral_0^p dp/(epsilon+p)=log(omega/m).
Hydrostatic equilibrium gives dH/dr=-d(log alpha)/dr; equivalently
omega alpha is constant. The zero-pressure surface of this positive
branch is omega(R)=m. Thus

 H_c=log(omega_c/m)<log(1+zeta)/2.

From the stipulated TOV equation and regular finite surface,

 H_c=integral_0^R G[mu(r)+4pi r^3p(r)]
                  /[r^2(1-2Gmu(r)/r)] dr.

No horizon means the denominator is positive throughout. Drop p>=0.
For fixed r>0, F_r(x)=x/(1-2Gx/r) has
F_r'(x)=(1-2Gx/r)^(-2)>0 on the admissible interval. The smaller uniform-
density surrogate mu_0(r)=M_g r^3/R^3 stays in this interval because
mu_0<=mu. Inserting it therefore lowers the integrand, including its
denominator, with the correct direction:

 H_c>=integral_0^R (G M_g r/R^3)/(1-2C r^2/R^2) dr
     =-(1/4)log(1-2C),  C=G M_g/R<1/2.

The last integral follows by substituting y=r^2/R^2. Combining with the
strict upper bound on H_c gives log(1-2C)>-2log(1+zeta), hence exactly

 C<[1-(1+zeta)^(-2)]/2.

For a nontrivial positive-pressure solution the dropped pressure term is
strictly positive on an interval, but strictness of (A) already follows
from the finite-density chemical cap. No saturation or optimality claim
is established. SI restoration is C=G M_g/(R c^2). There is no baryonic
profile, MOND constitutive law or a0 in this argument.

4. Independent scattering normalization and the zero dictionary

The canonical interaction terms are
-lambda Phi*^2 Phi^2-g X*Phi^2-g X Phi*^2. Differentiating with respect
to external fields gives contact vertex -4i lambda and cubic vertices
-2ig. For two same-charge Phi particles, charge flow permits the X
s-channel exchange. There are no X t/u exchanges for this external-charge
assignment. With standard i M invariant-amplitude convention,

 i M_tree=-4i lambda+(-2ig)^2 i/(s-M^2+i0),
 M_tree(s)=-4lambda+4g^2/(M^2-s)

away from the bare source pole. Its real zero is
s_z=M^2-g^2/lambda. Since lambda>g^2/Delta, 4m^2<s_z<M^2;
the zero lies in the elastic two-Phi physical range below the tree pole.
For equal masses in the CM frame s=4(m^2+k^2), so

 s_z=4omega_cap^2,
 k_z^2=(s_z-4m^2)/4=Delta eta/4=m^2 zeta.

Thus the compactness bound uses zeta=k_z^2/m^2 (not k_z/m itself) and can
be written C<[1-(1+k_z^2/m^2)^(-2)]/2. The raw phrase 'using k_z/m in
place of zeta' is safely read as this dimensionless dictionary, not a
literal unsquared substitution. The raw equations (B) fix the square.
The amplitude zero and branch cap are both tree/classical consequences
of the same polynomial coefficients. A tree cancellation does not prove
zero exact scattering, a protected lifetime or a radiatively exact EOS.
