PRIMARY VERDICT: conditional on a new Newtonian radial material action.
The CE019/020 constrained energy does admit an ordinary conserved-displacement
kinetic parent. Its positive vector stiffness is exactly an ordinary positive
barotropic internal energy in the radial sector. This is a construction in a
specified comparison model, not derivation of the original carrier species,
its original independent-P variational principle, or a covariant completion.

1. Exact density identity; no exterior material is inserted

Use G=a0=1 and retain the CE019/020 normalization

 E[m] = int_0^infinity { r^2 w(u)-Q(r)m/r^2
                  + beta/2 [r^2 u'^2+2u^2] } dr,
 u=m/r^2, rho=m'/(4pi r^2), R(u)=u^2/(1-u),
 w(u)=-u^2/2-u-log(1-u), beta>0.

Q is a prescribed enclosed nonnegative external mass, not another copy of m.
Assume regular settled center m=O(r^3), finite total m(infinity)=S, and the
stated finite-energy/sub-saturation domain. Direct differentiation gives

 r^2 u'^2+2u^2 = m'^2/r^2 - 2(m^2/r^3)'.                 (1)

Thus the entire-space stiffness contribution is exactly

 E_beta = beta/2 int m'^2/r^2 dr = int e_beta(rho) d^3x,
 e_beta(rho)=2pi beta rho^2,
 p_beta=rho e_beta'-e_beta=2pi beta rho^2 >=0,
 dp_beta/drho=4pi beta rho >=0.                           (2)

Both endpoint terms vanish for this regular-center finite-stock class.
On a matched annulus the inner endpoint is a fixed constant only if the
inner radius and enclosed mass are fixed. A singular settled point mass at
r=0 is outside this identity's endpoint assumptions. One must not drop that
endpoint term for a variable core. Although u=S/r^2 has nonzero gradient
energy in the exterior, the exact integration identity converts the complete
energy to an interior local density energy; discarding the exterior tail
before the variation would give a wrong boundary term.

For physical m=r^2u/G, the conventional energy normalization is
 E_h=(h/(2G)) int [r^2u'^2+2u^2]dr.
Then e_h=2pi G h rho^2 and dp_h/drho=4pi G h rho. This follows by the
same identity; beta=h in the normalized equations above. If a gradient
coefficient is quoted in a differently normalized action, match the actual
prefactor before using this dimensional translation.

2. A real conserved-displacement action and shell equation

Let a in [0,S] be conserved enclosed settled rest mass. Let Rmat(a,t) be a
strictly increasing radial material map in the occupied open interval,
with regular center, no crossings and finite int Rmat_dot^2 da. Its inverse
defines m_R(r,t). Consider the proposed action

 A = int dt { (1/2) int_0^S Rmat_dot(a,t)^2 da - E[m_R] }. (3)

It conserves the total positive stock identically. Its kinetic energy is
positive in actual mass coordinates, not a guessed independent-P inertia.
For a material virtual displacement xi(r)=delta Rmat(m(r)),
 delta m=-m' xi. The radial energy first variation has bulk coefficient

 F = delta E/delta m = R(u)-Q/r^2-beta Delta_v u,
 Delta_v u=u''+2u'/r-2u/r^2=4pi rho'.                     (4)

Integration of delta A therefore gives, wherever rho>0,

 Rmat_ddot = F = R(u)-Q/r^2-4pi beta rho'
           = R(u)-Q/r^2-p_beta'/rho.                     (5)

For beta=0 this reduces exactly to the already recorded CE028/031 one-shell
equation. Equation (5), the density-energy identity and the edge variation
are the additional beta>0 result here. No zero-stiffness formation/adiabatic
claim is imported. For time-independent Q, (3) conserves T+E. A time-varying
externally imposed Q does work and is not a reciprocal assembly history.

The residual w already includes the settled gravitational self energy once:
the positive full constitutive field energy -u-log(1-u), minus the Newtonian
self term u^2/2, leaves w. If Q is held fixed its gravitational cross energy
is -int Qm/r^2. Making Q live requires its own kinetic/internal energies and
the complete Newtonian mass energy; this radial fixed-source certificate
cannot be assigned to a live source by declaration (cf. CE059).

3. Chemical potential, free surface, and exterior reaction

More generally add a local internal energy int e(rho)d^3x to the residual
zero-stiffness energy E0, with e(0)=0. Write F0=R(u)-Q/r^2 and

 mu0(r)=int_r^infinity F0(s) ds.

Fubini's formula for delta m=int_0^r 4pi s^2 delta rho(s)ds gives
delta E0=int mu0 delta rho d^3x. Thus

 mu_total=mu0+e'(rho),
 mu_total'= -F0+e''(rho)rho',
 mu_total=constant on occupied equilibrium,
 mu_total>=that constant in admissible vacuum.            (6)

The shell force is -mu_total'; the pressure is p=rho e'-e. Free material
surface displacement against vacuum gives p(E^-)=0. For (2), this requires
rho(E^-)=0. Hence a no-surface-mass finite edge obeys

 m(E)=S, m'(E)=0,
 u(E)=S/E^2, u'(E)=-2S/E^3.                               (7)

There is no reflecting wall or fictitious exterior density. In the original
gradient variables the same shape variation has edge coefficient
 -beta m'(E^-)^2/(2E^2) delta E, which also forces (7). The zero density
edge, rather than a jump of a positive pressure to vacuum, is essential.

The exterior is m=S,rho=0,u=S/r^2. There is no exterior shell EL because
there is no material there. The vacuum inequality in (6) penalizes adding
test mass there. It is not a fluid stress with nonzero density outside.
The *interior* stress (2) is an ordinary positive pressure; the exterior
constraint reaction is a chemical-potential obstacle, not an additional
gravitating population. This distinction is the precise escape from the
global-active-independent-P premise of CE079's finite-stock theorem.

At a tapering edge rho'(E^-)<=0, so occupied equilibrium requires

 R(S/E^2)<=Q(E)/E^2,
 E^2>=S+S^2/Q(E) when Q(E)>0.                              (8)

The inequality is necessary, not a full edge-selection equation. When Q is
constant beyond E, it is also sufficient for the exterior chemical potential
to increase away from its edge value, because r^2 R(S/r^2)=S^2/(r^2-S)
decreases. Positive nondecreasing Q beyond E only strengthens that conclusion.

4. Positive radial material inertia and Hessian

At an occupied equilibrium F=0, take eta=delta m=-m'xi. The material second
variation of the energy is

 H[eta]=int { [R'(u)/r^2]eta^2
          +beta[r^2((eta/r^2)')^2+2eta^2/r^4] } dr >0     (9)

for any nonzero admissible smooth displacement of occupied mass. The extra
coordinate-curvature term is int F delta^2m; it vanishes because F=0 in the
occupied region, m'=0 at its edge and exterior variations vanish to first
order. The endpoint/finite-action conditions are part of this statement.
No separate free exterior polarization displacement is included in (3).

The quadratic kinetic energy is

 T2=(1/2)int m' xi_dot^2 dr
   =(1/2)int eta_dot^2/m' dr
   =(1/2)int [r^4/m'] delta u_dot^2 dr.                   (10)

The weight diverges at a zero-density edge in Eulerian field variables;
that is the material-inertia weight, not a negative kinetic coefficient.
Admissibility requires eta/m' have finite material velocity/displacement
and the integral in (10) be finite. One must not replace it by unweighted
independent-P inertia. Formally the occupied linear equation is

 eta_tt=-m'{R'(u)eta/r^2-beta Delta_v(eta/r^2)}.            (11)

The positive forms (9),(10) give nonnegative Rayleigh frequency squares on
this admissible radial linear domain. They do not prove existence of a
complete mode basis, a uniform gap, free-boundary well-posedness, stability
against crossings, arbitrary nonspherical dynamics, or relativistic causality.
The local sound coefficient from the pressure part is 4pi beta rho and
vanishes at the edge. The original theory's inertia must still be derived.
