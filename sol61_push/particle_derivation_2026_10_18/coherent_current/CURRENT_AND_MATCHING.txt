1. The particle action supplies a conserved charge and two species sources

At the recorded nonrelativistic order take

 H_NR = m Q + int [ |grad psi|²/(2m)+|grad b|²/(2M)
          +delta b†b +c3(b†psi²+psi†²b)+kc psi†²psi² ] d³x,
 Q=int(n_psi+2n_b), delta=M-2m,
 c3=g_c/(2m sqrt(2M)), kc=lambda_tot/(4m²).

The continuum Hamilton equations give, with ordinary number currents
j_psi=Im(psi*grad psi)/m and j_b=Im(b*grad b)/M,

 n_psi_dot+div j_psi=2R,
 n_b_dot+div j_b=-R,
 R=2c3 Im(b psi*²) at classical mean-field level.

The operator version is R=i c3(b†psi²-psi†²b), with the corresponding
expectation values and regulated local products. Thus

 q=n_psi+2n_b, Jq=j_psi+2j_b,
 q_dot+div Jq=0.

These are action-derived source/daughter species equations. No chosen
phenomenological transfer coefficient has been inserted. Source versus
daughter is not the same distinction as settled versus free material.

For psi=sqrt(n) exp(i theta), b=sqrt(n_b) exp(i(2theta+chi)), the exact NR
phase kinetic term is -q theta_dot-n_b chi_dot. The conversion energy is
2c3 n sqrt(n_b) cos chi, and R=+2c3 n sqrt(n_b) sin chi.
The common phase theta is cyclic; the relative phase chi supplies reversible
species exchange. Quantum creation from an empty daughter mode requires the
operator theory; classical psi=0 remains psi=0 without a perturbation.

The kinetic mass M and release energy delta are retained at the recorded NR
order; no exact Galilean-boost statement or relativistic stress identity is
being assigned to this finite-order rotating-frame Hamiltonian.

2. What can be identified with a positive gravitating density?

In the antiparticle-free NR sector the summed rest-mass density and flux are

 rho_rest=m n_psi+M n_b=m q+delta n_b,
 j_rest=m j_psi+M j_b,
 rho_rest_dot+div j_rest=-delta R.

The rest-mass deficit from a forward conversion is compensated by kinetic
and interaction energy; it is not a loss of total stress-energy or a vacuum
energy source. The full relativistic gravitational source also includes
kinetic, interaction, pressure and binding contributions.

The conserved density m q approximates rho_rest with the exact positive-sector
pointwise bound

 0 <= (rho_rest-mq)/rho_rest <= delta/(2m+delta)=delta/M.

At u_*=.02, M=2m sqrt(1+u_*²), this bound is approximately1.9994e-4.
This small number is a near-threshold positive-charge statement, not a bound
for a neutral relativistic bath. Equal daughter and antidaughter populations
have zero net U(1) charge and positive rest energy; no mJ0 identification can
represent their total mass. The signed current must not become the entire
positive cold inventory in that state.

A dual representation of this conserved current can supply div P=mq.
It gives the conserved positive density in this restricted sector, not an
exact identity for gravitational mass in every state. Separate dual-gauge
and constitutive-energy obligations are treated in ../density_duality/.

3. Threshold matching must retain the negative-frequency source term

The exact relativistic source propagator at two-daughter threshold decomposes
as

 1/(4m²-M²)=[1/(2m-M)-1/(2m+M)]/(2M).

The positive-frequency source-only NR square gives

 lambda_RWA=4m²(kc-c3²/delta)
            =lambda_rep+g_c²/[2M(M+2m)].

The analytic negative-frequency contribution subtracts precisely the second
term, so the fully threshold-matched quartic is lambda_rep and the leading
contact Gross-Pitaevskii coefficient is g_NR=lambda_rep/(2m²), in the
normalization e_contact=g_NR n²/2.

The earlier CE-FM-082 pressure calculation correctly parameterized its chosen
regulated NR square by lambda_RWA. That coefficient must not be silently
relabeled as exact relativistic threshold matching. Both are retained here.
The difference relative to lambda_min=g_c²/(M²-4m²) is

 (lambda_RWA-lambda_rep)/lambda_min=(M-2m)/(2M)
                                     =u_*²/4+O(u_*^4).

Relative to lambda_rep it is further divided by r_lambda. At the selected
u_*=.02,r_lambda=2 benchmark the relative shift is about4.9985e-5. This
matching correction does not remove the many-orders-of-magnitude contact
pressure length mismatch found at MeV masses. It does remove a normalization
ambiguity before deriving actual condensate/free scattering exchange.
