# A theoretical conversion-powered light engine

This concept assumes hypothetical particles and an interaction that have not
been observed. It is not a construction plan or a working propulsion system.

The mechanical sequence would be:

**Separate dark reactants → localized conversion → retain the new baryons →
direct escaping light backward → craft recoils forward.**

The reaction studied is a hidden-baryon carrier plus an opposite dark charge
becoming a neutron and photon. The neutron is ordinary matter. Stopping it
inside the craft transfers its recoil to the craft; letting light leave aft
provides external exhaust momentum. An isotropic source provides no average
thrust.

Conversion could be concentrated in the chamber by bringing the required
reactants together there. For the assigned example masses, the emitted photon
also lies below the threshold for the separate photon-triggered cold-carrier
reaction at rest. That helps isolate conversion. It does not make the light
invisible to ordinary matter, and it does not switch off gravity.

There are three firm costs:

* Even ideal directed light needs about300MW per newton of thrust.
* This example leaves most fuel energy as ordinary baryon rest mass; only
  0.053% becomes the prompt photon. Retained products accumulate aboard.
* Fuel, created matter and light all curve spacetime. The computed light-beam
  curvature is tiny; a strong controlled spacetime drive has not been derived.

The unresolved technology and physics are the particle identity, sufficient
conversion rate, fuel storage, product containment and efficient direction
of the gamma energy. Effects on surrounding matter can be reduced by spatial
separation and beam control, but complete absence of all effects is not
established.
