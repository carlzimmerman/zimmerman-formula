# Direct messenger collisions offer cooling, but do not capture the carrier

2026-10-13. The hypothetical charged messenger introduced in the preceding package couples directly to the carrier through h|Phi|²|S|². Real, cold messengers therefore provide an energy-transfer channel without the photon-loop suppression. This is a conditional sympathetic-cooling route. The same calculation finds that a centimeter reservoir at the illustrative density is far too thin to capture passing carriers. The reservoir itself is not constructed or experimentally justified.

## Collision and energy transfer

Use one scalar carrier of mass m and a distinguishable messenger of mass M_S. The contact invariant amplitude has magnitude h. The nonrelativistic total elastic cross section is

\[
\sigma_{\Phi S}=\frac{h^2}{16\pi(m+M_S)^2}.
\]

This uses the two-body scattering normalization already checked against PDG in prior packages; there is no identical-final-state factor. Scattering is isotropic in the center-of-mass frame at this order. For an initially stationary target and scattering-angle cosine x,

\[
\frac{K'}{K}=\frac{m^2+M_S^2+2mM_S x}{(m+M_S)^2},\qquad
f\equiv\frac{K-\langle K'\rangle}{K}=\frac{2mM_S}{(m+M_S)^2}.
\]

Both momentum and energy are conserved; lost carrier kinetic energy goes into the messenger. If each successive target is independently at rest, then after exactly N collisions,

\[
\langle K_N\rangle=K_0(1-f)^N.
\]

This collision-count expectation is not a mean cooling trajectory versus time: collision rates vary with speed and the energy distribution broadens. With a finite-temperature bath, reverse heating also occurs. Real cooling requires ongoing heat removal from the bath.

Sympathetic cooling is an established mechanism in other systems, for example [Delannoy et al., quant-ph/0101130v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0101130). That work emphasizes thermal contact and spatial overlap; it is not evidence for the proposed messenger. No measured rate or atomic scattering length is imported here.

## Conditional numerical screen

Set m=1 MeV, h=1, carrier K=1 eV and prescribed stationary messenger density n_S=1e25 m^-3. The initial speed is about 424 km/s. Convert the cross section to SI with (hbar c)².

| Messenger mass | Cross section (m²) | Mean energy loss/event | Events for mean energy below 1% | Mean free path |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 MeV | 6.402e-30 | 16.53% | 26 | 15.62 km |
| 100 MeV | 7.594e-32 | 1.961% | 233 | 1317 km |
| 1 GeV | 7.731e-34 | 0.1996% | 2305 | 129350 km |

In the 10 MeV case, a carrier that remains immersed has an initial collision rate 27.14 s^-1 and initial fractional mean energy-loss rate f n_S sigma v=4.486 s^-1. This is an instantaneous drift for the stated initial energy, not a verified one-second cooling time or an exponential law.

But for a straight 1 cm crossing the optical depth is only n_S sigma L=6.402e-7. The interaction probability is approximately that number. Quoting the 27 collisions per second without accounting for the much shorter residence time would therefore be misleading. A mean of 26 collisions cannot be assigned to a freely escaping carrier.

The mean-field potential of a nonrelativistic messenger density is

\[
U_\Phi=\frac{h n_S}{4mM_S}
\]

in natural units. At the same benchmark it is repulsive and only 1.921e-9 eV. This uniform energy shift is neither a trap geometry nor a barrier capable of reflecting a 1 eV carrier. A denser spatially structured reservoir would require a new, self-consistent calculation.

## Photon-annihilation competition

The same messenger matches C=-alpha h/(48 pi M_S²). Compare the initial collisional energy-loss rate with the cold overlapping-mixture annihilation rate n_opposite a, where a=2 C²m²/pi. At M_S=10 MeV and K=1 eV, equality requires n_S/n_opposite approximately 3.879e-6. This shows a possible separation between direct cooling and loop-induced annihilation at appropriate densities. It does not assure survival at the prior dense-condensate benchmark or during escape. The comparison assumes uncorrelated collision partners and no other losses.

## Reservoir requirements remain physical obligations

S is an added, electrically charged, unobserved particle. Merely assigning it a density and zero velocity does not explain production, trapping, cooling, stability or experimental viability. A reservoir of one unit-charge species at 1e25 m^-3 carries charge density 1.602e6 C/m³ before neutralization. A realistic neutralizing medium, correlations, electromagnetic fields, heat removal and their interactions with the carrier must be included. The stationary independent-target approximation is a kinematic diagnostic, not a validated plasma state.

The displayed quartic preserves carrier charge and messenger particle number at the vertex. For M_S>m, low-energy carrier-antiparticle annihilation into a real messenger pair is kinematically closed, but other radiative channels and the completion of the model remain relevant. No claim is made that the hypothetical messenger's laboratory preparation is simpler than carrier preparation.
