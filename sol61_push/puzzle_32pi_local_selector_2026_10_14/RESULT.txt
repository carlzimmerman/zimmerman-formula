U = U0 exp(phi^2/F^2), f = beta log(phi/F), beta,F,U0,phi positive.

m_eff^2/H^2 = 12(1-z)/(gamma/beta+6z).

Target gamma=3/(128 pi) remains an input, not a prediction.

2. Nonlinear, massless, fixed-metric local bound

For a Schwarzschild mass m, E4=48m^2/r^6. Dropping U' and cosmological
terms, the exact static test-field equation is
  [r^2(1-2m/r) phi']' = -48 beta m^2/(r^4 phi).
Set x=r/m, y=m phi/sqrt(beta), q=-x(x-2)y'. On any assumed positive,
horizon-regular solution with y(X_R)=eps>0 and X_R>=4,
  q(2)=0, q'=48/(x^4 y), y'= -q/[x(x-2)].
Thus q increases and y decreases. Write Q=q(X_R). For 3<=x<=4,
  y(x)<=eps+Q log(3)/2.
Integrating q' over [3,4] yields
  Q(eps+kQ)>=I, k=log(3)/2, I=37/108,
  Q>=qmin=2I/[sqrt(eps^2+4kI)+eps].
Since y>=eps, Q-q(X)<=16/(eps X^3). For 2<X<X_R,
  [phi(mX)-phi(mX_R)]/phi(mX_R)
    >= [qmin-16/(eps X^3)] J/eps,
  J={log(1-2/X_R)-log(1-2/X)}/2.
When the right side is negative, positivity supplies the stronger zero bound.
These inequalities assume existence of the positive regular solution; they
do not prove existence. Lean formalizes the algebraic inequality steps, not
the differential equation, integrations, or physical approximations.

A linear control with f'=alpha held fixed is
  delta phi=2 alpha/(mr)[1+m/r+4m^2/(3r^2)].
It solves the linearized equation and is horizon regular. Large fractional
values invalidate linearization; the bound above does not use linearization.

3. Assigned benchmark, and its important limitation

Assign isolated black-hole mass 4e6 solar masses, a0=9.360324825e-11 m/s^2,
phi_v=a0/c^2, H=sqrt(32 pi/3) phi_v, boundary 100 kpc, test radius 10 kpc.
At beta=gamma/12 the massless fractional lower bound is about 55146.
Within this massless model, a fractional difference <=0.1 requires
beta <=2.04433e-15, implying the homogeneous rate bound <=3.28830e-12.

THIS IS NOT A BOUND ON THE FULL EXPONENTIAL-POTENTIAL THEORY. Even though
H times outer radius is only 1.86033e-5, a large field excursion can make
the nonlinear potential important. A small vacuum mass times radius alone
does not control the potential along a large excursion. Large beta can also
invalidate the massless approximation directly. The calculation diagnoses
a tension in an unscreened approximation, not an observational exclusion.
There is no new observation or fit here, and no realistic galaxy model.

4. Opposite limit: local equilibrium gives a concrete equation

On fixed Schwarzschild-de Sitter, E4=24H^4+48m^2/r^6. If scalar gradients
are negligible, U'=f'E4. Dividing by the vacuum stationary equation gives
  z_local exp(z_local) = z_v exp(z_v) [1+2m^2/(H^4 r^6)],
  z_local = W_0(z_v exp(z_v)[1+2m^2/(H^4 r^6)]),
  phi_local/phi_v = sqrt(z_local/z_v).
W_0 denotes the principal real Lambert W branch. Positive input gives one
positive local algebraic root. This is exact within the stated algebraic
approximation; it is not a solution of the full spatial equations.
If both z_v and z_local are small, the ratio becomes
  sqrt(1+2m^2/(H^4 r^6)).
The assigned benchmark gives 7.88586 at 10 kpc. Its crossover radius
r0=(2m^2/H^4)^(1/6)=19.8507 kpc scales as m^(1/3), not sqrt(m).
Increasing beta alone does not remove this algebraic forcing. The actual
profile requires matching gradients, horizon conditions, and metric response.

5. What this advances and what remains open

The candidate now has an explicit local-response equation and a conditional
nonlinear charge bound. Vacuum stability alone is insufficient to establish
an approximately uniform acceleration scale. The decisive remaining problem
is a nonlinear, backreacting boundary-value calculation, including the
physical matter/acceleration coupling. Neither tested limit rules out all
intermediate solutions. No result here derives gamma=3/(128 pi), protects
additive vacuum shifts, proves novelty, or establishes new physics.
