DERIVATION OF LOWER BOUND
On [r0,R], g>0 and r^3g nondecreasing. A bounded orbit with apocentre ra<=R
has j^2<=ra^3g(ra). Its effective potential is nondecreasing beyond ra, so
  E<=Phi(R)+j^2/(2R^2).
At the wall,
  vr(r0)^2<=2[Phi(R)-Phi(r0)]-j^2(1/r0^2-1/R^2)
            <=2 DeltaPhi.
Therefore Pr(r0)<=2rho(r0)DeltaPhi. Combine with exact annulus virial:
  2K=I-4pi r0^3 Pr(r0), I=integral r g dM,
to obtain
  max(0,I/2-r0 M'(r0)DeltaPhi)<=K<=I/2.
Here M' is cold target mass derivative, with density understood as the
one-sided limit above the inner wall; baryon density is not included in M'.
At fixed cold density and static baryons W is fixed. Adding W gives a narrow
allowed interval [Emin,Emax]. The initial energy lies below Emin in every C
cell, and above Emax in every B cell. Wall pressure cannot hide the mismatch.
No velocity isotropy, entropy maximization or specific mixing rate assumed.
This applies to ordinary nonnegative stationary orbital populations with a
natural outer edge and well-defined finite boundary moments. A singular
surface mass or extra confining force would require a different model.

IMPLICATION FOR THE ACTION
An evolution claimed to attract both benchmark initial states to this target
must explicitly account for energy exchange in BOTH directions, or openly
change the retained mass, baryonic state, boundary flux or final density.
A one-way positive-Q sink from cold matter into dark energy cannot accomplish
both tasks while all those quantities are fixed. An effective BGK heating
step is not automatically a contradiction if its partner supplies the energy;
the partner's loss and regular dynamics must be derived and checked.
No statement is made that real halos begin in IC-C. If the proposed action
restricts allowed initial states, that restriction needs independent physical
justification, rather than exclusion of difficult states after seeing results.
