MICROSCOPIC DENSITY TEST: A BRANCH AND RESPONSE-SLOPE OBSTRUCTION
2026-10-14. Base 97f3b0789f81e08f8353b7c82386d52f1c7d790a.
Parent: puzzle_32pi_source_audit_2026_10_14/RESULT.txt.

Verdict: the tested stable, single-component, local-equilibrium condensate
cannot produce the desired quadratic source-versus-force response. Its
positive-X fractional-power action is derivable, but its negative-X MOND
continuation is not its equilibrium ground state. This closes one concrete
microscopic route, not all media or modified-gravity theories. The original
32 pi coefficient-selection problem remains open.

1. Start from a density, not a chosen fractional-power action

Use a nonrelativistic complex condensate with number density n>=0, phase theta,
mass m>0 and repulsive three-body energy
 U(n)=g n^3/3+E0, g>0.
In density-phase variables its local Lagrangian, before neglecting gradients
of the density, is
 L=n X-U(n)-(grad n)^2/(8mn),
 X=theta_dot-m Phi-(grad theta)^2/(2m).
Here Phi is an externally specified Newtonian potential in this local matching
test. A homogeneous background theta_dot supplies the chemical potential.
Density gradients are omitted only for the local equation of state; they
cannot be omitted near interfaces, vortices or a depleted condensate.

Equilibrium elimination of n requires the global supremum
 P(X)=sup_{n>=0}[nX-g n^3/3-E0].
For X>0, n=sqrt(X/g), giving
 P(X)=2 X^(3/2)/(3sqrt(g))-E0.
For X<=0 every n>0 lowers nX-g n^3/3, so the maximum is at n=0:
 P(X)=-E0.
The n=0 endpoint is a limit of the density-phase description, where the phase
itself ceases to describe a condensate excitation.

Thus the fractional exponent is explained on the positive branch. The formula
P(X)=2X sqrt(|X|)/(3sqrt(g))-E0 for negative X is NOT obtained by this density
minimization. It would have positive n=P_X but negative susceptibility P_XX,
whereas the actual negative-X ground state is empty. Using that continuation
as if it had been microscopically derived would be an invalid step.

2. A broader equilibrium restriction

For any density energy U(n), the equilibrium pressure defined as a supremum
of affine functions nX-U(n) is convex in X wherever finite. At a smooth stable
branch, n=P_X>0 and susceptibility P_XX>=0. If U is twice differentiable and
strictly convex there, P_XX=1/U''(n)>0.

The negative fractional-power branch is concave, not convex. There is an exact
three-point witness, independent of offsets. For P(X)=A X sqrt(-X)-E0 on X<0,
A>0, take X1=-1, X2=-9, weights 5/8 and 3/8. Their average is -4. The weighted
pressure is -43A/4-E0, while P(-4)=-8A-E0, exceeding it by 11A/4.
This violates the convexity inequality. The arithmetic violation is exact;
the convexity of the supremum follows directly by evaluating every affine
function at the weighted argument and taking the supremum.

3. Static force response: the decisive condition

Let v=|grad theta|>0, fix the local background chemical potential and Phi,
and write X=mu_eff-v^2/(2m). The phase current magnitude is
 J(v)=n(X)v/m.
On a stable equilibrium branch with n>0,
 d log J/d log v = 1-v^2 P_XX/(m P_X) <=1.
This is a local constitutive slope at fixed mu_eff. It is not a claim about
the total radial derivative along a halo in which Phi or chemical state varies.

With a linear phase coupling to baryons, the phase-mediated acceleration is
proportional to v, while the integrated baryon source is proportional to J.
The desired deep-MOND constitutive law requires J proportional to v^2, hence
d log J/d log v=2. It cannot hold in this stable one-component class.
The full interpolation tested in the parent would require an even larger
local slope in its allowed domain. Choosing a different positive chemical
potential, the strength g, or an energy baseline does not change the inequality.

This result concerns the stipulated phase-mediated response. It does not rule
out a halo whose ordinary gravitational mass imitates a rotation curve, nor
does it rule out additional fields, finite-temperature two-fluid states,
nonlocal density-gradient effects, or nonequilibrium response. Those change
the assumptions rather than supplying a counterexample to the stated result.

4. Ground-state energy does not fix the cosmological constant

At positive density, pressure is 2g n^3/3-E0 and the internal energy density is
g n^3/3+E0. Their sum is g n^3>0; including rest-mass energy adds mn>0.
This is not a vacuum equation of state epsilon+P=0. The condensate's internal
energy cannot simply be substituted for a cosmological constant.
Moreover E0 is still independent of the density equation and susceptibility.
The matter response does not determine its gravitational matching. The
microscopic model therefore supplies neither the required MOND branch nor a
predicted vacuum/acceleration ratio of 32 pi.

5. Literature cross-check and an explicit escape condition

Berezhiani and Khoury, Theory of Dark Matter Superfluidity, arXiv:1507.01019v2,
Sec. 4.1, likewise distinguish a stable positive-X branch without the desired
MOND regime and an unstable negative-X MOND branch. Sec. 4.2 introduces
finite-temperature variables F(X,B,Y), including an example extra Y^2 term
that changes the time kinetic term while preserving the static profile.
Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.01019 (Secs. 4.1-4.2, Eqs. 63-65).
These facts are a cross-check, not a claim that their full microscopic model
is identical to the simple three-body condensate used here. This obstruction
is consistent with known work and is not presented as a new discovery.

The concrete escape requires an additional state variable. In the rest frame
of a normal component, X=Y-v^2/(2m). For an action F(X,Y), the static current
depends on F_X but the time kinetic coefficient involves
F_XX+2F_XY+F_YY. Those quantities need not have the one-variable relation above.
For the phenomenological F=P_-(X)+A Y^2, A>0, the time coefficient is
P_-''+2A. It can be positive despite P_-''<0. This illustrates which assumption
must change; it is not a derivation of A or a proof of stability of the normal
component. A full two-fluid microscopic construction, its mixed modes, and its
vacuum normalization remain missing. Selecting A by hand repeats the earlier
matching problem and is not a derivation of 32 pi.

6. Evidence and next discriminating task

Ten exact SymPy checks verify the density elimination, derivatives, static
slope, convexity witness, equation-of-state sum and baseline invariance.
Four Lean statements verify the negative-X endpoint inequality, the stable
response-slope bound, incompatibility with slope two, and the exact convexity

and matter/gravity matching are not formalized in Lean. This is self-review.

The next viable route must specify a second component or other additional
state variable before claiming a healthy microscopic response. It must derive
the joint susceptibility matrix and the baryon coupling, retain the normal
component's perturbations, and compute the total stress. A one-variable
repulsive density energy is now an explicitly rejected route under the
conditions above. No new value of the target coefficient has been obtained.
