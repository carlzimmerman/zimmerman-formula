2. Independent quadratic expansion
Write theta=omega t+pi and phi=y x+chi. Directly from the definitions,
 X=X0+2omega pi_dot+pi_dot^2-|grad pi|^2,
 U1=chi_dot-y pi_x/omega.
Since Y^2=partial phi.partial phi+U^2, its quadratic increment is
 Y^2=y^2+2y chi_x+|grad chi|^2
     -2y chi_dot pi_x/omega+y^2 pi_x^2/X0.
The chi_dot^2 terms cancel. At this background U0=0, so A'(y) first enters
A(Y)U^2 at cubic order. Expansion of P gives
 P2=(kappa+Delta)pi_dot^2/2-kappa|grad pi|^2/2.

For arbitrary differentiable F at y>0,
 F2=F''(y)chi_x^2/2+[F'(y)/(2y)]|grad_perp chi|^2
    -F'(y)chi_dot pi_x/omega+F'(y)y pi_x^2/(2X0).
Combining -F2 with A(y)U1^2/2 gives exactly
 L2=A chi_dot^2/2+B pi_dot^2/2

The mixed term does not rescue a negative spatial coefficient. Given the

This independently reproduces the stated polynomial and confirms the plus

4. Exact cone criterion and angular endpoint
Assume energy positive so A,B,C,E>0. Put alpha=C/A, beta=E/B and
rho=d^2t/(AB)>=0. The normalized polynomial is
 z^2-(alpha+beta+rho)z+alpha beta.
Its discriminant is (alpha-beta)^2+2rho(alpha+beta)+rho^2>=0;
its product and sum are positive, so both roots are real and positive.
Substitution at alpha and beta gives a nonpositive polynomial, proving
z_plus>=max(alpha,beta), z_minus<=min(alpha,beta).

The exact condition for z_plus<=1 is
 A-C>=0, B-E>=0, (A-C)(B-E)-d^2t>=0.
Necessity follows from the root inequalities and det M(1)>=0. For sufficiency,
these are exactly M(1)>=0. For v>1,
 d[M(v)/v]/dv=K+H/v^2>0,
so M(v) is positive definite and has no characteristic root above 1.
Together with positive roots this proves sufficiency, including equality.
It also verifies that the matter-timelike covectors v>1 lie in the same
positive branch of the two-field hyperbolicity cone.

Here A-C=q-gamma y t>=Z0>0 and B-E=Delta+r t. The matter-cone determinant is
 f(t)=Delta(q-gamma y t)-gamma y r t^2.
At t=0 cone inclusion forces Delta>=0. Its derivative is then
 f'(t)=-gamma y Delta-2gamma y r t<=0.
Thus the all-angle determinant condition is equivalent to f(1)>=0, namely
 Z0 Delta-gamma y r>=0.
This is exactly the stated Delta inequality. It is positive and therefore
also implies B>0 and B-E>0 once kappa>r is imposed. Conversely, positive
energy forces kappa>r, and the radial cone condition forces the same Delta
inequality. The iff claim, including its endpoints, follows.

The principal matrix at the matter cone is semidefinite, while the quadratic
Hamiltonian remains strictly positive because kappa>r, A>0 and B>0. These
are different matrices, so this is not an inconsistency.
Strict Delta inequality makes M(1) positive definite for every direction,
