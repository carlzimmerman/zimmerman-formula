1. Keep conversion separate from real mass transport

Let rho_s, j_s be settled cold mass density and radial mass flux, and
rho_u, j_u the unsettled component. Both are the SAME material cold sector.
With conversion rate C per volume,

 partial_t rho_s + r^(-2) partial_r(r^2 j_s) = C,
 partial_t rho_u + r^(-2) partial_r(r^2 j_u) = -C.

Total mass is conserved. Relabelling u as s cannot by itself move mass,
change velocity support, or supply/release binding energy. C>=0 encodes
irreversible conversion only if the theory identifies settling with this
material label; it is not automatically a property of a moving density cut.

Suppose the settled profile is exactly rho_*(r,t) H(R(t)-r). If its mean
radial flux is zero, distributional differentiation gives

 C=H(R-r) partial_t rho_* + rho_*(R-,t) Rdot delta(r-R).       (1)

For a static target all conversion lies at the front. Its mass rate is
dot M_s=4 pi R^2 rho_*(R-) Rdot. If the target changes throughout the
interior, pure front conversion no longer maintains it with j_s=0. A negative
partial_t rho_* locally also contradicts C>=0 under this zero-flux ansatz.
This is not a contradiction if settled material can redistribute: with no
interior conversion, the required interior flux instead obeys

 r^2 j_s(r,t)=-int_0^r s^2 partial_t rho_*(s,t) ds,

assuming regular flux at the centre. That flux has kinetic and energy costs.

2. Conservation specifies front speed only AFTER incoming flux is known

For a single total-density discontinuity with no surface mass storage or
singular mass source, the total continuity equation requires

 [j]-Rdot [rho]=0,
 Rdot=(j_out-j_in)/(rho_out-rho_in),                    (2)

where brackets mean exterior minus interior. All densities/fluxes in (2) are
TOTAL cold quantities, including any unsettled material on either side.
One must not substitute the settled density alone if components overlap.

In the restricted illustration j_in=0, rho_in>rho_out, and inward j_out<0,
Rdot>0. Specifying the cosmic amount or final radius does not determine j_out.
If both fluxes vanish and the density jumps, Rdot=0: a prescribed growing
front with no transport cannot satisfy continuity. When [rho]=0, (2) requires
[j]=0 and supplies no speed; division by the jump is then invalid.

Equivalently, in a fixed catchment of total mass M_C and known exterior
unsettled distribution, a mass equation can determine a final R implicitly.
It says nothing about which streams are captured, their travel time, torques,
or the receiver's energy transfer. This is why a mass-budget edge is not
already a dynamical free-boundary law.

3. The orbital cutoff adds an independent constraint

In a smooth time-dependent spherical mean potential Phi(r,t), specific
angular momentum L is conserved under the gravitational motion. Define

 E(t)=v^2/2+Phi(r,t),
 q=Phi(R,t)+L^2/(2R^2)-E(t).

Since Edot=partial_t Phi(r,t), direct differentiation gives

 qdot=[Phi_r(R,t)-L^2/R^3] Rdot
                  +partial_t Phi(R,t)-partial_t Phi(r,t).   (3)

An additional nongravitational specific power P adds -P to (3). Torques
add L Ldot/R^2 to the cutoff derivative and require their own orbit account.
Equation (3) is instantaneous; replacing partial_t Phi(r,t) by an orbit
average is a further adiabatic approximation, not an identity.

For the nondegenerate outer-turning branch, q>=0 labels orbits whose outer
turning radius is at most R in the instantaneous potential. Keeping this
population from leaving the domain requires qdot>=0 at its boundary in
deterministic phase-space evolution. Keeping every boundary characteristic
ON the boundary is the stronger qdot=0 condition. A single Rdot does not
generally make that stronger equality hold at every L and orbital phase in
an evolving potential. This does not prove the weaker inequality impossible.

Control: static Phi and R give qdot=0. Static Phi and increasing R give
qdot>0 for L^2<R^3 Phi_r(R): the expanding domain includes the old orbits.
Neither control guarantees the appropriate mass enters newly included radii.
Thus the mass-front condition and orbital-support condition are distinct.

4. Result and next discriminating requirement

Mass conservation yields (1)-(2), and mechanics yields (3). These equations
can reject inconsistent front prescriptions, but leave the incoming phase-
space flux, conversion physics and energy receiver unknown. Merely solving
the final mass equation or specifying Rdot cannot close all three.

A real capture model must supply the incoming f_u(r,v,t), a conversion rule
or interaction, and its energy/torque accounting, then satisfy these identities
while approaching the target. Neither an arbitrary absorbing boundary nor
the earlier imposed degenerate diffusion supplies that derivation. No claim
that all moving-boundary models fail, and no revised acceleration kernel.
