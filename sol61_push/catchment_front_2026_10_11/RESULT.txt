1. Keep conversion separate from real mass transport

Let rho_s, j_s be settled cold mass density and radial mass flux, and
rho_u, j_u the unsettled component. Both are the SAME material cold sector.
With conversion rate C per volume,

 partial_t rho_s + r^(-2) partial_r(r^2 j_s) = C,
 partial_t rho_u + r^(-2) partial_r(r^2 j_u) = -C.

Total mass is conserved. Relabelling u as s cannot by itself move mass,
change velocity support, or supply/release binding energy. C>=0 encodes
irreversible conversion only if the theory identifies settling with this
material label; it is not automatically a property of a moving density cut.

Suppose the settled profile is exactly rho_*(r,t) H(R(t)-r). If its mean
radial flux is zero, distributional differentiation gives

 C=H(R-r) partial_t rho_* + rho_*(R-,t) Rdot delta(r-R).       (1)

For a static target all conversion lies at the front. Its mass rate is
dot M_s=4 pi R^2 rho_*(R-) Rdot. If the target changes throughout the
interior, pure front conversion no longer maintains it with j_s=0. A negative
partial_t rho_* locally also contradicts C>=0 under this zero-flux ansatz.
This is not a contradiction if settled material can redistribute: with no
interior conversion, the required interior flux instead obeys

 r^2 j_s(r,t)=-int_0^r s^2 partial_t rho_*(s,t) ds,

assuming regular flux at the centre. That flux has kinetic and energy costs.

2. Conservation specifies front speed only AFTER incoming flux is known

For a single total-density discontinuity with no surface mass storage or
singular mass source, the total continuity equation requires

 [j]-Rdot [rho]=0,
 Rdot=(j_out-j_in)/(rho_out-rho_in),                    (2)

where brackets mean exterior minus interior. All densities/fluxes in (2) are
TOTAL cold quantities, including any unsettled material on either side.
One must not substitute the settled density alone if components overlap.

In the restricted illustration j_in=0, rho_in>rho_out, and inward j_out<0,
Rdot>0. Specifying the cosmic amount or final radius does not determine j_out.
If both fluxes vanish and the density jumps, Rdot=0: a prescribed growing
front with no transport cannot satisfy continuity. When [rho]=0, (2) requires
[j]=0 and supplies no speed; division by the jump is then invalid.

Equivalently, in a fixed catchment of total mass M_C and known exterior
unsettled distribution, a mass equation can determine a final R implicitly.
It says nothing about which streams are captured, their travel time, torques,
or the receiver's energy transfer. This is why a mass-budget edge is not
already a dynamical free-boundary law.

3. The orbital cutoff adds an independent constraint

In a smooth time-dependent spherical mean potential Phi(r,t), specific
angular momentum L is conserved under the gravitational motion. Define

 E(t)=v^2/2+Phi(r,t),
 q=Phi(R,t)+L^2/(2R^2)-E(t).

Since Edot=partial_t Phi(r,t), direct differentiation gives

 qdot=[Phi_r(R,t)-L^2/R^3] Rdot
                  +partial_t Phi(R,t)-partial_t Phi(r,t).   (3)

An additional nongravitational specific power P adds -P to (3). Torques
add L Ldot/R^2 to the cutoff derivative and require their own orbit account.
Equation (3) is instantaneous; replacing partial_t Phi(r,t) by an orbit
average is a further adiabatic approximation, not an identity.

For the nondegenerate outer-turning branch, q>=0 labels orbits whose outer
turning radius is at most R in the instantaneous potential. Keeping this
population from leaving the domain requires qdot>=0 at its boundary in
deterministic phase-space evolution. Keeping every boundary characteristic
ON the boundary is the stronger qdot=0 condition. A single Rdot does not
generally make that stronger equality hold at every L and orbital phase in
an evolving potential. This does not prove the weaker inequality impossible.

Control: static Phi and R give qdot=0. Static Phi and increasing R give
qdot>0 for L^2<R^3 Phi_r(R): the expanding domain includes the old orbits.
Neither control guarantees the appropriate mass enters newly included radii.
Thus the mass-front condition and orbital-support condition are distinct.

4. Result and next discriminating requirement

Mass conservation yields (1)-(2), and mechanics yields (3). These equations
can reject inconsistent front prescriptions, but leave the incoming phase-
space flux, conversion physics and energy receiver unknown. Merely solving
the final mass equation or specifying Rdot cannot close all three.

A real capture model must supply the incoming f_u(r,v,t), a conversion rule
or interaction, and its energy/torque accounting, then satisfy these identities
while approaching the target. Neither an arbitrary absorbing boundary nor
the earlier imposed degenerate diffusion supplies that derivation. No claim
that all moving-boundary models fail, and no revised acceleration kernel.

5a. Single encounter, static spherical potential and boundary

An incoming element at r=R has specific angular momentum L and radial velocity
v_r<0. Its energy is E_in=E_R(L)+v_r^2/2, where
E_R(L)=Phi(R)+L^2/(2R^2). Suppose a zero-width interaction at R preserves L
and changes only radial velocity. Its outgoing energy necessarily satisfies

 E_out=E_R(L)+(v_r,out)^2/2 >= E_R(L).

For the nondegenerate outer-turning branch L^2<R^3 Phi'(R), confinement to
apocentre <=R requires E_out<=E_R(L). Both conditions together force

 v_r,out=0, E_out=E_R(L), Delta E_removed=v_r,in^2/2.

Radial acceleration after this event is inward, so the material can enter
an orbit with apocentre R. This is an inelastic radial impulse, not ordinary
conservative gravitational capture. It retains rest mass and angular momentum
but still needs an energy receiver and a physical impulse mechanism.

5b. The resulting stationary orbit population has the wrong edge shape

After phase mixing in the frozen potential, such events at the same R populate
f(E,L)=K(L) delta(E-E_R(L)), up to the choice of positive orbit weights K.
On a nondegenerate L interval below the circular endpoint, section 6 of
kinetic_selector gives delta_r=E_R-V_eff(r,L)=a(L)(R-r)+O((R-r)^2).
Direct velocity integration then gives

 rho(r)=(4 pi/r^2) int L K(L)/sqrt(2 delta_r) dL
        ~ const*(R-r)^(-1/2)

for finite nonzero positive weights with the displayed integral finite.
This edge divergence is locally mass-integrable but is not the stipulated
finite-density jump. It differs from the integrable ENERGY distribution
f~[E_R-E]^(-1/2), which yields a finite spatial edge. Confusing these two
inverse-square-root factors would give a false completion.

A finite-width interaction can remove more energy after r<R and distribute
final apocentres below R. That is a genuinely different route, requiring its
energy-loss distribution and receiver to be calculated. It cannot be replaced
by the zero-width impulse above. Nearly circular populations, evolving Phi,
torques and finite-age unphase-mixed streams also lie outside this result.

5c. A smoothly advancing front does not automatically fix the distribution

As a restricted illustration let Phi be fixed, R(t) strictly increasing, and
capture at each time assign E_cap(L,t)=Phi(R(t))+L^2/(2R(t)^2). For fixed L,
 E_cap,dot=a(L,t) Rdot.
If the mass injection rate per dL is I_L(t), the final orbit mass per dE dL
is n_E=I_L(t_cap)/|E_cap,dot(t_cap)| before further evolution.

For a regular front-fed allocation I_L=Rdot k_L(t), with k_L finite and
a>0, n_E=k_L/a is finite even as the front slows. With finite nonzero radial
period, f=n_E/(8 pi^2 L T_r) is finite on an L interval away from L=0.
Its near-edge density contribution therefore vanishes as sqrt(R-r), rather
than approaching a nonzero constant. A singular residence/injection pattern
or another process is needed for the required energy accumulation.

This is not an exclusion of every advancing front: the allocation I_L, moving
potential, subsequent interactions, and capture away from the front were
restricted explicitly. It shows why simply choosing a front speed does not
derive the target phase-space population. Total front-fed conversion from
section 1 is proportional to Rdot for a finite target edge density; positing
nonzero total front-only injection while Rdot=0 violates that particular ansatz.

6. FOLLOW-UP: FINITE-WIDTH RADIAL DRAG HAS NO FINITE-TRANSIT ENERGY FOLD

Test a concrete mass-preserving, angular-momentum-preserving interaction:
radial acceleration a_drag=-gamma(r) v_r, gamma>=0, in a finite spherical
layer r_i<r<R. Phi is static, L fixed, gamma continuously bounded. No claim
that this phenomenological gamma is derived from the proposed dark sector.

On an inward passage with v_r<0 throughout the layer, put
V_eff=Phi+L^2/(2r^2) and E=v_r^2/2+V_eff. Then exactly

 Edot=-gamma v_r^2,
 dE/dr=gamma(r) sqrt(2[E-V_eff(r)]).                    (4)

The derivative is positive with respect to increasing r; the actual inward
motion decreases r and therefore decreases E. This sign matters.

Let E_out=T_L(E_in) be energy at the fixed exit radius r_i for entry at R.
Differentiate (4) with respect to E_in. The variational solution is

 dT_L/dE_in
 =exp(int_R^r_i gamma(r)/sqrt(2[E(r)-V_eff(r)]) dr)
 =exp(-int_entry^exit gamma dt)>0.                     (5)

For finite integrated damping Gamma=int gamma dt, the map is strictly
increasing, with nonzero Jacobian. It compresses energy intervals but does
not create a fold or an inverse-square-root singularity from a bounded
incoming energy distribution. In a neighborhood with uniformly bounded
Gamma, the outgoing distribution is bounded by the incoming bound times
exp(Gamma_max). This is a local result for complete, regular inward transits.
It excludes turning-point/long-residence limits where the assumed bound fails.

In particular, near a regular crossing of the capture threshold E_out=E_R(L),
the captured energy distribution is finite on the captured side. Phase mixing
with finite nonzero radial period does not change that into f~q^(-1/2).
For a regular nondegenerate L interval its contribution to spatial density
vanishes as sqrt(R-r), by the earlier orbit integral. Thus this finite-transit
drag mechanism alone does not produce the exact finite-density jump.

This does NOT exclude an observationally narrow smooth edge. Large but finite
Gamma can produce a compressed energy band. Locally a small incoming spread
Delta E is reduced by exp(-Gamma), corresponding to an apocentre spread
Delta r approximately exp(-Gamma) Delta E/a(L). Neither a required numerical
width nor a physical Gamma has been calculated here, so no empirical failure
or formation-time exclusion follows from the exact-jump result.

6a. A permanent dissipative layer keeps evolving the captured population

If the same radial drag continues after capture, its energy change per radial
cycle is

 Delta E=-int_cycle gamma(r) v_r^2 dt.

It is strictly negative for any orbit spending positive time with gamma>0
and v_r!=0. A stationary captured population on those orbits therefore needs
compensating energy input or a rule that disables/reverses the interaction.
Mass and angular momentum conservation alone do not supply that rule.
Orbits avoiding the layer and circular orbits are exceptions; their survival
does not select the required distribution of energies and L.

Combining (5) with this cycle identity distinguishes three mechanisms rather
than treating them as interchangeable: one-pass finite drag gives a smooth
energy map; infinite-residence limits can make that map singular; persistent
drag changes the supposed endpoint. A successful capture model must specify
which occurs, include its receiver, and show the desired supported final state.
