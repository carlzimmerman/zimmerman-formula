Route 1: exact phase-information discriminator (completed)
---------------------------------------------------------
Target hypothesis: ordinary gravity sees rho_b = rho_d + rho_h, while
the additional settling source depends on condensed rho_d (stars plus
cold gas), and not directly on hot rho_h. Compare instantaneous states
with the same total baryon density, cold mass, positions and velocities.
Let state A have (rho_d,rho_h)=(r,0) and state B have (0,r), r>0, in a
region; heating/phase preparation energy is accounted separately.

The current PM representation maps A and B to the same state: density,
catchment code, mass, scale factor and all fixed inputs are identical.
Every deterministic source functional of that representation therefore
returns the same answer. A genuinely phase-sensitive target whose source
changes on this pair requires different answers. Hence the current
representation cannot implement that target for both states.

This is an information obstruction, not a theorem excluding cold energy.
It does not require the two preparations to have equal thermal energy.
It does not assume the nonlinear phantom density is linear in rho_d.
If physical admissibility forces a unique phase fraction from the represented
state, that relation must instead be supplied and justified; CFG636 supplies
a fixed census fraction, not a thermal evolution equation.

Required distinction for a future test:
 (i) at fixed rho_d, changing rho_h leaves the DIRECT extra-source input
     unchanged, although ordinary gravity and subsequent evolution change;
 (ii) phase conversion at fixed total rho_b changes the extra-source input;
 (iii) removing all baryons removes the baryon-triggered source.
These are mathematical requirements, not new numerical results. Do not
expect the entire force or full time history to be invariant in (i).

Route 2: minimal reciprocal dynamics (conditional construction)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Work in a closed Newtonian domain, smooth fields, no boundary flux,
no explicit time dependence. Let f(x,v) be cold MASS phase-space density,
rho_c=int f dv. Let rho_d and rho_h be separately transported baryonic
phases. Consider a total energy

 H = K_c + K_b + U_b + W_grav[rho_c+rho_d+rho_h]
     + W_int[rho_c,rho_d] + H_receiver.

This is a class of models, NOT a specified successful action. W_int must
be supplied; it introduces no prescribed force until that is done.
For a differentiable W_int, write mu_c=delta W_int/delta rho_c and
mu_d=delta W_int/delta rho_d (specific energies for mass densities).
The interaction accelerations are -grad mu_c and -grad mu_d. Hot baryons
have no direct interaction term in this restricted ansatz. All mass still
sources ordinary gravity. A translation-invariant W_int is required for
internal momentum balance; arbitrary fixed spatial gates do not suffice.

For phase conversion Gamma>0 from hot to condensed,
 d_t rho_d + div j_d = Gamma,
 d_t rho_h + div j_h = -Gamma,
 d_t rho_c + div j_c = 0,
the exact chain rule and integration by parts give

 d_t W_int = int [j_c.grad mu_c + j_d.grad mu_d + mu_d Gamma] dx.

Interaction-force work on moving matter cancels the first two terms.
The last term remains: phase conversion changes interaction energy even
when no material moves. It must be balanced by thermal/receiver/reaction
energy at rate -int mu_d Gamma dx (in addition to ordinary latent heat
and momentum/kinetic changes of the phase transfer).

This identifies a missing term in any future model that simply switches
the coupling when gas cools. It does NOT establish a missing term in
CFG636, which does not claim to evolve that cooling process.

If an additional cold collision operator C[f] is proposed, its local
mass moment must vanish for pure redistribution: int C dv=0. Its energy
moment int (v^2/2) C dv must appear with opposite sign in the receiver,
and its momentum moment must have a specified reaction partner. These
constraints do not determine C, an equilibrium, or a settling rate.
They provide checks on a proposed completion; none is claimed here.

Self-review
-----------
The phase-pair argument is exact under its stated representation and target.
The energy identity is exact under the smooth, closed-domain hypotheses.
Dropping the boundary assumption adds the corresponding energy flux.
Nonlocal W_int is allowed if its functional derivatives exist. Dependence
on temperature or other fields adds chain-rule terms; the displayed ansatz
is deliberately restricted, not a universal no-go theorem.
