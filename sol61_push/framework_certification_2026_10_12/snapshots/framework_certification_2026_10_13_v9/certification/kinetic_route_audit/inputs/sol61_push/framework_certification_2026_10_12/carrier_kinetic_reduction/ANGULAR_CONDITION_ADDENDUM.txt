The total sigma_ab(w) used in the stated Maxwell heat moment additionally
requires isotropic outgoing COM scattering, as supplied by the low-energy
s-wave/contact approximation. Isotropic INCOMING velocity populations alone
are insufficient. Near the source/daughter pole, the exchanged-line differential
cross section may have angle dependence and cannot inherit this qualification
without checking the actual differential kernel.

Derivation from the already tested component energy identity
For COM velocity V and relative vector w, elastic scattering gives
 DeltaE_psi=mu V.(wprime-w), |wprime|=|w|.
For a rotationally invariant differential kernel about incoming w, integrating
outgoing azimuth eliminates transverse wprime components:
 integral wprime dσ = w integral cos(theta) dσ.
Therefore
 integral DeltaE_psi dσ = -mu V.w σ_T(w),
 σ_T(w)=integral [1-cos(theta)] dσ.
For isotropic dσ/dOmega=σ_ab(w)/(4pi), integral cos(theta)dσ=0,
so σ_T=σ_ab and the original stated total-cross-section expression applies.
For a general angular kernel it is σ_T, not totalσ_ab, in the heat moment.
Without axial/rotational symmetry a tensor/vector angular moment is required;
no scalar transport cross section is silently assumed for an anisotropic medium.

Independent incoming Maxwellian populations with zero means and variances sa,sb
satisfy E[V|w]=(m sa-M sb) w/[(m+M)(sa+sb)]. Including collision speed gives
 Q_psi = npsi nb mu (M sb-m sa)
         <σ_T(w) w³> /[(m+M)(sa+sb)],
 Q_b=-Q_psi.
Thus the same-temperature condition m sa=M sb remains the zero of this
instantaneous moment. Persistent Maxwell distributions, physical bath lifetime,
source conversion/replenishment and transport closure remain unproved.
Total collision count uses totalσ; momentum/heat transfer generally uses its
appropriate angular moment. They are not interchangeable rates.
