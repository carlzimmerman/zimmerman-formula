2. Exact stress identity

The free-particle momentum flux for a particle is p*p^T/E. With v_i=p_i/E_i,
the forward conversion changes the spatial kinetic stress by
 DeltaT=p1*p1^T/E1+p2*p2^T/E2-P*P^T/E_s
       =(E1*E2/E_s)(v1-v2)(v1-v2)^T.
Proof: substitute P=E1*v1+E2*v2 and expand; coefficients of the two diagonal
outer products and cross terms combine into the displayed square.
The coefficient is positive, so DeltaT is PSD and rank one if v1!=v2. Inverse
conversion changes it by -DeltaT. This holds in each common inertial frame with
positive energies; the numerical stress matrix is frame dependent.

This is the particle kinetic stress jump of a COMPLETED ON-SHELL EVENT. It is
not the full interaction-inclusive stress during coherent field conversion.
It is generally anisotropic. A scalar thermodynamic pressure needs an ensemble
and frame prescription; mixed forward/inverse reactions can have either net sign.

beta along z, gamma_b=1/sqrt(1-beta^2):

Both daughters are on shell; their energy/momentum add to the source exactly.
Using |p_i|^2/E_i=E_i-m^2/E_i gives

          =(M^2/E_s)*epsilon^2*(1-beta^2*n_z^2)
             /(1-beta^2*epsilon^2*n_z^2).
This is strictly positive for M>2m and finite |beta|<1. At beta=0 it is
M*epsilon^2; at threshold epsilon=0 it vanishes. This result does not require
adiabatic source elimination or a fitted halo temperature.
