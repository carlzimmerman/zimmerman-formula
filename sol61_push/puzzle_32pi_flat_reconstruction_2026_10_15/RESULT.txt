NEW RESULT
A nonzero scalar gradient permits inverse reconstruction of a static solution
with constant physical circular speed across an annulus of radius ratio20.
This goes beyond the previous single turning point. The potential is chosen
by the reconstruction, not the parent's quartic potential and not a prediction.
The reconstructed potential is concave along the sampled profile and the
constrained radial perturbation quadratic form is negative. We therefore
have a new failure mechanism to test: a flat canonical-scalar force profile
can exist locally but carry a growing radial mode.

Separately, in the weak-field, constant-coupling, scalar-force-dominated limit,
the logarithmic profile implies EXACTLY U_chichi=-2/r^2. An explicit Dirichlet
trial perturbation has negative quadratic form for log(r2/r1)>2pi/sqrt(7),
i.e. a radius ratio exceeding approximately10.7491. This limit calculation
is analytic; the full reconstructed annulus calculation is numerical with
symbolic constraint checks. Neither is a universal no-go for modified gravity.

1. What is changed and what is prescribed
Retain F=1+phi^2, xi=M=1, canonical Einstein scalar chi and Jordan metric
A^2 g_E, A=1/sqrt(1+phi^2). Replace the assigned quartic V by a potential
reconstructed along one monotone profile. Choose a desired circular speed
squared k=1e-6. This choice imposes the flatness to explore its consistency;
it does not derive k, a0, a source-mass relation or the number32pi.
The exterior annulus has no ordinary matter. No regular baryonic interior,
large-radius boundary, or globally defined potential extension is supplied.

2. Exact inverse-reconstruction equations
Use ds_E^2=-exp(2nu)dt^2+dr^2/N+r^2dOmega^2 and write
 x=phi^2, n=1-N, a=r nu', w=r(log A)', u=r^2 U,
 f^2=x/(1+7x), w=(k-a)/(1-k).
This last identity enforces the physical matter-frame circular speed
 V_c^2=(a+w)/(1+w)=k exactly.
The Einstein radial constraint and the evolution equations give
 u=n-2Na+Nw^2/(2f^2),
 dot(x)=-2(1+x)w,
 dot(n)=-2Na+Nw^2/f^2,
 dot(a)=-[(1-u)a+u]/N,
 dot(nu)=a,
where dot denotes d/dlog(r).
Reconstruct U=u/r^2. Define chi_dot=-w/f. The scalar equation requires
 G=r^2 U_chi=N[chi_ddot+(1+a+N_dot/(2N))chi_dot].
Direct differentiation of the reconstructed potential verifies
 (u_dot-2u)/chi_dot=G identically, not just at sampled points.
Also r^2 U_chichi=(G_dot-2G)/chi_dot.
Monotone x and nonzero w let U be defined as a single-valued function of chi
on the sampled interval. They do not supply a global extension.

3. Actual finite reconstruction
Initial r=1, x=.5, a=1e-10, u=1e-12; w=(k-a)/(1-k).
N is fixed by the radial constraint,
 N=(1-u)/(1+2a-w^2/(2f^2)).
Integration runs from r=1 to20, with two tolerance/step settings and801 samples.
It preserves x>0, N>0, w>0 and U>0 at the checked points. Results:
 physical Jordan radius span approximately20.00005992,
 maximum |N-1| approximately7.43e-10,
 minimum U approximately1.00e-12,
 r^2 U_chichi between approximately-2.00019169 and-1.99854480.
Two ODE settings differ in state by at most approximately5.0e-16 at samples.
This is strong numerical consistency for a deliberately constructed local
annulus, not a certified global solution or an observational fit.

4. Analytic reason for the negative curvature
In a weak metric with nearly constant nonzero coupling magnitude f, a
scalar-dominated constant V_c^2=k requires
 chi'= -C/r, C=M k/f,
 chi=chi0-C log(r/r0).
The flat static canonical equation chi''+2chi'/r=U_chi then fixes
 U_chi=-C/r^2,
 U_chichi=-2/r^2.
Integrating along the profile gives
 U(chi)=U0-C^2/(2r0^2) exp[2(chi-chi0)/C].
U0 can keep U positive over a finite interval, but cannot change its second
derivative. Thus positive potential energy by itself does not protect the
radial profile from instability. The exact reconstructed solution above
closely realizes this limit. Varying f, appreciable tensor contribution,
noncanonical kinetics, matter, or time dependence can change this reduction.

5. A trial-function instability criterion in the analytic limit
Perturb the canonical scalar and set psi=r delta_chi. On a finite shell
r1<r<r2 with Dirichlet delta_chi=0 at both ends, the radial quadratic form
(up to an overall positive angular factor) is
 Q=integral[psi'^2-2psi^2/r^2]dr.
Choose psi=sqrt(r) sin(pi log(r/r1)/L), L=log(r2/r1).
A direct exact integration gives
 Q=pi^2/(2L)-7L/8.
It is strictly negative if 4pi^2<7L^2. With positive kinetic weight, a negative
Rayleigh trial form on the regular finite interval implies a negative squared
frequency, hence a growing radial mode for this linearized problem.
The radius ratio exp(2pi/sqrt(7)) approximately10.749087 is a sufficient
negative-trial threshold, not a universal astrophysical instability scale.
No conclusion here applies to all modified kinetic or time-dependent theories.

6. Include the gravitational perturbation constraints
The previous test alone would hold the metric fixed. To audit that loophole,
perturb the full static Einstein-scalar system in areal-radius gauge:
 chi -> chi+eta(t,r), N -> N+deltaN, nu -> nu+deltaNu.
For a time-dependent mode the tr Einstein equation gives
 deltaN=-r N chi' eta/M^2.
The radial Einstein equation gives
 deltaNu'=(r^2 U/M^2-1)deltaN/(2rN^2)
           +r chi' eta'/(2M^2)-r U_chi eta/(2M^2 N).
The linear Hamiltonian constraint is satisfied by deltaN above using the
background equations; this is checked symbolically in check.py.
The lapse combination reduces to
 deltaNu'+(deltaN/N)'/2
 =[(1-r^2 U/M^2)chi'-r U_chi]eta/(M^2 N).
Substituting into the perturbed scalar equation yields
 exp(-2nu) eta_tt-N[eta''+(2/r+nu'+N'/2N)eta']+V_eff eta=0,
 V_eff=U_chichi+[2r chi' U_chi-(1-r^2 U/M^2)chi'^2]/M^2.
This accounts for constrained radial metric perturbations, rather than simply
assuming the metric response is small. It addresses this radial sector only.
The growing-mode momentum constraint has no independent time-independent
integration component; that component is not the instability tested here.

Multiplying the equation by exp(nu)r^2/sqrt(N) gives positive kinetic weight
W_t=exp(-nu)r^2/sqrt(N) and the quadratic form
 Q_full=integral exp(nu)sqrt(N)r^2[eta'^2+(V_eff/N)eta^2]dr.
Use eta=r^-1/2 sin(pi log(r)/log20) on the reconstructed annulus.
Numerical quadrature gives
 Q_fixed_metric approximately-0.9738731013996,
 Q_full approximately-0.9738731013861.
The maximum correction to r^2 U_chichi from the eliminated metric constraints
is about9.04e-12. The sign survives the full radial correction by a wide
numerical margin. A801/401-point quadrature comparison is recorded in stdout.
This numerical sign is not interval-arithmetic certification. The exact
negative form in section5 is a separate analytic limiting statement.
The boundary conditions are scalar Dirichlet conditions on this finite annulus;
eta=0 implies deltaN=0 there. The lapse is determined by its constraint and
a time-normalization choice. No globally matched material boundary has been
constructed. Smooth compact approximations to the trial function have the
same negative sign when their corrections are sufficiently small.

7. What the new calculation does and does not establish
We now have more than a single stationary-speed point: a local constant-speed
inverse reconstruction and an explicit stability obstruction for that example.
This gives a discriminating design condition for the next proposal: it must
produce the radial force profile while changing the constrained perturbation
operator enough to avoid the negative direction, not merely adjust an additive
vacuum constant. Noncanonical response or time-dependent stresses are possible
new hypotheses; they have not been shown to work here.
No32pi prediction, microscopic potential selection, source-mass scaling,
global boundary matching, observed galaxy fit or full-theory completion.
The prescribed flat profile is an engineering test, not a prediction.
