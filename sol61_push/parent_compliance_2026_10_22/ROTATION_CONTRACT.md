# Conservative rotational drive: bounded continuation

The passive scalar softens. Test one explicit independent drive rather than end with an unspecified source. A single conserved rotational coordinate theta has positive inertia I(chi)=I0+b/chi, b>0. Its Lagrangian is

 L=I(chi) theta_dot^2/2 + Mchi chi_dot^2/2 - mchi^2(chi-chi0)^2/2 - e^2/(2chi).

At FIXED angular momentum J=I theta_dot, the effective energy is J^2/[2I(chi)]+V(chi)+e^2/(2chi). Do not substitute fixed angular velocity into this energy. This toy represents a possible rotational coupling; it is not derived from existing theory and has new input b. A field-level precursor would be a positive susceptibility-dependent vorticity kinetic term, but no covariant constraint or causality claim is made for it.

Checks before execution: Routh reduction; positive kinetic Hessian on chi>0; drive condition at chi0; unique equilibrium in three declared examples I0=b=mchi=chi0=Mchi=1, e=.1, J=0,1,3; stable local curvature; angular velocity/inertia changes reported rather than hidden. For these examples monotonic E' follows from E''=1+e^2/chi^3-J^2/(chi+1)^3: positive for J<=1; for J=3, establish the positive curvature at the root only, not global uniqueness without a separate root count. Root isolation may use the exact polynomial and its positive real roots. A sign mutation must fail.

Success is a conservative finite-dimensional example of rotationally driven stiffening with a counted angular-momentum reservoir. It does not imply galaxy formation, dwarf excess, a universal vorticity selector, a covariant parent, or an observationally allowed modification of baryon inertia. Failure would close only this toy drive.
