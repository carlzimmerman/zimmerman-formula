1. CANONICAL SIGNS AND FACTORS

For each complex scaled field q=q_R+i q_I and momentum Pi=p_R+i p_I,
the REAL canonical momenta are 2r^2 p_R and 2r^2 p_I. On a radial basis
orthonormal in integral r^2 dr, real coefficient brackets are therefore
{q_R,p_R}={q_I,p_I}=1/2. Put the two species into each vector and order

 e=(Re F, Re X, Im Pi_F, Im Pi_X),
 o=(Im F, Im X, Re Pi_F, Re Pi_X),
 J0=(1/2)[[0,I],[-I,0]].

At the real rotating-frame background, conjugation/time parity makes the
quadratic grand Hamiltonian block diagonal,
 H2=(1/2)e^T He e+(1/2)o^T Ho o.
The exact canonical equations BEFORE charge/phase reduction are

 e_dot=J0 Ho o,
 o_dot=J0 He e.

Both displayed J0 signs are correct. The full Poisson matrix is
[[0,J0],[J0,0]], which is skew because J0^T=-J0. Confusing this with
[[0,J0],[-J0,0]] would give the wrong Hamiltonian signs.

The code's basis sqrt(2/R)sin(j pi r/R)/r has the claimed continuum
normalization. Its midpoint radial quadrature also gives the identity
Gram matrix for the resolved sine modes j<N, up to floating error. The
canonical factor 1/2 must nevertheless be retained in a dynamical test.

2. CHARGE TANGENT AND U(1) ZERO MODE

Let c=gradient_e Q_bar at the background, where Q_bar=GQ. The linear charge
constraint is c^T e=0. With background real amplitudes F0,X0 and imaginary
momenta P0,P1, the common positive-phase generator in the odd block is

 z=(F0,2X0,-P0,-2P1) projected onto the radial basis.

Direct differentiation of the code's charge convention gives exactly
 z=-J0 c.
The overall sign is immaterial to its span. A stationary U(1)-invariant
Hamiltonian has Ho z=0. The physical odd space is a quotient by span(z),
and the physical even space is c-perpendicular. In the unreduced system,
 d(c^T e)/dt=c^T J0 Ho o=z^T Ho o=0.
Thus the charge tangent is invariant when the phase-kernel identity holds.

A small phase Rayleigh quotient z^T Ho z/(z^T z) is NOT a sufficient
kernel test: Ho z can have orthogonal components even if that scalar is
small. Record its full norm, a scale-relative norm, the z+J0c residual,
and the basis Gram error.

3. EXACT SYMPLECTIC PROJECTION

Choose a matrix E with Euclidean-orthonormal columns spanning c-perpendicular.
One convenient PAIRED odd basis is
 O=-2 J0 E.
It is orthonormal and spans z-perpendicular. In the reduced coordinates
e=E x, o=O y, set He_r=E^T He E and Ho_r=O^T Ho O. Then

 A=E^T J0 O=(1/2)I,
 x_dot=(1/2) Ho_r y,
 y_dot=-(1/2) He_r x.

This is the charge-fixed, phase-quotiented canonical system, not an
independent deletion of an arbitrary eigenvector from each block.

Independent orthonormal null-space bases E and O are also valid, but the
cross bracket must actually be computed:
 A=E^T J0 O,
 x_dot=A Ho_r y,
 y_dot=-A^T He_r x.
For the stated orthonormal spaces A is invertible and A A^T=A^T A=I/4.
It is generally NOT a skew J0 matrix of the old dimension. Reusing J0
unchanged after separate null-space projections is wrong. Equivalently,
one may pull back the symplectic form and invert it; the same reduced
bracket follows. If the basis Gram matrix differs from I materially,
whiten the basis or derive the bracket with its mass matrix first.

4. PROOF OF THE GROWING-PAIR IMPLICATION

Assume Ho_r is positive definite. Then
 x_ddot=-B He_r x, B=A Ho_r A^T>0.
If B=L L^T is a Cholesky factorization, this second-order matrix is similar
to the real symmetric matrix

 S=-L^T He_r L.

By Sylvester inertia, S has a positive eigenvalue exactly when He_r has a
negative eigenvalue. Each positive eigenvalue sigma of S produces real
first-order exponents s=+sqrt(sigma) and -sqrt(sigma), with a nonzero odd
partner reconstructed from the first-order equations. The number of real
pairs equals the number of negative eigenvalues of He_r, counted with
multiplicity. Under these hypotheses all squared exponents are real;
zero exponents need separate treatment.

With paired bases A=I/2 one may instead factor Ho_r=L_o L_o^T and compute
the symmetric squared-growth matrix
 S=-(1/4)L_o^T He_r L_o.
This avoids interpreting spurious tiny complex parts of a nonnormal
first-order matrix. Check agreement with the correctly assembled full
reduced generator [[0,A Ho_r],[-A^T He_r,0]] and its +/- symmetry.

This theorem concerns COMPLETE canonical Hessian blocks, including both
fields and their momenta. It does not contradict the earlier warning
that a negative spatial daughter operator K_- alone need not imply
growth in a gyroscopic system: that weaker spatial condition does not
establish positivity of the complete complementary canonical block.
