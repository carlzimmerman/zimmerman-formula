# Can admissible relative vector or flat data repair the local clock equation?

No smooth transverse relative vector can repair a nonzero real decaying scalar on one torus Laplace-eigenvalue shell. The obstruction survives the allowed frozen scalar and linear homogeneous vacuum flat data on an open time interval. A weighted necessary condition also excludes an explicit genuine two-shell family f=cos x+αcos2x, for every real α, even allowing weak integrable divergence-free vector shifts of arbitrary spectrum.

These are scoped compatibility results for the unchanged projected action about coincident de Sitter. They do not classify all multishell/infinite-field preparations, other backgrounds, matter sources or altered operators. Relative vectors must first satisfy the actual Einstein momentum constraints: they cannot be freely assigned as additional longitudinal shifts.

## Actual first-order vector sector

Use n=3, K>0, H>0, a=exp(Ht), M=2K in each Einstein sector, the shared unitary clock θ=t and the parent projected action. Let each spatial vector perturbation be ∂iF_j+∂jF_i, ∂iF_i=0, with contravariant shift S^i_T, ∂iS^i_T=0. The gauge-invariant metric vector combination for an individual Einstein sector is W_i=Fdot_i−S_i. The projected interaction has no quadratic vector term: its leading I is |∇ν|²/a²a0², and its quadratic vacuum-volume difference depends only on the first-order scalar volume trace D. A transverse spatial vector has zero trace. This is not an assumption that the full interaction has independent metric coordinate symmetries.

The vector kinetic term follows directly from the ADM extrinsic curvature. For a plane transverse F_y(t,x), the spatial metric can be written exactly as the flat pullback a²[dx²+(dy+F_x dx)²+dz²]. Its determinant is a6 and its intrinsic curvature is zero. The linear mixed extrinsic curvature is δK_xy=δK_yx=(∂xW_y)/2, with zero trace. Hence one Einstein sector gives K a³(∂xW_y)²/2 at quadratic order. For pure relative data ±(F,S)/2 the two sectors sum to

L_vector,rel=(K a³/4)∫|∂W|²,

or Ka³k²|W|²/4 per complex transverse Fourier normalization. Real cosine averaging adds its usual one half. Rotation and the two transverse polarizations give the general finite-k transverse statement.

Varying the actual transverse shift therefore gives k²(S_T−Fdot_T)=0. For every k>0, S_T=Fdot_T and W_T=0. The remaining vector variation is then automatically satisfied; F_T is a **quadratic flat relative direction**. Only common coordinate transformations are symmetries of the full two-metric theory, so this is not permission to remove every relative F as full-theory gauge. Conversely, any smooth divergence-free V(t,x) can be represented as Fdot_T by integrating it in time with transverse initial F; allowing all such V is generous enough for the rescue test. A homogeneous uniform shift is also divergence-free and adds no vector-gradient action.

This sector has no first-order metric-vector curvature source. Its availability does not prove nonlinear physical health or that scalar-plus-vector initial data extend to the full action.

## Clock current with these allowed states

The independently normalized clock variation in the previous local-clock report gives, for arbitrary relative scalar lapse ν and relative contravariant shift ΔS,

E_θ,2=2Ka div[(Δν)ΔS+νdot∇ν].

This is the actual Euler density, not the spatially integrated equation. It follows from δa_i=−π_it+∂i(S^jπ_j)−[(∂t−S·∇)lnN]π_i, retaining temporal acceleration and projector variations before perturbation expansion. The first-order acceleration difference is ∇ν; spatial tracefree modes enter its clock variation only through the permitted shift. Pure transverse vectors have no scalar lapse contribution. Common first-order metric/clock data cannot change the leading quadratic relative current: the clock functional vanishes identically for equal metrics and is even in relative data by exchange.

For ν=a^(−1)f, Δf=−k²f, the scalar constraints supply S_scalar=2H∇ν/k² and νdot=−Hν. Add an admissible transverse shift V. The current divergence becomes

−3H div(ν∇ν)+div[(Δν)V]
=−3H div(ν∇ν)−k² V·∇ν,

using divV=0. On a compact torus a nonzero real f has a nonzero extremum. At that point ∇ν=0, so the vector contribution is zero for **every smooth** V, regardless of its time dependence or spectrum. The scalar term is +3Hk²ν². Thus E_θ,2=6KaHk²ν²≠0 there. Arbitrarily many transverse vectors cannot repair this shell.

There is an instructive inadmissible cancellation: V_long=−3H∇ν/k² cancels the scalar current everywhere, but divV_long=3Hν≠0. It is a longitudinal scalar shift, not an allowed transverse vector, and the source-free scalar lapse/shear equations have already fixed that shift. It cannot be inserted by calling it vector flat data.

The clock's whole linear Euler operator at coincidence vanishes. Therefore a nonzero E_θ,2 on these first-order data cannot be canceled by regular second-order metric or clock corrections. It represents a compatibility obstruction, also encoded by the second-order metric Noether identities, not an extra independent propagating equation.

## Frozen and homogeneous relative flat data

The source-free finite-k frozen scalar Z0(x) adds S_flat=−∇Z0/(Ha²), while its lapse time derivative is zero. At a nonzero extremum of the decaying eigenfunction f, its current-divergence contribution is k² f ΔZ0/(Ha³). The pure decay contribution is 3Hk²f²/a²; the transverse-vector term still vanishes there.

The actual homogeneous vacuum branch, not an arbitrarily chosen time-dependent lapse, has r=3ζ∞+2asinh[p/(m0a³)]. Its first-order relative lapse is c0+c3a^(−3). The time derivative of this homogeneous lapse adds a current contribution 3Hk²c3 f/a⁴ at the same extremum. Constant c0 has no effect. Relative homogeneous tracefree spatial data similarly have no direct leading acceleration/current term; this statement is not their full dynamical classification.

At the fixed extremum the three allowed scalar terms have distinct powers a^(−2), a^(−3), a^(−4). The nonzero leading coefficient 3Hk²f² cannot be canceled on an open time interval by the two constant flat-data coefficients, and the vector term is zero pointwise. An instantaneous adjustment is not continuation. A prescribed arbitrary homogeneous lapse time history would change the actual constraint premise.

## General weighted test and explicit multishell continuation

For a real zero-mean smooth f with general spectrum on a flat torus define u=(−Δ)^(−1)f and w=Δf. The pure decay scalar constraints give ν=f/a, S_scalar=2H∇u/a. A vector repair would require

V·∇w=(H/a)R,
R=div[f∇f−2w∇u].

For any smooth test function G, a periodic divergence-free V satisfies ∫G(w)V·∇w=∫V·∇A_G(w)=0, where A_G'=G. Therefore ∫G(w)R=0 is necessary. This also holds for integrable weak divergence-free V, because w is smooth and A_G(w) is an allowed smooth periodic test. Equivalently the forcing has to have zero integrated contribution on the relevant level sets/superlevels of w; a zero unweighted total integral alone is insufficient.

This does not make the test sufficient: even all weighted moments vanishing would not construct a smooth V or satisfy nonlinear metric/clock constraints. No general multishell existence is inferred.

For the explicit two-shell torus profile f=cos x+αcos2x, u=cos x+(α/4)cos2x, take G(w)=w². Direct exact trigonometric integration gives

∫_0^(2π) w²R dx=−(3π/2)(64α⁴+40α²+1)<0

for every real α. Extending uniformly in the transverse directions only multiplies this by their positive volume. The necessary weak-vector condition fails, including α≠0 genuinely involving two eigenvalues. Thus no periodic integrable transverse shift can repair this particular multishell family.

Adding frozen Z0 or the admitted c3 homogeneous relative lapse adds weighted forcing with different powers a^(−3) and a^(−4) to the leading a^(−2) scalar forcing. At each time the transverse weighted contribution is still zero. The nonzero leading weighted coefficient above cannot vanish on an interval by tuning constant flat-data coefficients. We do not claim the same polynomial sign for arbitrary multishell functions/phases or general time histories.
