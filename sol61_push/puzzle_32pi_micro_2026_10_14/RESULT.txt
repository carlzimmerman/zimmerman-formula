1. Constructive route: derive the logarithmic potential from microscopic states

Let independent microscopic variables y>=0 have baseline density p0(y)=exp(-y).
Couple a dimensionless source j>=0 by multiplying the statistical weight by
exp(-j y). Direct integration gives
 Z(j)/Z(0)=1/(1+j),  <y>_j=1/(1+j).
Define the macroscopic polarization coordinate x=1-<y>, so
 x=j/(1+j), j=x/(1-x).
With x=u/a and j=g/a this is exactly u=ag/(a+g), hence
 g_N=g-u=g²/(a+g).
These are ensemble means, exact for any number of independent constituents.
This identifies a concrete statistical mechanism for saturation: positive
microscopic amplitude decreases to zero as j grows.

The normalized cumulant generator of a constituent is
 K(h)=log <exp(hy)> = -log(1-h), h<1.
Its Legendre transform for a positive mean m is
 I(m)=sup_h [hm-K(h)]=m-1-log m,
 attained at h=1-1/m. Then m=1-x yields
 I(1-x)=-x-log(1-x)=phi(x).
For N independent constituents this is the large-deviation rate function.
It supplies the constrained free-energy density in the thermodynamic limit;
it is NOT the exact finite-N probability density including its prefactor.
For number density ncell and temperature T, matching the potential coefficient
requires ncell T=a²/Kgrav, Kgrav=4piG (kB=1).
The result is W-W(0)=(a²/Kgrav)phi(u/a), exactly the corrected local shape.

2. Absolute normalization test — distinct from the rate function

One concrete realization is the classical harmonic oscillator with canonical
coordinates and Hamiltonian H0, whose y=H0/T has this exponential distribution.
Using the usual phase-space cell 2pi hbar, its baseline partition function is
 Z0=T/(hbar omega). An additive microscopic energy Eoff multiplies Z0 by
exp(-Eoff/T); normalized probabilities and the rate function do not change.
Per unit volume (with constant oscillator parameters),
 free energy f=ncell Eoff-ncell T log[T/(hbar omega)],
 energy epsilon=ncell Eoff+ncell T.
The rate-function minimum I(1)=0 fixes a RELATIVE free energy, not either
absolute quantity. Interpreting W(0) as energy therefore gives, conditionally,
 C=1+Eoff/T.
Zero offset gives C=1, not16pi. C=16pi needs Eoff/T=16pi-1. The microscopic
oscillator has not fixed that offset. Interpreting W(0) as free energy instead
gives C=Eoff/T-log[T/(hbar omega)], a different expression with free state and
frequency parameters. Neither identification is automatic for gravity.

Thermal energy is not automatically cosmological vacuum energy. In an
extensive equilibrium fluid with zero chemical potential and no extra work
terms, epsilon+p=T s. At T>0,s>0 this excludes p=-epsilon. A fixed-number
oscillator medium instead requires its chemical potential and mechanical
stress to be specified; it cannot evade the missing stress calculation by
calling the rate function 'vacuum'. No vacuum equation of state is claimed.

3. Quantum continuation: an independent higher-order test

Quantize the oscillator: E_k=hbar omega(k+1/2), k=0,1,... . Put z=hbar omega/T>0.
 Z(z)=exp(-z/2)/(1-exp(-z)), nbar=1/(exp(z)-1).
The same source scales H0 to(1+j)H0. Its baseline-normalized response is

This approaches1 in the classical z->0 limit and0 at zero temperature.
Zero-point energy by itself supplies no polarization response in this
Hamiltonian-scaling construction: the ground-state eigenvector does not vary.
This is not a no-go for other quantum Hamiltonians with state mixing.

The geometric occupation distribution has

this simple quantum-oscillator family reproduces the full desired kernel.
