1. The rational law has an exact occupied-spectrum representation

Write K=4piG, p=|P| and u=Kp. The target is
 W(p)=[-a0u-a0^2 log(1-u/a0)]/K,
 W_p=a0u/(a0-u), 0<=u<a0.

Consider independently saturating two-state sites with a REAL physical
conjugate source E and local Hamiltonian
 H_Delta(E)=d[Delta*1+Delta*sigma_x-E*sigma_z], d>0.
The identity term sets each zero-field ground energy to zero. Its ground
energy lowering and polarization are
 F_Delta=d[sqrt(Delta^2+E^2)-Delta],
 p_Delta=d E/sqrt(Delta^2+E^2).
Delta is a reduced gap in field units; the actual level splitting is 2dDelta.
Let w(Delta)dDelta be occupied dipole magnitude per physical volume, rather
than a bare count of available modes. Then the positive distribution

 w(Delta)=p_sat a Delta/(a^2+Delta^2)^(3/2), Delta>=0,

gives exactly
 p(E)=integral w(Delta) E/sqrt(Delta^2+E^2)dDelta
     =p_sat E/(a+E),
 F(E)=p_sat[E-a log(1+E/a)].

This follows from an elementary primitive and a convergent energy integral;
an Abel inversion proves uniqueness within the stated independent-site
spectral class. The material energy is the convex Legendre transform of F.
The physical ground energy is lowered by F; it is not increased by F.

Rotational isotropy can be supplied without assuming all dipoles aligned.
For uniformly distributed fixed axes, use the different positive inventory
 w_axis(Delta)=p_sat a Delta(4a^2+Delta^2)/(a^2+Delta^2)^(5/2).
Its total unprojected dipole inventory is 2p_sat; orientation averaging
projects saturation to p_sat and produces the same radial rational law.
The source operator and axis/gap relation remain explicit microscopic inputs.

The ensemble starts with independent constants a and p_sat. To match the
gravitational target requires BOTH
 a=a0, p_sat=a0/K, equivalently chi(0)=p_sat/a=1/(4piG).
Then its Legendre energy is precisely W, and subtracting Kp^2/2 gives
 (W-Kp^2/2)_p=u^2/(a0-u).
No relation between the ensemble inventory/gaps and G was generated by the
spectral integration. That critical normalization is still a physical
matching obligation, as is a prediction of a0.

The spectrum is singular in two useful ways: its weight is proportional
to Delta near zero, producing the weak-field E|E| cusp, and proportional
to Delta^-2 at infinity, producing the 1/E saturation deficit. The first
gap moment diverges, so perfect saturation has infinite excitation cost.
A finite-state finite-Q cell with bounded internal Hamiltonian cannot have
the exact logarithmic barrier: for H0-E D, e(E)+E D_max stays bounded by
the variational principle. Its finite avoided crossings instead give a
square-root saturation with 1/E^2 deficit. Finite polynomial tree elimination
also gives algebraic energy branches, including algebraic critical branches;
it cannot equal the exact logarithm on an open field interval.

Finite cutoffs yield an explicit controlled approximation, not the exact
endpoints. If the same spectrum is retained only on ell<=Delta<=L, the
weak-field cusp requires max(ell,a^3/L^2)<<E<<a without retuning its slope.
To compare the strong-field deficit to the full inventory, require
 a<<E<<min(L,a^3/ell^2).
The exact cutoff expression and error bounds are in
rational_completion/RATIONAL_COMPLETION.md. Independent review discovered
the UV slope qualification; the final statement retains that correction.
Because the occupied spectrum reaches zero gap, a uniform adiabatic
finite-frequency elimination has not been established by the static result.

This construction was obtained by solving for the spectrum needed by the
target. It is an exact conditional model, not a prediction of the original
scalar action. The distinction remains essential despite the exact match.

4. A quantitative restoring-kernel obstruction

B>=0, fixed density vertex q and nonnegative local susceptibility s,
 chi(z)=s+q^dagger Kmat(z)^-1 q,
 chi'(z)=-(Kmat^-1 q)^dagger B(Kmat^-1 q)<=0.
Hence its material inverse-density kernel h=1/chi is nondecreasing.
The required positive K/z kernel decreases. Any uniform relative-error
approximation across a momentum ratio R obeys the sharp bound
 epsilon>=(R^2-1)/(R^2+1).
Even a factor 2 band therefore needs at least 60% kernel error; a factor 10
band needs at least 98.02%. These are material-kernel errors, not fitted
acceleration errors. Lowering the stable source gap does not reverse this
momentum dependence. h-K/z is strictly increasing, so a connected admitted
stable band has at most one Jeans crossing, rather than a scale-independent
critical cancellation. The exact proof and stated exceptions are in
static_kernel/STATIC_KERNEL.md.
