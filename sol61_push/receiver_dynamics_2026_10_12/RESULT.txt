1. CLOSED REDUCED DYNAMICS

 E=Dq+H, H=(pi^2+omega^2 phi^2)/2,
 S=-(A-q)^2/2,
 qdot=m pi^2(A-q), phidot=pi,
 pidot=-omega^2 phi-m D pi(A-q).
q represents a balanced transfer between populations; this scalar channel
neither creates mass nor specifies spatial momentum transport.

The constant Poisson matrix has L_phi,pi=1, L_pi,phi=-1. Define
 v=(pi,0,-D), M=m v v^T. Then M is positive semidefinite,
 M grad E=0, L grad S=0, and zdot=L grad E+M grad S is exactly the system above.
Consequently Edot=0 and Sdot=m pi^2(A-q)^2>=0. There is no Q/pi division.
The term '-mDpi(A-q)' is the reciprocal energy reaction. Removing it breaks
energy conservation; reversing the dissipative bracket makes S decrease.
This is a reduced degenerate-Hamiltonian/dissipative construction, not a
derived covariant action or a claim that S is physical thermodynamic entropy.

2. EXACT GLOBAL CAPACITY RESULT

A-q(t)=A exp[-m int_0^t pi(s)^2 ds], so 0<=q<A at finite time.
The energy identity gives H(t)=H0-Dq(t). q is increasing and bounded;
oscillator coordinates remain bounded and the smooth solution is global.

If H0=0, phi=pi=0 and q stays zero, even for D<0: there is no spontaneous
classical excitation in this channel.
For H0>0:
 D>0 -> q_infinity=min(A,H0/D).
 D<0 -> q_infinity=A.

Proof: q has a limit. If q_infinity<A, every limiting trajectory must have
constant q, because q(t_n+h) tends to that same limit for each fixed h and
smooth flows depend continuously on their bounded initial states. qdot=0
then forces pi=0 all along the limiting trajectory; pidot forces phi=0.
Thus H_infinity=0. Energy gives q_infinity=H0/D. For D>0 this determines the
energy-limited branch when H0<DA; for H0>=DA it rules out a limit below A.
For D<0 and H0>0, H=H0+|D|q cannot vanish, ruling out any limit below A.
No assertion of finite-time endpoint attainment is made.
