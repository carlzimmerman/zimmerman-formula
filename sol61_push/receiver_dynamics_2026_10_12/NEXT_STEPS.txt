2. DERIVE REAL TRANSPORT ENERGY AND MOMENTUM

Use cold phase-space density f_c(x,p), baryonic dynamical variables, the
gravitational field and a named receiver field with its conjugate variables.
State whether f_c is mass or number density; use the corresponding phase-space
measure consistently. Write ONE total energy, including cold and baryon kinetic
energy, gravitational self-energy with the proper half factor, interactions,
and receiver kinetic/gradient/potential energy. A prescribed static baryon
potential may be used only as a labelled diagnostic with its external reaction
ledger; it cannot certify isolated total-momentum conservation.

Calculate every variational derivative of this energy. Replace constant D by
the actual directional derivative of the FULL energy along each channel.
Start with a finite phase-space discretization: local velocity redistributions
with unchanged total mass and momentum, then spatial transfers with explicit
mass flux and receiver/baryon recoil. Declare the admissible channels before
testing; verify nonnegative occupations and boundary fluxes.

First concrete calculation: for mass weights f_alpha at fixed velocity bins,
write E_c=sum f_alpha |v_alpha|^2/2 + rho^T K rho/2 + rho^T Phi_b,
with rho=C f and a symmetric declared gravitational kernel K. Derive
partial E_c/partial f_alpha=|v_alpha|^2/2+[C^T(K rho+Phi_b)]_alpha.
Then add the dynamical baryon, receiver and interaction terms and recompute
the complete derivatives. Fixed-bin algebra is a diagnostic, not the final
continuity or momentum closure. Define each channel b_a and its actual cost
D_a=b_a dot grad_f E_total. Never retain the scalar theorem's constant D
when this derivative changes with the state.

Deliverable: DERIVATION.txt and symbolic mass, momentum and energy changes for
