common early density and duration measured in dynamical times does not select

Early cloud is a uniform, cold-only, self-gravitating virial sphere:
R_e=(3Mres/4pi rho)^(1/3), s^2=G Mres/(5R_e), t_dyn=(G rho)^(-1/2).
For a phase of N dynamical times at FIXED density and RMS relative speed
sqrt(6)s, define effective successful-capture cross section per mass S:
tau=rho S sqrt(6)s N t_dyn; F_capture=1-exp(-tau).
S includes actual cooling/capture probability; it is NOT geometric area.
This neglects baryons, tides, time-dependent collapse, depletion/merger spectrum,
heating and evolution of the cross section. Static Maxwellian/virial relations
are conditional benchmarks. No new original-field collision/PDE run was made.

2. Can velocity dependence remove the density contrast?
For independent centered isotropic Maxwellians, the relative-speed density
p(v|s)=v^2 exp[-v^2/(4s^2)]/(2sqrt(pi)s^3).
If s_d<=s_f, p_d/p_f <=(s_f/s_d)^3. Integrating against ANY common nonnegative
velocity-only successful-capture kernel S(v)*v with finite integrals gives
rate_d/rate_f <=(rho_d/rho_f)(s_f/s_d)^3.
Combining virial s proportional to M^(1/3)rho^(1/6) and duration N/sqrt(G rho):
    tau_d/tau_f <= (N_d/N_f)(M_f/M_d), provided s_d<=s_f.
The density cancels. This is a derived pointwise inequality, not a numerical
search over velocity exponents or an exhaustive claim about all physical DFs.

At COMMON density and N, required tau ratios exceed even this upper bound:
                   required vsFornax     maximum shared-kernel ratio
Draco                   48.16                    35.72
Sextans                339.70                    38.99
Both also fail the LeoI bound. Therefore any common velocity-only kernel fails
at equal density/clock under these assumptions, not just constant S.
For equal N, while the dwarf cloud is no hotter, changing density still cannot
repair that bound. To leave that branch requires density ratio above1275.9
Draco/Fornax or1520.6Sextans/Fornax (above46.2 and55.1 relative to LeoI).
This is NECESSARY only: becoming hotter removes this particular bound but
supplies no capture solution. The stronger1997/39096 values assume constant S.
An alternative with s_d<=s_guard requires N_Draco/N_Fornax>=1.348 and
N_Sextans/N_Fornax>=8.711; relative to LeoI >=2.654 and17.148. These permissive
bounds are not sufficient and need not be simultaneously achievable with one
kernel; no optimized physical kernel was constructed. Different populations,
phase distributions or potential-dependent capture invalidate the common-kernel
hypothesis and must be explicitly derived, not silently inserted as fitted rates.

3. Survival and spatial concentration are especially restrictive for Sextans
If assembly protects fraction f, later mass survival is s and final fraction
inside the aperture is A, then f*s*A >= F_required. Even f=A=1 needs
s>=.9885403 for Sextans: no more than1.146% of the REMAINING stock may be lost.
This differs from the older ~1.03% all-cold-stock margin because the assumed
retained component was already drawn and is held intact in this calculation.
At s=.99, ideal apertureA=1 requires f>=.9985256 and tau>=6.5195, rather than4.4689.
At s=.98 no amount of capture fixes the fixed-stock shortfall. At s=.99 and
A=.99 the product.9801 is also insufficient even with perfect capture.
For a final Plummer remaining component with scale b and R=647.853pc,
A=R^3/(R^2+b^2)^(3/2). Perfect capture/no loss permits b<=56.90pc;
with1% loss even perfect capture requires b<=20.32pc. A more extended or
stripped remnant needs additional supply or changed stellar/inventory inputs.
No total-stock loss from radiation or fragmentation has been inferred from
outside-lump periodic-box energy: that is neither escaped rest mass nor a
survival measurement. Sextans has no new SFH M/L input. At Upsilon<~1.97957
its old central fixed-stock aperture budget already fails at retention.1.

4. Physical decision and executable next steps
Early dense assembly is a viable CONDITIONAL exposure loophole, not a derived
mechanism. A successful theory must explain why the cold progenitors occupy
these very different density/clock/velocity regimes, export orbital energy
and angular momentum (not just rest binding), and protect Sextans' stock.

Next discriminators:

  cold DF and duration from the actual source/assembly model. Evaluate
  tau=integral rho(t)<S_cap(v,t)v>dt, with conserved mass/energy/current.
  Do not identify stellar SFH age with cold assembly age; CFG827 found no
  significant residual age trend after stellar M/L correction.
- Use the actual mediator-model fragment collisions to determine phase- and
  impact-parameter-averaged capture, radiation, mass loss and torque. Fusion
  only cools orbital energy when escaped energy exceeds released rest binding;
  this packet imports no cooling rate from geometric cross section/coarsening.
- Forward-evolve the compact remnant through gas loss, stellar assembly and
  tides. For Sextans, test the s*A ceiling BEFORE adjusting profile normalization.
  Include any unprotected cold background still inside Fornax/LeoI apertures.
- Fit spatially resolved stellar kinematics for the predicted compact remnant,
  and re-solve the source with matched normalization. Frozen CFG822 control
  remains FAIL; no amended criterion or observational verdict is claimed.
