2. General initial-rest theorem (not restricted to a chosen potential)

Use any differentiable U(x)>0. At u=x'=0 the Friedmann constraint gives h²=U/3.
The existing two homogeneous equations give
 eps u'-24 Fx h² h'=-U'+24 Fx h^4,
 8 Fx h² u'+2 h'=0,             Fx=1/(4x³).
Define
 q=3x³U'/U²,   w=[(2/3)U²]/[eps x^6+(2/3)U²],  0<w<=1.
The tensor kinetic coefficient is Q_T=1. Exact elimination gives
 c_T²=F_T=1+w(2-q),
 c_s²=1-w²(2-q).
Scalar kinetic is eps+(2/3)U²/x^6>0. Consequently 1<q<3 is a
sufficient simultaneous positivity condition at all eps>=0 (including the
formal eps=0 limit). At eps=0 it is also necessary. This is a theorem
about initial-at-rest states, NOT a theorem for evolving u!=0 states.
The finite-eps scalar formula matters: c_s²=2-c_T² only when eps=0.
check.py verifies these identities directly by eliminating both equations.

3. An explicit strong but shaped restoring potential

For ANY n>=1 choose
 t=tanh(4n log x),  A=1/(16n),  v=1-A t²,
 U_n(x)=3x²/v.
This is an additional model choice, not a derived microscopic interaction.
Since -1<t<1, 15/16<=v<=1. Thus U_n is positive and nonsingular on x>0.
The added potential U_n-3x² is nonnegative and vanishes only at x=1.
 U_n(1)=3, U_n'(1)=6, U_n''(1)=6+6n.
The chosen vacuum and its coefficient are preserved. The scalar mass is
 m_eff²/Hstar²=6n/(eps+6)>0,
which can be made arbitrarily large. At eps=1e-6 it differs from n by a
relative 1.67e-7; comparisons with the old nu=n model have this small change.

Differentiating t gives x t'=4n(1-t²), and therefore
 q=2v-xv'=2-t²/(8n)+t(1-t²)/2.
Using t²<=1 and -1<=t(1-t²)<=1 gives the conservative exact bounds
 11/8<=q<=5/2.
These imply, for EVERY x>0, n>=1, eps>=0 at initial rest,
 c_T²>=1/2,      c_s²>=3/8.
This explicitly defeats the inference that strong restoring mass necessarily
causes the earlier initial gradient failure. The quadratic SHAPE caused that
failure; it is not a no-go for all stabilizers. Arbitrary initial velocity,
later times, matter, nonlinear spatial health and ultraviolet behavior are
not covered by these inequalities.

5. Exact extra branch explains the restricted attraction

At constant x,h, stationarity requires q=2. Here it factorizes as
 q-2=t[4n(1-t²)-t]/(8n).
Besides t=0 (x=1), there is exactly one additional root with |t|<1:
 tstar=(sqrt(1+64n²)-1)/(8n),
 xstar=exp(atanh(tstar)/(4n))>1.
The other quadratic root is below -1 and cannot be a tanh value.
 xstar(10)=1.0654957256; xstar(100)=1.0092648546.
For 1<x<xstar, q>2 and initial-rest acceleration points downward; for
x>xstar, q<2 and it points upward. The two moving-away tests start above
xstar. This sign test does not by itself prove the complete phase-space basin.
As n grows, xstar-1 is asymptotic to log(16n)/(8n), narrowing the right side.

There is a general obstruction to demanding a globally inward initial-rest
force in this fixed inverse-square f class. If a C1 positive U on [1,infinity)
satisfies U(1)=3 and q(x)>2 for ALL x>1, set Y=1/U. Then
 Y'=-q/(3x³)<-2/(3x³).
Integrating on any interval [1,X0] yields some positive deficit d:
 Y(X0)=1/(3X0²)-d. Continuing the inequality gives
 Y(X)<=1/(3X²)-d for all X>=X0,
which becomes negative at finite X, contradicting U>0. Thus a globally
positive potential cannot have strictly inward at-rest acceleration everywhere
above this vacuum. This is a self-reviewed calculus argument, not Lean-
formalized here. It does NOT rule out all global attractors with more complex
trajectories or different f, fields or couplings.

6. Why this still cannot select 32pi²

For any C>0, F=-1/(8x²) is the same function after rescaling by Hstar.
Our chosen physical potential is V_C=M²(2Cb²/3)U_n(a/b). Thus the dimensionless
background, kinetic coefficients and speed checks are identical for every C.
C rescales physical time/mass units and sets Lambda/b²=2C; these dimensionless
vacuum health tests cannot uniquely select C=16pi, equivalently Aarea Lambda
=32pi² under the earlier Aarea=pi/b² convention. This is an explicit family
of alternative coefficients, not merely failure to notice the desired value.
A microscopic matching condition or an additional dimensional physical
constraint must break this freedom. The new potential introduces n and b;
neither is a coefficient-selection mechanism. Vacuum shifts are not protected.
