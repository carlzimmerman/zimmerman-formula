Sourced nonlinear polarization: formation test, 2026-10-12
Conclusion: the tested conservative model does NOT form its prescribed
static force profile within the declared 10% tolerance by the tested time.
This is a bounded formation failure, not a proof of impossibility or a
refutation of the user's acceleration relation. No complete theory obtained.

What was actually solved
------------------------
Full nonlinear radial equations of the effective two-field model, fixed
positive compact baryon density, initially Phi=P=all velocities=0.
rho_b is a Gaussian exp(-r^2/8), cut at r=8 and normalised to integral
rho_b*r^2 dr=1. Units C=a0=Z=I=1. Source coupling rho_b*Phi is included
in the conserved Hamiltonian. Baryons are held fixed; their assembly and
backreaction are not simulated. The initial-value problem therefore starts
with a prescribed source and a field out of equilibrium.

Radial finite volumes have an energy-adjoint gradient and divergence.
The static target is constructed on that same mesh from enclosed baryon
flux and the exact inverse-deficit interpolant. Its force residual is
checked independently of whether evolution approaches it. No relaxation
toward a supplied target is inserted into the evolution equations.

    t=560 25.94%, 580 25.88%, 600 25.81%, 620 25.77%, 640 25.74%.

   Formation gate: FAIL in this tested interval and radial range.

Allowing error does not mean silently expanding the acceptance band. The
10% physical-profile tolerance was declared before the first sourced run.
The roughly 26% discrepancy survives refinement substantially better than
that discrepancy. The 2.2% check is a bounded numerical diagnostic, NOT
a rigorous continuum error bar or an observational uncertainty estimate.
Refinement simultaneously changes space/time resolution. No convergence
order proof, nonlinear stability theorem or infinite-time exclusion follows.

Why the wave-packet successes were insufficient
----------------------------------------------
Removing free perturbation energy from a central window does not prove
formation around a permanent source. In the earlier constant-background
linear system the mode equation is
 omega^4-(k^2+a)omega^2+k^2(a-1)=0, a=1/(1-B').
For a>1 the low frequency tends to sqrt(a-1) at large k, with
 omega_low^2=(a-1)[1-1/k^2+O(k^-4)].
Its group velocity tends to zero as sqrt(a-1)/k^3. Thus the model contains
short-scale modes that transport energy poorly despite its positive energy.
This is an analytic local diagnostic, not an attribution of the measured
26% discrepancy to a specific mode; no mode decomposition was run here.

Decision and remaining physical work
------------------------------------
Do not promote 'polarization waves export energy' to 'cold matter settles
into the law'. The current conservative response model fails the concrete
finite-time formation test. Longer times, other initial conditions and a
physically derived dissipative channel remain untested continuations.
Changing Z/I or adding drag just to pass would introduce undetermined
response parameters, not derive a mechanism. A pressure/gradient repair
must also be checked for changes to the static law and for its energy source.

The field still has no independently conserved cold mass, physical response
times, relativistic metric/lensing completion or derivation of kappa=1/2.
The next high-value obligation is a carrier-derived relaxation/transport
kernel and receiver, with their fluctuation and energy budget, or an explicit
source-assembly history that produces the target without ad hoc relaxation.
This is not yet a full solution even though the prescribed static boundary
problem itself has been constructed and verified.
