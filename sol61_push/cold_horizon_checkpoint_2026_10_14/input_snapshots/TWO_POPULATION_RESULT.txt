2. Exact fixed-enclosed-supply split

F=G Mf/(a0 r^2), S=T+F. Given total cold enclosed mass Mc and static tether,
  T^2/(1-T)=B+F=B+S-T;
  T=(B+S)/(1+B+S), F=S-T.

F>=0 iff S^2+B S-B>=0, or

placed entirely in this force-free static state. At finite positive B+S the

3. Local positivity is a separate obligation
In units G=a0=1, let M(r)=Mb(r)+Mc(r). The aperture split is
  Mt=r^2 M/(r^2+M), Mf=Mc-Mt.
Differentiate BEFORE interpreting it as two densities:
  Mt'=[r^4(Mb'+Mc')+2r M^2]/(r^2+M)^2;
  Mf'>=0 iff Mc' M(2r^2+M)>=r^4 Mb'+2r M^2.
For a point baryonic source, this requires Mc'>=2r M/(2r^2+M).
Thus nonnegative enclosed Mf alone does not establish a physical free density.
Exact control outside prescribed compact sources: r=1, Mb=1, Mc=2,
Mb'=Mc'=0. It gives Mt=3/4, Mf=5/4, but Mf'=-9/8. The aperture passes the
mass test and fails density positivity. Imposing the zero-stiffness constitutive
law throughout a finite-support exterior therefore fails this particular
two-fluid interpretation. A finite edge can instead be a region where that
constitutive equation ceases to apply; boundary stress/stiffness must be solved.
This is not an exclusion of all finite halos or all two-population mechanisms.
