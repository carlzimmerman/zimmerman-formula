## 2. A genuine conditional selection from charged horizons

Define geometric mass m=GM/c² and charge length Q in the Einstein-Maxwell-Lambda metric

\[
f(r)=1-2m/r+Q^2/r^2-r^2/L^2,\qquad L=\sqrt{3/\Lambda}.
\]

Suppose b<c are distinct outer black-hole and cosmological horizons. Eliminating m and Q² from f(b)=f(c)=0 gives

\[
2m=(b+c)\left[1-(b^2+c^2)/L^2\right],\quad
Q^2=bc\left[1-(b^2+bc+c^2)/L^2\right].
\]

The equal-temperature condition uses the same Killing-time normalization at both horizons: f'(b)+f'(c)=0. Direct substitution factors its left side as

\[
\frac{(b-c)^2(b+c)[(b+c)^2-L^2]}{L^2b^2c^2}.
\]

For distinct positive horizons this forces

\[
\boxed{b+c=L,\quad bc=mL,\quad |Q|=m.}
\]

This is the known lukewarm branch, independently rederived here and checked against [Romans, Sec3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9203018v1). Its surface gravities have equal magnitude \(c^2\sqrt{1-4m/L}/L\). In rationalized units, |Q|=m corresponds exactly to the weak-field self-gravity-canceling charge/mass ratio above. For positive Lambda, lukewarm does not mean the zero-temperature extremal branch.

There is also an actual entropy selection criterion, with an essential restriction. Put b=Lx, c=L(1-x), m=Q=Lx(1-x), 0<x<1/2. Differentiate \(S=\pi(b^2+c^2)/\ell_P^2\) with respect to m BEFORE setting Q=m. At fixed Q,

\[
S_{,m}=0,\qquad
S_{,mm}=-\frac{2\pi}{\ell_P^2x^3(1-x)^3}<0.
\]

Thus this family is a local maximum of the summed horizon entropy under fixed-charge mass variations. Three independent finite differences of the quartic horizons verify the curvature. The fixed-charge premise is also explicit in [Kastor–Traschen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/9311025v1). Their radiation comparison concerns a massless scalar; it is not a production calculation for our charged massive carrier.

If Q is allowed to vary independently, however,

\[
S_{,Q}=-2\pi L/\ell_P^2\ne0
\]

at Q=m>0. The two horizon electric potentials also differ by Q(1/b-1/c)=1-2x. Equal temperatures therefore cannot be promoted to full chemical equilibrium or a prediction of every particle's charge/mass. Conserved global charge, exchange channels and particle microphysics remain necessary.

## 4. A concrete energy-export interaction, then its covariant correction

First try a new neutral potential chi with a positive wave action and a derivative density coupling:

\[
L_\chi=\frac{\dot\chi^2/c_\chi^2-|\nabla\chi|^2}{2K}
 +\tau\rho\dot\chi.
\]

In SI chi has potential units and tau is a time. The bath Hamiltonian is

\[
H_\chi=\frac{Kc_\chi^2}{2}(\pi_\chi-\tau\rho)^2
 +\frac{|\nabla\chi|^2}{2K}>0.
\]

The sourced wave equation and matter acceleration are

\[
\ddot\chi/c_\chi^2-\Delta\chi=-K\tau\dot\rho,
\qquad \mathbf a_\chi=\tau\nabla\dot\chi.
\]

Continuity gives the exact exchange identity: the bath gains \(-\tau\int\dot\rho\dot\chi\,d^3x\), the matter gains the opposite, and outgoing wave flux removes energy from a finite domain. Stationary rho and zero incoming chi allow chi=0, so this sector does not change the static kernel. This is an explicit conservative exchange interaction, not friction inserted by hand.

But a conserved spherical cloud has zero integral of dotrho. For a homologously breathing Gaussian of mass M, scale R and fractional amplitude epsilon, the radiating source transform is

\[
\delta M(k)=-\epsilon M(kR)^2e^{-(kR)^2/2}.
\]

Consequently its prescribed weak-mode power is

\[
\overline P=\frac{G\tau^2\omega^4}{2c_\chi}
 \epsilon^2M^2(\omega R/c_\chi)^4e^{-(\omega R/c_\chi)^2}.
\]

For the assigned galaxy R=rM=9.452 kpc, V=165.23 km/s, omega=V/R, cchi=c and tau=1/Hinfinity, the energy-loss time is 3.36e10 Gyr. A weak-coupling tau=.1/omega case takes 45.6 Gyr and introduces possible moving-source radiation when the cloud outruns the wave speed.

There is an even more basic covariance check: simply replacing rho dotchi with \(J^\mu\nabla_\mu\chi\) makes the interaction a boundary term when \(\nabla_\mu J^\mu=0\). That naive covariant replacement has no bulk radiation source. The density-derivative interaction is not adopted as the final parent.
