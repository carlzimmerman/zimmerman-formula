1. A possible density selector with a physical source operator
For an initially uniform virial cloud, Ei=-3GM^2/(10Ri), define
lambda=J sqrt(|Ei|)/(G M^(5/2)). For a later uniform spherical state,
I=2MR^2/5 and Trot>=J^2/(2I). The virial energy budget requires
R >= Rrot=(125/9) lambda^2 Ri. This is a NECESSARY angular-momentum floor,
not an attained rotating equilibrium; flattening/bar instability are omitted.
For common initial mean density and common R/Rrot, final density is proportional
to lambda^-6. The CE088 constant-capture density contrasts can therefore be
represented by lambda_Fornax/lambda_Draco=3.549 and
lambda_Fornax/lambda_Sextans=5.826. This is much less dramatic than the density
contrast, but these spins have NOT been predicted or measured.

Rotation must not also be counted as random collisional motion. The authoritative
refinement takes R=2 Rrot: half the virial kinetic energy is ordered rotation,
half random. Random RMS collision speed is reduced by sqrt(2); matching the
same ideal constant capture exposure then requires density higher by2^(3/4).
Relative spin/density ratios are unchanged. At lambda_Fornax=.18, one constructed
common initial density .011871Msun/pc3 has dynamical time136.8Myr and gives:

These reproduce the ideal CE088 exposure densities with separate kinetic shares,
not an evolution. The first two clouds still presuppose delivery of the whole

predict where cooling stops or which mass is protected.
For lambda_Fornax=.05 the same construction has an initial dynamical time~6.4Gyr,
so it is not automatically early. High spin and long assembly duration cannot
be freely exchanged without testing the formation clock and stability.

Uniform external gravity about the inertial COM supplies no spin torque. A
quadratic external potential has tau_i=-epsilon_ijk T_kl I_jl. In a planar frame
with diagonal tidal tensor, tau_z=(Tx-Ty)Ixy, with
Ixy=(I1-I2)sin(2theta)/2. A spherical or aligned patch has zero torque.
Misaligned triaxial patches can have different dimensionless spin even under the
same shear. If all other DIMENSIONLESS torque histories are held equal, Fornax
at45degrees and Draco/Sextans at8.18/4.94degrees illustrate the needed normalized
spin ratios. These angles are fitted geometric examples, not evidence for actual
alignments, probabilities or TOTAL-J ratios across different masses.
With e=Ixy/(M Ri^2), tg=sqrt(Ri^3/GM), the simple fixed-shape torque gives
lambda=sqrt(.3) e (T tg^2)(Delta_t/tg).
For e=.1, lambda=.18 in one tg requires shear comparable to several times the
cloud's self-gravity scale; a bound one-crossing seed cannot just be assumed.
Torquing before collapse or over a longer history needs an explicit source and
inertia evolution. Present/pericentric scalar tides tested in CFG838/839 are not
this primordial tensor history; their verdicts were not re-audited here.

2. Energy export need not be radiation from the entire halo
A core can lose energy by transferring heat to outer fragments. That differs
from the earlier isolated two-fragment fusion statement Kf=Ki+B-Eescaped:
core heat flow can matter even if no radiation leaves the WHOLE halo. It does
not supply a transport rate, and it need not put most halo mass into the core.
Balberg/Shapiro/Inagaki's gravothermal-fluid study provides an example of this
channel in a different SIDM model: https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0110561.
Only abstract/search metadata read; its rates/profiles/core fractions are NOT
imported into the ARGON action. A high central density is not proof of a core
containing nearly all Sextans' stock.

An isolated endpoint energy check illustrates the receiver burden. For initial
Sextans stock2.540e7Msun in a uniform virial cloud of radius5R=3239pc, a final
uniform virial core with mass fraction.988540 and radius25.21pc releases
6.37e46J of binding energy. If the allowed1.146% noncore stock alone escapes and
carries this energy, its speed at infinity is~469km/s. With1% ejected it is~502km/s;
with.1% it is~1588km/s. The remaining noncore material is assigned zero energy at
infinity in this limiting example. These are nonrelativistic energy budgets,
not demonstrated collisions, ejection distributions or angular-momentum balances.
They show that a small permitted mass loss does not by itself create an absolute
energy-conservation prohibition; the fast-tail production mechanism is missing.
Photons/neutral radiation, outer bound material or escaping fragments would
require their own reciprocal budgets. No radiative/thermalization kernel inferred.

3. Could the compact mass survive?
Tested static Plummer remnants in the prescribed exponential-law MW field with
host baryonic mass6e10/8e10Msun. Compact remnant self-gravity is assumed Newtonian.
For a circularized orbit evaluated at an ASSUMED pericentre Rp,
T=Omega^2-Phi''=(g/Rp)[3-s/(exp(s)-1)], s=sqrt(G Mhost/a0)/Rp.
For Plummer scale b and initial mass M, the self-consistent Jacobi truncation is
rt=sqrt[(GM/T)^(2/3)-b^2], if positive. Mbound=M F(rt),
F(r)=r^3/(r^2+b^2)^(3/2). No time-dependent tidal shock, gas heating, orbit decay,
halo response or actual Sextans pericentre is supplied.

This is an aperture-mass normalization under the stated truncation, not a

Important survival bookkeeping: s*A is the surviving fraction times its
post-stripping aperture fraction. If rt>R, stripping ONLY outer material leaves
M(<R) unchanged: s*A=F(R). Do not count outer mass stripping once as total loss
and again as an unchanged normalized aperture fraction. CE088's s*A ceiling
remains correct, but its independent loss/Plummer examples are not a prediction
of the correlated tidal truncation here. Violent expansion or inner heating
can still reduce the inner mass and must be evolved explicitly.
