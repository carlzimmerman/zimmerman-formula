Checked 2026-10-22 against primary full-text PDFs through the web tool. No local PDF cache or source-file hash was created; the URLs and locators below record the evidence consulted, not a locally authenticated cache. No novelty claim is made.

## Chandrasekhar (1943)
S. Chandrasekhar, “Dynamical Friction. I. General Considerations: The Coefficient of Dynamical Friction”, Astrophysical Journal 97, 255–262.
Published scan: https://blackholes.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/gritting/pdf/astrophysics/Chandrasekhar_Dynamical_Friction.pdf
The ADS PDF endpoint was inaccessible. The accessible scan identifies the author, title, journal and pagination. Section 1, equations 6 and 10–12 treat drift and diffusion together and recover Maxwellian equilibrium; section 2 derives encounter effects. This supports retaining diffusion in an equal-mass population. It does not establish the collision operator, scattering cross section or capture efficiency for the present extended field cores.

## Ostriker (1999; preprint 1998)
E. C. Ostriker, “Dynamical Friction in a Gaseous Medium”, Astrophysical Journal 513, 252–258. Checked arXiv astro-ph/9810324v1, dated 20 October 1998: https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/9810324
Equation 12 gives the force prefactor; equation 16 on PDF page 10 gives the collisionless comparison with F(X)=erf(X)-2X exp(-X²)/sqrt(pi), X=V/(sqrt(2)sigma). Equations 14–15 distinguish the gaseous wake. The opening discussion describes background heating as the reaction to orbital energy loss. The circular isothermal torque reduction in this checkpoint assumes a light collisionless Maxwellian bath, a small perturber, and a scale-separated Coulomb logarithm. It cannot be imported unchanged into a hot gas or equal-mass core bath.
