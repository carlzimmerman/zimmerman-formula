# DOOR D5 — Exploit the linear-acceleration / orbit split
STATUS: OPEN | RANK: 15 | COST: S | KILLS FAST: no

> Read `../02_HOUSE_RULES.md` and `../04_FRAMEWORK_FACTS.md` before starting. One door per cycle.
> κ = ½ is FITTED, NOT DERIVED — nothing in this door changes that unless it says so explicitly.

## The door
Established: the MI action works **exactly** for torsion-free (hyperbolic, linearly accelerated) motion and
fails for orbits — and MOND is about orbits. Rather than treating that as a defeat, use it: find a physical
system where the motion IS torsion-free and the framework therefore makes an **exact** prediction with no
suppression and no approximation.

## Why it works with the framework
It turns a known limitation into a test. The framework is exactly right somewhere; find where and go look.

## Concrete first calculation
1. Enumerate torsion-free or nearly-torsion-free accelerated systems at low acceleration: radial infall,
   free-fall in a uniform field, the turning points of a highly eccentric orbit (where v -> 0 so the torsion
   `|a|/tau_1 = v/c -> 0`), linear accelerator geometries.
2. For each, compute the framework's exact prediction and the size of the effect.
3. Rank by observability.

## Settles if / refuted if
CONFIRMS: a real system with small torsion and a measurable effect ⇒ a clean test where the theory has no
wiggle room.
CLOSES: every torsion-free system has an unobservably small effect ⇒ say so with the numbers, and the split
stays a theoretical curiosity.

## Known walls — do not rediscover
`|a|/tau_1 = v/c` exactly — that is proven. Radial/eccentric orbits are the obvious candidates and the corpus
already has "ephemeris de/dt" as an open item; check it before duplicating.
