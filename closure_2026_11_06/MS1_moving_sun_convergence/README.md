## Route chosen, on the equations, before any number

Route 2 (drop q_B, keep q) is rejected. By AC15 S1b, q_B is the khronon's own global-Bernoulli constraint
evaluated on the simulated flow, so it cannot be dropped as "the wrong proxy". The reason it converges
slowly is dq_B = -M_inf^2 w dw: velocity truncation error amplified by 5.5e4-2.4e5. That is a reason, not a
bound.

Route 1 is taken: a three-rung Richardson / GCI bracket on the coarse, medium and fine rungs on disk. A
fourth rung was not run; it would take about 25 h per M_inf.

## What the ladder shows (measured)

- **q_B falls at every rung in every series** (168 of 168 series: coarse > medium > fine). At M234 the fall
  speeds up from medium to fine. For G1 Mars the worst cell goes 5.0e-2, 4.1e-2, 2.3e-2 (R about 2), and
  24 q_B series are DIVERGENT. So the three rungs are not in the asymptotic range there, and no
  extrapolated bound exists. At M490 every q_B series is MONOTONE with p 0.51-2.09, and the largest upper
  bound is 0.055 of the line (G1 Mars).
- **The q reading** varies by about 1e-5 to 1e-3 of the line between rungs, with no consistent direction:
  0 of 168 series fall monotonically. It has 15 DIVERGENT and 13 PRE-ASYMPTOTIC series.
- **The planted control explains the q failures.** A planted 0.1x anomaly is exact and the same on every
  grid, yet it reads 0.1015, 0.1015, 0.0989 on the three rungs. That is the grader's nearest-edge readout
  radius, the same residual as AC24's P1 (up to 4.6 %). It turns TINY base series into DIVERGENT planted
  ones. So the readout's own grid jitter is larger than the q signal's grid change, and the three-rung test
  cannot resolve the q reading. The q failures sit at that jitter level, up to 1.3e-3 of the line.
