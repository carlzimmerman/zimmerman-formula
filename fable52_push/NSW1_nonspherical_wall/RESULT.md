## 1. The answer

SWT1 proved two things for a sphere around point baryons. For discs and non-spherical walls:

- **Compensation survives exactly.** The exterior effective mass equals M_b to 1.7e-12 in every solved case (120 spheroid
  cases, 18 free-boundary walls, two grids each). Beyond its projected extent a law-on region of any shape lenses as M_b.
- **The wall pressure has a closed form with a tangential field** (new; certified in Lean by three routes):
  p_wall = (a0^2 / 4 pi G) [u - ln(1 + u) - n^2 / (2 (1 + u)^2)], u the law's field just inside the wall, n its normal part.
  It is SWT1's W when the field is normal and rises to J's bracket when the field runs along the wall.
- **The cap generalises and holds.** p_wall <= a0 |g_out| / (4 pi G) at every wall point (Lean), so the total normal force is
  at most a0 M_b Gamma_w with Gamma_w = (integral of |g_out| dA) / (4 pi G M_b). Gamma_w reaches 2.62 in the family (2.01
  among admissible cases), yet F_n itself never exceeds 0.64 a0 M_b. The largest column a wall element carries is 0.645 of
  a0 / (4 pi G), that is 34.5 / 41.7 M_sun / pc^2 (bound 53.4 / 64.6); among admissible cases 0.069 (3.7 / 4.4).
- **Shape buys nothing at equal reach.** Flattening or stretching a wall moves the force toward that of the nearest wall
  point and lowers the cost per unit volume, but the law's field inside is lost with it: a flattened bubble does not keep the
  law in its own plane beyond about its half-thickness (in-plane deviation 40 to 84 % at q <= 0.5, a = 10 r_M), and its
  lensing reach shrinks in step. Measured against the sphere of the same reach, the force gains at most 21 % and the
  allowance loses up to a factor 4.7.
