## What is now implemented

The nonradial l=m=2 retarded **kinetic** response of the chosen PN28 canonical
waterbag, including reciprocal current feedback, gravity and scalar spatial
response in the explicitly declared fast-scalar limit. No isotropic fluid
closure, fitted damping or externally imposed torque efficiency is used.
The original FR01 dilute baseline, compact endpoint and diffuse control are
reconstructed from immutable input snapshots.

The computations resolve finite-pulse work and torque in the specified
frequency set. Positivity is a finite Hamiltonian result, not continuum
stability. Full versus bare differences are combined collective effects,
not evidence that the scalar alone supplies a new mechanism.

## What has not followed

The first unsolved implication is **energy/angular-momentum exchange → a
sustained change of the spherical mass profile**. The first-order quadrupole
has zero spherical average. A mean core expansion requires a second-order
l=0 calculation or conserving finite-amplitude evolution. It must respect a
finite baryonic energy/angular-momentum reservoir and track the radius
enclosing the original FR01 core mass. No step here reverses the original
31.4% compact-state contraction.

Late quadrupole amplitudes are not numerically resolved enough to identify a
physical damping rate. Local external work within the core is not retained
heat. Some finite-duration responses decrease when the pulse is lengthened;
there is no justified frequency-independent drag/absorption law. The longer
compact pulse remains limited by Krylov convergence and is not physical
evidence for either strong or weak sustained transfer.

Finite scalar speed, nonlinear vacuum crossing, formation/state selection,
the independent B/V normalization, the universal acceleration and cluster
gravity remain open. Dimensionless pattern speeds were scaled to each
background separately, so cross-state ratios are not a common physical-bar
experiment. The prescribed quadrupole is not a live measured stellar bar.
