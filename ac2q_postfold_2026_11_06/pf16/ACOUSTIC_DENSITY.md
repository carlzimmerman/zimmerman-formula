# A local density cannot cancel native acoustic compression

This is a test of a particular transport-theorem route, not a native weak
nonexistence theorem. Work on the compact on-branch packet domain, with
h,R>0, c²=h/(rR), and A_q=(1-c²+r c c_r)/2>0. Derivatives in r hold A fixed.

The exact coupled equations retain the following highest-gradient terms:

    n r=-r c² e(theta)+bounded,
    n theta=-e(r)/r+bounded,
    div_2(n+sigma c e)=e(theta)+sigma(c_r-c/r)e(r)+bounded.

The bounded remainders include the actual native auxiliary rates and connection
under the existing compact/variation assumptions. Let rho>0 be a C1 function
of r,theta and any additional variables whose n/e derivatives are bounded.
This covers A,P and zeroth-order metric/clock coefficients in the bootstrap.
It does NOT automatically cover B=n beta, E=e beta, first metric derivatives,
clock gradients beyond r,theta, or history-dependent variables.

Set D_sigma=n+sigma c e. For div_2(rho D_sigma) to be free of both compressive
derivatives as a universal identity in this compact state domain, its two
coefficients must vanish:

    rho-r c² rho_r+sigma c rho_theta=0,
    sigma rho(c_r-c/r)+sigma c rho_r-rho_theta/r=0.

Eliminate rho_theta. The necessary condition is

    rho(1-c²+r c c_r)=2 A_q rho=0.

Since A_q>0, no such positive rho exists. Thus a regular density depending only
on these local states cannot provide the bounded-divergence acoustic flux
needed by this proposed nearly-incompressible transport route. Normalizing the
acoustic field by its positive time component does not avoid the obstruction:
that normalization can be absorbed in the proposed local density.

The native scalar current does provide bounded-divergence control for the
clock-normal direction under bounded auxiliary derivatives. That direction is
n, not either acoustic direction n+sigma c e. Its density cannot be silently
reused to control acoustic compression.

## Limits

This excludes a universal state-only cancellation formula. It does not exclude
a nonlocal/history-dependent density, a density with additional uncontrolled
derivative variables, or a solution-specific construction. Nor does it prove
that an unweighted weak transport equation has no uniqueness theorem. The
actual weak equations remain defined; only this shortcut to the requested
dynamical theorem fails. No new physical term is implied by that failure.
