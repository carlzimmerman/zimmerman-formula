# Korean fried chicken, and the beer to drink with it

Not physics. This is a real recipe, kept here at the owner's request. It has
nothing to do with the folders in this repository that use "fried chicken" as a
label for a research exercise.

In Korea the pairing has its own word: **chimaek** (치맥), from *chikin* (fried
chicken) and *maekju* (beer).

## What makes it Korean

- A thin starch crust instead of a thick flour batter. It fries hard and glassy.
- Two fries: a cooler one to cook the meat, a hotter one to crisp the crust.
- Sauce goes on at the last moment, in a thin coat, or not at all.
- Cold pickled radish on the side to cut the fat.

## Ingredients (serves 3 to 4)

**Chicken**

- 1.4 kg (3 lb) chicken wings, split into drumettes and flats, tips removed.
  Boneless thigh in 5 cm pieces also works.
- 2 tsp fine salt (10 g)
- 1/2 tsp ground black pepper
- 1 tbsp finely grated ginger
- 1 tbsp rice wine (cheongju or mirin), optional

**Coating**

- 120 g potato starch (about 3/4 cup). Cornstarch works; the crust is a little
  less crisp.
- 30 g plain flour (about 1/4 cup)
- 1 tsp baking powder

**Frying**

- 1.5 to 2 litres neutral oil with a high smoke point (peanut, rice bran,
  canola or sunflower)

**Yangnyeom glaze (sweet and spicy, the red one)**

- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 3 tbsp gochujang (Korean chilli paste)
- 3 tbsp ketchup
- 3 tbsp rice syrup, corn syrup or honey
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp brown sugar
- 1 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tsp gochugaru (Korean chilli flakes), optional, for more heat
- 2 to 3 tbsp water

**Soy garlic glaze (ganjang, the brown one)**

- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 6 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp finely grated ginger
- 4 tbsp soy sauce
- 3 tbsp rice syrup or honey
- 2 tbsp brown sugar
- 2 tbsp rice wine or mirin
- 1 tbsp rice vinegar

Each glaze is enough for the whole batch. For half and half, make half of each.

**Pickled radish (chicken-mu)**

- 500 g Korean radish (mu) or daikon, peeled, in 1.5 cm cubes
- 240 ml (1 cup) water
- 120 ml (1/2 cup) white vinegar
- 100 g (1/2 cup) sugar
- 1 tsp salt

**To finish**

- Toasted sesame seeds, crushed roasted peanuts

## Method

1. **Pickle the radish, the day before if you can.** Stir the water, vinegar,
   sugar and salt until dissolved; no heat needed. Pour over the radish in a
   jar and refrigerate. Good after a few hours, best after a day. Keeps two
   weeks in the fridge.

2. **Season the chicken.** Pat it very dry with paper towel. Toss with the
   salt, pepper, ginger and rice wine. Leave uncovered on a rack in the fridge
   for at least 1 hour, up to overnight. Dry skin is what lets the crust stick.

3. **Coat.** Mix the starch, flour and baking powder. Toss the chicken in it,
   press the coating on, shake off the excess. Rest on a rack for 10 minutes.
   The coating will look damp and patchy; that is right, and it stops the
   starch falling off in the oil.

4. **First fry, 165 °C (330 °F).** Use a heavy pot no more than half full of
   oil, at least 5 cm deep, with a thermometer. Fry in batches so the oil stays
   above 150 °C: 8 to 10 minutes for wings, 5 to 6 for boneless thigh, until
   pale gold and cooked through (74 °C / 165 °F inside, minimum). Drain on a
   rack, not on paper. Rest 10 minutes.

5. **Second fry, 190 °C (375 °F).** 2 to 3 minutes, until deep golden and the
   crust sounds hard when tapped. Drain on the rack.

6. **Make the glaze while the chicken rests.** Warm the oil in a wide pan over
   medium heat, cook the garlic (and the ginger, for soy garlic) for 30 seconds,
   add everything else, and simmer 2 to 4 minutes until glossy and thick enough
   to coat a spoon.

7. **Toss and serve at once.** Turn off the heat, add the hot chicken to the
   glaze, toss for 20 to 30 seconds for a thin even coat. Sesame seeds and
   peanuts on top. Radish on the side.

For plain chicken (huraideu), skip the glaze and serve with a small dish of salt
and pepper. Half plain and half sauced (ban-ban) is the usual order in Korea.

## Why the steps matter

- **Starch, not flour.** Starch forms almost no gluten, so the crust sets thin
  and brittle instead of bready.
- **Two fries.** During the rest, water moves from the meat into the crust and
  softens it. The second, hotter fry drives that water off. This is why the
  crust stays crisp under sauce.
- **Baking powder.** It makes small bubbles in the crust, which gives more
  surface and a lighter crunch.
- **Thin glaze, last moment.** A crust that sits in sauce goes soft in about
  ten minutes, however well it was fried.

## Safety

- Dry chicken spits less. Lower pieces in away from you.
- Never fill the pot more than half full; the oil rises when the chicken goes in.
- Keep a lid next to the stove. If the oil catches fire, cover it and turn off
  the heat. Never use water.

## The beer

**The short answer: a very cold, pale, crisp, fizzy lager.** That is what
Korean chicken shops pour, and it is the right call. The chicken is rich, salty
and often sweet and hot. A light lager does not compete with any of that: the
carbonation scrubs the fat off the tongue, the cold calms the chilli, and the
low bitterness leaves the glaze alone.

| Chicken | Best | Also good | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain (huraideu) | Korean pale lager, or a pilsner | Helles, Kölsch | Stout, porter |
| Yangnyeom (sweet, spicy) | Korean pale lager, as cold as it goes | Witbier or hefeweizen | Strong, bitter IPAs |
| Soy garlic | Korean pale lager | Vienna or amber lager, dunkel | Sour beers |

- **One bottle to buy:** Cass Fresh or Terra. Hite is the same idea. Kloud is
  all malt and a little fuller, good with plain or soy garlic chicken.
- **Outside Korea:** a Japanese rice lager (Asahi Super Dry, Sapporo), a
  Mexican lager, or any clean pilsner.
- **Why not an IPA with the red one:** bitterness and alcohol both make chilli
  burn harder, and the hops fight the sweet glaze. With plain chicken a
  moderate pale ale is fine.
- **Serving:** 2 to 4 °C, chilled glass, draft (saeng-maekju) if you can get it.
- **Somaek:** one shot of soju (50 ml) topped with about 120 ml of lager, a
  ratio near 3 to 7. Traditional with chicken; stronger than it tastes.

## Keeping and reheating

Fried, unsauced chicken keeps two days in the fridge. Reheat on a rack in a
200 °C (400 °F) oven or an air fryer for 6 to 8 minutes, then glaze. Do not
microwave; the crust turns to leather.
