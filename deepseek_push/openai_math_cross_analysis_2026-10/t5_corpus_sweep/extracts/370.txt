**Abstract**

We prove the subcritical Hénon–Lane–Emden conjecture: for every dimension *n ≥* 2, positive powers *p,q*, and real weights *A,B*, the system has no strictly positive entire solution when (*n* + *A*)*/*(*p*+ 1) + (*n* + *B*)*/*(*q*+ 1) *> n −*2. Solutions need only be continuous at the origin and classical elsewhere, without a condition at infinity. For *n ≥* 3 and *A,B > −*2, combining this result with the radial existence theorem of Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini proves Phan’s Conjecture C in full: a positive radial entire solution exists whenever the strict inequality fails.

**1 Introduction**

The Hénon–Lane–Emden system is

*−*Δ*u* = *|x|* *A* *v* *p* *, −*Δ*v* = *|x|* *B* *u* *q* in ℝ *n* *\{*0*}.* (1.1)

Its subcritical nonexistence conjecture asks whether the weighted hyperbola alone rules out positive solutions, without symmetry or conditions at infinity. The unweighted case *A* = *B* = 0 is the classical Lane–Emden system. The scalar subcritical Liouville theorem of Gidas and Spruck is a starting point for this question [5]. For systems, Mitidieri developed a Rellich identity and early nonexistence regions [11, 12]. Serrin and Zou proved the unweighted three-dimensional assertion under a growth condition;

Poláčik, Quittner, and Souplet removed that condition, and Souplet settled dimension four [14–16]. Li, Li, and Wei obtained a further unweighted nonexistence region for *p,q ≥* 1 in every dimension *n ≥* 5 [9, Theorem 1.1]. Liouville theorems also serve as inputs to rescaling arguments for a priori bounds and singularity estimates [6,14]. For the weighted system, Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini identified the critical hyperbola in the regular radial theory under admissible weights [1]. Phan formulated the unrestricted weighted question for *A,B > −*2 as Conjecture C and established bounded, low-dimensional, and conditional cases [13]. Fazly and Ghoussoub obtained bounded and stability-based results for nonnegative weights [4]; Li and Zhang subsequently proved the weighted conjecture in dimension three without a boundedness assumption [10]. Results in restricted exponent ranges extend to dimensions four and five [8]. More recently, Huang and Zou stated the all-real-weight, origin-continuous assertion as Conjecture B and proved cases with stability, decay, or energy hypotheses [7]. Xu and Luo obtained a higher-order weighted theorem over the full exponent range for radial solutions subject to regularity and growth conditions [17]. The full nonradial question in higher dimensions is the subject of this paper.

*−*Δ*u* = *v* *p* *, −*Δ*v* = *u* *q* *in* ℝ *n* *.*

In particular, positivity at the origin is part of the hypothesis, but differentiability there is not. No radial symmetry, boundedness, integrability, finite energy, stability, or growth or decay condition at infinity is required. Theorem 1.1 resolves Huang and Zou’s Conjecture B positively and yields Corollary 1.2 by setting *A* = *B* = 0. For *n ≥* 3 and *A,B > −*2, its nonexistence conclusion joins the radial existence theorem of Bidaut-Véron and Giacomini [1, Theorem 1.4(i)] to prove Phan’s Conjecture C in full, including existence on the critical hyperbola. Corollary 6.2 records this derived classification. The weak regularity at the origin is genuine: positivity and continuity, rather than a prescribed order of vanishing or smoothness there, are all that is assumed. The weighted assertion cannot be inferred merely by applying the unweighted assertion at the same exponents. For instance, *n* = 5, *p* = *q* = 4, *A* = *B* = 3 satisfies Equation (1.2), whereas 1*/*(*p* + 1) + 1*/*(*q* + 1) = 2*/*5 *<* 3*/*5. Likewise, the unweighted input in Phan’s transfer argument must remain within its actual scope; weighted subcriticality does not imply that input [13].

**The weighted argument.** Rellich–Pohozaev identities and spherical estimates have long been central to system Liouville proofs [11,13,16]. Chen and Li used Newtonian integral representations with moving planes under integrability hypotheses [2]; Cheng and Huang later formulated an energy-estimate criterion for the unweighted conjecture [3]. The passage from a uniform local weighted-energy bound to nonexistence by origin-centered dilation also appears in Li [8, Section 2]. Here a localized potential-kernel virial identity and an energy-relative interval-pressure estimate supply that bound throughout the subcritical range. There are two difficulties in passing from unweighted source layers to Equation (1.1). Trans- lations do not preserve the equation, and an endpoint of a layer *{|x|* *B* *u*(*x*) *q* *> t}* has value (*t/|x|* *B* ) 1*/q*, not the constant *t¹* */q*. We address both directly. First, origin-centered dilations give uniform source masses and truncated-potential bounds on a fixed ball. A compactly supported virial identity then retains the exact spatial-weight coefficients. Second, the interval comparison is applied with directions in a small cap around the radial direction and with weights constant on each retained interval. Retained intervals are short enough that their endpoint weight variation is small. The discarded intervals are controlled by polar integration of source masses and a high/low layer decomposition. All errors are arbitrarily small relative to a fixed localized energy, up to a universal additive constant. No bound on that energy is assumed in obtaining the error estimates. The weighted subcritical gap then supplies such a bound, and dilation gives a contradiction. This combination of spatially varying directional cutoffs, intervalwise infima, and energy-relative layer errors is the part of the method that may be useful for other weighted potential systems. The elementary interval comparison itself is reproved below.

**Organization.** Section 2 treats the origin, the low-exponent cases, and the potential estimates. Section 3 proves the localized virial identity and compares the two localized interaction marginals. Section 4 gives the geometric interval inequality. Section 5 proves its weighted pressure consequence, including the needed layer-error bounds. Section 6 completes the proof by scaling and derives the existence–nonexistence classification from the radial theorem.
