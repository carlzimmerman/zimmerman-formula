History and significance.Coulomb multi-marginal transport arises in the strong-interaction limit of density functional theory. In this setting the description of optimal configurations by measure- preserving maps is usually called the Monge or co-motion ansatz. The co-motion formulation was developed in the strictly correlated electron literature; see Seidl, Gori-Giorgi and Savin [11] and the account by Friesecke, Gerolin and Gori-Giorgi [7]. The distinction between equality of optimal values and attainment by maps is essential. Colombo and Di Marino [3, author version, Theorem 1.1] proved that the Coulomb Kantorovich minimum equals the infimum over measure-preserving cyclic maps for identical non-atomic marginals. This provides approximating maps without asserting that a minimizing map exists. Section6gives a direct proof of equality of the two infima for the particular marginal constructed here. For two particles, Cotar, Friesecke and Kl¨uppelberg [5, arXiv version, Theorem 3.6] proved existence and uniqueness of an optimal map for absolutely continuous marginals in every dimension. For arbitrary particle number on the line, Colombo, De Pascale and Di Marino [2, Theorem 1.1] constructed optimal cyclic maps for identical nonatomic marginals with finite transport value. Pass [9] constructed Coulomb optimizers that are not induced by maps for radial data; the existence of such an optimizer does not exclude a different optimizer of Monge form. Colombo and Stra [4] gave counterexamples to the prescribed radial quantile-cyclic constructions. Bindini, De Pascale and Kausamo [1] subsequently constructed smooth densities for the reduced radial cost of the planar three-particle problem for which none of the four prescribed maps III, DDI, DID and IDD is optimal. This radial cost is obtained by minimizing the Coulomb interaction over angles; it is different from the Coulomb cost on the line. Idea of the proof.The construction has two parts: a geometric condition that forces every optimal plan onto two prescribed families of triples, and a mass imbalance that prevents a preserving map from choosing between them. Let *B* be a small ball about zero, and let *e₁,e₂* be the first two coordinate unit vectors. We construct smooth diffeomorphisms *Yk,Wk*, for *k* = 1*,*2, from *B* onto small components near*ek,−ek*, respectively. The two families are

(*x,Yk*(*x*)*,Wk*(*x*))*, x∈B, k∈{*1*,*2*},*

together with their coordinate permutations; see Figure1.

The triples (0*,ek,−ek*) have the same Coulomb cost and the same central gradient. This allows the two local families to share a supporting potential on *B*. Completing this potential on the four outer components gives an inequality *c*(*x₁,x₂,x₃*) *≥u*(*x₁*) + *u*(*x₂*) + *u*(*x₃*), with equality precisely on the two families. Strict cost gaps exclude the other triples of distinct centers; the Coulomb singularity excludes repeated components after the neighborhoods have been made small enough. For a smooth probability measure *ν* on *B*, we form the marginal *µ* by giving the central component measure *ν/*3 and each outer component measure (*Yk*)#*ν/*6 or (*Wk*)#*ν/*6. Randomly choosing a branch and permuting its three coordinates then gives an optimal coupling with this common marginal. Every optimizer must saturate the supporting inequality. In particular, on the central component, a deterministic map must choose one of the four branch images. If it chooses a branch *H* on a Borel set *S⊂B*, it sends mass *ν*(*S*)*/*3 into *H*(*S*), which has marginal mass only *ν*(*S*)*/*6. This contradicts preservation on every selection set of positive mass. The exact image-measure relation, rather than the component totals alone, excludes arbitrarily irregular choices of branch. Sections2–5establish the mass obstruction, construct and certify the two local families, and form the smooth marginal and an explicit optimizer. Section6constructs preserving maps whose costs approach that optimum. Section7extends the construction to every inverse-power Riesz cost in every Euclidean dimension at least two.
