Here B's four SLACS gaps (lensing mass versus dynamics mass, CFG33's pipeline: canonical and alt footings, V and I aperture readings) are re-derived with independent code and paired with the LCDM side on identical bootstrap resamples, over CFG86's 54 declared cells. Independence as disclosed in the docstring: the agent read the helper code of h53 (lines 1-215), so its B architecture follows it; its projection is its own (integration-by-parts form; agrees with a brute-force trapezoid to 2.9e-5); the LCDM side is the CFG86 code reused (`cfg86_lcdm_copy_reference.py`), not new; the CFG33/CFG80 outputs were opened only after both runs.

**What the agent would attack.** (1) The robust statement is a difference between the models: B sits about 0.12 dex above LCDM (median +0.118 over 54 cells, at 7.5 sigma or more in the base cell). That is not 'B-specific': 'specific' needs LCDM's own gap inside 2 sigma, which depends on the halo tie, mass and redshift conventions. (2) W = 0.519 sits about 2 points above the 50% not-supported line, and 5 of the 54 cells have |z_stat| within 1.7 to 2.3; the thresholds are a declared rule, and the extended cells give W = 28%. (3) Reproducing CFG33 to 1e-6 shows the shared pieces are the same, not that they are right: Auger's f_*, the Prugniel-Simien deprojection (0.5% off the exact Sersic law), the M_JAM/2 at r_1/2 convention from h9, the constants, and the Moster / Duffy / Dutton-Maccio inputs (from memory). (4) The halo-free gap +0.191 is the common floor: B removes only about 0.032 of it (phantom about 14 to 20% of the Einstein mass in projection) and LCDM about 0.154, so D measures the difference in projected dark mass inside R_E, not a property of the law. (5) Not checked: the sigma-aperture and V/I ambiguity beyond the four cells, paired estimator variants beyond the base cell, and a redshift-consistent B (B is z-free here). (6) Pairing shares the same 187 calibrators, which is right for the question but lets calibrator systematics cancel in D and not in each model's own gap.
