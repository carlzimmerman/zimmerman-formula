**Bottom line.** ADF22.5 has no stellar mass (no M\*, no HST detection, no continuum on disk), so its only baryons are the CO gas, and what the row measures is the **CO conversion the dynamics allow, not a₀**. With the lowest conversion in use (α_CO,min = 0.8, r_J1 = 1: a lower limit, the headline) g_bar is 2.06 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² against g_obs = 8.38 × 10⁻¹⁰ (D = 4.06, y = 2.2) and the row has a root: **s\* ≤ 27.6 (a₀ ≤ 2.6 × 10⁻⁹ m s⁻²; Monte Carlo 68 % 19.8–38.9, 95 % 13.8–53.3; recipe half-width 0.45 dex), a bound 5.8× above the rival law's 4.72 and 27.6× above FLAT: VACUOUS under the frozen rule (it excludes neither law).** With the Galactic conversion (α_CO = 4.36) the gas alone exceeds the dynamics: **FLOOR (D = 0.746; 94 % of the draws have no root)**. Under this geometry **the dynamics allow α_CO ≲ 3.25; the conversion at which each law reproduces g_obs exactly is 3.01 (FLAT), 2.61 (CFG223's PROXY) and 2.21 (a₀ ∝ H(z))** (post hoc, same estimator): the two laws differ by 0.135 dex in the conversion they need, the two conversions in use by 0.736 dex, so the row cannot separate the laws. A stellar mass can only lower the bound: for an assumed log M\* = 10.5 the bound is s\* ≤ 7.2, at 10.8 it is ≤ 1.4, and above 10.87 the row is a floor. κ = ½ is FITTED. No law statement is made.

## What the numbers say
- **The dynamics.** V_ext = 303 +16/−18 km s⁻¹ at R_ext = 3.55 kpc is g_obs = 8.95 a₀ (M_dyn(< R_ext) = V²R/G = 7.6 × 10¹⁰ M☉, a scale). That is a near-Newtonian radius: a₀ does not set it unless the baryons are known to a few per cent (CFG240 T3), and here the baryons are known to a factor 5.5 (the conversion).
- **The conversion equivalents (closed form).** The floor boundary D = 1 is α_CO = 3.251; s\* = 1 (FLAT) at 3.004 (the main run's 0.01 dex grid) = **3.007 exactly**; the rival law's s = 4.72 at **2.205**; the PROXY's s = 2.72 at **2.610** (`cfg283_posthoc_alpha_equiv.py`, written after the main run, not a frozen item: the main run's own shift to s\* = 1 is +0.575 dex = 3.00). All three lie between the declared lower limit (0.8) and the Galactic value (4.36). **They depend on the geometry**: the assumed R_e = R_ext/1.2 = 2.96 kpc (R_e × 1.5 moves g_bar by −0.23 dex, and D = 1 to α_CO ≈ 5.6; R_e / 1.5 by +0.14 dex).
- **The knobs on the B1 bound (Δ log₁₀ s\*).** geometry +0.29 (R_e × 1.5) / −0.20 (R_e / 1.5) / +0.14 (spherical); gas conversion −0.32 (r_31 = 0.6) / −0.19 (helium × 1.36); radius +0.08; kernel P2 +0.09; inclination +0.07 / −0.05; pressure +0.01 / +0.03. All eleven variants keep a root; individually none moves the bound below 13 (r_31 = 0.6 gives 13.2), and the quadrature recipe range is 9.7–78. The all-baryon bands are ±0.15 dex [17.1, 42.6] and ±0.30 dex [9.6, 63.7]; the gas bands ±0.213 dex [13.6, 50.6] and ±0.671 dex [no-root corner, 162].
- **The M\* ladder (assumed stellar masses added to the B1 gas in the same disc; NOT data):** log M\* 9.5 → D 3.59, s\* ≤ 23.4; 10.0 → 2.87, ≤ 17.0; 10.5 → 1.75, ≤ 7.16; 10.8 → 1.12, ≤ 1.44; 11.0 → 0.787, FLOOR. The floor boundary is at log M\* = 10.866, s\* = 1 at 10.820 (the closed form returns s\* = 1 to 0.005 dex, M7). A literature stellar mass would place the row on this ladder; **up to log M\* = 10.5 the bound (≤ 7.2) stays above the rival law's 4.72**.
- **The SELFTEST (what the estimator can do on this row).** 100 worlds exactly on the law at s = 2 with the B1 baryons, 0.15 dex scatter and 10 % V errors: 89 rooted and 11 at the floor; in the 89 rooted worlds the 95 % interval contains s = 2 in 79 (89 %); s\* median 2.12, 16–84 % [0.61, 6.12]. At y = 2.2 the row is conditioned, so **if the gas mass were known the estimator would recover a₀ to a factor of about 3 (1σ); the vacuity is the conversion, not the estimator.**
- **MUTATE=1** (V_ext and σ_ext × 2): B1's s\* rises from 27.6 to 546 (409–724 in 68 %), and B2 gains a root, s\* ≤ 72; both reactive and the roots equal the independent inversion to 2e-16.

## What this lane cannot say
It does not measure a₀ at z = 3.09. The baryons are the CO gas under a conversion that spans a factor 5.5 between the two declared values; L′ comes from a 7σ CO(3–2) detection (the line-flux formula reproduces the table to +1.7 %, C6); there is no stellar mass, no HST detection and no continuum gas route on disk; the source is a protocluster member (tides, environment); the beam (1.86 × 1.55 kpc) leaves four rings out to R_ext = 3.55 kpc (1.9 beams) with a single outer radius; the disc scale is an assumed R_ext/1.2; and CO(3–2) → CO(1–0) with r_31 = 0.6 would raise the B1 gas by 1.67 (−0.32 dex on the bound). A floor with B1 would have been robust against any added baryon; the root found is an upper bound only if the gas mass is at least 0.8 L′ and the geometry is right.
