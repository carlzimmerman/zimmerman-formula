**Label (both footings): NOT DIAGNOSTIC (model-selection-limited).** The one perturber with a strong light limit (J0946) sits at
g ≈ 4–10 a0, where the law adds only ~10–20%: if it were truly dark (M/L > 120), no baryon-sourced law could make it, under either
reading — that solution is excluded by ×13 or more. But the 2025 re-analysis that models the satellite's light (L ≈ 2.5e8 L☉, a
dwarf galaxy) fits the lens data better, and the law applied to that light lands on the measured mass (median 10^8.8 vs 10^8.9).
Which published solution is right decides the verdict; the lens data alone do not.

**What would decide it (framework-native, stated now):** the framework requires every lens perturber to carry its own baryons,
with M_b ≈ M_obs/ν (light ≳ M_b/8). For J0946: an independent detection of the satellite's light at L ≳ 7e7 L☉ (deep JWST/HST
imaging of the perturber position, or its spectrum) keeps the framework alive; a deep limit below ~5e6 L☉ that survives the
light-modelling choice kills it. For B1938 V: ~2e5–1e6 M☉ of baryons inside 80 pc (an ultra-compact dwarf or dense star cluster,
the class Powell+2025 do not exclude); light ~1e4–1e5 L☉ at z = 0.88, beyond any current telescope. Lensed-GW wave-optics
distortions probe the same question with no light needed (GW question 1).

**Limits:** lens-model masses (profile choices), the papers' cosmology for distances (percent-level), Υ ceiling 8 (old,
gas-free), spherical treatment of projected masses, possible line-of-sight positions. Fixes the record's L99, which stated the
prediction but never confronted a measured perturber.
