# CFG923: does the framework's own formation clock bind the dwarfs' cold energy late enough? CONDITION TEST. Class: canonical CONTRADICTED, alt UNDERDETERMINED (exactly at the threshold); OVERALL UNDERDETERMINED

**Label: CONDITION TEST** of the late-binding escape that CFG920 found for route D-i of CFG915/916. Criteria: `FROZEN_CRITERIA.md`,
committed alone first (d60088d9c), block 920–929, no amendments. κ = ½ FITTED. Footings 9.3603e-11 / 1.1312e-10 m/s² are kept apart
and never pooled. Raw data and framework-native quantities only. Cold energy is real gravitating mass; no particle species is added.
Not "theory closed". Nothing here says the data favour the framework.

## The question

CFG920 found that at yield log y = −0.5, seven gas-free dSphs (six on the alt footing) hold D-i's required post-formation loss
only if their cold energy was bound after 51–99.9% of their star formation. That contradicts D-i's "bound at formation". It could
instead be a posit of its own. The record already has a formation clock for cold energy formed at rest:
- **C-U, CFG763 (the primary clock):** f = 1 − e^{−s/τ}, one τ for every system, τ ∈ [0.724 can / 0.832 alt, 3.474] Gyr (F2 window OPEN).
- **C-O, CFG764:** τ = k·t_orb, k ∈ [0.457 can / 0.525 alt, 1.196] (O1 OPEN).

This lane asks whether that clock, with its frozen bracket and no per-dwarf fit, puts each dwarf's binding at or after the
fraction F_need that CFG920 requires.

**Prior-work check:** no lane compares the CFG763/764 clocks with dwarf SFHs or with CFG920's timing map (criteria §2).

## Clock to binding epoch (declared rules)

- **Origin:** the SF onset, 13.3 Gyr lookback (CFG827/920). Baryons must be assembled before they form stars, so this is the latest
  origin the clock allows. That choice favours late binding.
- **Primary binding epoch:** s_bind = τ (the mean formation time of the cold mass). The binding lookback is therefore 13.3 − τ:
  - **C-U:** 12.58 to 9.83 Gyr canonical (12.47 to 9.83 alt).
  - **C-O:** τ = k·2π r½/(√3 σ_obs), with t_orb = 0.05–0.35 Gyr for the seven dSphs. Binding falls 12.89–13.28 Gyr ago.
- **F_bind** is the Weisz+14 cumulative SFH fraction at the binding epoch. It uses CFG920's `t_of_F` mapping in forward form.
- **Rescue rule:** a dwarf is rescued iff F_bind ≥ F_need. CFG920's post-binding loss falls with F, so any later binding also holds;
  the condition sets no upper limit.
- **Classes per dwarf:**
  - LATE ENOUGH: rescued at both bracket ends;
  - TOO EARLY: rescued at neither end;
  - BRACKET-DEPENDENT: rescued at the slow end only.

## Reading

1. **The framework's own clock binds the cold energy too early for most of the dwarfs that need late binding.**
   - The universal clock finishes binding within 0.7–3.5 Gyr of the onset (by 9.8 Gyr ago).
   - The required binding moments of four dwarfs lie well after that: Hercules 0.64, Cetus 4.6, Tucana 6.7 and Andromeda I 8.4 Gyr ago
     (canonical). Even at τ = 3.47 Gyr the clock has already formed 76–97% of their cold energy before that moment (f_pre 0.756,
     0.852, 0.918, 0.974).
   - The orbital clock binds within 0.4 Gyr of the onset, inside the first SFH bin. Every one of the seven is TOO EARLY on both
     footings.
2. **Where it works, it works because the stars are ancient, not because the clock is slow.**
   - Andromeda VII is LATE ENOUGH because 98% of its stars predate 12.59 Gyr, so even the fastest universal clock binds after F_need.
   - Leo IV and CVn II are rescued only in the slow half of the bracket.
3. **The two isolated gas-free dSphs are the hardest cases.** Cetus and Tucana formed stars latest (τ90 4.7 and 6.5 Gyr). Their
   chemistry wants binding 3–7 Gyr after the slowest clock has finished (3.2 / 5.2 Gyr canonical, 3.3 / 7.4 alt). They are TOO EARLY on both clocks and both footings.
4. **So the late-binding escape is not supplied by the record's clock** for the majority of the dwarfs that need it, on the canonical
   footing. On the alt footing it fails for exactly half. Late binding therefore remains available only as a **new posit**: a binding
   trigger or a clock that the record does not contain, and one that would have to act 4.9–12.7 Gyr after the onset in some dwarfs
   (t_need 8.4 to 0.64 Gyr ago) and not in others. CFG920's other escapes (a low effective Fe yield, metal-enhanced winds ζ ≈ 3–19) are untouched by this lane.

## Ungraded reports

**R1: tides (CFG838/839).**
- The late-binding requirement does not follow tidal exposure:
  - the two hardest cases are isolated field dwarfs with no host tide (Cetus, Tucana);
  - the satellites in the set have present-day log T from −3.3 to −2.5 (Andromeda I −0.9) and pericentre log T_p −2.2 to −1.4.
- Late binding is therefore consistent with CFG838/839's no-tide result only if the binding trigger and the post-binding loss are
  internal (winds, the quench), not host-driven. A host-triggered binding (e.g. at infall) could not serve the field pair.
- The record's clock is non-environmental by construction, so it is consistent with no-tide. It simply binds too early.
- The universal clock binds before τ90, where CFG920 places the W2 terminal loss, across the whole bracket for four of the seven
  (CVn II, Andromeda I, Tucana, Cetus) and only at the fast end for Leo IV. For Andromeda VII and Hercules it binds after τ90,
  since their star formation was over by 12.6 Gyr.

**R2: field dwarfs (CFG873–875).**
- The gas-free field pair (Cetus, Tucana) needs late binding at the central values, and neither clock provides it.
- The gas-bearing field dwarfs (Aquarius, Phoenix, Leo T) have no timing requirement. Their issue in CFG920 is unseen loss, not
  timing.
- CFG873 (E_F +0.199 ± 0.068, D −0.150 ± 0.097 canonical), CFG874 (c −0.100 ± 0.091) and CFG875 (k −0.19 ± 0.15) are all UNDECIDED.
- Late binding does not conflict with them. The field pair's small excess (+0.13 / +0.17 dex) is exactly what makes their L_req
  small (1.8 / 2.2) and their F_need large. Nothing here separates satellite from field behaviour.

## What this means for CFG915/916/920

CFG920 left D-i's ×4.3 condition UNDERDETERMINED. For the metal-poor gas-free dSphs it named three rescues: a low Fe yield,
metal-enhanced winds, or late binding.

This lane removes the third rescue **as a consequence of the record's own clock**:
- CFG763's universal clock and CFG764's orbital clock both finish binding long before the required moment for the majority of the
  dwarfs that need it on the canonical footing, and for exactly half of them on the alt footing;
- Cetus, Tucana, Andromeda I and Hercules fail on every clock.

Late binding survives only as a new, unmotivated trigger, and the strongest demands for it come from isolated dwarfs.

Two limits keep the overall class at UNDERDETERMINED rather than CONTRADICTED:
- the alt footing sits at the threshold;
- the requirement itself dissolves at 1σ (V-1σ).

A decisive version needs CFG920's per-dwarf F_need with propagated uncertainties, or [α/Fe] and MDF shapes that pin the Fe yield.
