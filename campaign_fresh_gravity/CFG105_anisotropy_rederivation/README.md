**The anisotropy solver (independent implementation).** Jeans equation d(nu s_r^2)/dr + 2 beta nu s_r^2/r = -nu g (g the inward acceleration: baryons through the law, plus NFW debris for the rule). Integrating factor F(r) = exp(2 int beta dln r); solution s_r^2(r) = int_0^inf exp[(ln F + ln nu)(r e^x) - (ln F + ln nu)(r)] g(r e^x) r e^x dx with the boundary condition nu s_r^2 -> 0 at infinity, evaluated on demand at every radius by Gauss-Legendre quadrature (16 panels x 24 nodes on x in [0, 40]); no tabulated radial grid, no interpolation, no edge truncation; it accepts any ln nu(r) and any beta(r), with F analytic for constant beta (r^(2 beta)), Osipkov-Merritt (r^2 + r_a^2) and saturating Osipkov-Merritt. Line of sight (Binney-Mamon): r = R cosh u, z = R sinh u, s_los^2(R) = int (1 - beta(r) R^2/r^2) nu s_r^2 cosh u du / int nu cosh u du, Gauss-Legendre on u in [0, 25] (10 panels x 24 nodes), nu the 3-D density with slope gamma_i (Alabi's is a 3-D slope). Aperture: a point evaluation at each bin's median projected radius over the outer bins R > max(R_e, 2 kpc); the offset is the mean of log10(s_obs/s_pred) over those bins, then the 16 galaxy offsets give mean and SEM (ddof = 1); the outer bins span R/R_e = 1.4 (10th percentile), 3.1 (median), 8.0 (90th), 15.5 (max).

**Attacks (the Osipkov-Merritt and cutoff rows are the important ones).** (1) **Radially varying beta: the constant-beta bracket does not carry over.** Osipkov-Merritt beta = r^2/(r^2 + r_a^2) with the tracer power law extended to infinity, r_a/R_e = 1, 2, 3, 5, 10: law (canonical) +0.4, -0.6, -0.8, -0.4, +0.9 sigma; rule -2.2, -3.0, -3.3, -3.1, -2.0 sigma: the law's deficit vanishes and the rule over-predicts. **This is not local anisotropy: the Jeans integral runs to infinity, where beta -> 1 with gamma near 2.5 makes s_r^2 tend to v^2/(gamma - 2), roughly 5 times the isotropic value.** Post hoc: cutting the tracer off reverses it: beta = +0.5 law 3.38 sigma (no cutoff), 3.97 (r_c = 50 R_e), 4.98 (20 R_e); beta = +0.9 law 2.42, 4.56, 6.52; OM r_a = 3 R_e law -0.77, +1.88, +3.66. With a finite GC system radial orbits worsen the deficit instead of shrinking it, so the shrinking at large beta comes from the infinite extrapolation, not from anything measured. (2) Saturating OM (post hoc), beta -> b_m at large r: b_m = 0.5 (the NGC 5846 red-GC pattern) with r_a = 1, 3, 10 R_e gives law 3.05 / 2.62 / 2.91 sigma and rule 0.24 / -0.12 / 0.40; b_m = 0.75 gives law 1.5-2.3 sigma; only a 0.75 saturation at r_a = 3 R_e (1.54 sigma canonical, 1.07 alt) gets the law under 2 sigma. (3) Slope-radius dependence nu = r^-gamma [1 + (r/5 R_e)^2]^(-Delta/2): Delta = -0.5 (flatter outward) gives law 2.71 sigma at beta = 0 and 1.12 at +0.5; Delta = +0.5 and +1 give 4.3-5.9 sigma; the outer tracer slope matters as much as beta. (4) Projected versus 3-D slope: if the published gamma were projected (3-D = gamma_i + 1) the law rises to 6.2 sigma at beta = 0 and 8.0 at +0.5; with gamma_i - 1 it flips sign (-0.2 at beta = 0 and -5.9 at +0.5); Alabi's relation is 3-D, so the baseline holds if that reading does. (5) Aperture: averaging s_los^2 over each bin's member radii instead of the bin median shifts sigma by 0.01-0.04 (negligible). (6) Which galaxies drive the rule's beta dependence: the change from beta = -0.5 to +0.5 averages -0.025 dex and correlates with gamma_i (r = +0.86; law +0.97); it is carried by the low-gamma massive galaxies (NGC 4649 -0.079, NGC 4486 -0.073, NGC 4374 -0.069, NGC 4365 -0.054, NGC 5846 -0.053, NGC 3607 -0.038, NGC 2768 -0.034), while the gamma > 3 galaxies NGC 3377 and NGC 7457 move the opposite way; the rule's 2.06 sigma at beta = -0.5 is not robust (leaving out any one of about ten galaxies puts it at 1.72-1.92; removing NGC 4494 raises it to 2.95); at beta = +0.5 the rule stays below 1.1 sigma under every leave-one-out.
