# CFG791: the hypothetical cold-energy photon ramjet

**HYPOTHETICAL.** This assumes a portal converting intercepted cold energy to light with efficiency η. No such portal is
derived; CFG789/790 bound it.

**The performance is set by the areal mass M/A and by η alone.**

| M/A | η | e-fold τ₀ | to 0.1c (proper) | α Cen: proper / Earth time | arrive at | asymptotic accel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 mg/m² (D1, D3) | 1 | 57 d | 0.78 yr | **2.1 / 5.6 yr** | 0.998c | 3.1 g |
| 1 mg/m² | 0.1 | 570 d | 7.8 yr | 14 / 16 yr | 0.87c | 0.31 g |
| 1 mg/m² | 0.01 | 5,700 d | 78 yr | 94 / 95 yr | 0.24c | 0.03 g |
| 1 g/m² (D2) | 1 | 157 yr | — | 570 yr | 0.03c | 0.003 g |

**Physics:**
- Exact relativistic bookkeeping gives α = (η ρ A c²/M) β/(1 + β).
- Thrust/drag = c/v > 1, so the craft always accelerates.
- Unconverted cold energy passes through freely (collisionless). MUTATE shows that absorbing it would kill the thrust.
- Intake power at 0.1c: 1.8 × 10¹¹ W for a 100 km² intake.

**Controls:** K1 1e-4; K2 1e-14; K3 by construction.

**Reading:** a single-graphene-sheet intake (about 1 mg/m²) with a perfect portal would reach α Centauri in about 2 years of ship time.
Everything hinges on the portal and on η.

κ fitted; not theory closed.

**CORRECTION (2026-10-15, caught by CFG792 K1).** The areal densities in the first version of this README were labelled
1000× too heavy. 100 kg over 100 km² is 1 mg/m² (about one graphene monolayer, 0.77 mg/m²), not 1 g/m². 1 t over 1 km² is
1 g/m². The script and its numbers were right; only the labels were wrong. A realistic thin sail (1 g/m²) with a perfect portal
needs about 570 yr to reach α Cen.
