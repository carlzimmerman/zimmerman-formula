# CFG796: where could a cold-energy converter live? (theory only)

| branch | verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Cold mass is field polarization (bound charge) | Only field energy is releasable, which is 1/2.6 × 10⁶ of rest mass. Depolarizing it on device scales costs a stiffness penalty of 3 × 10³⁴ (1 m) and still 1.4 × 10¹² at 1 AU. **FORBIDDEN BY STIFFNESS.** |
| 2. A new local sink in ordinary matter | No such term exists in the record. It would have to be ≥ 2 × 10⁵ off in natural matter and stimulated ~10³⁴. **UNMOTIVATED.** |
| 3. A horizon (black hole) | The record's only sink: black holes destroy cold mass (PAPER50). A 10⁹ kg hole radiates 3.6 × 10¹⁴ W for 2,700 yr. But it gains only 7 × 10⁻⁴⁵ kg/s of cold energy against a loss of 4 × 10⁻³ kg/s, so cold energy supplies 10⁻⁴² of its power. **CONVERTER EXISTS IN PRINCIPLE; cold energy is not its fuel.** |

**Reading:** within the record, the only thing that destroys cold mass is a horizon, and at any buildable scale cold energy
contributes nothing to a horizon's output. The stiffness that lets the theory pass Cassini (L* ≥ 5.75 pc) is what seals
cold energy off from every device scale. A usable converter needs new physics beyond the committed theory.

K1 (Hawking power) and K2 (L*/AU) pass. MUTATE (L* = 0) makes branch 1 device-reachable, so the test bites.

κ fitted; not theory closed.
