## Reading
By the frozen rule no law is "consistent with" the PC row (flags nnnn). By the post hoc diagnostics, the row does not test the gravity law: the measured circular accelerations are about 0.4–0.7 of what the local law predicts for these baryons (the Newtonian floor is respected by the most complete galaxies), and the stellar-mass scale alone, set between the colour recipe and zero, spans s\* = 0.10–0.60 (observed-frame) or 0.26–1.22 (rest-frame). The pre-flight predicted a low bias from selection; the data say the offset is mostly something else in the chain. Which part of the chain it is cannot be told from these tables: the width frame (0.36 dex), the stellar-mass scale (up to 0.8 dex), the profile type (0.28 dex between all-types and double-horned), the HI truncation of cluster galaxies (CFG212's caveat) and the missing inclinations all act in the same direction, and there is no same-pipeline local control.

## What would settle it (stated, not pursued)
(i) The paper's definition of W20 / W50 (observed or rest frame): the owner's go to fetch Gogate+2020 (arXiv:2302.12197). (ii) A same-pipeline local control: the identical chain on a local HI-selected sample with W50, M_HI and colours (for example ALFALFA with SDSS photometry), which needs a download and the owner's go. (iii) Axis ratios from the optical imaging (BUDHIES IV), which would remove the inclination assumption and most of the selection problem. Without these the BUDHIES row stays a lower-quality, uncalibrated coverage row.

## Addendum 2026-10-01 (width frame resolved; nothing re-run; no number above changes)
Written after the owner's explicit yes to fetch the BUDHIES data paper (Gogate et al. 2020, arXiv:2302.12197; e-print 12,197,349 bytes, sha256 `a9f0ef68…a285`, logged in `data_assembly/FETCH_MANIFEST_2026-10-01.jsonl`) and after reading it. This addendum is appended; the text above is untouched.

- **The width frame is resolved: the catalogue widths are galaxy-rest-frame velocity widths.** The paper converts the observed frequency width to a velocity width with W^obs = (Δν_obs / ν_rest)(1 + z) c (its Eq. lw1) and calls them observed rest-frame line widths; the table columns (7)–(8) are those widths from the R4 cubes (R4 = 38 km s⁻¹ velocity resolution), "not corrected for instrumental broadening". So **branch R (k = 0) is the one that applies and the primary "observed-frame" branch (k = 1) is not the paper's frame**: the applicable PC row is the rest-frame one, **s\* = 0.260 (68 %: 0.215–0.397; 95 %: 0.109–0.520)**, with the statistics-only pulls −3.6 (FLAT) / −3.9 (RIVAL) and the recipe-widened −1.8 / −1.9 printed above. It is still outside both law bands and the row stays **not a calibrated point**; the README's earlier sentence that the primary widths were "observed-frame on the evidence of the mass formula only" is superseded by this.
- **The instrumental correction in my chain is not the paper's.** The paper derives W₅₀^corr = W₅₀,R^obs − 0.29 R (R the velocity resolution in km s⁻¹; Bottinelli et al. 1990 would give 0.19 R), i.e. **11 km s⁻¹ at R4**, not the 19 km s⁻¹ instrumental part inside CFG260's δ = 28 km s⁻¹ (19 instrumental + 9 turbulent, declared UNVERIFIED). Using the paper's value would lower δ by 8 km s⁻¹ (to 20); the effect on s\* is NOT recomputed here. The local-control lane carries δ per sample as a declared knob (the ALFALFA widths are already corrected for instrumental broadening and smoothing in their catalogue; the BUDHIES ones are not).
- **BUDHIES stays OFF the chart** (not even an open marker) until the width → V → baryon chain is calibrated on a same-pipeline local HI sample (the owner's rule, relayed by the High-z peer). Open item (ii) of "What would settle it" is the local control (ALFALFA widths and SDSS colours, owner-approved download; assembled under `data_assembly/alfalfa_sdss_local_control/`); item (iii), axis ratios from the optical imaging, is still open.
