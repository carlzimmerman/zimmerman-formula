## What the numbers say
- **The change from CFG283.** Adding the measured stars moves the lowest-gas row from "s\* ≤ 27.6, vacuous" (CFG283 B1) to "s\* ≤ 1.40, unresolved" (this B1), a factor 20, and the stars-only row (new) to ≤ 6.9. The stars carry 65 % of the B1 baryons (g_\*/g_bar), so the stellar mass and its error (+0.20/−0.36 dex) decide the row.
- **The conversion equivalents (closed form, given the stars):** the floor boundary D = 1 at α_CO = 1.063; FLAT at 0.873; PROXY at 0.564; the rival law at 0.249. **A conversion at or above the Galactic 4.36 is a floor by 2.3×; the dynamics leave room for only 1.06.** For the two larger-a₀ laws the baryons they need are below the lowest-conversion gas (0.8), which is why the nominal B1 bound (1.40) lies below them; that statement is not made, because the row is UNRESOLVED.
- **The knobs on B0 (Δ log₁₀ s\*):** the thin-disc inclinations from the axis ratios of the JWST F444W (57.3°) and ALMA 870 µm (54.5°) images against the kinematic 75° ± 6° **+0.35 / +0.43**; M\* × 10^−0.30 **+0.51** (M\* × 10^+0.30 a floor); geometry R_e × 1.5 +0.32, R_e / 1.5 −0.19, spherical +0.24, the stars as the measured Sérsic n = 3.21 thin disc +0.25 (g_\* lower by 0.13 dex), R_e ± 1σ ±0.07; kernel P2 +0.17; pressure +0.02 / +0.04; inclination ± 1σ +0.09 / −0.06; radius +0.04. **On B1** the same knobs are larger: thin-disc inclinations +0.64 / +0.75, M\* −0.30 +0.65, R_e × 1.5 +0.66, spherical +0.55, the Sérsic stars +0.40; M\* +0.30, R_e / 1.5, r_31 = 0.6 and helium × 1.36 all turn B1 into a floor.
- **The inclination** (kinematic 75° ± 6° from the ALPAKA CO morphology against 54.5–57.3° from the JWST and ALMA axis ratios of a thin disc, a 3σ disagreement) is the largest single knob; a lower inclination means a larger deprojected V and a larger g_obs.
- **The SELFTEST (what the estimator can do on these baryons).** 100 worlds exactly on the law at s = 2 with 0.15 dex scatter: on the B0 baryons 67 are rooted and 33 are floors, the 95 % interval contains s = 2 in 64 of the 67 rooted worlds, rooted s\* median 2.6 (16–84 % 0.62–9.1); on the B1 baryons 66 rooted and 34 floors, coverage 56 of 66, rooted s\* median 4.1 (1.2–13.5). **A world exactly on the law is a floor a third of the time, and the rooted-only median sits above the truth (the floors are the low-s\* worlds), so a rooted row near the floor boundary is an upper bound, not an estimate.**
- **MUTATE=1** (V_ext and σ_ext × 2): B0's s\* rises to 212, B1's to 125, and B2 gains a root, s\* ≤ 28.0; all roots equal the independent inversion to 4e-16.

## What this lane cannot say
It does not measure a₀ at z = 3.09. The stellar mass is **one SED fit** (CIGALE, Chabrier IMF, non-parametric SFH, no AGN term mentioned, near-IR flag Y) with errors of +0.20/−0.36 dex that **was read through a summarising tool**, not from the published table; **the JWST F444W image has two peaks, neither at the dust position, and the dust and stellar peaks are offset (a possible line-of-sight superposition of protocluster galaxies), so the SED mass may include a companion that is not in the CO disc whose rotation was measured**; ALPAKA I's own SED fit found ID 24's mass unconstrained for lack of optical/near-IR coverage; the kinematic and morphological inclinations disagree by 3σ; the stars are an n = 3.2 profile approximated by a Freeman disc in the headline (the Sérsic thin-disc knob raises the bound by 0.25 dex); the gas is the CO under a conversion spanning a factor 5.5, with r_31 unmeasured here; the beam leaves one outer radius; and at D = 1.1 (B1) a few per cent of baryon mass decide between a root and a floor. **B0 is an upper bound only if the SED mass belongs to the rotating disc; a floor with B0 would have been robust against added gas.**
