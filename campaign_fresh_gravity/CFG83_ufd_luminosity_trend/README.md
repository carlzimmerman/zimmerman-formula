# CFG83 -- do the ultra-faint offsets follow luminosity? (frozen test on on-disk data)

**Question (frozen before the first run, in the script docstring).** CFG78's independent re-derivation noticed that the eight binary-corrected ultra-faint offsets look luminosity-ordered (faintest most over-dispersed), which would suggest a luminosity- or Upsilon_V-dependent systematic instead of a flat offset. Is there a trend of the offset log10(sigma_obs/sigma_pred) with M_V (primary) or r_half? Pass line: p < 0.01 counts as detected. Sample (i): 40 systems (31 resolved + 9 limits), bare law, Upsilon_V = 2, stars only, RAR kernel; sample (ii): the 8 CFG78 systems (no censoring, exact permutation over all 8! orderings), at f = 0 / free / 0.7. On-disk data only (`lvd_dwarf_mw.csv`, `arroyopolonio2026_ufd_binary_corrected.tsv`).

**Result: the 40-object sample does not show a trend; the 8-system ordering is flagged but does not clear the pre-declared Bonferroni bar; selection alone reproduces the 40-object tau.**

| sample (i), 40 systems, offset vs | censored Kendall tau_c | p | ATS slope (68%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M_V (primary; canonical and alt) | +0.182 | 0.093 | +0.053 dex/mag [+0.014, +0.081] |
| log r_half (primary) | -0.143 | 0.193 | -0.19 [-0.33, -0.02] |
| M_V, rule / LCDM (Duffy full) | +0.058 / +0.085 | 0.60 / 0.43 | |
| log r_half, rule / LCDM | -0.22 / -0.26 | 0.041 / 0.015 | |

- No secondary row reaches p < 0.01. A resolved-only Spearman gives rho = +0.45 (p = 0.011), +0.44 with limits at their upper bounds (p = 0.005) and -0.07 (p = 0.68) with the limits ranked at the bottom: the result depends entirely on how the 9 limits are treated.
- **A constant Upsilon_V cannot remove the slope:** the slope vs M_V stays +0.049 to +0.054 over Upsilon_V = 0.1 to 1000 (deep-MOND scaling shifts the level, +0.44 at Upsilon_V = 1 to +0.29 at 4, not the slope).
- **Sample (ii), the 8 CFG78 systems (Boo I, Car II, Hyd I, Leo IV, Leo V, Ret II, Seg 1, Wil 1):** tau_b vs M_V is +0.64 (exact p 0.031) at f = 0 and +0.79 (p 0.0055) at f free and f = 0.7 for the law; the rule gives the same taus; LCDM (Duffy full) +0.71 / +0.86 (p 0.014 / 0.0018). The trend is model-independent (it lives in the data ordering). Per-system law offsets at f free ordered by M_V: Boo I -0.03, Leo IV +0.14, Hyd I +0.20, Car II +0.21, Leo V +0.15, Ret II +0.42, Wil 1 +0.60, Seg 1 +0.58. The declared Bonferroni bar for secondary rows is 3.3e-4 (45 correlated rows: the same eight systems through several models); the smallest p is 0.0018, so these are **flagged, not detected**. An n = 8 Kendall p below 0.01 needs tau of about 0.79 (a weak test), the bootstrap slope intervals at n = 8 should not be trusted, and a flat-offset Monte Carlo with the paper's errors gives a chance of 0.003 to 0.005 of a tau this large (measurement noise alone does not produce the ordering).
- **Selection Monte Carlo on the 40 (flat true offset a in {0, 0.15, 0.30}, literature errors, limits as one-sided 95% bounds, detection threshold k = 1, 1.5, 2 sigma; 9 cells of 1000 data sets):** the censored-Kendall false-positive rate at p < 0.01 is 0.06 to 0.37 (pass line 0.02, so the check is not selection-clean), the mean simulated tau vs M_V is +0.13 to +0.23 and the observed +0.18 sits inside it, and the selection-aware MC p of the observed tau is 0.29 to 0.71 in all nine cells. A resolved-only Spearman has a false-positive rate of 0.11 to 0.62 under a flat offset, so its p = 0.011 is uninformative. The MC rests on the declared detection model and is a null-model check, not a fit.

**Controls.** Passed: Spearman and tau-b match scipy to 1e-12; the uncensored permutation p matches scipy's asymptotic p (0.0301 vs 0.0302); a hand-worked censored example (S = +2, tau = 1/3); an injected slope +0.08 on n = 200 with 45 censored gives p = 1e-5 and ATS +0.065; a zero injection gives -0.012; a permuted injection loses the trend (p = 0.33); the law's closed forms hold. MUTATE (offsets permuted among systems) removes the trends on the 40 (p = 0.99 and 0.85) and the 8 (0.72); 200 further permutations give fractions with p < 0.01 of 0.000 and 0.015 (on the 40, where the real data show no trend, the MUTATE run is uninformative; the injection controls carry the bite). **Failed as declared and kept (both runs exit 1):** C1e (null calibration with 9 censored on the faint side) at 0.117 against [0.02, 0.09]: the censoring depends on x, so permutation is not exchangeable and the censored-Kendall permutation p on the 40 is anti-conservative, which is why the selection MC is the arbiter; a post-hoc control with censoring independent of x gives 0.037 and passes (amended in the docstring); a wrong deep-MOND constant in the agent's control C2 (1/18, not 4/81) was fixed without affecting any prediction.

**Reading.** There is no evidence that the ultra-faint offset is luminosity- or Upsilon_V-dependent on the 40-object sample; the eight-system ordering is real as a rank correlation (p about 0.002 to 0.03) but flagged only, on eight objects, and it appears identically under the law, the rule and LCDM. A flat offset is not established either: the data cannot separate flat from mildly luminosity-dependent. The decisive test remains multi-epoch per-star dispersions (CFG51, CFG66). This is a null-trend outcome the frozen plan allows; not evidence for or against the framework; kappa = 1/2 is FITTED.
