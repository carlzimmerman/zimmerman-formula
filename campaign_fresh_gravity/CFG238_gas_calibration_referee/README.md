## 5. Plain answers

### 5a. The z slopes at fixed L_IR; is the L_IR conditioning selection-driven; power

- **The slopes reproduce** (section 1). With the L_IR term they are -0.10 to +0.06 per unit log10(1+z) with SE 0.04 to 0.08; without it they are 2.3 to 4.4 sigma in 8 of the 9 cases (dax alpha_CO is 0.3 sigma; four are quoted in the README). The L_IR coefficients (-0.058 alpha_CO; +0.04 to +0.09 others) are what produce the difference.
- **How much fixed luminosity is there?** z and L_IR correlate at r = 0.78 to 0.84 (VIF of log10(1+z) given L_IR 2.6 to 3.4). Local galaxies (z < 0.6) reach L_IR 12.57; at L_IR >= 12.0 there are 11 local galaxies in ad and 5 to 7 in the other tables, against 106 (ad) and 34 to 53 at z >= 1.6. By the frozen rule (>= 8 and >= 8) **the ad table has one overlap bin (12.0 to 12.5: 10 local, 17 at z >= 1.6) and the other seven (table, factor) cases have none**: for them the "fixed-luminosity" comparison is an extrapolation of a linear L_IR term (frozen disagreement (iii)). In the one overlap bin the z >= 1.6 minus z < 0.6 contrast of log alpha_CO is -0.015 +- 0.053 (slope-equivalent -0.026 +- 0.094; fixed-effects slope -0.036 +- 0.085); for kappa_H the same bin gives +0.018 +- 0.069. These are consistent with zero and 1.7 times less precise than the README's +-0.050.
- **Is the conditioning selection-driven?** Partly, in two ways. (i) **Functional form.** The within-L_IR-bin permutation of z (preserves the L_IR-z joint, removes the within-bin link; 5,000 draws, seed 2382) has a null mean of -0.070 to +0.032, not zero: the linear L_IR term does not absorb all the L_IR dependence, so the slope carries a systematic of 0.5 to 1 SE. Subtracting the null mean leaves b between -0.044 and +0.066 (all within 1.0 sigma). The null SD is 0.76 to 0.85 of the bootstrap SD (the README SE is, if anything, conservative). A curvature of 0.06 per dex^2 alone would create |b| = 0.11 to 0.19 in the linear model but <= 0.05 in a binned model. (ii) **Survey membership.** z is nearly collinear with the sample flags (VIF 18 to 21 with them). A 0.10 dex calibration offset on the SMG-flagged rows moves the with-L_IR slope by only +0.00 to +0.03 (the linear L_IR term absorbs it: my frozen estimate of 0.10 was wrong) but moves the without-L_IR slope by +0.10 to +0.13. With the sample flags as fixed effects the slopes become +0.22 +- 0.14 (ad alpha_CO), **+0.69 +- 0.21 (dax alpha_CO, 3.4 sigma), +0.50 +- 0.17 (xa alpha_CO, 2.9 sigma), +0.46 +- 0.15 (xa X_CI, 3.1 sigma)** and, in the rest, +0.28 +- 0.18 (dax X_CI), -0.05 +- 0.16 (xd X_CI), -0.22 +- 0.12 (ad kappa_H), -0.43 +- 0.23 (dax GDR), -0.13 +- 0.17 (xd GDR); by the frozen rule that is disagreement (i). The estimator is poorly conditioned (VIF about 20, SE 2 to 3 times the README's) and it is a within-sample slope (post hoc: within the SMG-flagged rows at z >= 1.6 alone, dax alpha_CO +0.72 +- 0.26, xa alpha_CO +0.47 +- 0.21, xa X_CI +0.47 +- 0.17, ad +0.20 +- 0.18 over a log10(1+z) span of 0.3 to 0.4); I read it as: the README's statement holds for the between-sample comparison under a linear L_IR term and does not hold as a statement about drift within a sample.
- **Power.** The minimum detectable slope at 80% power (two-sided 5%) is 0.145 per unit log10(1+z) for ad alpha_CO (0.13 kappa_H; 0.16 to 0.23 in the others; a binned model 0.18 to 0.27). A real change of 0.05 dex across z = 0 to 2.5 (slope 0.09) is detected with probability 0.43 (ad alpha_CO), 0.21 to 0.52 across the nine; 0.10 dex with 0.63 to 0.98. So "no drift larger than about 0.08 to 0.12 dex across z = 0 to 2.5" is what the data say at 80% power; "no drift" is not.
- **Extra finding: the CO excitation correction itself moves the slope.** The 12 rows with a nonzero JCorr change the raw ad L_850/L'_CO slope by +0.047 (0.6 of its SE); the README slopes use the corrected luminosities.

### 5b. Are the optimised conversions circular?

- **Yes, for agreement; no, for the relative drift of the raw luminosities.** The catalogue masses are exactly factor x luminosity: log M_H2 = log alpha_CO + log L'_CO + JCorr to 0.0003 dex (335 rows) and = log L_850 - log alpha_850 - 0.134 (the He factor, log 1.36 = 0.1335) to 0.0005; the same holds in the daX, xa and xd tables to 0.0003 to 0.015 dex (daX dust 0.05). So the tracer masses of one galaxy agree to 0.001 dex by construction, and "agree" carries no information about accuracy.
- **What is fitted and what is left over.** The optimisation uses z-independent prior means, prior widths from the intrinsic scatter, a flat prior on M_H2 and a normalisation fixed by assumption (kappa_H = 1884, X_CI = 1.6e-5; Dunne+22 repo HTML). Three things are not measured by it: the absolute scale, the split of any relative drift between tracers (the prior share w), and the z dependence the prior shrinks away. The informative leftover is the raw ratio. At fixed L_IR the raw ad log(L_850/L'_CO) slope is +0.072 +- 0.081 (0.9 sigma); alpha_CO carries +0.056 (78%) and alpha_850 +0.016 (algebra: the two sum to the raw slope to 2e-4). The README's +-0.050 on alpha_CO is therefore 0.62 of the observable relative-drift uncertainty (+-0.081). The toy optimiser (C6: masses agree to 0 for any input; a relative drift d shows up as -w d and (1-w) d; a common-mode drift of 0.30 per unit log10(1+z) shifts the masses by exactly 0.30 and the factors by 1e-16) states the limit.
- **Random-tracer control.** Replacing every L_850 by another galaxy's at matched L_IR still gives exact agreement of the optimised masses (1.8e-15), but the within-bin SD of the raw ratio is 0.233 for the real pairs against 0.448 for shuffled ones (0.52; xd 0.236 against 0.471): the tracers are physically coupled beyond L_IR, and that coupling (not the agreement) is what the catalogue measures.
- **Per-galaxy uncertainty.** Catalogue e_logMH2 medians are 0.110 / 0.107 / 0.107 / 0.108 (ad, B1 to B4), 0.083 to 0.089 (daX), 0.099 to 0.111 (xa), 0.098 to 0.104 (xd). The SD of log alpha_CO within a bin is 0.118 (z < 0.6) and 0.177 / 0.182 at z >= 1.6. "Known to 0.03 to 0.10 dex" is true of a bin mean (K_direct 0.02 to 0.03), not of a galaxy.
- **Route dependence (same galaxies, different tables).** log(alpha_CO,daX / alpha_CO,ad) has a z slope of +0.118 +- 0.026; daX/xa -0.097 +- 0.026; xa/ad +0.215 +- 0.047 per unit log10(1+z): the model dependence of the z trend of one galaxy set is 0.1 to 0.2, larger than the README's +-0.05.
- **What the residual tests, and what it cannot.** It tests the scatter and z trend of the relative tracer ratio. It cannot test the level of the mass scale, a drift shared by all tracers, or accuracy.

### 5c. The ACE extrapolation: prescription bracket or z-evolution?

- **Reproduced:** R = -2.37 (SD 0.18, N 15); offsets -0.671 / -0.520 / -0.213 at the OLS slope / 0 / +1; K 0.34 / 0.26 / 0.12.
- **It is an extrapolation.** Stripe82 covers 12+log(O/H) 8.65 to 8.86 (0.21 dex, slope -0.49 +- 0.33); ACE covers 8.29 to 8.61 and **all 15 galaxies lie below the lowest Stripe82 value** (ACE's mean is 0.19 dex below it). Delta(s) = -0.520 + 0.307 s exactly; it crosses zero at s = 1.69 and is -0.152 (K 0.089) at ACE's own slope 1.2, -0.259 (K 0.138) at Stripe82's own Leroy+11 slope 0.85. The OLS slope is 4.1 to 5.2 sigma below 0.85, 1.0, 1.2 (analytic; 3.6 to 4.5 bootstrap). Stripe82 subsamples give -0.65 (Z <= 8.85), -0.67 (Z >= 8.70), +0.09 +- 0.67 (Z >= 8.75). The 0.67 is the OLS-extrapolated number and is not a physically motivated local relation.

### 5d. Can a common-mode bias be bounded from anything on disk?

No. The diagnostics of CFG224 and CFG224b are blind to it by algebra (M4: every pair difference, the hat variances and the raw ratios are unchanged to 0 and 2e-15 under +0.30 dex on all tracers and under a shared z-dependent shift). The candidate anchors are inequalities of dynamical masses: Stripe82 (z < 0.2) M_dyn is the mass inside the CO-emitting region while M* and the integrated HI are galaxy totals, and 68% of the galaxies violate M* + M_CO + M_HI <= M_dyn already (59% with the gas lowered by 1 dex): not a bound; Amvrosiadis+25 (z 2 to 4; alpha_CO = 0.92; M_dyn(< 10 kpc) for 12 sources): 33% violate at any gas scale from -1 to 0 dex (M* alone exceeds M_dyn in 4 of 12), 50% at +0.3 dex, 67% at +0.6 dex: at most a one-sided, N = 12 statement, and M_dyn is the kinematic model output of the lane under test (in MOND-like dynamics it is not the baryonic mass), so it is not law-independent; NOEMA3D log(M* + M_CO) against the DysmalPy log M_bary differs by 0.05 +- 0.18 (6 of 10 within 0.1; a model parameter). The normalisation anchors named by Dunne+22 (X_CI = 1.6e-5 from absorption lines, kappa_H = 1884 from local metal-rich discs and dust models) are local values assumed to hold at z > 1.6. **What would be needed:** per-galaxy total gas (H2 + HI + He) at z >= 1.6 from a method independent of the CO, dust and [CI] conversions and of the dynamics under test (H2 rovibrational emission, HD, absorption-line abundances in the galaxies themselves, or a dynamical decomposition with a pre-declared law-independent model in an inner-baryon-dominated regime); none is on disk.
