## 2. The door's exact model

**Reading P (the only reading this door tests).** rho_D is a *phantom* source: it enters Poisson's equation and nothing else. It has no pressure, no dynamics of its own, no independent inertia, and is not a conserved cold matter component. The Newtonian-limit field equations are

    nabla^2 Phi = 4 pi G [ rho_b(x) + rho_D(x) ],
    rho_D(x) = integral d^3x' K(|x - x'|) rho_b(x'),   K = K(s) >= 0 or signed, of dimension length^-3,

equivalently in Fourier space Phi_k = -4 pi G (1 + Khat(k)) rho_b,k / k^2, i.e. G_eff(k) = G (1 + Khat(k)), with Khat(k) = integral d^3s e^{-i k.s} K(s). Baryons move in Phi (test-body force -grad Phi). This is the Newtonian limit of Hehl-Mashhoon / Chicone-Mashhoon / Rahvar-Mashhoon nonlocal gravity, in which a spatial nonlocality of the gravitational field is described, in the weak-field regime, as an effective dark-matter density linearly related to the baryon density. Time nonlocality (the field's memory of the accelerated observer's past) and the tetrad/constitutive-relation structure of the relativistic theory are NOT modelled (section 6).

**Why not a "matter" reading (Reading M).** If rho_D were a real cold matter component with prescribed density K*rho_b, it would need a non-gravitational force to hold that density (its velocity would have to follow K*J_b, not a geodesic). That is an enclosed-mass energy exchange between baryons and fluid and falls under CFG48 G4, CFG70 and CFG72 (energy 23 to 318 times orbital, reaction 0.06 to 22 g_law). Reading M is therefore explicitly NOT tested here; it is already excluded in the record.

**Known kernels (as recalled; tested for completeness, not as the basis of the verdict).** With the convention above (K of dimension length^-3):
- power-law (Chicone-Mashhoon-type, mu0 -> 0): K(s) = 1 / (4 pi lambda0 s^2);
- Rahvar-Mashhoon: K(s) = (1 + mu0 s) exp(-mu0 s) / (4 pi lambda0 s^2), total integral 2 / (lambda0 mu0); galaxy-fit ranges quoted in the task as lambda0 about 3 to 10 kpc and mu0 about 0.1 kpc^-1 (recalled values 0.06 to 0.1 kpc^-1 also declared);
- Hehl-Mashhoon: the original spatial kernel, a 1/s^2-type form of the same family, covered by the general LTI theorem rather than a separate fit;
- a general **linear translation-invariant (LTI) kernel**: any K(|x - x'|), signed or positive, any radial profile, on a fixed constant set. The self-consistent variant rho_D = K*(rho_b + rho_D) (kernel acting on the total density) is also linear in rho_b and translation-invariant, so it is covered by the same theorem (rho_D = [K/(1-K)]-hat rho_b).

## 3. What a translation-invariant kernel can and cannot do

**3.1 The required kernel for a point mass is unique and closed-form.** For rho_b = M delta^3(x), the map gives rho_D = M K(r) exactly (no deconvolution ill-posedness), so the kernel that reproduces the target for a point mass of mass M is

    K_M(r) = rho_D^target / M = 1 / [ 4 pi r_M r sqrt(r^2 + r_M^2) ],    r_M = sqrt(G M / a0).

Its Fourier transform (Struve function, to be verified by quadrature in phase 2) is

    Khat_M(k) = F(k r_M),    F(z) = (pi / 2z) [ I_0(z) - L_0(z) ],

with F(z -> 0) = pi / (2 z) (the 1/s^2 tail, K -> 1 / (4 pi r_M s^2)) and F(z -> infinity) = 1 / z^2 (the 1/s inner behaviour, K -> 1 / (4 pi r_M^2 s)).

**3.2 It is not universal.** K_M depends on the baryon mass through r_M ∝ M^(1/2). A universal LTI kernel has fixed constants, so it cannot equal K_M for two different masses. The same-constants requirement of G1 is exactly the mass-independence of K, and K_M violates it for every pair of masses.

**3.3 The exact scaling theorem (linearity obstruction).** For any LTI kernel with fixed constants and any baryon profile, rho_D[lambda rho_b] = lambda rho_D[rho_b], so M_D scales like lambda and g_tot = G (M_b + M_D)/r^2 like lambda. Then

    C_model[lambda rho_b] = G r rho_D (M_b + M_D)  scales as  lambda^2,      C_target[lambda rho_b] = (a0/4 pi) M_b(<r)  scales as  lambda.

Hence at fixed profile shape and size, **C_model / C_target ∝ M_b exactly** (a factor lambda between the two), for every LTI kernel, every profile shape, and both a0 footings. For a point mass: C_model / C_target = 4 pi r_M^2 B(r) with B(r) = r K(r) [1 + m(r)], m(r) = 4 pi integral_0^r K s^2 ds, both mass-independent, and r_M^2 ∝ M.
