## 4. What this says

1. **The amount.** Wide lenses need at least 3 × 10¹¹ M☉ inside 12.6 kpc that law S and Salpeter stars do not supply,
   and the need falls steadily with Einstein radius: 99.7 %, 88 %, 62 %, 32 % of lenses across the four classes.
2. **Not a compact central mass.** Put at the centre, even the least excess lensing allows would make the stars move
   1.7–1.8 times as fast as measured (more for a nearer source). This holds at all three stellar normalisations and on
   both footings (isotropic orbits).
3. **The slope is a reading.** At Salpeter stars the extra component is near isothermal, and SLACS with real source
   redshifts gives the same 2.2. With stars 0.10 dex heavier, anything down to a uniform sheet is allowed. The gate
   needs either the stellar normalisation fixed to better than 0.1 dex or dispersions at two radii.
4. **If this component is cold dark energy, it has to:**
   - reach 3 × 10⁴ to 1 × 10⁵ times its cosmic mean density at 12.6 kpc (every family but the uniform sphere);
   - be cold: in hydrostatic balance with slope 1–2.5 its pressure is 0.8–1.5 × 10⁻⁶ of ρc² (a velocity dispersion of
     about 280–330 km/s for the isothermal case);
   - hold, if isothermal, 4–8 × 10¹² M☉ inside 300 kpc, which is 8–17 times the central galaxy's stellar mass.
5. **Against the 5.41 share (arithmetic on the table, added after the run).** At 5.41 units of cold component per unit
   of baryons, the group would have to hold this many central-galaxy stellar masses of baryons inside 300 kpc: 0.4–0.9
   (γ = 2.5), 1.6–3.0 (isothermal), 4.6–9.4 (γ = 1.5), 11–22 (γ = 1), 27–61 (uniform). The baryon content of these
   groups is not measured here, so this is a requirement, not a pass. A steep component fits inside a modest group; a
   sheet-like one needs a cluster's worth of baryons.
6. **No state is constructed.** These are the numbers a constructed state would have to meet. The framework still has
   none, and nothing here bears on a₀ or κ.

## 5. Limits

Spherical; one family at a time; isotropic orbits in the words; the two apertures are only a factor 2 apart;
photometric stellar masses; no source redshifts; dispersions from EUC5's fits, whose mock gate failed and whose scale
is a post hoc calibration (its interval is carried into every bound). The "near" case is an assumption about sources,
not a bound.
