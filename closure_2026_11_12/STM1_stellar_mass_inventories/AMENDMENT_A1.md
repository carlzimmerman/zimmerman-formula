## A1.1 Initial versus present-day mass (rule C only)

Imig et al. 2025's headline disc mass (5.27) is, by the paper's own text (Sec. IV.3 "Total
Surviving Mass" and Sec. VII), the total INITIAL stellar mass, including mass since lost to winds and
supernovae; the paper itself gives the present-day surviving mass (luminous plus remnants) as
3.7 x 10^10. The common convention (rule C) is a present-day mass. Rule C therefore uses a paper's own
present-day value where its headline is an initial mass, and scales the error by the same factor.
Rule H (headline) still uses 5.27, as frozen. Disclosed: this was decided after seeing that it lowers
the I25 row.

## A1.2 Helium where a gas paper does not say

Where a cold-gas paper does not state whether its masses include helium, the low end of the cold-gas
range uses the mass as stated and the high end uses it times 1.36. Where the paper states that helium
is included (McMillan 2017's HI disc; Kalberla's HI values, which McMillan 2017 Sec. 2.3 reports as
including helium on the authors' clarification) it is used as stated in both. Heyer and Dame 2015 could
not be read in full; its total molecular mass is taken from Rice et al. 2016 (Sec. 4.1), which cites it,
and is marked secondary.

## A1.3 Reported, not graded

- R0 normalisation: Bovy and Rix 2013 state their disc mass at R0 = 8 kpc and give the slope (+3.0 per
  kpc). The value at the R0 the other rows use (8.2 to 8.33) is printed, not graded.
- McMillan 2017 gives no systematic number (only "of the same order as the statistical"); the row with
  the statistical error doubled in quadrature (0.57 x sqrt 2) is printed, not graded.
- Composite rows (I25, BR13) add Portail et al. 2017's bar/bulge (1.88 +- 0.12) to a disc integrated
  through the centre; this may double count the inner disc. Printed as a caveat; the frozen rule is
  applied unchanged.
