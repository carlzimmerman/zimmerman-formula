# Lane U -- renormalisation-group improved gravity (asymptotic safety) and the IR behaviour of G and Lambda

## Bottom line
- **No RG-improved-gravity paper derives a0 or the coefficient 1/2 (32 pi).** Every one inserts its scale: a cutoff-identification constant (xi, kappa, alpha), a running exponent (q ~ 1e-6, nu ~ 1e-6, eta = 1) or a fitted crossover scale (k*); none gets a universal acceleration or the baryonic Tully-Fisher law, and the one genuine IR-fixed-point galaxy model (G ~ 1/k^2) has a transition acceleration ~3.5e-4 a0 that scales as M^(1/3).
- **Sharp structural result (`u01`-`u04`; 86/86 checks).** For ANY fixed-point running G = g/k^2, Lambda = lambda k^2 the vacuum quantity is G rho_Lambda = lambda k^2/(8 pi): **g drops out** (by the definition rho_Lambda = Lambda/(8 pi G): an identity, stated only because it shows the puzzle's left side carries no fixed-point coupling except lambda). The only mass-free acceleration from {c, G, Lambda, hbar} is c^2 sqrt(Lambda) x (pure number), so an RG-derived a0 must be c^2 k_X/zeta, and **G rho_Lambda/a0^2 = lambda_X zeta^2/(8 pi)**: it is NOT a pure fixed-point number (lambda_X is scheme dependent, zeta is a free identification constant). "= 4" means **zeta^2 lambda_X = 32 pi**; required zeta = 8 sqrt(pi) = 14.18 (EH termination scale), sqrt(12 pi) = 6.14 (cosmic-time scale), 16.7-25.6 (UV fixed point, 10 schemes). The pi of the puzzle sits in zeta^2, not in any fixed-point number (lambda* is algebraic in the optimised scheme, rational = 1/4 in another; g* carries the pi but drops out).
- **hbar issue resolved:** fixed-point running carries hbar only through g = hbar G k^2/c^3; the IR-fixed-point coupling of the galaxy/cosmology models is g*_IR ~ (l_Pl H0/c)^2 ~ 1e-122, i.e. it IS the hierarchy hbar G Lambda/c^3, undetermined by the flow (the papers say so). The puzzle in fixed-point variables is `32 pi (a0/a_Pl)^2 = g*_IR lambda*_IR` -- an identity, not a derivation.
- **Verdict: SHARP NO-GO (scoped to RG improvement with a cutoff identification); nothing derived; kappa = 1/2 stays FITTED.** One conditional, already-known implication: the Bonanno-Reuter fixed-point cosmology has Omega_Lambda = 1/2 and Lambda = (3/2)H^2 exactly, so an a0 tracking sqrt(Lambda) would evolve as H(z) (the record's rival to the flat a0(z)).

## 2. The hbar issue (`u03`, 28/28)
- Fixed-point running: g = hbar G k^2/c^3 is the dimensionless coupling, so G(k) = g* c^3/(hbar k^2). The naive force G(k = xi/r) M/r^2 = g* c^3 M/(hbar xi^2) is an acceleration but **r-independent, linear in M, and proportional to 1/hbar**; the improved-action extra term (c^2/2) dG/G0 (Reuter-Weyer 5.14 / Rodrigues et al. 2.10) is g* c^2 r/(xi^2 l_Pl^2), M-independent. Neither is a sqrt(M) law.
- That term is hbar-free iff g* = xi^2 hbar G/(c^3 L^2) for a classical length L: **g*_IR must be proportional to hbar**, numerically (l_Pl H0/c)^2 = 1.4e-122 (Reuter-Weyer's own (5.25) gives 2.2e-122 for xi_hat = 1, z_tr = 1). So the IR fixed point is not "an O(1) universal number"; its value is the cosmological-constant hierarchy, and the Bonanno-Reuter paper says it is not computed.
- Buckingham (sympy): every acceleration monomial in {G, c, Lambda, M, hbar} is c^2 sqrt(Lambda) (G M sqrt(Lambda)/c^2)^e (hbar G Lambda/c^3)^f. **The unique hbar-free, mass-free one is c^2 sqrt(Lambda).** The data agree: a0/(c^2 sqrt(Lambda)) = 0.13 while eps = hbar G Lambda/c^3 = 2.9e-122, so any extra factor eps^f is excluded beyond |f| < 0.01 (a0 is hbar-free over 120 orders of magnitude). So the RG can only supply the pure number multiplying c^2 sqrt(Lambda); it cannot supply a new classical scale. This is exactly the puzzle's form a0^2 = c^4 Lambda/(32 pi) (no G, no hbar) -- and exactly why the coefficient is where the RG has nothing to say.
- Restatement (identity, not a result): with a_Pl = c^2/l_Pl, `32 pi (a0/a_Pl)^2 = hbar G Lambda/c^3 = g*_IR lambda*_IR`; numerically 4.7e-122 (a0 = 1.2e-10) vs 2.9e-122 (Omega_Lambda = 0.7). The RG converts the puzzle into "why is the undetermined IR fixed-point product this number".
- **What would produce a MOND acceleration in this language** (my construction; not in the papers): with G(k) = G0 (k_tr/k)^q and an acceleration-based identification k = zeta g_N/c^2, the naive force has exponents (r, M) = (2q - 2, 1 - q), i.e. deep MOND a = sqrt(a0 G M)/r **only for q = 1/2**, with a0 = c^2 k_tr/zeta (hbar-free: k_tr is a wavenumber). The improved-action reading gives (2q - 1, -q): no BTFR. The IR/UV fixed-point exponent q = 2 gives a ~ r^2/M (decreasing with M). So the construction needs three inserted ingredients: eta_N = -1/2 (neither Gaussian 0 nor fixed-point -2), the acceleration identification, and k_tr, zeta.

## 3. Is G rho_Lambda/a0^2 a pure fixed-point number? (`u01`, `u02`, `u04`)
**Answer: no.** Two independent reasons.
1. *g drops out (identity).* `G(k) rho_Lambda(k) = Lambda(k)/(8 pi) = lambda k^2/(8 pi)` (`u02` B). In the Bonanno-Reuter cosmology G(t) = (3/8)(1+w)^2 g* lambda* t^2, Lambda(t) = 8/(3(1+w)^2 t^2), rho = rho_Lambda, Omega_Lambda = 1/2, and **G rho_Lambda/H^2 = 3/(16 pi) for every w** (the one genuinely fixed ratio -- of the vacuum density to H^2, not to a0). The RG scale itself, k/H = sqrt(3/(2 lambda*)), depends on lambda*: only Lambda and g*lambda* are fixed (verified with 20 symbolic checks, including a wrong-xi and a constant-Lambda control that fail as they must).
2. *The scale a0 is not a fixed-point object.* With a0 = c^2 k_X/zeta: `R = G rho_Lambda/a0^2 = lambda_X zeta^2/(8 pi)`.

**pi-content.** All pi's are loop measure (1/(4 pi)), the Bose integral pi^2/6 of the exponential shape, and Einstein's 8 pi. In the optimised scheme lambda* is the root of the rational cubic 14x^3 - 41x^2 + 59x - 24 (x = 1 - 2 lambda) -- algebraic, **no pi** -- while g* = pi x algebraic (the loop pi survives in g*, cancels in G rho_Lambda). In Litim's scheme lambda* = 1/4 exactly. So in `R = lambda zeta^2/(8 pi)` nothing in the fixed point compensates the 8 pi: **R rational needs zeta^2 proportional to pi**. (Caveat from the wave-2 audit: pi-counting depends on the variable held fixed; the statement here is in the variables (lambda_X, zeta), where Lambda = 32 pi a0^2 <=> lambda_X zeta^2 = 32 pi; identifications from angle factors give zeta^2 in Q pi^(2n), so an odd power of pi in zeta^2 is unmotivated, not excluded.)

Candidate scales k_X and what "= 4" needs (`u04`, 12/12):
- **X1 UV fixed point** (irrelevant at 1e-10 m/s^2, listed for completeness): R = lambda* zeta^2/(8 pi) = 0.006-0.014 at zeta = 1 and 0.97-2.26 at zeta = 4 pi; zeta_req in [16.7, 25.6].
- **X2 the EH termination scale.** In the Einstein-Hilbert flow the threshold functions blow up when P(z) = z + R0(z) + w vanishes at z = 0, i.e. at lambda = Lambda/k^2 = 1/2 for every regulator with monotone P (checked for four; regulators with a dip in P, e.g. exponential s = 3, 1/(1+z)^2, have the pole at 0.458, 0.445). Hence **k_term^2 = 2 Lambda, hbar-free and classical, no pi** (Reuter-Weyer eq 49 "k_term ~ sqrt(Lambda)", made exact); in the framework's units k_term = sqrt(6) H_Lambda. The 1/2 is the "-2 lambda" argument of the threshold functions, i.e. the -2 Lambda_k of the graviton Hessian (visible in Bonanno-Reuter 2.7-2.10), and it is convention-tied: with R0(0) = a it moves to a/2 (mutation checked), i.e. it is degenerate with zeta. The puzzle a0 = c H_Lambda/Z then reads **k_term = 8 sqrt(pi) a0/c^2, zeta_req = 8 sqrt(pi)**. This is the only place in the whole RG lane where a classical, hbar-free number times sqrt(Lambda) arises naturally as an IR scale; it reproduces the FORM a0 ~ c^2 sqrt(Lambda), not the coefficient, and Reuter-Weyer place it at the Hubble scale, not at galactic accelerations.
- **X3 the cosmic-time scale** (k = xi/t, xi fixed by Bianchi; Lambda t^2 = 8/3 at w = 0): R = zeta^2/(3 pi); zeta_req = sqrt(12 pi) = 6.14.
**Near-miss, reported so it is not mistaken for a result:** Milgrom's 2 pi applied to 1/t (a0 = c^2/(2 pi t), t the cosmic time; note H = 4/(3t) here) gives R = 4 pi/3 = 4.19, 4.7% above 4. Uninformative; no (X, zeta) in the menu gives exactly 4.
Decoy warning (`u04` E): Shapiro-Sola-Stefancic's coefficient of H^2 in G rho_Lambda is 3 nu/(8 pi) = 1/(32 pi^2) at their conservative nu = 1/(12 pi). That 32 pi^2 is the one-loop 1/(2(4 pi)^2), unrelated to the puzzle's; it would give "a0" = H/(8 sqrt(2) pi) = 0.028 H, 5x below the framework's. Likewise g* lambda* = 3 pi/32 in Litim's scheme is a Planck-ratio product with a factor 2.2 scheme spread; it is not a relation between Lambda and a0.

## Conditional implication (not a new test)
If one imposes a0 = (c/2) sqrt(G rho_Lambda) on the Bonanno-Reuter running rho_Lambda: a0 = sqrt(3/(64 pi)) c H = c H/8.19 and a0 ~ H(t) (rho_Lambda ~ rho_matter ~ (1+z)^3); at z = 0 that is 0.845 x the framework's Omega_Lambda = 0.7 value, i.e. further below the SPARC a0 (which is already 15% above the framework). The framework's distinctive law is flat a0(z); the fixed-point era predicts a0 ~ H(z) if a0 follows the running vacuum density. This restates the record's known rival law; it depends on the imposed relation, which no RG step supplies.

## What is NOT established
- That no RG mechanism can give a0: I audited the published ones and one construction of my own (q = 1/2 with k ~ g/c^2, needing three inserted ingredients). The truncation itself is unreliable in the IR (the EH pole is a truncation artefact, as Reuter-Weyer say) and the IR fixed point is postulated, not derived.
- The UV numbers are from one flow family (Bonanno-Reuter type A, harmonic gauge) with nine regulators plus Litim's gauge limit; they are not a full scheme survey and are not claimed to be the true fixed point. Lauscher-Reuter's type-B/gauge families and larger truncations (f(R), etc.) were not recomputed.
- The near-miss (2 pi with cosmic time) is not excluded as a structure, only shown to be uninformative on this menu.
- The 5-6 papers not audited in full (Bonanno-Platania-type black-hole and cosmology papers) do not touch a0 as far as their abstracts say.
- Nothing here derives kappa = 1/2. Own errors fixed openly: (i) my first `u03` crossover radius used r_S = 2GM instead of 2GM/c^2 (gave r_nc ~ 2.6e8 kpc for 1e11 Msun) -- fixed, now 574 kpc at H0 = 67.4 (Reuter-Weyer: 560 kpc at 70); (ii) my first `u04` assumed every exponential-shape regulator has its pole at lambda = 1/2, false for s > 2 (P'(0) = 1 - s/2 < 0) -- the check now separates monotone from non-monotone P; (iii) `u02` first failed a "G depends only on g* lambda*" check because of my own substitution; (iv) the recall that Reuter's exponential regulator gives lambda* = 0.348 was NOT used (only opened sources are cited); my computation gives 0.359.
