## 6. The escape that is not: the anisotropic stress cannot carry the lensing

The static halo is held by an anisotropic stress, and in the deep field it is exactly **Pi_rr = -Pi_tt**: the
radial "dispersion" is *negative*, sigma_r^2 = -v_inf^2/2 with v_inf^4 = G M_b a0, while sigma_t^2 = +v_inf^2/2.
No kinetic gas can do that; a medium with a shear modulus can. So the natural move is: maybe the *stress* carries
the lensing, and the missing 30-100 M_b does not have to exist.

It does not work, and the arithmetic is one line. The pressure correction to the lensing source is of order
(v_inf^2/2)/c^2: for a 200 km/s galaxy that is 2e-7, against a shortfall of a factor 5 to 18. The active
(Tolman) mass density of a static spherical source is rho + (p_r + 2 p_t)/c^2, and p_r + 2 p_t = Pi_tt here, so
the correction is the same 2e-7. `hy4_crux_check.out` section B prints it for log M_b 9 to 15 on both footings
(2.0e-7 / 2.2e-7 at log M_b 11). Computed: (Pi_rr + Pi_tt)/Pi_tt falls to 6.6e-3 at 100 r_M, so the deep-field
identity is exact and the correction dies as 1/x.

So: the lensing signal is mass, and the mass is not there. **The anisotropic stress tells you what the medium is;
it does not pay the amount.**
