## Normalized claim and assumptions

Let I=[l,r] be a compact interval of length D>0, with a smooth static background satisfying B'=C rho, cs² rho'=−rho g, g=F(B,a_ref exp chi)>0 and J chi''=U'(chi)−T(g,a). Here C=4piG>0, cs²,J>0 are constants, rho>0, lambda=b_g>0, q=−b_chi, T=−W_chi and m=U''−T_chi at fixed g. On the closed interval all coefficients are bounded, rho and lambda are bounded away from zero, and the constitutive law is smooth in this nonzero-field patch. U=S0[cosh(2chi)−1]/4 is an added input, S0>0. Reference normalization a_ref>0 is fixed in this static problem.

For real perturbations xi,psi,eta in H^1_0(I), xi is material displacement, psi=delta phi, eta=delta chi. The claim is the exact constrained potential Hessian and its square decomposition, followed by a sufficient positivity criterion A>0, Bstar>0, A Bstar>Dmix². The claim also includes local existence of backgrounds with boundary data *induced* by an initial-value solution. Positive tau and sigma give positive kinetic energy; no scalar constraint eliminates psi here.

Dependency graph: Q/R constitutive smoothness and the assumed SD1 action -> stationary background equations -> mass-constrained Hessian -> hydrostatic integration by parts and flux identity -> exact square decomposition -> Dirichlet Poincare/Cauchy bounds -> strict positivity. Local ODE existence independently supplies an admissible background patch. None of these edges depends on FGF023 output, literature mechanisms, observational fits or a small numerical eigenvalue.

## 1. Independent derivation of the constrained Hessian

The relevant static energy is

E=integral[e(rho)+rho phi+(W(phi',chi)+J chi'^2/2+U(chi))/C] dx,

where e=cs² rho[ln(rho/rho_ref)−1], rho_ref>0. The first variations are e'(rho)+phi in the density direction; rho−B'/C in the phi direction; and (−J chi''+U'−T)/C in the chi direction. The latter two vanish on the background with Dirichlet field variations. Hydrostatic balance gives (e'(rho)+phi)'=cs² rho'/rho+g=0. Thus the first density derivative is a spatial constant chemical potential.

A material displacement with xi=0 at both walls gives delta rho=−(rho xi)' and integral delta rho=0. Along any sufficiently smooth mass-preserving perturbation path, integral delta²rho=0 also. In particular, pushforward by x=X+epsilon xi(X) has delta²rho=(rho xi²)'' and zero mass variation because xi vanishes at the endpoints. The chemical-potential contribution to the second derivative is therefore zero; it is not silently omitted. Arbitrary second-order field path terms vanish by their background Euler–Lagrange equations. The Eulerian constrained Hessian consequently is

2V=integral{ cs²[(rho xi)']²/rho −2(rho xi)'psi
 +(lambda psi'^2−2q eta psi'+J eta'^2+m eta²)/C }dx.

The cross coefficient is W_gchi=b_chi=−q, and W_chichi+U''=U''−T_chi=m; these derivatives hold fixed g. The kinetic form is integral[rho xi_t²+(tau psi_t²+sigma eta_t²)/C]/2 and is positive for the declared positive inertias. No kinetic cross term is generated by the stated action at a static background.

## 2. Every integration and sign

Expanding the density square and integrating its mixed term gives

integral cs²[(rho xi)']²/rho
=integral{cs² rho xi'^2+cs²(rho'^2/rho−rho'')xi²}
 +[cs² rho'xi²]_l^r.

Differentiating cs² rho'=−rho g yields cs²(rho'^2/rho−rho'')=rho g'. The matter-potential coupling gives

integral −2(rho xi)'psi=integral 2rho xi psi'−[2rho xi psi]_l^r.

The background flux identity is B'=lambda g'+b_chi chi'=lambda g'−q chi'=C rho. Hence g'=(C rho+q chi')/lambda. Completing the psi' square subtracts

(C rho xi−q eta)²/(C lambda)
=C rho²xi²/lambda−2rho q xi eta/lambda+q²eta²/(C lambda).

Combining with rho g' xi² leaves **positive-sign** residuals rho q chi' xi²/lambda and 2rho q xi eta/lambda. Thus exactly

2V=integral{cs²rho xi'^2
 +lambda/C [psi'+(C rho xi−q eta)/lambda]²
 +J eta'^2/C+(m−q²/lambda)eta²/C
 +rho q chi' xi²/lambda+2rho q xi eta/lambda}dx
 +[cs²rho'xi²−2rho xi psi]_l^r.

All displayed endpoint terms vanish for xi=psi=eta=0. No field derivative/flux endpoint condition was imposed. In particular psi'=0 or eta'=0 is not an extra wall condition.

Setting eta=0 alone leaves the chi' contribution. Setting chi'=0 alone leaves the xi eta coupling and the scale-sector quadratic. Only freezing eta and chi' together recovers FGF015's algebraic fixed-scale expression (or the separate formal q=0 limit removes the new cross terms). A constant chi SD1 equilibrium with rho>0, B'>0 and Q/R q>0 generally fails U'=T(g,a), as established by SD1's uniform-scale obstruction. The comparison therefore is not an existence assertion for a constant-chi matter solution in SD1. A field-only Schur bound m−q²/lambda>0 is insufficient to sign the remaining matter/scale terms.

## 3. Sufficient positivity condition

Define d(x)=rho q/lambda, u(x)=rho q chi'/lambda, v(x)=(m−q²/lambda)/C. Dirichlet Poincare gives integral xi'^2 >= pi²/D² integral xi² and the analogous eta inequality. Therefore, dropping no possibly negative term without a lower bound,

2V >= integral lambda/C [psi'+(C rho xi−q eta)/lambda]²
 +A||xi||²+Bstar||eta||²−2Dmix||xi||||eta||,

with the root's A=cs² rho_min pi²/D²+inf u, Bstar=J pi²/(C D²)+inf v and Dmix=sup|d|. The last step is |integral d xi eta|<=Dmix||xi||||eta||. For A,Bstar>0 and A Bstar>Dmix², the real 2x2 matrix with diagonal A,Bstar and off-diagonal −Dmix is positive definite. Hence every nonzero pair (xi,eta) has positive energy. If both vanish, the square is integral lambda psi'^2/C, positive unless psi is constant; its zero Dirichlet trace then forces psi=0.

On a short closed interval around any fixed regular background point, rho_min has a uniform positive lower bound, while u,v,d have uniform finite bounds. A and Bstar grow as positive D^-2 terms and their product as D^-4; thus the sufficient inequalities eventually hold. This shrinks an existing local IVP solution and uses its induced endpoint data at each size. It is not a theorem that arbitrary previously fixed boundary data remain solvable as D changes, nor a necessary stability threshold. Failure of the sufficient inequality alone gives no unstable mode.

For a conservative linear system with these positive kinetic and potential forms, this is an energy stability statement for the declared longitudinal wall problem. It is not a claim about a dissipative fluid, heat bath, moving/free walls, nonlinear continuation or all spatial perturbations.

## 4. Background existence and support

For Q and R on B>0 and a=a_ref exp chi>0, the first-order vector field (B,rho,chi,s,phi)'=(C rho,−rho F/cs²,s,(U'−T)/J,F) is smooth locally. Initial B,rho>0 and finite chi,s,phi admit a unique local solution. Positivity of B and rho persists on a sufficiently short interval by continuity (rho also has the explicit exponential solution along g). Choose a compact subinterval contained in this domain. Endpoint phi,chi values are then fixed boundary data, with wall pressures matched to the background and total mass equal to its integral rho. There is no assertion of a global or arbitrary two-point boundary-value solution.

The equilibrium satisfies the physical local force balance cs²rho'=−rho g and B'=C rho. It contains neither rho−rho0 subtraction in the field source nor a distributed force canceler that makes uniform positive matter stationary in nonzero g. External walls/field boundary values support a bounded patch; this does not make it an isolated self-gravitating system. Excluding g=0 prevents a silent extension through the central degeneracy.

## Supplemental Dirichlet static reduction: independently passed

The separately pinned `DIRICHLET_REDUCTION.md` is correct. For f=C rho xi−q eta and A=lambda, varying integral A(psi'+f/A)^2/C gives (A psi'+f)'=0. Its constant k is forced by the **two** Dirichlet endpoints: k integral A^-1=integral f/A. The minimizer exists in H^1_0 and has psi_min'= (k−f)/A. If psi=psi_min+w with w in H^1_0, the cross term is 2k integral w'/C=0, so the residual is exactly integral A w'^2/C and the minimum is (integral f/A)^2/(C integral A^-1). This is a positive semidefinite rank-one term on (xi,eta), with nullspace integral f/A=0; it is not strictly positive on every nonzero pair.

For the proposed algebraic sine fixture with constant A,C,rho, eta=0 and xi=sin(pi x/D), k=2C rho/pi and I_min=4 C rho² D/(A pi²)>0. Naive cancellation psi'=−f/A produces endpoint jump −2C rho D/(A pi), explicitly violating the second wall. This fixture is correctly labelled algebraic rather than a hydrostatic equilibrium. Dropping the square for a sufficient lower bound remains valid; replacing it by zero in an exact minimized energy generally fails. With tau>0, this is a zero-frequency/static elimination only.
