# A zero scale gradient is not itself an instability certificate

This is a local analytic control, not a claim about continuing a long slab
with unchanged left endpoint data. Use the same regular Q/R equilibrium ODE
at an interior point x=0 with B0>0, rho0>0, chi0=0 and w0=chi'_0=0.
For the inherited cosh U, U'(0)=0 while T(g0,a_ref)>0, so
w'(0)=chi''(0)=-T(g0,a_ref)/J<0. Smooth local existence holds forward and
backward while B,rho remain positive. Consequently w changes from positive
at small negative x to negative at small positive x. All original energy
coefficients are smooth and finite through the crossing; only the weighted
factorization dividing by w is singular there.

Restrict this solution to [-epsilon,+epsilon] with its induced Dirichlet field
values and wall pressures. The original FGF023 Poincare bound applies to
regular backgrounds independently of the sign of w: positive gradient terms
grow as epsilon^-2 while lower-order bounds remain finite. For sufficiently
small epsilon it certifies strictly positive quadratic energy. Thus the
same action has boundary-maintained stable patches containing a transverse
zero of chi'. The existence argument uses the general bound; it is not the
specific FGF023 initial family w_i>0,chi_i=0 at the left wall.

This disproves the inference 'weighted-square criterion fails at w=0,
therefore the physical quadratic energy becomes negative'. It does not
prove that a previously selected longer slab stays stable after its first
turning point, nor that a fixed astrophysical boundary problem is solvable.
No numerical zero-crossing or spectrum was computed. MOND Q/R source,
both a_ref footings and separate frozen histories remain as in the parent.
