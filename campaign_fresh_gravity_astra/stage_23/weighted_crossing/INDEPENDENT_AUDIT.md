## Completion and smooth density

Cauchy-Schwarz maps L2(A dx) derivatives continuously into L1 derivatives.

Its zero-integral kernel is a closed Hilbert subspace. Integration from the
left endpoint identifies that subspace with exactly X_A: absolute continuity,
both endpoint traces and finite weighted energy follow in both directions.
The displayed uniform and L2 estimates follow from the same inequality.
This proves completeness without inserting a center condition.

The proposed smooth-density construction is valid. Cutting away shrinking
neighborhoods of zero/endpoints loses vanishing L2(A dx) mass; on the remaining
compact sets the weight is bounded above and away from zero. Ordinary smooth
approximation there is equivalent to weighted approximation. Continuity of
the integral functional makes the integrals of the approximants tend to zero.
Subtracting that integral times a fixed integral-one smooth function repairs
the zero-integral condition with a vanishing correction. Antidifferentiation
then gives smooth functions compactly supported in the full interval, possibly
constant rather than zero near the center. Their weighted convergence proves
density. Neither a center jump nor two independently chosen center traces

## Supplied spectral and linear-wave construction

An implicit exhaustion step can be made explicit. If a nonzero subspace
remained orthogonal to every chosen eigenvector, any unit vector there would
