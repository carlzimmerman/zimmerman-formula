# Independent gapless-spectrum check, before reading lane implementation

Raw input supplied by the medium worker: a four-component Clifford Hamiltonian with two filled negative eigenvalues, E(k)=sqrt(v^2 k^2+m^2), m=lambda |u|, a circular two-dimensional cutoff |vk|<=Lambda. The following reconstructs its vacuum-energy integral directly from the density of states. No claim about a physical gravitational medium is assumed.

The occupied-state density gives Delta F = -[1/(pi v^2)] integral_0^Lambda x [sqrt(x^2+m^2)-x] dx. Therefore

Delta F = -[(Lambda^2+m^2)^(3/2)-Lambda^3-|m|^3]/(3 pi v^2).

For nonnegative m much smaller than Lambda, this is -Lambda m^2/(2 pi v^2)+m^3/(3 pi v^2)-m^4/(8 pi v^2 Lambda)+O(m^6). With a separately specified positive quadratic counterterm cancelling the first term, the leading cubic is positive. A sheet-to-volume multiplicity nu is required before comparing with a three-dimensional energy density; no such multiplicity is fixed by the integral.

For the canonical generalized remainder derivative K Wrem'(u)=u^2/(a0-n u), integrating near u=0 gives Wrem=u^3/(3 K a0)+n u^4/(4 K a0^2)+O(u^5). Matching the cubic to the fermion integral times nu would require a0=pi v^2/(K nu lambda^3). These are microscopic parameters, not a prediction of a0. The pure fermion remainder's quartic coefficient is negative, whereas every target n>0 has a positive quartic coefficient. Additional analytic interactions can change that coefficient; the match then requires new input. There is no finite capacity pole in the displayed spectrum at finite u.

Thus the specified gapless spectrum can generate the desired nonanalytic sign, but neither its full constitutive law nor n nor the gravitational normalization follows. Its source is a mass bilinear in the Hamiltonian. Identifying its internal vector with physical spatial polarization, and identifying the external conjugate field with baryonic gravity, require their own dynamics. The statements here concern this specified integral only.
