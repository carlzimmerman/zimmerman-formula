## S1. Real constrained matter and its correct potential

Gianguido Dall'Agata and Fotis Farakos, *Constrained superfields in
Supergravity*, [arXiv1512.02158v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.02158v2),
22January2016. Header and actual section3.2 were read directly.

Exact pins: Eqs63-70 impose S(A-Abar)=0 and solve the removed imaginary
scalar, matter fermion and auxiliary. Eqs71-75 give K, W, the unitary-
gauge component action and constrained potential. Eqs76-79 specialize
to the real canonical scalar, with V=f(a)^2-3g(a)^2. Eqs80 and the
following paragraph discuss vacuum and EFT/cutoff requirements.
Section3.1 Eq62 and the adjacent caution explain why a removed auxiliary
cannot be restored using an ordinary unconstrained potential formula.

Application verified here: the source's formula permits the local
holomorphic f_eta branch and yields precisely the displayed U_eta.
Application not established: a complete microscopic UV origin of the
constraints or a supersymmetric higher-gradient MOND/fluid action.

## S2. Ordinary matter-coupled action conventions

Marjorie Schillo, Ellen van der Woerd and Timm Wrase,
*The general de Sitter supergravity component action*,
[arXiv1511.01542v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.01542v1),4November2015.
Header, section2.3 Eqs17-18 and its action/function conventions were read.
It explicitly starts with ordinary off-shell chiral supergravity and its
Kahler metric/covariant-derivative formula before imposing nilpotency.
The finite unconstrained S,T calculation uses that ordinary formula,
not the real-constrained formula from S1. The general supergravity
textbook referenced by S2 was not independently obtained or checked.

Renata Kallosh, *Matter-coupled de Sitter Supergravity*,
[arXiv1509.02136v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1509.02136v1),7September2015,
was authenticated and read as a framework cross-check. The version
record lists onlyv1; the initially probedv2 was unavailable and is not
used. No novel external spectral or nonrenormalization theorem is inferred
from its abstract or from the existence of de Sitter solutions.
