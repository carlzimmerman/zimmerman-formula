## Chiral bosonic action

Muneto Nitta and Shin Sasaki, *BPS States in Supersymmetric Chiral Models
with Higher Derivative Terms*, arXiv:1406.7647v3, 4 November 2014.
[Exact version](https://arxiv.org/html/1406.7647v3).
Eqs.2.2-2.3 define the superspace operator and bosonic components;
Eq.2.7 fixes the single-field auxiliary equation and its factor two.
Eqs.2.10,2.16 and2.23 give the noncanonical branch and potential. The
tensor coefficient may admit spacetime derivatives, as stated below
Eq.2.2; its lowest component determines the bosonic action. This supports
testing regular Lambda(X), not assuming its quantum health. Translation:
K_AA*=g, Lambda unchanged, varphi=A. The dictionary is verified; a healthy
MOND force, tuned discriminant and gravitating vacuum are not supplied.

## Fermion coefficient and real-scalar decomposition

Justin Khoury, Jean-Luc Lehners and Burt Ovrut, *Supersymmetric P(X,phi)
and the Ghost Condensate*, arXiv:1012.3748v3, 1 June 2011.
[Exact version](https://arxiv.org/html/1012.3748v3).
Eq.21 gives the higher-derivative operator including its 3i|F|^2/2
fermion kinetic contribution; Eq.22 decomposes its complex-scalar
quartic invariant. Combined with the canonical kinetic term this gives
Z_psi=g+3Lambda|F|^2 on a constant scalar/auxiliary background. The
coefficient does not prove nonsingular interacting auxiliary elimination.
The timelike chi ghost follows from our use of Eq.22, not a source MOND
claim. The source also distinguishes bosonic absence of F kinetic from
fermionic interactions containing F derivatives.

Michael Koehn, Jean-Luc Lehners and Burt A. Ovrut, *Higher-Derivative
Chiral Superfield Actions Coupled to N=1 Supergravity*, arXiv:1207.3798v2,
9 August 2012. [Exact version](https://arxiv.org/html/1207.3798v2).
Secs.2 and4 provide an adjacent normalization/branch check, with our
Lambda=16 T in that paper's convention. It treats a supergravity
extension and makes no complete fermionic health claim there. It is not
used to identify our rigid fold's potential with observed dark energy.

## Vector convexity class

Igor Bandos, Kurt Lechner, Dmitri Sorokin and Paul K. Townsend,
*ModMax meets Susy*, arXiv:2106.07547v3, 6 October 2021.
[Exact version](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.07547v3).
Eqs.2.18-2.21 give ell(S+D^2/2,P)-xi D, its D equation and convexity
conditions. Eqs.3.15-3.16 are necessary signs from electric convexity in
all Lorentz frames; Eq.3.22 recovers them from nonsuperluminal propagation
on all purely magnetic backgrounds. These are not a sufficient general
all-background causality theorem. Its FI-type invariant differs
fermionically from standard FI despite the same bosonic linear term.
Verified for the stated class; generalized explicit-D actions and our
neutral-gradient portal require separate component/health calculations.

## Standard FI and exact Born-Infeld elimination

Sergei M. Kuzenko, *The Fayet-Iliopoulos term and nonlinear self-duality*,
arXiv:0911.5190v3, 10 May 2010.
[PDF identifying v3 on page 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0911.5190).
Eqs.11-17 give the bosonic BI/FI action, D solution and on-shell action.
Set T=1/g_source^2 and B=sqrt(1+xi_FI^2/T). Canonical photon
normalization and epsilon_displayed/T_eff=1-1/B are our direct deductions.
Eqs.12,21,25-26 also verify the ordinary analytic vector class with
standard FI. The constant is a displayed rigid normalization; the text
after Eq.16 treats it as irrelevant to electromagnetic dynamics. It
does not fix a positive gravitational cosmological constant or a MOND
source amplitude. A field-dependent FI replacement is our diagnostic,
not an operator established by this paper.
