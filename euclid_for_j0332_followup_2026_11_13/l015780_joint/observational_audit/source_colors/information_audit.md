# L015780 source-color information audit

## Observed image information

Inspected `raw_g_diagnostic.png` generated directly from released HSC_G_BGSUB, RMS, model.fits lens/source components, and VIS-reference source/artefact masks. The raw g image contains an extended curved feature following the principal VIS arc, including the bright knot at west ≈−2″, north ≈−1.8″. This persists after subtracting the released lens component; the west-positive counterimage is also visible. Thus g-band source information exists in the pixels, independently of quoting the fitted integrated flux. This is a visual information audit, not a calibrated detection significance or an independent redshift measurement.

The supplied g PSF is broad: after normalizing its sum 0.98979944, encircled energy is 38.9% at 0.5″, 74.6% at 1″, 90.3% at 1.5″. Masking an object's central pixels does not remove its PSF wings. A SW masked feature adjoins the arc and leaves positive residuals; the NE contaminant is also evident. The source mask is based on VIS and the g registration offset is ≈0.025″. The released source morphology in g was fixed from VIS, with a fitted band normalization and registration. It cannot establish that the same spatially resolved source color has been measured everywhere. Lens color gradients, neighbor wings, correlated sky noise, PSF mismatch, and source color gradients remain systematics.

Catalogue lensed-source g/r flux ratio ≈0.6454 (g−r≈0.475 mag), from 5.32375/8.24853 µJy. These are conditional deblended model fluxes; their tiny quoted statistical intervals must not define a redshift likelihood. A redshift likelihood should be fitted to the images and include those nuisance effects.

## Primary passband and IGM evidence

Official Subaru HSC sensitivity page: https://subarutelescope.org/Instruments/HSC/sensitivity.html . It distinguishes center-filter and area-weighted transmissions and explicitly says CCD QE, dewar window, corrector, mirror, and vignetting also matter for total throughput. Observed area-weighted g filter table downloaded: https://subarutelescope.org/Instruments/HSC/txt/wHSC-g.txt . This is filter transmission ONLY, not a complete system response. Main g passband is approximately 4000–5500 Å (primary filter paper https://doi.org/10.1093/PASJ/PSY056 ). Small out-of-band measured values/noise in this table must not be treated as a calibrated red-leak response.

Primary IGM model: Inoue et al. 2014, https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0677 ; journal https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/442/2/1805/985753 . It models neutral-hydrogen Lyman-series and continuum attenuation statistically, rather than declaring every observed wavelength below Lyα identically dark. Its updated continuum attenuation differs substantially from the older Madau prescription. Mean attenuation is not an individual-line-of-sight deterministic bound.

Using atomic transition wavelengths as a wavelength-location diagnostic: Lyα reaches the approximate g red edge at z≈3.52; the Lyman limit reaches it at z≈5.03. These are NOT redshift limits. At z≈4 the main g band samples rest ≈800–1100 Å; at z≈5 it samples ≈667–917 Å. A substantial g arc is therefore qualitatively uncomfortable for an extreme-high-z ordinary galaxy with standard escaping-ionizing-flux and IGM assumptions, especially once the whole passband is below the Lyman limit. However intrinsic UV/ionizing SED, stochastic IGM transmission, emission lines, foreground contamination, and full-system leakage prevent a strict upper bound from this audit. Do not impose z<3.5 or z<5 as an observed fact, and do not use this audit to exclude the theory.

## Minimal defensible next likelihood

1. Fit all available pixels jointly (g,z,MEGACAM r,PANSTARRS i,VIS,Y,J,H) with actual band PSFs, registration uncertainties, separate foreground galaxy color gradients, and explicit neighboring-object light templates extending beyond their masks. Permit at least two source spatial/color components instead of only one fixed VIS color map. Validate g and r arc color using separated arc apertures away from neighbor wings and a separately predicted counterimage.
2. Obtain the original-survey total response curves, observing/filter metadata and zeropoint/calibration uncertainty for each band; current filter-only g curve is insufficient for quantitative synthetic photometry. Check band Galactic-extinction handling from the original pipeline before any extra correction.
3. Use a documented physical stellar-population/nebular library (with flexible age, metallicity, dust, emission-line strengths and an explicit ionizing escape-fraction range) plus Inoue IGM and line-of-sight variation. Include an AGN alternative if allowing extreme ionizing continua. Use z>0.42827, fit a broad redshift domain, and report multimodal solutions, not only a best template.
4. Estimate covariance/systematic floors from empty matched apertures, injection/recovery of sources in this actual lens+neighbor field, PSF-star variations, astrometric perturbations, and held-out arc/counterimage discrepancies. Calibration priors come from survey calibration documentation. No numerical ad hoc floor has been chosen here: without these checks a numeric floor would be arbitrary. Do not turn catalogue conditional errors into a full SED uncertainty.

Conclusion: real raw g arc information makes a joint SED/image analysis worthwhile and can potentially discriminate extreme-high-z rescue scenarios. This bounded audit supplies no conservative numerical upper-z confidence limit. Current lens-capacity calculations must keep z scenarios explicit until the image deblend+SED likelihood is calibrated.
