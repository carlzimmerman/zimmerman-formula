# Scoping: a dynamics-independent gas-mass anchor at z >= 1.6 (data front, 2026-09-30)

Request (orchestrator thread): which PUBLIC data could pin alpha_CO / delta_GDR / alpha_[CI] at z >= 1.6 without rotation curves or dynamical masses; target band <= 0.10 dex (CFG228/229). **Nothing was downloaded.** Only paper pages were read. The fetch tool cached four PDFs outside the repository (Dunne+22 2.5 MB, Coogan+19 2.7 MB, Birkin+23 1.6 MB, Heintz & Watson 0.89 MB) and I read those as text; no archive, ALMA, JWST or VizieR data were touched. Everything below is from the paper text, marked by how it was read. Nothing is from memory except where marked.

## Bottom line
1. **No public route pins the common-mode gas calibration at z >= 1.6 to 0.10 dex.** The best dynamics-free route is an absorption-derived alpha_[CI](Z) (Heintz & Watson 2020, 19 absorbers, z 1.96-3.38). It needs a per-galaxy metallicity, and I could not reproduce its claimed 0.2 dex scatter from its own Table 1 (section 2).
2. The absolute scale of Dunne+22 (the 407-galaxy cross-calibration) is **assumed, not measured** (Milky-Way-like delta_GDR = 135, or X_CI = 1.6e-5 taken from Heintz & Watson for the subset without dust). It constrains RELATIVE scales of CO, [CI] and dust to 0.11-0.15 dex, and says nothing on common-mode.
3. The metallicity scale itself carries 0.14-0.18 dex calibration systematics (Coogan+19; Birkin+23 use 0.16 dex), which with a slope of about -1 already exceeds the 0.10 dex band for any metallicity-dependent correction. (My arithmetic, slope x systematic.)
4. Only three objects with CO + [CI] + dust (Dunne+22 on disk) also have an independent metallicity that I could verify, and two of them also have rotation data (section 3).

## 2. Reproducibility check on R1 (committed: `gas_anchor_scoping/build_hw20_table.py`, `heintz_watson2020_table1.csv`, `hw20_check.txt`)
- Table 1 transcribed (19 rows). The paper's Eq. 3, log alpha_[CI] = log(1.30e-4) + log N(H2) - log N(CI*), reproduces the tabulated value within 0.05 dex for 12 of 19 rows. **Seven do not**: J0643-5041 (table 0.20, Eq. 3 gives 2.18) and J0551-3638 (table 2.25, Eq. 3 gives 0.20) look like swapped values; 120815A, 150403A, 190114A, J1232+0815, J1444+0126 differ by +0.14 to +0.68 (possibly multi-component sums; not resolved from the text).
- The rms of the 19 tabulated points about the paper's quoted relation is **0.64 dex** (median absolute residual 0.33); recomputing alpha from Eq. 3 gives 0.59 dex (median 0.39). I could not reproduce "0.2 dex". Only the 4 objects within |log Z| < 0.4 scatter at 0.25 dex. The paper's scatter may be defined differently (a fit with intrinsic scatter, or the error on the relation); I have not checked the published erratum if there is one.
- Fit sensitivity: unweighted slope -0.93, intercept 1.35; error-weighted (floor 0.05) slope -1.20, intercept 1.49; with Eq. 3 values -1.01, 1.19 (unweighted) and -1.19, 1.44 (weighted). The solar-metallicity alpha_[CI] therefore ranges 10^1.19 to 10^1.49 = 15 to 31 (paper: 10^1.33 = 21; Dunne+22 adopt 17): a spread of about 0.3 dex from fit choices alone.

## 4. What a committed test could do (no download needed beyond the papers)
- Apply Heintz & Watson's alpha_[CI](Z) to objects with [CI] and a Z, and compare with alpha_CO-based masses: they do this themselves for the Valentino+18 sample and report agreement; I did not reproduce it. Error budget for one object at Z~Zsun by my arithmetic: intercept 0.21, fit-choice spread ~0.15, Z scale 0.16-0.18 x slope ~1.1, per-object scatter 0.2-0.6 -> 0.35-0.7 dex. **That is 3-7 times the 0.10 dex target.**
- A sample-level (stacked) version beats the per-object scatter by sqrt(N), but not the common-mode terms (intercept, Z scale).
