## 2. Anisotropy of galaxy dynamics relative to a direction on the sky (gates G7/G8)
- **Positive claims of anisotropy.** Zhou, Zhao & Chang, arXiv:1707.00417 (ApJ 847, 86): hemisphere comparison of the MOND acceleration scale in 147 SPARC galaxies; maximum hemisphere at (l, b) = (175.5°, −6.5°) with a₀ = 1.10 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², minimum at (355.5°, +6.5°) with 0.76 × 10⁻¹⁰, mean 1.02 × 10⁻¹⁰, "anisotropy level 0.37 ± 0.04"; they say isotropic mock data cannot reproduce it. Chang, Lin, Zhao & Zhou, arXiv:1803.08344 (Chin. Phys. C 42): dipole magnitude 0.25 ± 0.04 toward (l, b) = (171.30° ± 7.18°, −15.41° ± 4.87°), not reproduced by isotropic mocks; **the authors themselves say it is "premature to claim that the Universe is anisotropic" given the small samples and observational uncertainty.**
- **What is missing.** I found **no published test of BTFR or RAR residuals versus direction relative to the CMB dipole** and no independent confirmation or rebuttal of the two papers above in what I searched. Whether the SPARC sky distribution (non-uniform) can produce such a hemisphere signal is not addressed in the abstracts. The CMB dipole direction was not looked up here, so the angle between it and (171°, −15°) is not stated.
- **Adjacent (not RC).** Stiskalek, Desmond & Lavaux, arXiv:2509.14997: CosmicFlows-4 Tully–Fisher zero-point dipole of 4.1 ± 0.9% in H₀ and Pantheon+ supernova dipole of 2.3 ± 0.6%, interpreted by the authors as local flows or systematics ("no evidence for local H₀ anisotropy"). A separate claim of ~3% at 3.9σ toward (142° ± 30°, 52° ± 10°) is named in a search summary only (**UNVERIFIED**).
- Reading: the only quantitative published statements are positive anisotropy claims at the 25–37% level in a₀ from one group on one data set, with their own caution; nothing published bounds the effect against the CMB direction.

## 3. River model of gravity and MOND from flowing space
- Hamilton & Lisle, arXiv:gr-qc/0411060 (Am. J. Phys. 76, 519, 2008): space flows like a river through a flat background, objects move by special relativity; for a spherical hole the river falls inward at the Newtonian escape speed, reaching c at the horizon; rotating holes add a Lorentz twist. The abstract does not say whether this is a re-description of GR; the Painlevé–Gullstrand form it uses is a coordinate form of the Schwarzschild solution (from my knowledge, **UNVERIFIED** in these abstracts).
- Braeck & Grøn, arXiv:1204.0419: a river model of space in which space flows radially toward the singularity in Schwarzschild and toward infinity in de Sitter.
- **No MOND-from-flowing-space proposal found.** Nearest vacuum-based statements: a review that states 2πa₀ ≈ cH₀ ≈ c²(Λ/3)^{1/2} and mentions theories in which this coincidence is natural and "strong connections with effects of the quantum vacuum" (arXiv:1605.07458, the page text as summarised; authorship not confirmed, UNVERIFIED); a search summary saying that Volovik-type vacuum approaches recover a₀ ~ c²Λ^{1/2} (**UNVERIFIED**, source not opened); Ultraweak vacuum excitations (arXiv:0904.1272) explicitly does not derive MOND. Dark electromagnetism (Finster et al., arXiv:2312.08811) gets an inverse-distance force from a U(1) with square-root-of-mass charge; not a flow.
- Consequence for the gates: a river-model flow reproduces GR exactly (a prescribed flow can carry any g(r)), which is the "restatement" the gates already exclude; I found no dynamical law for a Λ-flow in the literature.

## 4. Generalized Einstein-aether MOND (Zlosnik, Ferreira & Starkman) and later status
- Zlosnik, Ferreira & Starkman, arXiv:astro-ph/0607411 (PRD 75, 044017, 2007): a non-minimally coupled unit vector field ("aether") as an alternative to dark matter; the abstract covers Newtonian, relativistic and cosmological regimes.
- **GW170817:** Oost, Mukohyama & Wang, arXiv:1802.04303 (PRD 97, 124023): the bound −3 × 10⁻¹⁵ < c_T − 1 < 7 × 10⁻¹⁶ implies |c₁₃| ≤ 10⁻¹⁵ for standard Einstein-aether, leaving a two-dimensional part of parameter space. arXiv:1801.03382 (authors not captured): Einstein-aether has five and generalized TeVeS six polarizations; the speed bound "forces one propagating mode in generalized TeVeS to exceed light speed, effectively ruling out the theory in most parameter regimes"; **whether this covers the Zlosnik–Ferreira–Starkman generalized Einstein-aether is not stated in the abstract.**
- **Cosmology:** Trinh, Pace, Battye & Bolliet, arXiv:1811.07805: generalized Einstein-aether vs Planck: w_de = −1.06 (+0.08, −0.03) (CMB), models with w_de = −1 match ΛCDM, lensing amplitude ~2σ high as in ΛCDM.
- **Successors that pass the GW speed and cosmology tests:** Skordis & Zlosnik AeST, arXiv:2007.00082 (PRL 127, 161302): a relativistic MOND theory that matches the CMB and linear matter power spectra and is ghost-free at second order. Blanchet & Skordis relativistic khronon, arXiv:2404.06584 (JCAP 11 (2024) 040): MOND at galactic scales for stationary systems, GR with Λ in strong curvature, CMB agreement, stable Hamiltonian above wavenumbers ~10⁻³¹ eV.
- Not opened: a pulsar constraint on Einstein-aether from PSR J1738+0333 (arXiv:2605.01436) and stability and universal-horizon papers: named in search snippets only.
- Note for 11C: these theories carry a **unit timelike vector** (isotropic in each rest frame), which is the 11C reading of the owner's picture; none was found with a spatially directional flow.

Not found. What exists: timelike-aether and khronon MOND (item 4); moving dark energy models (item 8) that put a dark-energy rest frame different from the matter frame but do not derive MOND; Milgrom's remark that a₀ has a cosmological connotation (item 3). No published directional-flow MOND with stated anisotropy tests was found in the abstracts read.

## 6. Henneaux–Teitelboim unimodular gravity (Λ as the dual of a 4-form field strength)
- Formulation (abstract-level): HT is "a fully diffeomorphism-invariant formulation of unimodular gravity" with two non-dynamical fields, the scalar (which plays the role of Λ) and a three-form whose exterior derivative is the spacetime volume element (Dynamical Henneaux–Teitelboim gravity, arXiv:2303.12842, which adds kinetic terms to both, studying spherical and FLRW backgrounds).
- Classical content, from a search-result summary (**UNVERIFIED**, the source among several results not opened): action S = ∫d⁴x {√g (M²R/2 − L_QFT − Λ) + (1/3M²) ε^{μνλσ}(∂_μΛ)A_{νλσ}}; varying A gives ∂_μΛ = 0, so Λ is a **constant of integration**, not a coupling; varying Λ gives the spectator equation ε∂A = −3M²√g; a spectator 4-form F = 4∂_{[μ}A_{νλσ]} can be defined.
- Quantum/status: Bufalo, Oksanen & Tureanu, arXiv:1505.04978 (EPJC 75, 477): in the diffeomorphism-invariant version Λ is "an unspecified value of a variable" unrelated to any coupling constant, and the standard unimodular path integral differs from GR in two respects. Carballo-Rubio, Garay & García-Moreno, arXiv:2207.08499 (CQG 2022): unimodular gravity uses transverse diffeomorphisms and Weyl rescalings with a fixed volume form; the only substantive difference found from GR is that Λ is technically natural in the former.
- Not answered by these abstracts: which quantities are gauge and which physical in the HT construction, and any link between the conjugate vector/three-form and galaxy dynamics.
- Unrelated causal-set-style proposal: Shaw & Barrow, arXiv:1010.4262: Λ as a field fixed by a causally restricted variational principle, predicting Ω_k0 = −0.0056 s_b/0.5; explicitly not three-form or unimodular.

## 7. Three-form dark energy
- Koivisto & Nunes, arXiv:0907.3883 (PLB): the minimally coupled canonical three-form can give scaling, possibly transient acceleration and phantom crossing; an effective potential gives an intuitive picture.
- Koivisto & Nunes, arXiv:0908.0920 (PRD 80, 103509): background dynamics and linear perturbations, (super)inflating attractors and saddle points for inflation or dark energy, effects on matter perturbations, and a discussion of stability and equivalence with other formulations.
- Recent constraints: Bouhmadi-López, Chiang, Boiza & Chen, arXiv:2512.09991 (JCAP): a late-time 3-form with a Gaussian potential can reproduce a positive Λ when the potential does not evolve and can be quintessence-like or phantom-like; MCMC with Planck PR4, DESI DR1 BAO, Pantheon+, DES Y1 and Cepheid calibrators gives H₀ from 67.89 ± 0.36 (ΛCDM) to about 68.29 km/s/Mpc. Other three-form papers (arXiv:1212.2541 coupled, 1608.01679 interacting, 2606.27436 multi-probe) were named in snippets only (**UNVERIFIED**).
- The w(z) equations and stability conditions are in the papers' bodies; not read.

## 8. Dark energy with flow or momentum; sound speed and anisotropic stress
- Moving dark energy: Maroto, arXiv:astro-ph/0512464 (JCAP 2006): if the CMB, matter and dark-energy rest frames differ, an observer at rest in the CMB frame can still see a dipole; bulk-flow inferences would be biased if dark-energy motion is ignored. Maroto, arXiv:astro-ph/0609218: dark energy as a perfect fluid with its own rest frame. Beltrán Jiménez & Maroto, arXiv:0811.3606 (JCAP 0903:015): large bulk flows at 100–300 Mpc/h could indicate moving dark energy at decoupling. No numerical bound on the dark-energy velocity is in these abstracts.
- Sound speed: Probing the sound speed and clustering of dark energy, arXiv:2511.22478: with DESI DR2 BAO, Planck 2018 and Union3, for time-varying w, log₁₀ c_s² = −3.00 (+2.9, −0.99); an EFT approach supports c_s² ≈ 0.3–0.4; earlier Planck-era analyses left c_s² largely unconstrained (constant w ≈ −1).
- Anisotropic stress: arXiv:2004.13917 (parametrised post-Friedmannian, Pantheon + BAO + H₀ + Planck): −0.30 < g₀ < 0.32 and 0 ≤ c²_m < 0.01 to keep deviations from ΛCDM within 10% at k ~ 0.01 h/Mpc; −0.15 < g₀ < 0.16 for 5%.

## 9. Links between a dark-energy current/flux and galaxy dynamics or MOND
No paper found that ties a dark-energy current or flux to galaxy dynamics. What exists is the numerical coincidence a₀ ~ cH₀/2π ~ c²(Λ/3)^{1/2} (Milgrom, as summarised above) and vacuum/superfluid approaches (Volovik-type, UNVERIFIED) that claim a₀ ~ c²√Λ; emergent-gravity variants that treat dark energy as an elastic medium whose response to baryons is the "MOND force" (a search summary, **UNVERIFIED**; Verlinde's paper and its tests are in `DOORS_LITERATURE_STATUS_2026-09-29.md`).
