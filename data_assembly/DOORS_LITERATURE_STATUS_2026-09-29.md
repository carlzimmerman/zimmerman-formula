# Ten doors: what the published literature says (data front, 2026-09-29)

Scope: for each door in `campaign_fresh_gravity/closure_map/TEN_DOORS_GATES_2026-09-29.md`, the key papers and what
each one states about (a) a MOND-like / phantom-halo density, (b) a published no-go or observational test.
**Method and limits.** Every claim below was read from the arXiv *abstract page* (HTML) of the cited paper on 2026-09-29,
by a page-summariser, not from the full text. So each row is **abstract-level**: it says what the authors state, not
whether the body supports it, and I did not check any derivation. No author was contacted, nothing was downloaded.
Where a statement rests on a search snippet I did not open, it is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Nothing here is a calculation,
and none of it grades a door against G1–G5 (that is the calculation thread's job).

## Corrections to my own earlier notes
- arXiv:2403.11301 is Capozziello, Mazumdar, Meluccio, "Nonlocal Gravity and Dark Energy" (infinite-derivative gravity,
  concludes nonlocal corrections "could be an efficient mechanism" for dark energy). My earlier search snippet had it under a
  different title ("Can nonlocal gravity really explain dark energy?"); the two do not match, so I do **not** cite it
  for a negative result. It is not a Deser–Woodard or Maggiore–Mancarella paper.
- arXiv:1907.02047 (Belgacem et al.) is about the RT nonlocal model's GW luminosity distance, not a constraint from
  GW170817; it is a *prediction* for future detectors.

## Door 5 — Collisional relaxation f(E,L) (β = −(3/2) ρ_b/ρ̄_b as in the door's own definition)
This is the door with the least published support because the mechanism is this project's own; the literature is the **positivity
constraint on the distribution function**, which any spherical f(E,L) with a prescribed β(r) must satisfy.
| paper | stated | relevant |
|---|---|---|
| An & Evans, arXiv:astro-ph/0511686, ApJ 642, 752 | Cusp–central-anisotropy theorem: with density ∝ r^−γ near the centre, the limiting β cannot exceed γ/2 | Central constraint on any prescribed β. |
| Ciotti & Morganti, arXiv:1001.3632, "On the global density slope-anisotropy inequality" | Proposes the inequality holds at all radii for spherical systems with positive distribution function, offered as analytical evidence and numerical cases (the abstract calls it a conjecture-level statement); their companion arXiv:1006.2344 is named in a snippet only, **UNVERIFIED** | Global check on β(r) versus the density slope. |
- What follows for the door, stated as a check and not a result: a door with β = −(3/2) ρ_b/ρ̄_b has β<0 wherever baryons are present; γ ≥ 2β is then trivially satisfied by those inequalities when β ≤ 0, so these papers do **not** rule it out (my reading of the abstracts; not verified in the derivations, and the calc thread should confirm before relying on it). The binding constraints are then G1/G3 and stability, not positivity.
- Not found: any paper testing a baryon-tracing β against rotation curves. **UNVERIFIED** that none exists.
