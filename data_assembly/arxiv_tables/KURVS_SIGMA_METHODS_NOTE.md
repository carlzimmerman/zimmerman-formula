# What the KURVS-CDFS paper says about its dispersion profiles and pressure support (data front, 2026-09-29)

Source: the paper's TeX on disk, `~/new_physics/_external_data/arxiv_src/2305.04382/kurvs_I_arXiv_May2023.tex` (Puglisi+2023, arXiv:2305.04382); line numbers refer to that file. Quotes are short; **inferences are marked (mine)**.
Written on the request of the "Complete gravity theory" chat, from files already on disk (no download, no contact). It reports what the authors state; it does not evaluate it.

## (1) Are the σ(R) profiles corrected for beam smearing?
- **The plotted σ(R) is the observed profile, not beam-smearing corrected.** Instrumental broadening is corrected: "the velocity dispersion is corrected for the instrumental broadening measured from the width of the sky emission lines" (l. 325). The figure caption separates "the observed and beam smearing corrected measure of the intrinsic dispersion, σ₀" as two horizontal lines on the profile (l. 344); the profile points are the observed ones (my reading of that caption, consistent with l. 323: "Maps for the Hα flux, ... observed velocity dispersion").
- The beam-smearing correction applies **only to the single σ₀ value**: "we apply a beam-smearing correction by using the prescriptions provided by Johnson+18 ... within 3.4 times the disc scale radius" (l. 389), and "we assume that the beam smearing correction is negligible in the outer disc, and we do not apply beam smearing corrections for kinematic parameters computed beyond R′₃.₄D" (l. 390). σ₀ = weighted mean of ≥ 3 pixels with S/N ≥ 3 beyond R′₃.₄D or R′₂.₂D (l. 398), corrected with Johnson+18 Table B1, "based on the radial extent at which the velocity dispersion is measured and the velocity gradient of the galaxy" (l. 401). Table note: "Intrinsic velocity dispersion, corrected for instrumental broadening and beam smearing" (l. 499).
- Check the authors give: outer-disc σ versus full-profile σ, both beam-smearing corrected, ratio 1.13 ± 0.26 (l. 403–404).

## (2) Intrinsic dispersion or line width including unresolved motions?
- The maps come from a Gaussian fit to the Hα+[NII] complex in each spaxel after adaptive binning (l. 315–325): the line width is a free parameter, "imposed to be wider than that of the sky lines" (l. 322, "particularly relevant ... in the outer regions"), then corrected for the instrumental width. Nothing removes unresolved bulk motion, non-circular motion, clumps or outflows: it is the fitted line width of the ionised gas. The authors flag pixels sampling "broad components that do not trace the overall kinematics of the disc" and clip them (l. 341, l. 387, l. 521).
- (mine) Beyond R′₃.₄D the profile therefore includes any unresolved velocity gradient over the PSF that was not corrected, in addition to turbulence; whether that is small is what the authors assume at l. 390.

## (3) What the authors say about pressure support / asymmetric drift, and whether it is applied to V
- **Not applied to the outer velocities in the tables.** Appendix table note: velocity at the maximal extent is "inclination-corrected" (l. 882); velocity at R′₆D is "inclination-corrected" (l. 885); at R′₃D "inclination- and beam-smearing corrected" (l. 883). No pressure-support correction is in those columns.
- **Applied only in the inner-kinematics f_DM analysis:** "we must correct the 'beam smearing corrected' rotation curves for pressure support ... using the Burkert+10 formalism, considering as our characteristic velocity dispersion σ₀ the value from the Hα velocity dispersion profiles" (l. 629–631); GalPaK3D is "adopted ... only to model the inner regions of the galaxies' discs" (l. 626). So the correction uses one σ₀ per galaxy.
- Their own caveats: "measurements of the circular velocity in high-redshift discs are largely dependent on the pressure-support correction, especially for low-mass galaxies ... and at large radii, because the correction increases as a function of radius" (l. 766); "Pressure support corrections for high-redshift galaxies assume a constant characteristic value for the velocity dispersion throughout the disc, and a vertical geometry which are currently poorly constrained by data" (l. 767); AO-assisted IFU data are needed (l. 768); colder tracers such as molecular gas "will be important to understand the impact of feedback and outflows on measurements of the velocity dispersion, as these processes are expected to affect mostly the ionised gas" (l. 769).
- Local comparison: no pressure correction is applied to z ~ 0 THINGS discs because σ is much lower there (l. 1060–1061).

## (4) S/N and PSF at 4–7 R_d; can the outer σ be dominated by PSF or non-circular motions?
- PSF: average seeing FWHM 0″.57 (l. 268); the rotation-curve pseudo-slit radius of 0″.3 "roughly correspond[s] to the 1σ width of the seeing ... (~0″.25)" (l. 361). Effective radii are ~1.5× the PSF size on average (l. 612).
- S/N: adaptive binning until S/N(Hα) ≥ 5, binning radius up to 4 spaxels (a 9 × 9 block of 0″.1 spaxels), otherwise the spaxel is masked (l. 317–319); σ₀ uses pixels with S/N ≥ 3 (l. 398). **No S/N or PSF value specific to 4–7 R_d is given.** The paper states that the outer rotation-curve slopes "can be robustly recovered with minimal corrections" (l. 613) and that 70 h reaches R_6D for the average system (Fig. caption, l. 291).
- Per-galaxy appendix notes (l. 930–1040) flag asymmetric or irregular σ profiles for KURVS 4, 5, 10, 12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22 and outer-edge broad-component pixels for KURVS 6, 12, 16, 19; for KURVS-13: "the outer velocity dispersion profile is consistent with being flat within the uncertainties"; for KURVS-15: "shows some asymmetries in the outer regions"; for KURVS-17 the outer pixels "have large uncertainties". Excluding the clipped pixels "does not change the outer rotation curve shape" (KURVS 6, 12, 16, 17, 19).
- (mine) At z ≈ 1.5, 7–13 kpc is about 0″.8–1″.5, i.e. about 1.4–2.6 seeing FWHM (using ~8.5 kpc/″, an approximation not in the paper). Binning to 9 × 9 spaxels makes neighbouring outer points correlated and averages over 0″.9. Whether the outer σ is dominated by PSF-smeared inner motion, clumps or non-circular motion is not tested in the paper; for KURVS-15 and others it flags asymmetries, and for a few objects it names outflows or broad components (l. 544).

## (5) Thick discs; V/σ at the outermost points
- Thick discs: the paper uses a Freeman thin-disc model as an interpolator (l. 380: "a razor thin disc model may not be a good approximation for high-redshift star-forming galaxies, which mostly present thick and turbulent discs"), the GalPaK3D disc has a Gaussian thickness scale height 0.15 R_eff (l. 615), and it says a disc with q₀ = 0.2 "would decrease the velocity of the baryonic component by ~10 per cent at the effective radius" (l. 640), within the f_DM uncertainties. "More observations are needed to constrain the discs' intrinsic thickness" (l. 639). Stellar inclinations assume q₀ = 0.2 (l. 184).
- V/σ: defined as V(R′₆D)/σ₀ with the corrected σ₀ (l. 411); the sample median is 1.6 ± 0.2 (16th–84th percentile 1.0–2.0); all but three galaxies have V/σ₀ ≥ 1; the discs sample uses V/σ₀ ≥ 1.5 (l. 412–416). The authors give no V/σ at radii beyond R′₆D or at the outermost single point.

## What the paper does not say
- It does not state that the constant-σ Burkert correction was tested against the measured outer σ(R); the only check on radial dependence is the 1.13 ± 0.26 outer-to-full ratio (l. 403), which refers to radii beyond 2.2–3.4 R_d, not 4–7 R_d.
- It does not give σ(R) uncertainties beyond the 1σ from the line fit, and it does not quantify contamination of the outer σ by unresolved bulk motion.
