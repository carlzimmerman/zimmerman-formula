# Fact-check of `AMENDMENT17_DRAFT_NOT_FILED.md` (76009607a) against ESA's draft DR4 data model (data front, 2026-09-29)

Read only. The draft amendment and every frozen file were not edited. Source: the draft data-model PDF held outside the repo (`~/new_physics/_external_data/gaia_dr4_draft_datamodel/gaia-dr4-prerelease-draft-data-model.pdf`; zip sha256 d807eae98acbeec0a0af2ce6a5d7d352298df6f270a1f13207cc8d1becf42c66; PDF issue D revision 0, dated 2026-05-20).
Page numbers are the PDF's printed page numbers (= PDF page index + 1). **The PDF is a draft that says it may change without notice, so every finding is "draft 2026-06-26, confirm on release day".** Verdicts: true / false / unclear.

| # | claim in the draft amendment | verdict | what the PDF says (page) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the NSS group has ten tables: `nss_acceleration_astro`, `nss_epoch_flags`, `nss_masses`, `nss_multiple_orbits`, `nss_multiplicity`, `nss_non_linear_spectro`, `nss_resolved_pair`, `nss_two_body_orbit`, `nss_vim_fl`, `optical_pair` | **TRUE** | Chapter 10 "Non-single stars" (contents; chapter starts p. 725) lists exactly these ten: nss_acceleration_astro p. 725, nss_epoch_flags p. 741, nss_masses p. 744, nss_multiple_orbits p. 750, nss_multiplicity p. 776, nss_non_linear_spectro p. 777, nss_resolved_pair p. 788, nss_two_body_orbit p. 811, nss_vim_fl p. 843, optical_pair p. 851. **One access point the draft does not mention:** nss_epoch_flags is served by DataLink ("not available through the main archive TAP interface", retrieval type EPOCH_FLAGS_NSS, p. 741); the other nine are labelled [TAP] |
| 2a | `nss_resolved_pair` holds "joint astrometric solutions for two resolved sources ... a common parallax, differential positions and proper motions"; the draft (d) expects a joint solution of the pair itself "to be linear-motion only" | **FALSE as a description; the expectation is not what the PDF's model list allows** | The table's `solution_type` values (pp. 789–791) are: `ResolvedPairFixed` (common proper motion and parallax), `ResolvedPairLinear` (common parallax, different proper motions), **`ResolvedPairAcceleration`** (adds an acceleration term), **`DiverseAcceleration`** ("a significant acceleration term not associated with the visible-pair motion for at least one of the components. Likely a hierarchical triple or quadruple system"), **`ResolvedPairJerk`** (acceleration and jerk) and **`DiverseJerk`** (same "likely a hierarchical triple or quadruple system"). So orbital-type (acceleration and jerk) models exist in this table, and the two `Diverse*` types are explicitly hierarchical-system flags: exactly the contaminant cut 12 is for. The table also has `curvature_relative_motion` (p. 804), `total_mass_from_separation_au` "in the case of a physical resolved pair with acceleration motion" (p. 807), and quality flags including `ORBVEL` "the orbital velocity for a linear model is consistent with a Keplerian orbit" (p. 805) |
| 2b | separation range: "The sample's pairs are at least 8″ apart"; is ≥ 8″ plausible in this table | **UNCLEAR** | The table has `separation` (mas, p. 803) and `separation_au` (p. 803) columns but **no minimum or maximum separation is stated** in the pages read. The only radius statement near it is in `nss_multiplicity` (p. 776): "a given source may be involved in more than one pair (with a maximum 20 in a radius of 4 arcsec) in crowded fields". (My inference, not stated: this suggests the resolved-pair search radius is 4″, in which case pairs ≥ 8″ apart would not be in the table; I cannot confirm from the draft.) The PDF has no "8″" or "250 pc" statement |
| 3 | `nss_epoch_flags` is per-transit flags only, with no solution of its own | **TRUE, with two details** | "This table contains the flags indicative of whether certain observations were used or not for the non-single stars processing ... used in combination with the epoch tables" (p. 741). Its 11 columns: `solution_id`, `source_id`, `source_id_secondary`, `solution_type`, `transit_id`, `al_ccd_used`, `al_used`, `g_used`, `bp_used`, `rp_used`, `rv_used`. **It does carry `solution_type`** ("the non-single star model which has been adopted for the published solution") and `source_id_secondary` ("left empty for non-resolved ... models"), so a row identifies which NSS model a source has, but holds no model parameters. It is a DataLink product, not a TAP table (p. 741) |
| 4 | `optical_pair` "names chance alignments" | **TRUE, and it carries one acceleration item** | "pairs of sources analysed in the context of the resolved binary pipeline, and for which a best astrometric solution corresponds to that of two chance alignment sources ... the astrometry solution to use is that tabulated in gaia_source and only the separation derived by the resolved binary pipeline is reported here" (p. 851). Five columns confirmed: `solution_id`, `source_id`, `source_id_secondary`, `separation` (mas, "average angular separation"), `acceleration_flag` (byte). **`acceleration_flag`** "indicates whether one of the sources in the optical pair was identified as an unresolved acceleration solution" (bit 1 = the brighter source, bit 2 = the fainter; p. 852). So a nonzero flag is a pointer to an acceleration solution of a component; it is not a solution itself and presumably duplicates `nss_acceleration_astro` (my inference) |
| 5 | `nss_multiplicity`: four columns; anything not derived from another NSS solution | **columns TRUE; the second half UNCLEAR** | Columns (p. 776; an earlier version of this note said p. 777, which was wrong): `solution_id`, `source_id`, `number_of_pairs` ("Number of source identifiers involved in a pair with the source_id of interest"), `related_sources` (long array "listing all source identifiers with which the source_id forms pairs"). The table lists "all pairs involving a given source" from "DR4 processing of resolved pairs" (p. 776) and is meant to let users check consistency of astrometric solutions of triplets AB, BC, AC. It holds pair membership, not solution parameters, so it is a pair list, not a non-single-star solution; whether every listed pair also has a row in `nss_resolved_pair` is not stated in the draft |
| 6 | anything contradicting the rule "count tables carrying a non-single-star solution" | **YES, four points** | (i) `nss_multiplicity` carries **no solution** (a list of related source ids), yet the amendment's (a) counts it among the solution-carrying tables. (ii) `nss_masses` is "masses derived from the non-single stars (NSS) solutions with orbital parameters" (p. 744; its `combination_method` values include `PAIR_M1` and `ORBITAL_*`, `SB*`, `VIMO_*`, i.e. derived from other tables); it is derived, not a primary solution, so counting it is redundant with the tables it comes from. (iii) `nss_epoch_flags` carries `solution_type` and `source_id_secondary` (a per-row model label) though no parameters, and is DataLink-only, so a TAP-based loader cannot read it. (iv) The own-pair exemption (b) would exempt `Diverse*` and `ResolvedPair(Acceleration/Jerk)` entries, which the PDF describes as acceleration/jerk (and for `Diverse*` "likely a hierarchical triple or quadruple system") solutions of the pair's own components; item 2a shows the amendment's premise (linear-motion only) does not match the model list |

## The pieces of the PDF the amendment would want to cite
- Page 789: the solution-type list starts (`ResolvedPairFixed`, `ResolvedPairLinear`, `ResolvedPairAcceleration`), p. 790: `DiverseAcceleration`, `ResolvedPairJerk`, p. 791: `DiverseJerk`.
- Page 776: `nss_multiplicity` intro (the only stated radius, 4″, and only for crowded fields), page 777: its four columns.
- Page 741: `nss_epoch_flags` (DataLink, flags only); page 851–852: `optical_pair`.
- Page 744: `nss_masses` (derived).

## What this does not settle
Whether DR4's final model keeps these solution types; whether ≥ 8″ pairs enter `nss_resolved_pair` at all; how many pipeline pairs would appear in it. Those are release-day questions.
