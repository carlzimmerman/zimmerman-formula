Does cold energy relax onto the law? (CFG748)

Collisionless shells starting at rest. m(<r)/r = 1 is the law. The mass-starved start stays short at 50 r_M. The cold-infall start failed its energy-conservation control (3.2e-3 against 1e-3), so treat its curve with that caveat.

Different starting states (CFG747, t = 2000–3000)

Every start lands within 2% of the law out to 5 r_M, but not beyond. This lane failed its frozen outer-radius criterion (R1 FAIL) and its force-check bar (1e-6) sat below floating-point noise. The result is inner halo only.

Orbiting cold energy is unbound (CFG749)

Total initial energy, in the same units. Binding is the baryons' alone, −1.50. Any orbital speed of 30% of the law's circular speed pushes the total positive, so the mechanism needs cold energy nearly at rest.

Settling time of the cold medium (CFG743, CFG699)

Solid: oscillation period at the cold medium's own halo density (CFG743). Dashed: period if the medium sits at the cosmic mean density (CFG699), 36–113× too slow. Bar: 1.38 Gyr, ten periods in a Hubble time. A short period is not settling, since CFG744 shows settling also needs damping.

Damping needs a light independent field (CFG744)

One-crossing time r/c_P, the exponent-free lower bound. If the polarization is the cold medium (waves at 20–220 m/s) it fails by orders of magnitude. An independent field at c_P = c relaxes within a Hubble time. That leaves the cold energy as a separate component, with baryon-state dependence as the open selector.

All values are read from the committed lane READMEs and result JSONs (CFG743, 744, 747, 748, 749, 699). The Israel–Stewart fluid form (η = pτ/2α, Kn = √2/4α, Re = 4α) has no result curve of its own, so it is not plotted.

Do groups survive the selector? (CFG745)

Shift in group mass from the selector, for two group gas-fraction values ν_g. The bar is the 2σ limit, 0.0645. Three of four scenarios sit at or just above it (TENSION). The test brackets ν_g rather than computing it per group.

Hot gas needed by early types against what eROSITA allows

M_hot inside 100 kpc in units of M*. The needed amount is below every allowed value, so the selector is not excluded. For the alt footing at Z = 1 the margin is thin (0.17–0.19 against 0.198). The hot gas around these galaxies is not measured.

Sealed prediction: X-ray luminosity

Predicted L_X (0.5–2 keV) range for three gas metallicities. Across all four scenarios and metallicities it sits 1.2–70× below the eROSITA stack's 1.1e40 erg/s. That is consistent with the stack but not a test, and the highest-metallicity case comes close.

Sealed prediction: hot-gas mass and falsifier

Predicted M_hot(<100 kpc). The tick on each bar is half its value. A measured M_hot for log M* 10.5–11.0 quiescent galaxies below half the tabled values kills the selector as the early-type explanation. The decisive measurement is a mass, from SZ (Y D_A² ≈ 2e-10 Mpc²) or X-ray profiles.

Values are from cfg745_results.json and cfg746_predictions.json. No SZ or X-ray profile data were read. The selector has no mechanism: nothing explains why cold mass is retained in proportion to hot gas.

F1 depends on L* only through (r/L*)². F2 is set by Earth's field. F3 has no offset: the supported source's basin is 8.7 μm across, inside the 5.8-cm source. The F2 source-dependent term is a second-order heuristic, not a solved AQUAL problem.
