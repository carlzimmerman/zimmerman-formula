Does cold energy relax onto the law? (CFG748)

Collisionless shells starting at rest. m(<r)/r = 1 is the law. The mass-starved start stays short at 50 r_M. The cold-infall start failed its energy-conservation control (3.2e-3 against 1e-3), so treat its curve with that caveat.

Different starting states (CFG747, t = 2000–3000)

Every start lands within 2% of the law out to 5 r_M, but not beyond. This lane failed its frozen outer-radius criterion (R1 FAIL) and its force-check bar (1e-6) sat below floating-point noise. The result is inner halo only.

Orbiting cold energy is unbound (CFG749)

Total initial energy, in the same units. Binding is the baryons' alone, −1.50. Any orbital speed of 30% of the law's circular speed pushes the total positive, so the mechanism needs cold energy nearly at rest.

Settling time of the cold medium (CFG743, CFG699)

Solid: oscillation period at the cold medium's own halo density (CFG743). Dashed: period if the medium sits at the cosmic mean density (CFG699), 36–113× too slow. Bar: 1.38 Gyr, ten periods in a Hubble time. A short period is not settling, since CFG744 shows settling also needs damping.

Damping needs a light independent field (CFG744)

One-crossing time r/c_P, the exponent-free lower bound. If the polarization is the cold medium (waves at 20–220 m/s) it fails by orders of magnitude. An independent field at c_P = c relaxes within a Hubble time. That leaves the cold energy as a separate component, with baryon-state dependence as the open selector.
