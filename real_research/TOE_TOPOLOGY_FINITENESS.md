So: no torus. A topological framework that "fits ours" must be a *different kind* of topology.

Banks–Fischler assumes a *constant* Λ → a *fixed* finite Hilbert space. **DESI says Λ is evolving** (§ the TOE
doc). Consequences, flagged as speculative:
- S_dS ∝ 1/H_∞² ∝ 1/ρ_DE(future). If dark energy **weakens** (the DESI w>−1 direction), the future de Sitter
  radius **grows**, S_dS **grows**, and the universe's DOF budget is **increasing** — the Hilbert space is not

  a finite quantum system" becomes "the universe is a finite quantum system whose size is *growing as dark
  energy fades*." A genuinely novel, if speculative, global picture forced by DESI.

Maybe, but it is **not required** and **not the framework's claim**:
- *If* the CMB low-ℓ anomalies are real and topological (COMPACT's open case), a horizon-scale compactification

  vs the *dimension* of the Hilbert space). The framework is **agnostic** about the spatial shape.
- The one *suggestive* link: a finite holographic DOF budget makes a *finite/compact* universe natural rather
  than surprising — but it does **not** pick a torus over any other compactification, and Planck already caps
  the scale at ≳ horizon. So: holographic finiteness *motivates* "the universe is finite," and *permits* but
  does not *predict* a horizon-scale spatial topology that COMPACT might still find.
