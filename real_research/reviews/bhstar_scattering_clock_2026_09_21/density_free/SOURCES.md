# Source and novelty boundary

See the parent [source ledger](../LITERATURE_AND_ROUTES.md) for the verified JWST
papers, catalogue, and the older spectral/temporal Comptonization literature.

This extension derives its transport result internally from the generator; it
does not import an unverified astrophysical scaling. The argument depends on
the explicitly defined transport model, elementary conditional expectations,
the stopped-generator identity with an integrability justification supplied
in the proof, and the parent's derived thermal-variance identity.

Additional bounded searches on 2026-09-21 used combinations of "mean escape
time", "mean delay", "sphere", "optical depth", "Thomson", "line width",
"exact", and "Dynkin". Searches found longstanding work on photon residence
times and spectral/time coupling. They did not establish global novelty for
the precise radial identity or the density-free band.

[Becker 2003](https://mason.gmu.edu/~pbecker/papers/komp/komp.pdf), sections 5.1–5.2,
describes photon escape and time-dependent Comptonization. Its constant escape
time example uses a specified spatial source eigenfunction. It must not be
confused with the central point-source boundary problem proved here. Both
belong to established radiation-transport physics.

[Noebauer and Sim 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41115-019-0004-9)
provides Monte Carlo radiative-transfer background. Its homogeneous-sphere
appendix uses constant emissivity and absorptive opacity; that escape-fraction
benchmark is a different problem from the conservative central-source delay.
No numerical benchmark from that appendix is used to certify our theorem.

The physically interesting additional statement is a falsifiable relation
between an independently measured radius, temperature, spectral variance and
mean scattering delay. A conditional prediction can be scientifically useful
without establishing either new fundamental physics or observational success.
