# The final attack: is the one surviving prediction even novel? (No.)

**Date:** 2026-06-02 · *literature check on the framework's sole distinctive claim.*

After two rounds of red-team, the framework's only distinctive content was the evolving-a₀
prediction, a₀(z)=a₀(0)E(z). This note checks it against the literature. It does not survive as
novel, and the observable is degenerate with ΛCDM — both confirmed by published work.

## 1. The evolving-a₀ idea is Milgrom's (2014), not new

**Milgrom, "Cosmological variation of the MOND constant," Phys. Rev. D (2014), arXiv:1412.4344.**
Milgrom himself proposed that the MOND acceleration constant decreases with cosmic time, tied to
the a₀≈cH₀ coincidence, and worked out the consequences:

> *"The proximity of the MOND acceleration constant with cosmological accelerations … points to
> its possibly decreasing with cosmic time."* … *"if a₀ is proportional to cH at all relevant
> times, this change amounts to a factor of ~2.5 since redshift 10."*

That **is** the framework's premise (a₀∝cH) and its evolving prediction. The framework adds only
(i) a specific coefficient Z=2√(8π/3) in place of Milgrom's 2π — which is non-unique and carries
~0 bits — and (ii) a specific exponent. (Note Milgrom's quoted "×2.5 since z=10" is *shallower*
than the framework's a₀∝E(z), which gives ×20.5 at z=10 — so the framework's steep √ρ_total form
is a particular, more aggressive choice within Milgrom's already-published idea, not a new idea.)

## 2. The observable is degenerate with ΛCDM — and the simulation exists

**Tian, Yu, et al., "ΛCDM with baryons vs. MOND: the time evolution of the universal acceleration
scale in the Magneticum simulations," MNRAS (2022), arXiv:2206.04333.** In ΛCDM hydrodynamic
simulations, the *apparent* best-fit a₀ **rises by ≈3 from z=0 to z=2.3** — with no fundamental
a₀ evolution at all, purely from halo/baryon evolution. Compare the framework: E(2.3)=3.46. So
ΛCDM and evolving-a₀ MOND predict **nearly the same apparent-a₀ rise** over the range where the
data exist (incl. the MUSE-DARK z≈0.9 point the framework leans on). This is exactly the Piece-3
[C] degeneracy — now confirmed by a published simulation, not just argued.

A related program (TFR redshift evolution as a modified-gravity test) is already active in the
literature; the framework's "decisive test" is a field others are already running.

## 3. What residual novelty remains (being maximally fair)

- The **specific coefficient** Z=2√(8π/3) — non-unique (29/5 fits the data value better), ~0 bits.
- The **specific exponent** p=1 (a₀∝√ρ_total) within Milgrom's evolving-a₀ idea — a choice, and
  the one the data (weakly, ~2σ) prefer over Milgrom's shallower readings.
- The **θ-coupled AeST construction** (a₀=cθ/3Z) and the exact δq⁰⁰=0 linear-CMB-safety result —
  a specific covariant *implementation*, possibly not previously written down in this exact form.

That is the honest residual: a particular implementation and exponent-choice within a 2014 Milgrom
proposal, whose observable signature ΛCDM already reproduces. **The physics idea is not the
framework's, and the one place the framework is sharper than Milgrom (the steep p=1 form) is the
place ΛCDM mimics best.**

## Bottom line

The sole surviving distinctive claim is **not novel** (Milgrom 2014) and **not distinctive from
ΛCDM** over the tested range (Magneticum 2022). Combined with the two red-team rounds, the honest
final standing is: a coherent, falsifiable, CMB-safe *implementation* of Milgrom's evolving-a₀
idea, with no original physics content that is both distinctive and confirmed. It remains testable
— but only as a tie-breaker against ΛCDM at z≳4 on extended galaxies, where the apparent-a₀
predictions of the two finally diverge — and that test has not been done.
