# Classical radial flow and energy stability of the smooth halo

18 October 2026. Claim ZAR-S2. Take the exact smooth distributions in [EXACT_SMOOTH_INVERSE.md](EXACT_SMOOTH_INVERSE.md), separately for the tethered and free populations, at fixed sufficiently small positive widths. Their densities and potentials are precisely the previous finite target. The claim is linear stability for dynamically accessible spherical radial perturbations, in the weighted energy norm below, with ordinary L1 control and classical evolution for smooth data. Uniform Ck stability, nonlinear stability and nonspherical stability are not claimed.

## The unchanged positive quadratic

Put w_i = -partial_E F_i on physical phase space. It is nonnegative, smooth and compactly supported, with

\[
\chi_i(r)=\int w_i\,d^3v<\infty,\qquad
W_* =\sum_i\int w_i\,d\Gamma<\infty.
\]

Define D_i = v partial_r + (L/r^3+a_i) partial_v. A smooth radial canonical generator g_i preserves L and gives the ordinary smooth perturbation h_i = -w_i D_i g_i. Set

\[
\mathcal A[h]=\sum_i\int h_i^2/w_i\,d\Gamma,
\qquad m_i(r)=C\int_{r_0}^r\int h_i(s,v,L)\,dv\,dL\,ds.
\]

Zero weights are omitted, and perturbations are extended by zero where w_i = 0. This is inherited from h_i = -w_i D_i g_i and its weighted completion; no arbitrary zero-weight component is permitted. The field matrix is exactly

\[
H(r)=\begin{pmatrix}
2G^2M_t/(a_0r^4)&-G/r^2\\
-G/r^2&-G/r^2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \delta a=Hm.
\]

Write B_i = -a_i'-3a_i/r, and

\[
\kappa_t^2=G(M_b+M_f)/r^3+G^2M_t^2/(a_0r^5),\quad
\kappa_f^2=G(M_b+M_t+M_f)/r^3.
\]

The fixed target has 0 < beta <= B_i <= K and kappa_i^2 >= b = GM_b/R^3 > 0. The [previous warm identity](../warm_radial_2026_10_18/ACCESSIBLE_STABILITY.md) now applies with one ordinary smooth DF for each species; packet labels are unnecessary. Explicitly, with the odd generator part g_o,

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_i&=C\int w_i g_o^2\,dv\,dL,\\
T_i&=C\int w_i r^2v^2[D_i(g_o/(rv))]^2\,dv\,dL,\\
z_i&=m_i^2/\mu_i,\qquad C\int w_i v^2\,dv\,dL=\mu_i.
\end{aligned}
\]

On Omega, m_i = -C int w_i v g_o dv dL and J_i >= z_i. All supported quantities are zero outside Omega, with no boundary trace assumption needed. Integration by parts and the actual force derivatives give

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q[h]&=\mathcal A[h]+\int m^THm\,dr\\
&=\mathcal A_{\rm even}+
\sum_i\int[T_i+B_i(J_i-z_i)+\kappa_i^2z_i]dr\\
&\quad+\int\frac{G\mu_t\mu_f}{r^2}
\left(\frac{m_t}{\mu_t}-\frac{m_f}{\mu_f}\right)^2dr.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here A_even denotes the generator-even, velocity-odd sector. Therefore, with c = min(1,b/K) > 0 and

\[
C_H=\sup_r\|\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\mu_i})H
\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\mu_i})\|_{\rm op}<\infty,
\]

\[
c\mathcal A\leq Q\leq(1+C_H/\beta)\mathcal A.
\]

The mass-map bound sum_i int m_i^2/mu_i <= A/beta follows first from B_i >= beta, so these estimates do not presume conservation or a solution.

## Classical linear evolution for smooth data

Each species has its own compact invariant physical support K_i. Both lie in a common compact subset of the hollow radial phase space. On a neighborhood of these supports all equilibrium forces are smooth. If needed they can be smoothly extended away from the occupied region for the purpose of a characteristic existence proof; the extension does not affect any occupied trajectory or field source.

The exact linearized equation is

\[
\partial_t h_i+D_i h_i=w_i v\,\delta a_i[m].
\]

For functions supported in this invariant compact region, h maps continuously into m and delta a in each Ck norm: integrating h over fixed bounded velocity and angular-momentum ranges and once in radius introduces no derivative loss. Multiplication by the fixed smooth w_i v is bounded on each Ck space. Background characteristic transport is a strongly continuous group on smooth compact data and has finite Ck operator bounds on every finite time interval.

The Duhamel successive-approximation series therefore converges on each finite interval in every fixed Ck norm. The resulting compatible solutions give a unique global classical smooth linear evolution for smooth radial accessible initial data. This statement permits derivative growth with time; it is not a uniform Ck stability estimate.

The source is itself generated by the radial potential perturbation, since -w_i D_i(delta Phi_i) = w_i v delta a_i. Thus evolution stays in the closure of accessible perturbations in A. It agrees with the bounded-source Hilbert flow already constructed in the previous checkpoint: the free group is unitary in A and the field source obeys

\[
\|\mathcal B h\|_{\mathcal A}^2
=\sum_i\int\mu_i(\delta a_i)^2dr
\leq (C_H^2/\beta)\mathcal A[h].
\]

Both constructions use the same Duhamel series; this gives the identification without assuming extra regularity of arbitrary energy-space initial data.

## Conservation and ordinary perturbation control

Let j_i = C int v h_i dv dL be the shell current. Continuity gives partial_t m_i = -j_i; the source has zero velocity integral. Consequently

\[
\frac12\frac d{dt}\mathcal A=\sum_i\int j_i\delta a_i dr,
\qquad
\frac12\frac d{dt}\int m^THm\,dr=-\sum_i\int j_i\delta a_i dr.
\]

For arbitrary finite-energy initial data these identities have the mild-flow interpretation justified in the previous checkpoint. For the present smooth data they also hold along the classical evolution. Hence Q is conserved and

\[
\mathcal A[h(t)]\leq\frac{1+C_H/\beta}{c}\mathcal A[h(0)]
\qquad\hbox{for all real }t.
\]

The finite W_* now adds the ordinary-function estimate

\[
\sum_i\|h_i(t)\|_{L^1(d\Gamma)}
\leq\sqrt{W_*}\sqrt{\mathcal A[h(t)]}
\leq\sqrt{\frac{W_*(1+C_H/\beta)}{c}}
\sqrt{\mathcal A[h(0)]}.
\]

Thus every completed accessible perturbation is an ordinary L1 function, rather than merely a distribution at a circular ridge. Small initial weighted energy controls all later weighted energy, L1 perturbation mass and the supported mass-map norm. This is not an estimate from the initial L1 norm alone, nor uniqueness among unrestricted distributional solutions.

The constants refer to a fixed warm equilibrium. In particular W_* need not remain bounded in the singular cold limit. The construction supplies no nonlinear, nonspherical, formation or temperature-selection theorem. The scope remains the same spherical deep-cubic effective model with its selected amplitude and population fraction.
