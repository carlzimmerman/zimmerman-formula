# Canonical labels and bounded first response work

19 October 2026. Claims ZAR-Q5 and ZAR-Q6. This advances the resonant-integration route from [checkpoint 07](../initial_reserve_2026_10_19/RADIAL_PROPAGATION_OBSTRUCTIONS.md). The uniform work estimate below is for the first field response to freely transported linear canonical data on compact eccentric orbital bands. It does not control full linear feedback, the nonlinear flow or the flat support edge.

## Exact nonlinear canonical energy labels

Let S_i^t be the actual characteristic flow and T_i0 the initial fixed-L canonical map. Define the advected label

\[
e_i(t,z)=E_i^F\bigl(T_{i0}^{-1}(S_i^t)^{-1}z\bigr),
\qquad f_i(t,z)=F_i(e_i(t,z),L).
\tag{18}
\]

The label is smooth whenever the finite-time flow and initial map are smooth. It avoids dividing by F_i,E at the flat cutoff. Put

\[
u_i=e_i-E_i^F,\quad
\psi_i=\Phi_i[f]-\Phi_i^F,\quad
\delta a_i=-\partial_r\psi_i,\quad
D_i^F=v\partial_r+(L/r^3+a_i^F)\partial_v.
\]

The actual transport of e_i is zero and its transport of E_i^F is v delta a_i. Hence exactly

\[
(\partial_t+D_i^F)u_i
=D_i^F\psi_i-\delta a_i\partial_vu_i,
\tag{19}
\]

\[
\boxed{\frac d{dt}(\psi_i-u_i)(t,S_i^tz)
=\partial_t\psi_i(t,S_i^tz).}
\tag{20}
\]

Thus the endpoint work cancellation survives nonlinearly. On the occupied set e_i is an old occupied energy, so e_i<=-beta_0. A bound ||u_i(t)||_infinity+||psi_i(t)||_infinity<=beta_0-eta would maintain the negative actual-energy gap eta, because E_i[f]=e_i+(psi_i-u_i). The old Lyapunov norm controls the potential part on r>=rho but does not bound the label displacement u_i or the accumulated term delta a_i partial_v u_i over infinite time.

At linear order h_i=F_i,E u_i and (19) reduces to (partial_t+D_i^F)u_i=D_i^F psi_i. For an initial canonical flow generated by g_i0, u_i0=D_i^F g_i0, a smooth extension even where F_i,E vanishes. The unresolved cutoff issue is uniform propagation of this extension, rather than an unavoidable finite-time division singularity for canonical data.

## A scalar oscillatory integral with actual resonances

Let a be integrable, bounded and uniformly continuous on the real frequency axis, extended by zero outside its support. Define its modulus omega_a(s)=sup_(|x-y|<=s)|a(x)-a(y)|. For any rho_nu>0 with finite Dini integral,

\[
\boxed{\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb R,\ t\ge0}
\left|\int_{\mathbb R}a(\nu)
\frac{e^{i(\omega-\nu)t}-1}{\omega-\nu}\,d\nu\right|
\le8\|a\|_\infty
+4\int_0^{\rho_\nu}\frac{\omega_a(s)}s ds
+\frac2{\rho_\nu}\|a\|_1.}
\tag{21}
\]

There is no frequency-gap assumption. To prove this, split at |nu-omega|=rho_nu and subtract a(omega) on the symmetric inner interval. The exterior is at most 2||a||_1/rho_nu. The inner difference is at most 4 int_0^rho_nu omega_a(s)/s ds. The constant part is a multiple of 2i int_0^rho_nu sin(tx)/x dx; splitting at x=1/t and integrating the remaining oscillatory tail by parts bounds its modulus by 8||a||_infinity. The t=0 case is zero, and truncation handles an endpoint at 1/t. All constants here are safe upper bounds, not optimal values.

## The actual first response and its frequency density

Use action-angle variables q=(J,L), theta, on compact eccentric equilibrium bands, with dr dv=dJ dtheta and Omega_i(q)>0. Put C_Gamma=4 pi^2 and dmathfrak_m=2 pi C_Gamma dJ dL. The actual orbit kernels are the double Fourier averages

\[
\mathcal K_{ij,kn}(q,q')=
\frac1{(2\pi)^2}\int\!\!\int
e^{-ik\theta}P_{ij}(r_i(\theta,q),r_j(\theta',q'))
e^{in\theta'}d\theta d\theta',
\]

\[
P_{ff}=P_{ft}=P_{tf}=-G/\max(r,r'),\qquad
P_{tt}=2c\int_{\max(r,r')}^\infty M_{F_t}(s)s^{-4}ds.
\tag{22}
\]

For canonical linear data h_jn(0,q')=in Omega_j F_j,E g_jn, first freely transport h_jn^0(t,q')=exp(-in Omega_j t)h_jn(0,q') and form its first potential response psi^0=P h^0. The source index n=0 contributes zero and is omitted from the sums and coarea formula below. This is not the solution of the coupled linear system.

Along a receiving equilibrium orbit with theta=theta_0+Omega_i(q)t, its work is a sum, over receiving k and source j,n, of

\[
-e^{ik\theta_0}\int
\nu\mathcal K_{ij,kn}(q,q')h_{j,n}(0,q')
\frac{e^{i(\omega-\nu)t}-1}{\omega-\nu}
\,d\mathfrak m(q'),
\quad \omega=k\Omega_i(q),\quad\nu=n\Omega_j(q').
\tag{23}
\]

Suppose the source actions lie compactly inside a band where |grad Omega_j|>=gamma_nu>0. Ordinary changes of variables in finitely many frequency charts give the coarea density

\[
a_{ik,jn}(q,\nu)=2\pi C_\Gamma\nu
\int_{\{n\Omega_j=\nu\}}
\frac{\mathcal K_{ij,kn}(q,q')h_{j,n}(0,q')}
{|\nabla(n\Omega_j)(q')|}\,d\sigma(q').
\tag{24}
\]

Formula (23) is then -exp(ik theta_0) times the scalar integral in (21). Compact smooth source cutoffs vanish near the frequency-chart boundaries. Consequently a Lipschitz integrand in these charts has a compactly supported Lipschitz frequency density, including its extension by zero. This supplies the Dini condition with no pole deletion.

Such source bands are available in the actual model. On a closed subannulus of the exact law region a_i^F=-C_i/r^2-D_i/r with C_i,D_i>0. At the circular limit,

\[
L_{c,i}=C_i r+D_i r^2,\qquad
\Omega_i(0,L_{c,i}(r))^2=C_i/r^3+2D_i/r^2.
\tag{25}
\]

The frequency decreases with r and L_c increases, so partial_L Omega_i<0 there. Smooth dependence of the small eccentric orbits retains this strict sign in a sufficiently small positive-J band. A compact band away from J=0 and the energy cap satisfies the stated submersion condition and lies in the original positive warm distribution.

## Summing receiving harmonics for these kernels

Fix a finite set N of nonzero source angle harmonics and take a smooth initial canonical generator supported in those harmonics and source bands. Constants and the source norm below may depend on N; no uniformity as that set grows is asserted. Receiving actions range in any fixed compact eccentric band. The source orbit has two simple radial turning points. Thus its angular occupation indicator has the uniform estimate

\[
|A_n(s+\eta;q')-A_n(s;q')|\le C|\eta|^{1/2},
\quad
A_n(s;q')=\frac1{2\pi}\int
\mathbf1_{\{r_j(\theta',q')\le s\}}e^{in\theta'}d\theta'.
\]

Indeed the angle measure near a simple turning point is at most a constant times the square root of the radial window. Averaging (22) over the source angle gives radial derivatives

\[
\partial_rP_{ff,n}=G A_n(r;q')/r^2,
\qquad
\partial_rP_{tt,n}=-2c M_{F_t}(r) A_n(r;q')/r^4,
\tag{26}
\]

with the Newtonian expression also applying to both cross kernels. Composition with the smooth receiving r_i(theta,q) is uniformly C^(1,1/2). Integrating one theta derivative by parts and applying a half-period shift to its Holder-half derivative gives

\[
|\mathcal K_{ij,kn}(q,q')|
\le C_n(1+|k|)^{-3/2}.
\tag{27}
\]

Separately, P_ij is Lipschitz in its radial arguments on the compact positive radial ranges. Smooth source orbit dependence therefore bounds the source-action Lipschitz norm of K independently of k. Frequency charts in (24) imply

\[
\|a_{ik,jn}\|_\infty+\|a_{ik,jn}\|_1
\le C_n(1+|k|)^{-3/2},\qquad
\operatorname{Lip}_{\nu}a_{ik,jn}\le C_n.
\tag{28}
\]

In (21) use omega_a(s)<=min(2||a||_infinity,Lip(a)s). Splitting its Dini integral where these bounds meet yields a bound

\[
C_n(1+|k|)^{-3/2}[1+\log(2+|k|)],
\tag{29}
\]

summable over k. There are finitely many source n. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\sup_{t\ge0,\theta_0,q\in B_i}
\left|\int_0^t\partial_s\psi_i^0
(s,\theta_0+\Omega_i(q)s,q)ds\right|
\le C_{B,S,N}\|g_0\|_{\mathrm{source}},}
\tag{30}
\]

where B_i is the receiving compact eccentric band, S the source bands, and the source norm is any fixed C^1 action norm on the finitely many Fourier coefficients large enough to bound (28). The derivative partial_s in (30) is the partial time derivative evaluated on the moving orbit. The estimate is linear in the generator and independent of time. It handles the actual diagonal resonances by integration, rather than assuming them absent.

The canonical initial data have h_jn=i nu F_j,E g_jn. Their resonant numerator in (24) is i nu^2 F_j,E K g_jn, not generally zero. Thus the bound is a cancellation/regularity result, not an automatic zero supplied by canonicality.

## Why this is not yet nonlinear confinement

The actual linear equation also has the feedback term

\[
h_{i,k}(t)=e^{-ik\Omega_i t}h_{i,k}(0)
+ik\Omega_iF_{i,E}\int_0^t
e^{-ik\Omega_i(t-s)}\psi_{i,k}(s)ds,
\quad\psi=\mathcal P h.
\tag{31}
\]

Its coefficients involve the fixed equilibrium susceptibility F_i,E, so feedback is order one even when the initial perturbation amplitude tends to zero. An O(amplitude) first-response bound cannot be iterated by calling this feedback an O(amplitude squared) remainder. A genuine bounded-resolvent or time-domain feedback estimate remains needed.

The source bands are also not invariant perturbation subspaces: F_i,E D_i psi generally creates response on other occupied orbits. Truncating feedback to those bands would change the equations. The estimates above do not cover receiving orbits approaching the circular line or flat support edge. Finally (19) contains nonlinear label gradients and uses the actual, rather than equilibrium, paths.

Thus (30) is a proved new part of the resonant calculation for verifiable canonical initial generators. The remaining all-time support theorem still requires uniform control of the full feedback and nonlinear label displacement, including the original support boundary. No future decay, spectral regularity or invariant-band assumption has been inserted as an established fact.
