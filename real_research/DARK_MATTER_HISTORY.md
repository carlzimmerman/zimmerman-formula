# A History of Dark-Matter Models, Read as a Ledger of "Predicted vs. Fitted"

**C. Zimmerman, June 2026.** *The honest publication-level history: where dark-matter modeling was genuinely
predicted, and where — and when — it began to accommodate the data. The mantra for each step:
**Measured** (the datum) → **Inferred** (the discrepancy, under assumed dynamics) → **Modeled** (the halo) →
**Predicted or Fitted** (the crux). ΛCDM's large-scale record (CMB, BAO, LSS) is genuinely predictive and is
flagged honestly at the end; the critique here is specifically galaxy-scale.*

---

## The one-line answer to "when did they start changing the models to fit the data?"

**Not with the halo idea itself (that was predicted), and not with NFW (that was a real prediction) — but with the
small-scale crises of the late 1990s–2010s** (cusp/core, missing satellites, too-big-to-fail, rotation-curve
diversity). Gravity-only cold dark matter made falsifiable predictions, the galaxy data **disagreed**, and each
standard fix **added a baryonic-feedback prescription whose strength was calibrated to reproduce the very cores,
counts, and diversity observed.** That is the documented turn from *predicting* to *accommodating*. The numbers
(halo mass per galaxy) get assigned either as **free per-galaxy fits** or via **abundance matching, which is tuned
by construction to the observed galaxy stellar-mass function.**

## 1. The discrepancies — measured, inferred, no halo yet (1933–1981)

| Paper | Measured | Inferred |
|---|---|---|
| **Zwicky 1933** (Helv. Phys. Acta 6,110); 1937 (ApJ 86,217) | Coma velocity dispersion ~1000 km/s | virial mass ≫ luminous → "dunkle Materie" *(but used H₀~558; magnitude inflated, existence survives)* |
| **Babcock 1939** | M31 rotation to 20 kpc | high outer velocities — attributed to **absorption / "new dynamics," NOT dark matter** |
| **Freeman 1970** (ApJ 160,811) | M33/NGC 300 21-cm vs exponential disk | "additional matter … at least as large as visible" *(cautious wording)* |
| **Rubin & Ford 1970; Rubin, Ford & Thonnard 1978/1980** (ApJ 238,471) | optical rotation curves **flat** across 21 spirals | mass ∝ r beyond the light *(connected to existing halo ideas, didn't derive one)* |
| **Bosma 1978/1981** (AJ 86,1825) | extended **HI** curves *well beyond the stars* | the decisive purely-observational leg |

**Verdict:** robustly **measured**; missing mass **inferred** under Newtonian dynamics. *No halo shape assumed.*
The founders were cautious — the "dark matter" reading was imposed later. **The logical fork (extra mass vs.
modified dynamics) is wide open at this stage** — which is exactly what MOND later exploits.

## 2. The halo — existence predicted, shape reverse-engineered (1973–1980s)

- **Ostriker & Peebles 1973** (ApJ 186,467): N-body disks are **bar-unstable** without a massive halo → a genuine
  **prediction of halo *existence*** (halo/disk mass ≳ 1–2.5), independent of rotation curves.
- **Ostriker, Peebles & Yahil 1974 / Einasto, Kaasik & Saar 1974** (two groups, opposite sides of the Iron
  Curtain): galaxy masses **underestimated ~×10**, rising to ~1 Mpc.
- **The singular isothermal sphere (ρ ∝ 1/r²):** gives a **flat rotation curve *by construction*** (M∝r ⇒ v²=const).
  It has independent physical motivation (relaxed isothermal gas), so it is **not pure curve-fitting** — but its
  adoption as *the* halo was driven because its **shape reproduces flat curves**, it needs an **ad-hoc core
  radius** to avoid a central singularity, and its **2 parameters are fitted per galaxy.**

**Verdict:** halo *existence* = **predicted**; halo *shape* (SIS) = **physically motivated but reverse-engineered**
to give flat curves, with **per-galaxy fitted** parameters. *First place the field accommodates rotation curves.*

## 3. NFW — the strongest genuine prediction; per-galaxy numbers fitted (1996–)

**Navarro, Frenk & White 1996/1997** (ApJ 462,563; 490,493): ρ(r) = ρ₀ / [(r/r_s)(1+r/r_s)²], inner slope −1
(**cusp**), approximately **universal** across CDM halos.

- **Predicted or fitted?** The *functional form* is an **empirical fit to simulation output** (still no accepted
  first-principles derivation of why CDM relaxes to NFW) — **but it is a real prediction**: an *output of CDM
  gravity*, fixed *before* confronting rotation data, falsifiably specifying a **cuspy** center. This is the
  strongest genuinely-predictive halo result in the story.
- **How the numbers get assigned (where fitting re-enters):** the 2 parameters are **M₂₀₀** and **concentration
  c = r₂₀₀/r_s**. Per galaxy they are **two free fit parameters** (often + stellar M/L as a third), or constrained
  by the simulated **c(M) relation** (which has enough scatter to absorb much freedom). The **galaxy↔halo mass
  link** comes from **abundance matching** (Vale–Ostriker 2004; Moster 2010/2013; Behroozi 2010/2013) — the
  stellar-mass–halo-mass relation built **by matching number densities, i.e. tuned by construction to the observed
  stellar-mass function.** Fitted, not predicted.

## 4. The crux — where the models were CHANGED to fit galaxy data (the small-scale crises)

In every case: gravity-only CDM **predicted**, the data **disagreed**, the fix **added calibrated feedback.**
*(Honest counterweight: feedback is real astrophysics — supernovae do inject energy. The critique is that its
strength/timing is **tuned**, and one mechanism must do several different jobs at once.)*

| Crisis | CDM predicted | Observed | The fix (calibrated) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cusp–core** (Flores & Primack 1994; Moore 1994; de Blok 2001) | central **cusp** ρ∝r⁻¹ | dwarfs/LSBs show **cores** | bursty-SN outflows heat the cusp into a core (Governato 2010; Pontzen & Governato 2012); **DC14 (2014)** makes the inner slope a *tuned function of M\*/M_halo* |
| **Missing satellites** (Klypin 1999; Moore 1999) | hundreds–thousands of subhalos | ~dozens | reionization suppression + feedback **tune which halos light up** *(partly eased by ultra-faint dwarf discoveries — data moved up too)* |
| **Too-big-to-fail** (Boylan-Kolchin+ 2011) | massive subhalos too dense | not seen | feedback cores, or **lower the MW halo mass** (change a parameter) |
| **Diversity** (Oman+ 2015) | nearly self-similar inner curves | **wide diversity** at fixed V_max | requires feedback (+ non-circular motions, inclination) to vary **galaxy-by-galaxy** — accommodation per object |

**This is the answer to your question.** The decisive shift from *predicting* to *accommodating* is the small-scale
crises, resolved by **subgrid feedback calibrated to reproduce the observed cores/counts/diversity.**

## 5. The MOND counterpoint — stated fairly (1983–)

**Milgrom 1983** (ApJ 270,365/371/384): **one** constant a₀≈1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², no per-galaxy halo. Predicted ahead
of time: **flat curves** automatically; the **Baryonic Tully–Fisher** V⁴=G M_b a₀ (McGaugh 2012); individual curve
*shapes* from baryons alone. The killer: the **Radial Acceleration Relation** (McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016, PRL
117,201101; Lelli 2017) — 2693 points, 153 galaxies, observed acceleration a **tight one-variable function of the
baryonic** one, scatter ~0.13 dex **consistent with all-observational**. In MOND this *is* the law; in ΛCDM the
**tuned feedback of §4 must *also* conspire to make a tight, one-variable relation** — natural in one theory,
engineered in the other. *(Whether ΛCDM produces the RAR "naturally" — Ludlow 2017 — or must fine-tune it is a
genuine, unresolved interpretive tension.)*

**But MOND's failures are real and on the ledger:** galaxy **clusters** (residual ×2 missing mass *even in MOND*);
the **Bullet Cluster** offset (lensing tracks galaxies, not the X-ray gas — needs extra dark mass even in MOND);
the **CMB third peak** (needs non-baryonic CDM; relativistic MOND / Skordis–Złośnik 2021 fits it only by *adding
fields*). At cluster and cosmological scales **ΛCDM is genuinely predictive.**

## 6. Fact-check of the high-z "scaling-MOND resolves the masses" idea — honest

- **(i) False as literally phrased.** The "impossible early galaxies" (Labbé+ 2023) have **stellar masses from SED
  fitting of starlight** — *not* dynamics, *not* a₀. Changing a₀ does **nothing** to them. The real tension is a
  **baryon-budget / star-formation-efficiency** problem (Boylan-Kolchin 2023), separate from a₀. High-z *dynamical*
  masses use **Newtonian** estimators (M~σ²r/G), also not a₀-based. So "they used local a₀" does not describe the
  methodology.
- **(ii) The logical core is real but applied wrong.** It is algebraically true that MOND needs *less* dynamical
  mass for given kinematics (M_b=V⁴/Ga₀), and that a *larger* a₀ lowers that mass — and there *is* a genuine
  evolving-a₀ literature (the a₀~cH coincidence). **But** (a) it's irrelevant to SED stellar masses, and (b) the
  evolving-a₀ data (MUSE-DARK III; Mayer 2022) find a₀ *increasing* with z and read it as galaxies being **more**
  dark-matter-dominated early — the **opposite** direction from "masses were overestimated." A real kernel, wrapped
  in an incorrect application. *(Consistent with `project_a0z_reconciled.py`.)*

## The through-line

The mass discrepancies (Zwicky→Bosma) are **measured** and **inferred** — solid. Halo **existence** was
**predicted**; the isothermal **shape** was **reverse-engineered**; **NFW** is a genuine **prediction** but its
**per-galaxy numbers are fitted** and abundance matching is **tuned to the stellar-mass function.** The decisive turn
to *accommodation* is the **small-scale crises**, fixed by **calibrated feedback.** **MOND** is the live galaxy-scale
alternative — one constant, no per-galaxy halo, the **RAR** as its strongest card — **but breaks at clusters, the
Bullet, and the CMB**, where **ΛCDM predicts.** The field's true, unresolved state: **ΛCDM accommodates at galaxy
scale and predicts at large scale; MOND predicts at galaxy scale and breaks at large scale.** That standoff — not a
clean win for either — is the honest history.
