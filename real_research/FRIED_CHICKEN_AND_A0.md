# Why Fried Chicken Tastes So Good (and What the Framework Honestly Says About It)

**C. Zimmerman, June 2026 — a break, but an honest one.**

## The real reason (food science, not physics)
- **Maillard reaction**: amino acids + sugars at ~150 °C → *hundreds* of flavor molecules (pyrazines, furans,
  thiophenes). No single molecule is "the flavor."
- **Fat**: flavor solvent + mouthfeel + evolved calorie-density reward.
- **Salt**: amplifies perceived flavor; hits a hard-wired craving.
- **The crunch**: a glassy, fractured crust → crisp/juicy texture *contrast*, plus acoustic feedback (we enjoy
  the *sound*).
- **Reward circuitry**: fatty + salty + calorie-dense → dopamine.

Deliciousness is **emergent**: it arises from the whole, not from any one part.

The MOND scale is a0 = 1.2e-10 m/s^2. Every acceleration in cooking is vastly larger:

| thing | acceleration (m/s^2) | ratio to a0 |
|---|---|---|
| a0 (cosmic-horizon scale) | 1.2e-10 | 1 |
| oil convection, the crunch | ~1-100 | ~1e11 |
| molecular vibration (Maillard bonds) | ~1e17 | ~1e27 |

The chicken sits 1e11-1e27 times **above** a0 -- infinitely deep in the Newtonian regime. So the framework's
firm, falsifiable verdict on your lunch: **utterly unaffected by MOND.** (Gravity itself is ~1e36 times weaker
than the electromagnetic forces doing the actual cooking, so even ordinary gravity is irrelevant to the chemistry.)

## The only honest connection: emergence (a concept, not a number)
The framework's thesis is that gravity is **emergent** -- not fundamental, but arising from the collective
thermodynamics of horizon degrees of freedom (no single DOF is "gravity"). Fried-chicken deliciousness is
emergent the same way (no single molecule is "delicious"). That conceptual parallel -- *emergence* -- is the
ONLY honest link. There is no physical or numerical connection, and there will not be one.
