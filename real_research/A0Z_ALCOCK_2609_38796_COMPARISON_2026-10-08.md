# a0(z) on MUSE-DARK: this repository's record vs Alcock (MNRAS Letter; arXiv:2609.38796), 2026-10-08

**Script:** `real_research/reviews/alcock_2609_38796_comparison.py` → `.out`, `_results.json`. It uses on-disk inputs only: the MUSE-DARK III stated-results CSV, the CFG262 stage-B JSON, and Alcock's Table 1, typed in from the paper.

**Source:** full text read from the published PDF (4 pages), 2026-10-08. This revision replaces the abstract-only version of the same day.

## The paper

**Bibliographic details**
- **Citation:** G. T. Alcock, *Is the acceleration scale of the radial acceleration relation tracking the cosmic expansion rate?*, MNRAS 551, L1–4 (2026), doi:10.1093/mnras/stag1485.
- **Author:** independent researcher, single author.
- **Dates:** received 2026 July 10, accepted 2026 August 3, arXiv:2609.38796 posted 2026-09-30.

**Method**
- **Data:** the four MUSE-DARK III z-bins (Ciocan et al. 2026b, A&A 709 L16, Fig. 3). The two end values are printed in the source; Alcock digitised the middle two and all the error bars (his Table 1).
- **Fits:** weighted least squares on the bins, treated as independent. Bars are read both as 1σ (c68) and as 95% intervals (c95). AICc is used for model choice, χ² with one degree of freedom for nested tests. Fits are run with and without a SPARC anchor (1.20 ± 0.26). Background is Ωm 0.307.

**Claims**

| Claim | Under c68 | Under c95 |
|---|---|---|
| Fitted exponent γ | 0.78 ± 0.15 | 0.78 ± 0.08 |
| Frozen a0 excluded | 5.3σ (6.6σ with anchor) | ≥10σ |
| (1+z)^1.5 excluded | 4.8σ | 9.5σ |
| a0 = A·E(z) | preferred: χ² 5.2/3, A = 1.40 ± 0.03 | in tension: χ² 19.9/3, γ below E(z) at ~4σ |

Further claims:
- "Faster than H(z)" depends on the anchor. With the amplitude free, the binned growth is mildly *shallower* than E(z).
- The exponent conclusions are invariant under z-independent rescalings of a0.

**Caveats he states himself (§4)**
- The exponent is still exposed to z-dependent systematics, "e.g. stellar-mass estimates degrading with z", which the survey "bound[s] but do[es] not eliminate".
- The bins share calibration, so treating them as independent is "optimistic" for the absolute significances.
- All significances are statistical only.
- The amplitude statements are conditional on the published mass scale.
- An evolving external-field effect (EFE) could steepen the apparent rise.

**Discussion points (§5)**
- A ΛCDM emergent rise is "plausible".
- He recommends a per-galaxy re-analysis with E(z) imposed.
- **Directly relevant here:** "frameworks tying a0 to the cosmological constant inherit the same pressure". This framework's flat law (w = −1), and its DESI-w variant (≈1.06 at z 0.4, ≈1.0 at z 1), are exactly what he says is under ~5σ statistical pressure.

## Where Alcock corrects this repository

The June 5 note repeated the survey's "faster than H(z), so it matches no cosmological law". It also said the E(z) branch "overshoots". Both readings fixed the amplitude at the local value. With the amplitude free, E(z) fits the four bins (χ² 5.2/3 under c68), and the binned exponent is *below* E(z). That June reading was anchor-dependent in the way Alcock identifies. It is corrected here.

## Where the disagreement remains

**1. Weight, not omission.**
Alcock names z-dependent mass systematics as the open exposure. The question is their size. With the survey's own a0-per-M* response (App. C: 1.10 at low z, 0.79 at high z), a mass drift of **0.13–0.19 dex across the four bins** brings γ to 0 (script section G). For scale:

- The survey states its unmodelled molecular gas at ~0.2 dex, and molecular fractions rise with z.
- Its own reconciliation shift grows by 0.25 dex from the lowest bin to the highest.

A flattening drift is therefore the size of a systematic the survey names, though nothing here shows the drift is present. The 5.3σ is statistical only, as Alcock says. On the systematic side it is not 5σ.

**2. The per-galaxy test he recommends has been run, by baryon route (CFG262, script section D).**
The table gives the implied-a0 change from the lowest to the highest z-third (z 0.52 → 1.20), against three expectations:

- flat: 0;
- E(z): +0.177 dex;
- Alcock's γ = 0.78: +0.125 dex.

| Baryon route | Δ (dex) | vs flat | vs H(z) | vs Alcock |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (i) fitted DC14 masses | +0.570 ± 0.094 | +6.1σ | +4.2σ | +4.6σ |
| (ii) SED M* + H2 gas | −0.115 ± 0.489 | −0.2σ | −0.6σ | −0.5σ |
| (iii) SED M* | +0.036 ± 0.138 | +0.3σ | −1.0σ | −0.6σ |

In the top third, the three routes differ by ×12.7 in level. CFG262 does not impose E(z) in the cube fits, so it is not exactly the test Alcock proposes. It does show that the rise travels with the mass route, which his bin-level fit cannot see.

**3. Smaller points.**
- His E(z) preference flips to ~4σ tension under the c95 reading. The exclusion of a frozen a0 holds under either reading, but only on the survey's mass scale.
- The ΛCDM-emergent reading (Mayer+2023: an apparent ×3 rise to z 2.3) means γ ≈ 0.8 is not specific to any a0 law. Alcock frames γ ≈ 0.8 as a target for simulations, which is fair.

## Bottom line

- Alcock's letter is careful and its numbers reproduce exactly.
- Its headline (frozen a0 out at 5.3σ, E(z) preferred) is **statistical, on the survey's own masses, under one reading of the error bars**. He says all three things.
- This framework's flat law is the "frozen" model he puts under pressure. That pressure stands on the survey's masses. It does not stand on SED masses (CFG262), and a 0.13–0.19 dex z-dependent mass drift would remove it.
- **Priority:**
  - Not claimable via Zenodo.
  - The repository's public June 5–21 GitHub record predates his submission, with a non-diagnostic verdict.
  - That June record also contained the anchor-dependent "faster than H(z)" error that Alcock corrects.
- **Neither side decides FLAT vs E(z).** The deciders remain a deep-MOND rotator at z ~ 2.5 (PAPER7) or self-calibrated discs (CFG385).

κ = ½ is fitted, not derived. Nothing here changes either footing.
