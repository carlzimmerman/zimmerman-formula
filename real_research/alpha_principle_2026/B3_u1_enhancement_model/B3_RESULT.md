# B3 result: how much of the Multiple Point Principle U(1) pass is derived, and how much is chosen?

**Bottom line.** The U(1) pass (1/alpha_Y(M_P) = 56.25 +- 4.5 against Y1's 55.234) classifies as **CONDITIONAL-WEAK**: not derived (several links are choices or unchecked inputs, and the 6 was named as the number needed before the lattice that gives it was applied), and not shown to be tuned (the text does not date the choices, and the one late revision of the numbers moved the headline rows both toward and away from the data). I independently re-derived the base factor 6 (unique under the permutation symmetry of the three U(1) factors, given the hexagonal-lattice premise), the Z2 and Z3 critical couplings (exact duality) and the Delta-gamma_eff assembly, re-measured the Z2 and Z3 plaquette jumps with a small Monte Carlo (5.9% and 4.0% below the printed values), and reproduced all 16 printed table rows to within rounding. The numerical closeness (0.23%) is not informative: 1/alpha_Y moves by 2.4 for each 0.01 in Delta-gamma_eff, which the model fixes only to about +-0.01 to +-0.02, about 45% of the final number is generated by that correction through a steep fitted curve, and 75% of the printed parameter range passes the stated 4.5 uncertainty. The pass does require an enhancement near 6 to 6.7 (3, 4 and 8.04 fail at every printed Delta-gamma_eff), so it is constrained, but by a trade-off between two model inputs, not by a derivation; the expected outcome was UNKNOWN with a prior toward phenomenological inputs, and the finding agrees with that prior only in part (the factor 6 is derivable; the normalisation, interpolation and Delta-gamma_eff weighting are not).

Order-of-events passages (all from the primary texts): the 1993 paper called a factor 6 'phenomenologically desirable' while the work was in progress; the 1996 U(1) paper states that the cubic lattice is at odds with experiment and that roughly 6 is needed before it applies the hexagonal lattice, which it describes as invented to realise the multiple point; the thesis says the model is justified a posteriori by phenomenological success; the 1997 summary says the interaction coupling was 'invented'. The text does not date the 1/2 weight on Z2, the choice of the starred rows, or q = 1. The star markers differ between the older table and the final table for the Wilson (Z2+Z3)/2 row. The 'improved' Delta-gamma step moved the headline rows both ways, measured against the authors' own extrapolated 54.8: Wilson (Z2+Z3)/2 from 50.5 to 53.8 (toward), Wilson Z2/2+Z3 from 55.5 to 59.7 (away), Villain (Z2+Z3)/2 from 53.0 to 51.8 (away). That mix argues against a data-driven revision but does not show the earlier choices were data-blind.

A later paper by one of the original authors (2024-25) says the earlier Abelian prediction 'was not so well predicted' and replaces the lattice U(1) step by a fitted construction. I found no independent re-derivation of the 6-to-8 enhancement or of Delta-gamma_eff, and no independent lattice study that tests the Z6-quotient picture.

## Classification (criteria of the pre-registration)

* Not DERIVED: L3b, L3c, L4, L5e, L6 and L8 are not DERIVED-INDEP.
* CONDITIONAL requires that each choice be fixed by a stated principle before the comparison and that the alternatives the paper itself considers stay inside the window. The principles are stated (tightest packing, Z_Nmax rule, direction counting, linear interpolation), and 12 of 16 printed variants stay inside the wide window. But the text shows the target factor was known before the derivation (L3a) and does not date the other choices, so the declared rule caps the class at CONDITIONAL-WEAK.
* Not shown TUNED: no passage shows a link selected because it improved the match; the ambiguity is unresolved, not resolved in the model's favour.

## Scope caveats

1. The sharp agreement with Y1 (0.23%) carries no weight: the model's printed spread is 51 to 69, and the slope is 2.4 per 0.01 of Delta-gamma_eff; the stated 4.5 is the right size.
2. The window passes because two inputs trade off (enhancement 6 needs Delta-gamma 0.093, enhancement 8.04 needs 0.023); the data fix one combination, not both.
3. The re-derivations test the arithmetic and the base geometry. They do not test the premises (the triple gauge group, a Planck-scale lattice, the Z6 identification, the independent-monopole approximation, the fitted curve at Delta-gamma = 0.06), and the volume factor 1.34 is untested.
4. CK7 compares metastable branches at the self-dual point on one lattice size with one seed per chain; it is a 10%-level check, not a precision measurement.
5. The reconstruction of the improved Delta-gamma values and the S3 argument for choosing 6 are mine; the paper states neither in this form.
6. MPP uses neither kappa = 1/2 nor Z.
