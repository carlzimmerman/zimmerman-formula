## 0. Purpose and what a fail means

Lane B2 could not meet its 3% precision: its line positions were hysteresis-bracket midpoints, only L = 4, 6 for SU(2), SU(3) not equilibrated. B4 replaces the sampling by flat-histogram (multicanonical, built by Wang-Landau iteration then frozen) runs with center-flip proposals and (SU(2)) Metropolis-corrected reflection "overrelaxation", so that the first-order lines are located by the equal-weight criterion on equilibrium double-peak histograms. Outcomes that count as results: SHARPER and PASS, SHARPER and FAIL, NOT SHARPER (precision reached is reported). B4 never gives the measured digits of alpha: even exact lattice numbers leave a systematic of about 1 unit in 1/alpha from the Planck-scale identification (Y1: moving M_Planck by a factor 3 shifts the targets by at most 1.2, 0.5, 1.2 in 1/alpha_(Y,2,3)) and from the inherited continuum-correction conventions. At best the mechanism is tightened to a few percent.

## (a) Observables and the definition of the points extracted

Lattices: 4-dimensional periodic L^4. Actions and observables exactly as B2 (normalisation of hep-ph/9311321 eqs (36)-(37)):
SU(2): S = beta_F sum_p (1 - x_p) + beta_A sum_p (1 - Tr_adj/3), x_p = (1/2) Tr U_p, Tr_adj = 4x^2 - 1. Per-plaquette observables X_F = <x_p>, X_A = <(4x_p^2 - 1)/3>.
SU(3): S = beta_F sum_p (1 - Re Tr U_p/3) + beta_A sum_p (1 - Tr_adj/8), Tr_adj = |Tr U_p|^2 - 1; X_F = <Re Tr U_p/3>, X_A = <(|Tr U_p|^2 - 1)/8>.
Totals A = N_p sum-type: A = sum_p (adjoint observable), F = sum_p (fundamental observable), N_p = 6 L^4. The Boltzmann weight is exp(beta_F F + beta_A A + const).

What the papers call the triple point (read in B1/B2; the lattice papers behind it were not read): the point read graphically from published fundamental-adjoint phase diagrams where distinct first-order phase boundaries meet: (beta_F, beta_A) = (0.54, 2.4) for SU(2), (0.8, 5.4) for SU(3). From B2's maps (disclosed prior knowledge) the structure for SU(2) is: phase I (X_A about 0.4, small plaquette order), phase II (X_A about 0.63, adjoint ordered, X_F small: the fundamental link signs, i.e. the Z2 center, disordered) and phase III (X_A about 0.65-0.75, X_F large). Three first-order lines: I-II (nearly horizontal at beta_A about 2.5, X_A jumps), I-III (steep, falls with beta_F, both X_A and X_F jump), II-III (nearly vertical near beta_F about 0.44-0.5, the Z2 gauge-theory transition seen at beta_A -> infinity at beta_F = 0.4407, only X_F jumps). The triple point is where the three meet; B2's operational "kink" is the junction of I-II and I-III.

PRIMARY DEFINITION (D-TP, same as B2 so that results are comparable): the triple point is the intersection of the two straight-line fits to the infinite-volume I-II line (segment S1, small beta_F) and the I-III line (segment S2, larger beta_F). The line point at each beta_F and each L is the EQUAL-WEIGHT coexistence value beta_A*(L, beta_F): the beta_A (obtained by single-run histogram reweighting of the flat-histogram data of the sampled run, no other run needed) at which the integrated weight of the high-A phase equals that of the low-A phase, the two phases being separated at the minimum of the reweighted P(A) between the peaks (the separation point is re-located at every trial beta_A). The equal-height criterion (the two peak heights equal) is computed as a SECONDARY estimator and reported; the primary is equal-weight because its finite-size corrections are the smaller ones (Borgs-Kotecky, Janke): the shift of beta_A*(L) is proportional to 1/V times ln(ratio of phase prefactors)/(latent heat per plaquette) plus exponentially small terms.

SU(2) grid of line points: beta_F in {0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4} (I-II line) and {0.55, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} (I-III line). PRIMARY segments: S1 = {0.2, 0.3, 0.4}, S2 = {0.6, 0.7, 0.8}. Alternative segments (reported as the fit-range systematic): S1' = {0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4}, S1'' = {0.3, 0.4}; S2' = {0.55, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9}, S2'' = {0.55, 0.6, 0.7}; a quadratic fit through S1' and S2' as a curvature test. The pure-adjoint point beta_F = 0 is also the known-result gate. If the grid cannot be fully run, points are dropped in this order: 0.1, 0.9, 0.55, 0.0 (never the primary segments).
SU(3) grid: beta_F in {0.0, 0.4, 0.8} (S1) and {1.2, 1.6} (S2), inherited from B2 Amendment 7 (which was fixed after B2 had seen its L = 4 map); added only if budget allows: 0.6, 1.0, 2.0. Alternatives: S1' = {0.4, 0.8}, S2' = {1.2, 1.6, 2.0}.
For the pure-adjoint point (beta_F = 0) and any single line, the first-order character is documented by the reweighted double-peak at beta_A* (peak separation in X_A, saddle depth).

SECONDARY DEFINITIONS (cross-checks; reported, never substituted for the primary after seeing results):
 V-method: the direct three-phase equal-weight point near the junction. A flat-histogram run in O = A + F (SU(2)) at base point near (0.48, 2.45) at each L gives the joint (A, F) series; the two-parameter reweighting exp(db_F F + db_A A) is applied; the three phases are assigned by the valleys of the reweighted P(O) (I < II < III in O); (beta_F, beta_A) is solved so that the three integrated weights are equal. Done at L = 4, 6 (and 8 if possible) only if the main program is complete; extrapolated with 1/L^4 as below. If the two definitions disagree by more than 2 combined sigma, the verdict is still taken from D-TP and the discrepancy reported as evidence of curvature/finite-size effects of the kink.
 Z2 line: (optional) the II-III transition at fixed beta_A = 3.0 by a scan in beta_F, to show that the vertical line passes within errors through the D-TP point.

The endpoints of the I-III line (critical endpoint at large beta_F) are not computed (not needed for the triple-point conversion); the U(1) row is untouched (the authors' model; lane B3 audits it).

## (b) Algorithms

Local updates (C, cc -O3): Metropolis multi-hit on each link. Proposals: (i) near-identity random rotation with an acceptance-tuned width (tuned in the thermalisation stage only, frozen before the Wang-Landau and production stages); (ii) CENTER-FLIP: U -> -U (SU(2)), U -> omega^(+/-1) U (SU(3)), a symmetric involutive proposal, as the last hit of every link (B2 found hot starts freeze in Z2/Z3 sectors without it); (iii) SU(2) only: "overrelaxation" as a Metropolis-corrected reflection u -> 2(u.w^)w^ - u about the direction of the fundamental staple sum (an involution, hence a symmetric proposal; accepted with min(1, exp(-dS)), which is 1 for the pure fundamental action and < 1 only through the quadratic adjoint term). Exact (non-corrected) overrelaxation is NOT valid with the quadratic adjoint term, as B2 noted. SU(3): reflection overrelaxation is attempted only if time allows (an Amendment will say so); otherwise SU(3) uses (i)+(ii) and this is a disclosed shortfall against the brief.
Flat-histogram sampling: the sampled weight is exp(-S - W(O)) where O is the total of A (line runs; V-method: A + F) and W(O) is a piecewise-constant table over nb = 400 bins on [O_lo, O_hi]. W is built by Wang-Landau iteration (ln f from 1 halved at each flatness event (min bin >= 0.5 of the mean over the range) until ln f <= 1e-3, or a sweep cap) and then FROZEN; all production chains use the frozen W (exact detailed balance, histogram corrected by W). If production is not flat enough (see gates), up to two refinement rounds W <- W + ln H are allowed (declared; a refined table is again frozen). The range [O_lo, O_hi] is set by two short canonical pilot runs at beta_A^0 -/+ delta (hot start below, cold start above; delta = 0.08 for SU(2), 0.25 for SU(3)): mean -/+ 6 standard deviations of the phase series on the outer sides; beta_A^0 is the B2 line value. Moves that leave the range are rejected once the configuration has entered it (a steep linear ramp pushes initial configurations in). Samples are recorded only inside the range.
Production: n_chain independent chains (different seeds, alternating hot/cold start) of the frozen weights per (L, beta_F), measuring (A, F) every few sweeps; a round trip = excursion from the lowest 15% of the range to the highest 15% and back.
Lattices: SU(2) L = 4, 6 for all line points; L = 8 only for as many line points as the budget allows (a cost estimate: the traversal time grows as L^8; a priori I expect at most one or two L = 8 points); L = 10 is NOT planned. SU(3) L = 4 for all line points, L = 6 for as many as possible (priority beta_F = 0.8, 1.2, 0.0, 1.6, 0.4); L = 8 not planned.

## (c) Finite-size ansatz, errors, validation gates (fixed in advance)

FSS ansatz: beta_A*(L, beta_F) = beta_c(beta_F) + a/L^4 (first-order scaling: shift proportional to 1/V), fitted by weighted least squares on all available L; with only two lattices the fit is exact. Alternative (systematic only, as in B2): beta_c + a/L^2; sigma_ext = max(|x_{L^-4} - x_{L^-2}|, |x(L_max) - x(L_next)|/2). Fit-range systematic = maximum deviation of the triple point over the alternative segments listed in (a), added in quadrature as one sigma.
Statistical error and autocorrelation: delete-one-block jackknife over contiguous blocks of the production chains (8 blocks per chain, pooled over chains); the block length must be at least twice the mean round-trip time of the chain; if it is not, the block number is reduced to a minimum of 4 per chain and the jackknife error is multiplied by sqrt(2 t_rt / block length); a point with fewer than 5 round trips in total is flagged UNDERSAMPLED and its error is inflated by sqrt(20/n_rt) (a point with fewer than 2 is dropped from fits and reported). The integrated autocorrelation time of the phase indicator at beta_A*, by Sokal windowing (c = 6), is reported as a diagnostic.
Triple-point error: Monte-Carlo propagation (20000 draws): each beta_A*(L, beta_F) is drawn from a Gaussian with its jackknife error, the FSS extrapolation, the S1 and S2 line fits and their intersection are redone on every draw; the 16-84 percentile half-width is the statistical+FSS error, and 1/alpha is evaluated on every draw (so the beta_F-beta_A correlation is kept; B2 neglected it). Systematics (fit range, sigma_ext, L^-2 alternative) added in quadrature.

Validation gates, all passed BEFORE production (the real run of each script exits 0 iff all of its internal gates pass; the physics RESULT is reported, not asserted; the MUTATE run exits 1 if the control fires, 3 if broken):
 G-P (algorithm + analysis on an exactly known first-order transition): 2D q = 20 Potts model, exact beta_c = ln(1 + sqrt(20)) = 1.7020...; the same Wang-Landau/multicanonical machinery (C, energy as the sampled variable), equal-weight criterion, jackknife and FSS, lattices L = 12, 16, 24, ansatz beta_c + a/V. Gate: the extrapolated beta_c within 3 sigma of the exact value and sigma < 0.005, with >= 10 round trips at every L. [The ansatz 1/V is the same as the gauge ansatz 1/L^4.] MUTATE: acceptance rule that drops the weight difference W(E') - W(E) (detailed balance of the flat-histogram chain violated): the reweighted double-peak position or flatness leaves the exact value and the gate must fail.
 G-C (gauge code against an independent canonical code): SU(2), L = 4, beta_F = 0.4: <X_A> and <X_F> at beta_A = 2.0 (phase I) and 2.9 (phase II) from the flat-histogram data (reweighted) agree within 4 sigma with plain Metropolis runs of B2's compiled program (compiled read-only from ../B2_lattice_mpp/src/su2.c with -DZFLIP, no edit), including the case where the flat-histogram run crosses the transition. MUTATE: acceptance missing the W-difference (as above): G-C must fail. The same gate is run for SU(3), L = 4, beta_F = 0.8, beta_A = 5.0 and 7.0 (B2's su3.c).
 G-K (known physics value): the pure-adjoint SU(2) transition (beta_F = 0): extrapolated beta_c within [2.40, 2.60] (the literature value about 2.5, as stated in the task; B2's brackets 2.52 +- 0.02). It is a coarse gate on purpose (my memory of the literature, not a digit).
 G-F (flat histogram, ergodicity) per production point: the minimum over bins of the production histogram >= 0.2 of its mean (after at most two refinements) and >= 5 round trips; failing points are flagged as above, not hidden.
 G-B2 (consistency, reported not gated): the L = 4 equal-weight value should lie inside the B2 hysteresis bracket expanded by one grid step; a violation is a flag on either code.
 G-CONV: b4_confrontation reproduces the printed 15.7 / 16.5 (SU(2)) and 17.7 / 18.9 (SU(3), documented rounding tolerance 0.6) from the papers' own triple points. MUTATE: C_adj = 1.

## (d) Precision criterion

"Sharper" for a coupling iff the 1/alpha_i(M_P) uncertainty from my OWN statistics + finite-size extrapolation + stated fit-range systematic (no error bar borrowed from a paper; the inherited exponentiation spread is NOT part of it) is <= 3% of the central value. Otherwise the precision actually reached is reported (and compared with the papers' 14% and 15%, and with B2's 5.5% and 16.7%). The sensitivity (from the conversion) is: SU(2) d(1/alpha_2)/d beta_A about 19, d/d beta_F about 8.5 per unit, so 3% of 49 (1.5) needs about +-0.05 in beta_A and +-0.1 in beta_F; SU(3) about 14 and 6.3 per unit, so 3% of 53 needs about +-0.07 and +-0.2 (these slopes are quoted from my reading of the conversion formulas and are recomputed in the confrontation script).

## (e) Conversion and the exponentiated-versus-not question

Conversion: `per_group()` of ../B2_lattice_mpp/b2_4_confrontation.py, inherited unchanged (reproduced in b4_confrontation.py with the same gate G-CONV); 1/alpha_i(M_P) = 3 x per-group value; Y1 targets from y1_lib read-only as B2 does (55.234, 49.203, 52.971; relative errors 0.23%, 0.021%, 0.12%).
Exponentiated versus not, from the primary text: hep-ph/9311321, section 5 (around its eqs (38)-(39)), introduces the exponentiated corrections as a crude way to estimate the error from omitting second-order perturbative terms, and says the exponentiation changes 1/alpha_crit by about one unit of about 20 (an uncertainty of about 5%); it does not say which version is preferred. hep-ph/9607278 (Tables 8-9) lists several procedures and averages them for the M_Z predictions. The printed headline Planck values (49.5, 56.7) are the exponentiated ones, so the exponentiated version is kept as the CENTRAL value only for comparability with B1/B2 (no preference is claimed), and the exponentiated-minus-not difference is carried as an inherited one-sigma in the verdict. The question therefore stays UNDECIDED by the paper; I will also report both versions separately against Y1 with the lattice-only error, so the reader can see the effect.

## (f) PASS/FAIL against Y1 (as B2, fixed)

z = (prediction - Y1 target)/sqrt(sigma_lat^2 + sigma_inherited^2 + sigma_Y1^2) with sigma_lat the total lattice error (statistics + FSS + fit-range systematic) and sigma_inherited = |exponentiated - not exponentiated|. Verdict: PASS if |z| <= 2 and the TOTAL sigma <= 5% of the value and the lattice sigma <= 3% (informative); PASS-WEAK if |z| <= 2 otherwise; FAIL if |z| > 2; UNDECIDED if the triple point was not identified or the point count/gates failed (the criteria of B1/B2, unchanged). The lattice-only z, and the z for each of the two conversions separately, are also printed. U(1): untouched, reported UNDECIDED (inherited chain; lane B3).

### Amendment 1 (written 2026-09-30 after the code was written and calibrated, BEFORE any production line point and before any gate result had been inspected except the Potts gate; calibration runs: SU(2) L=4 and L=6 at (beta_F, beta_A) = (0.4, 2.46), whose numbers are not used for any claim, and Potts q = 20)
What the calibration showed and what is changed accordingly (all procedure changes, none changes a definition, a criterion, the conversion, or the segments):
1. Wang-Landau flatness is evaluated over the bins visited so far (integer-valued variables such as the Potts bond count have unreachable bins) AND a stage may end only after the walker has touched the lowest and the highest 10% of the range since the last halving (otherwise ln f can be halved while only part of the range has been explored). The Wang-Landau table alone is NOT accurate enough (four independent Wang-Landau tables for the same SU(2) L=4 point gave equal-weight values scattering by 0.007 in beta_A and the frozen-table production histogram was far from flat at the range edges), so the refinement rounds are essential: up to 12 rounds (not 2), damped update W <- W + clip(ln(H/mean H), -3, 3), stop when min/mean >= 0.25 after at least two rounds. This changes the "up to two refinement rounds" of section (b).
2. The number of bins is chosen as range/(0.4 x smaller phase standard deviation) clipped to [60, 300] (about 100-150 in practice) instead of the fixed 400; fewer, wider bins make the refinement converge faster; the weights stay piecewise constant and the reweighting uses the identical function.
3. The pilot separation rule: if the two canonical pilot runs (delta = 0.08 SU(2), 0.25 SU(3) below/above the base value) are not separated by 8 standard deviations, delta is doubled (up to 4 times). The base value is B2's line value and only fixes the base point of the reweighting and the pilot.

Production target: >= 10 round trips (20 traversals) per point (SU(2) L = 4, 6) with batches of 4 chains; points reaching fewer than 5 round trips are flagged and their errors inflated as written in (c); SU(3) L = 6 and SU(2) L = 8 use a lower target (set when run, recorded in the log of each point).
5. The jackknife uses a thinned copy (at most 250000 samples) of the pooled data for speed; the number of round trips and the block-length rule use the full sample counts.
6. The Potts gate G-P: the lattices are L = 10, 12, 14, 16 (not 12, 16, 24: at L = 24 the local-update chain made only 4 round trips in the allowed number of batches and gave a value far from the exact one, an under-sampling the gate would have flagged), the sampled range is mean -/+ 2.5 standard deviations on the two sides (the fully ordered edge of the integer range, N = 2V, is a numerical trap for flat-histogram weights), integer bins. ### Amendment 2 (written 2026-09-30 about 21:15, BEFORE any grid point was run; after calibration runs at beta_F = 0.35, a value that is not in the grid; the numbers quoted below were seen)
Why: the full-range flat-histogram walk is too slow at L >= 6. Calibration: the full-range Wang-Landau + refinement chain for SU(2), L = 6, beta_F = 0.35 had not finished its first Wang-Landau stage after more than 20 minutes of CPU, because one traversal of the whole range takes of the order of 1e5 sweeps at L = 6 (SU(2) L = 4 needs about 4e3), and SU(3) is worse. (An earlier version of the Wang-Landau code applied the modification factor at every link update, i.e. 4V times too strongly per sweep; that was an implementation error, corrected before this amendment, and is the reason the earlier L = 4 Wang-Landau tables were so noisy.) Because the equal-weight beta_A is only logarithmically sensitive to the phase weights (a ln-weight error of 2 at L = 6 moves beta_A by about 0.001, the latent heat times N_p being about 1500), a density-of-states estimate good to a unit or two is enough, and it can be obtained from narrow windows without crossing the whole barrier:
WINDOWED WANG-LANDAU ESTIMATOR (WW): the range [O_lo, O_hi] of Amendment 1 is cut into the global bins; Wang-Landau (ln f from 1 to 1e-3, applied per sweep; flatness 0.5 over visited bins with the two ends touched, as in Amendment 1) is run independently in overlapping windows of 16 bins shifted by 8 bins (hard walls at the window edges, so each window is a short ergodic problem of a few standard deviations), in R replicas that differ in seed and in the start (alternately hot and cold, pushed into the window by the ramp); each replica's windows are stitched into one ln g(O) by matching their values on the interior of the overlaps (the two edge bins of every window are dropped); the equal-weight beta_A* is computed from each stitched ln g with the same valley rule as before; the estimate is the mean over replicas, the statistical error is the replica standard deviation divided by sqrt(R) times the 68% Student factor (R = 6 for L <= 6, factor 1.11; R = 4 for L = 8, factor 1.19). The spread between hot- and cold-start replicas is the test for hysteresis inside windows; it is inside the error. Hidden barriers inside a window (droplet versus slab at the same O) that affect all replicas equally would not be seen by this; that is a limitation, tested only through the L = 4 comparison below.
NEW GATE G-W (before WW values are used in the finite-size fit): at every L = 4 grid point the WW value and the pre-registered full-range flat-histogram production value (equal-weight, jackknife, Amendment 1 rules) agree to within 3.5 combined standard deviations, and the mean difference over the L = 4 points is at most 0.003 in beta_A. If G-W fails, the L = 4 production values are used at L = 4 and the WW values at L >= 6 are reported as exploratory, with the triple point UNDECIDED at the 3% level.
Consequences: WW is the primary line-point estimator at every L (so that the finite-size extrapolation compares like with like); the full-range production at L = 4 is the cross-check (and runs at L = 6 where affordable, non-gating); the production rules of section (c) (round trips, block jackknife) apply to production values only. L = 8 is attempted with WW for as many grid points as the budget allows, priority beta_F = 0.4, 0.7, 0.2, 0.6, 0.8, 0.3. The SU(3) grid uses WW at L = 4 and L = 6. G-C (flat-histogram code against B2's canonical code) is a statement about the sampling code and is unaffected; it is re-run once with the final binaries.
