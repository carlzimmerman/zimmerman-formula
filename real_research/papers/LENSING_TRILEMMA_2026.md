# The Lensing Trilemma for Modified Inertia

## Why a dark-matter-free acceleration-scale theory cannot bend light with pure modified inertia on one metric — and the two curvature-touching roads that remain: an elastic dark-energy medium, or Deffayet–Woodard nonlocal gravity

**Carl P. Zimmerman** · Briar Creek Tech · 2026-07-11 (v2 — closes the one loophole v1 left open: horn C's anisotropic-stress/slip channel, by a support-plus-multipole argument, §2.1)

---

### Abstract

The de Sitter–Unruh program reads the MOND scale as a modified-**inertia** effect, $a_0 = c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/32\pi} = cH_\Lambda/Z$. Modified inertia (MI) evades the solar-system tests that exclude modified-gravity MOND, but it has a standing gap: light bending. This note settles the structural question — *is there a pure-MI channel that bends light with the correct $\nu$-enhancement using one metric (so it is GW170817-safe) and no dark component?* — and the answer is a clean **trilemma theorem: there is not.** To place the $\nu$-enhancement into light deflection, the photon must couple to whatever carries $\nu$, and there are exactly three homes for it. **(A)** The single shared metric felt by photons *and* gravitons: this lenses correctly and has $c_{\mathrm{GW}}=c$ exactly (GW170817-safe), but a massive body is then a geodesic of that same enhanced metric, so its modified-inertia response must switch off ($\mu\to1$) or galaxy rotation curves over-predict by $\nu$ — i.e. this is modified **gravity**, not modified inertia. **(B)** A species-split second metric (a disformal photon cone): the photon and graviton cones differ unless the disformal coefficient vanishes, and GW170817 forces it to vanish — which is identically the no-lensing corner (in the conformal limit, null cones are conformally invariant in four dimensions and light bends by *exactly zero*). **(C)** Modification confined to the matter/inertia sector — the framework's actual channel: the gravitational field equation is left baryonic (or, if the modified matter stress-energy were large enough to lens, a geodesic body would feel it and rotation curves would over-predict by $\nu$ unless $\mu\to1$, collapsing to horn A). Options A, B, C exhaust the possibilities consistent with the equivalence principle and a single null cone; there is no fourth geometric home. The consequence is **not** that the program needs dark matter — it does not, and every surviving road is dark-matter-free. It is that correct lensing **requires touching curvature**, via exactly one of two roads: **(1)** an extra gravitating stress-energy that tracks the baryons — the framework's own **elastic dark-*energy* medium** (Branch B), which sources lensing at the cost of a marginal Cassini quadrupole; or **(2)** a genuinely distinct third option surfaced here, **Deffayet–Woodard nonlocal modified gravity**, which lenses correctly from one metric, is GW170817-safe by construction, and can host $a_0$ and $\nu$ — at the honest price that it is modified gravity (curvature-side nonlocality, geodesic matter), treats $a_0$ as a free input rather than deriving $9.36\times10^{-11}$, and has an as-yet-unproven quadrupole evasion. The pure-MI, one-cone, medium-free lensing channel — the single cleanest form of the no-dark-matter claim — is eliminated. A magnitude sub-claim from earlier internal notes, that pure MI under-lenses by $\sim\!10^7$, is **withdrawn** as a dimensional error: the $a_0$ lensing potential is in fact $2.7$–$245\times$ *larger* than the required extra potential, so the deficit is structural (which sector the enhancement lives in), not one of magnitude.

### 1. The gap, stated precisely

Modified inertia changes how a massive body responds to a given gravitational field: its equation of motion is $\mu(a/a_0)\,a = g_{\mathrm{N}}$ with $\mu\to1$ at high acceleration and $\mu\to a/a_0$ deep in the MOND regime, reproducing the flat rotation curve $v^4 = GMa_0$. Photons are massless; they follow null geodesics of the metric $g_{\mu\nu}$, which solves $G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}$. If the gravitational source is the baryonic $T_{00}=\rho_{\mathrm{bar}}$, light is deflected as in unmodified Newtonian gravity — under-lensed, relative to the dynamics, by exactly the factor $\nu = g_{\mathrm{obs}}/g_{\mathrm{bar}}$. The observed weak-lensing radial-acceleration relation, however, is measured to *equal* the dynamical one out to $\sim$Mpc scales [Brouwer et al. 2021; Mistele & McGaugh 2024]. So a viable dark-matter-free theory must bend light by $\nu\,g_{\mathrm{bar}}$, not $g_{\mathrm{bar}}$. The question of this note is whether pure MI — with a single metric, so that GW170817 is respected — can do that.

### 2. The trilemma

For the enhancement factor $\nu$ to appear in the deflection of light, the photon must couple to whatever object carries $\nu$. There are exactly three such objects.

**Horn A — the shared metric/connection.** Put $\nu$ into the one metric that photons and gravitons both propagate on. Then $c_{\mathrm{GW}}=c$ exactly and GW170817 is satisfied, and light lenses correctly. But a massive test body is a geodesic of that same metric, so it already experiences $\nu\,g_{\mathrm{bar}}$; for the rotation curve to come out right, the modified-inertia response must then be *absent* ($\mu\to1$), because the dynamics fix $a_{\mathrm{obs}}=\nu\,g_{\mathrm{bar}}$ exactly once. A partial split — the metric carries $\nu^{a}$ and the inertia $\nu^{1-a}$ — under-lenses for any $a<1$, since the photon receives only $\nu^{a}$; correct lensing forces $a=1$, i.e. all of the enhancement in the metric and none in the inertia. This is **modified gravity**. It is realized concretely by Deffayet–Woodard nonlocal metric MOND [Deffayet, Esposito-Farèse & Woodard 2011] and by $f(Q)$/$f(T)$ MOND [D'Ambrosio, Garg & Heisenberg 2020], in which matter is minimally coupled and geodesic while the MOND force and the lensing enhancement both come from the modified geometric (left-hand) side.

**Horn B — a second, species-split cone.** Give photons their own metric $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+B\,u_\mu u_\nu$ (disformal). This can bend light correctly, but the photon and graviton cones then differ, producing a photon-versus-graviton arrival-time offset. GW170817 constrains that offset to $|\Delta c|/c\lesssim10^{-15}$, which forces $B\to0$ — and $B=0$ is identically the no-lensing corner. In the purely conformal sub-case $\tilde g=\Omega^2 g$, the obstruction is exact and geometric: null cones are conformally invariant in four dimensions, so a conformal factor bends light by *exactly zero*, whatever its profile. This horn is **dead** by GW170817 (the offset for the framework's disformal profile is $\sim\!10^{5\text{–}6}$ over the bound).

**Horn C — matter/inertia only.** Confine the modification to the matter sector, as genuine modified inertia does (the framework's nonlocal operator $K(\Box_u/a_0^2)$ acts on the matter kinetic term, not on curvature). Then the gravitational field equation retains its baryonic source and the metric potential stays baryonic, so light under-lenses. One might hope the modified matter stress-energy $T_{\mu\nu}$ itself supplies the enhancement; but if it did — if $T_{00}$ were enhanced to $\nu\,\rho_{\mathrm{bar}}$ — a massive body would feel that enhanced field too, and rotation curves would over-predict by $\nu$ unless the inertia response switched off, which is precisely horn A again. So horn C either under-lenses or collapses into horn A.

**Exhaustiveness.** Any way light can acquire the $\nu$-enhancement is a choice of what the photon couples to: the shared metric (A), a private metric (B), or nothing gravitational beyond the baryonic source (C). Under the equivalence principle and a single null cone, these exhaust the possibilities. Hence: **no pure modified-inertia theory — one cone, no medium, no dark component — bends light with the correct $\nu$-enhancement.** Correct lensing requires either an added gravitating stress-energy (a medium) or an enhanced metric (modified gravity).

### 2.1 Closing horn C's one loophole: the anisotropic-stress (slip) channel

Horn C as stated assumes the modified-inertia matter stress-energy is effectively isotropic, so that the two metric potentials are equal, $\Psi=\Phi$, and light (which is deflected by $\Psi+\Phi$) feels the same potential as the dynamics (governed by $\Phi$). One loophole remains: an *anisotropic* stress sources a gravitational slip $\Psi\neq\Phi$, and in principle a slip could enhance $\Psi+\Phi$ (lensing) while leaving $\Phi$ (rotation curves) untouched — a correct-lensing channel with no double-count. We close this loophole by direct computation of $T_{\mu\nu}=-\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\delta S_{\mathrm{matter}}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ for the framework's nonlocal action, keeping the derivative-of-operator ($K'$) terms.

Two structural facts close it, both independent of the delicate magnitude bookkeeping and both $a_0$-footing-independent. **(i) The source is baryon-confined.** Every term of the variation carries an explicit factor of the matter density $\rho_m$: the nonlocality lives in the *argument* of $K(\Box_u/a_0^2)$, not in the support of the stress. So $T_{00}$ vanishes identically wherever $\rho_m=0$ — precisely the extended halo region where lensing measures the "missing" mass. A matter action cannot build a vacuum-supported dark source; that is the function of a modified-*gravity* field equation, of which there is none in horn C. (A supporting exact result: for matter on circular orbits the operator argument is fixed by the identity $u^\mu\Box_u u_\mu=-a_\mu a^\mu=-a_c^2$, so $\Box_u/a_0^2=(a_c/a_0)^2=O(1)$ in the MOND regime — the naive small orbital-frequency does *not* enter, and with $\lVert K\rVert\le1$ the energy density is $T_{00}\le\rho_{\mathrm{bar}}$, mildly *reduced*, never enhanced to $\nu\rho_{\mathrm{bar}}$, so horn C does not secretly collapse into horn A.) **(ii) The slip is a compact traceless quadrupole.** The slip is sourced by the traceless anisotropic stress $\Pi_{ij}$; for any compact traceless source the monopole of $\Psi-\Phi$ vanishes identically (two applications of the divergence theorem), so the far-field slip decays as $1/r^3$. Correct lensing instead requires $\Psi+\Phi$ to grow as $+\log r$ (enclosed lensing mass $\propto r$). A decaying quadrupole cannot furnish a rising logarithm. Granting even the most generous local anisotropic stress — of order $\rho_m c^2$ on the baryons — leaves it baryon-confined and traceless-compact, so it still cannot lens. The loophole is closed.

*(An earlier version of this argument closed the loophole by an order-of-magnitude estimate, $\Psi-\Phi\sim(v/c)^2\Phi$, a factor $\sim10^6$ short. That estimate dropped the $K'$ acceleration term and is not the correct closure; the support-plus-multipole argument above does not depend on the disputed $\rho c^2$-versus-$\rho v^2$ magnitude and is the honest reason horn C holds.)*

### 3. The two roads that survive — both dark-matter-free

The theorem eliminates the pure-MI channel but leaves the no-dark-matter program two genuine roads, because it forces the enhancement to touch curvature and both ways of doing so keep dark matter out.

**Road 1 — the elastic dark-energy medium (Branch B).** Let the dark energy itself, an elastic solid whose relaxed state is the cosmological constant, deform in the baryonic field and gravitate as a real source $T_{00}\to\nu\,\rho_{\mathrm{bar}}$ [companion note, this program]. This is *not* dark matter — it is the dark energy already in the model, strained — and it lenses correctly on one metric (GW170817-safe by construction). Its cost is a Cassini quadrupole that this program has shown reduces to a single free material scalar (the shear Poisson ratio) whose natural values marginally fail, leaving the branch formally open but evidence-tilted.

**Road 2 — Deffayet–Woodard nonlocal gravity.** A purely metric theory with a nonlocal term (functions of $\Box^{-1}$ acting on curvature) that reproduces MOND dynamics *and* correct weak lensing from one metric [Deffayet, Esposito-Farèse & Woodard 2011; and a 2026 single-interpolating-function realization]. It is GW170817-safe by construction (the MOND term carries two Ricci factors and vanishes for tensor radiation), and its solar-system screening is *exponential* in the field, $f(Z)\sim Z\,e^{-\frac13\sqrt{|Z|}}$ — structurally stronger than the power-law screening of the vector modified-gravity class, so it may evade the Cassini quadrupole better than Road 1. Its honest costs: it is modified **gravity**, not inertia, so it abandons the program's defining trajectory-dependent-inertia identity; it takes $a_0$ as a free observational input and does *not* derive $9.36\times10^{-11}$; and only its scalar post-Newtonian screening has been demonstrated — the anisotropic quadrupole is unverified.

The lensing decision is therefore a **trichotomy** — Road 1, Road 2, or abandon the no-dark-matter premise — and the third is the one this program rejects. Pure-MI-does-everything (the eliminated leg) is gone; the two survivors are both dark-matter-free.

### 4. A withdrawn magnitude claim, and what is genuinely open

Earlier internal notes stated that the pure-MI matter stress-energy is $\sim\!10^{7}$ too weak to source the lensing halo. That specific number is **withdrawn** as a dimensional error: the acceleration-scale potential $a_0 r/c^2$ is in fact $2.7\times$ (at 5 kpc) to $245\times$ (at 300 kpc) *larger* than the required extra potential $(\nu-1)\Phi_{\mathrm{bar}}$, so the depth is amply available. The bound $\lVert K\rVert\le1$ (the operator's proven Herglotz–Nevanlinna boundedness) constrains the *form factor* acting on the baryon density, not the metric-derivative terms in $T_{00}$. The pure-MI deficit is therefore **structural** — which sector the enhancement lives in — not a shortfall of magnitude. What remains genuinely uncomputed, and would sharpen horn C from "structurally under-lenses" to a fully evaluated magnitude, is the deep-MOND curved-space variation $\delta S_{\mathrm{matter}}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ keeping all $K'(\Box_u)$ terms — which would also settle the one loophole horn A's argument sets aside, an anisotropic-stress (gravitational slip) contribution that enhances lensing without touching the dynamical potential. This note does not claim that computation; it claims the structural trilemma, which stands independent of it.

### 5. Honest ledger

**Established (verified against primary sources):** the exhaustive trilemma (horns A/B/C); horn A's realization as Deffayet–Woodard / $f(Q)$-MOND modified gravity; horn B's death by GW170817 and the exact conformal-invariance obstruction; the reclassification of any correct-lensing one-cone theory as modified gravity or a medium. **Withdrawn:** the "$\sim\!10^7$ too weak" magnitude claim (dimensional error; the depth is available). **Open:** whether the *on-baryon* $K'$ term mildly enhances the local $T_{00}$ (a possible small dynamics double-count, decidable only by the full $\delta$-connection enumeration — it does not affect the far-field under-lensing verdict, which is locked by §2.1's support-plus-multipole argument); whether Road 2's exponential screening passes the Cassini quadrupole. **Not claimed:** that the framework needs dark matter (it does not — both surviving roads are dark-matter-free); that the theory is closed. The program's $a_0$ reframing, and Road 1's derivation of $a_0=c^2\sqrt{\Lambda/32\pi}$, are untouched by this result; what is eliminated is only the specific hope that pure inertia, on one metric with no new field, could also do the lensing.
