# Which a₀? The Coefficient of the Radial-Acceleration Relation Under Full Nuisances: A Population-Split Answer

**Carl P. Zimmerman** · Briar Creek Tech · 2026-07-02

## Abstract

The MOND acceleration scale is conventionally quoted as a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻², while a Λ-derived reading gives a₀ = c²√(Λ/32π) = 9.36×10⁻¹¹ — a 22% (0.11 dex) fork that decades of radial-acceleration-relation (RAR) statistics have failed to close because the coefficient is degenerate with the stellar mass-to-light ratio Υ. The standing defense of the lower value — that naive fits omit the per-galaxy distance and inclination nuisances known to be load-bearing — is testable, and this note runs the test: a full hierarchical fit of the SPARC sample (Li et al. 2018 methodology) on the Λ-reading's own interpolation ν(y) = √(1+1/y), with per-galaxy Gaussian priors on distance and inclination from the SPARC master table, a lognormal Υ prior, a profiled intrinsic scatter, and validation gates that reproduce both the published McGaugh-ν result (a₀ = 1.20×10⁻¹⁰ recovered at the published configuration) and the fixed-Υ anchor (0.108 dex at Υ = 0.70). The fit was adversarially verified, including one caught and corrected likelihood error (an inclination-transformation Jacobian) whose correction *strengthened* the adverse result. Four findings, reported at equal weight. **(1) The full-sample tension is real.** With nuisances properly marginalized, the full-SPARC optimum on the Λ-reading's own ν is a₀ = 1.24–1.37×10⁻¹⁰, and 9.36×10⁻¹¹ is excluded at >3σ-equivalent on every honest error treatment (naive Δχ² 161–270; galaxy-bootstrap ≈3.3σ; leave-out-most-influential holds; a global stellar-population zero-point cannot rescue it). The missing-nuisance defense is closed: the nuisances are in, and the tension survives them. **(2) But no single a₀ fits SPARC on any interpolation.** Gas-dominated and star-dominated sub-populations prefer coefficients differing by a factor ≈1.8 — on the Λ-reading's ν *and* on McGaugh's — so the "measured a₀" is entangled with population-level baryon systematics by construction, for every party to the dispute. **(3) The only mass-to-light-free measurement lands on the Λ value.** In gas-dominated galaxies Υ is dynamically irrelevant, making their preferred a₀ the cleanest coefficient statement the data can produce; with full nuisances it is 9.8×10⁻¹¹ — statistically centered on c²√(Λ/32π) (Δχ²(9.36×10⁻¹¹) ≤ 1.5 under both likelihood conventions). An earlier low-side preference (7.7×10⁻¹¹) from a simplified fit is retracted here as an artifact of the corrected Jacobian term. **(4) The high-side preferences cohere as one systematic.** The star-dominated SPARC preference and the independent KiDS weak-lensing preference sit a *common* ≈×1.4–1.6 baryon uplift from the Λ value (+0.146 dex in Υ; +0.20 dex in lensing baryons) — one coherent shift, in the two regimes where baryon accounting is least certain, rather than independent per-dataset failures. The honest conclusion is not a verdict but a diagnosis: **on current data the RAR coefficient is not a single measurable number.** The mass-to-light-free evidence centers on the Λ-derived value; the star-dominated bulk demands ≈1.4×10⁻¹⁰ or a coherent baryon uplift; and the deciding measurements are per-population baryon physics — independent bulge mass-to-light ratios and absolute baryon budgets — not more RAR statistics.

## 1. The fork, and why statistics alone never closed it

In the deep-MOND regime g_obs ≈ √(g_bar a₀), so a rescaling of the baryonic mass (Υ → λΥ) and of the coefficient (a₀ → a₀/λ) move the prediction identically: the RAR constrains the *product*, and the quoted a₀ is a convention anchored to an assumed stellar population scale. This is why the same data support a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ at Υ_disk ≈ 0.5 and a₀ = 9.36×10⁻¹¹ at Υ_disk ≈ 0.70 with nearly identical scatter (0.108 dex vs 0.122 dex on the respective ν's). Two things can break the degeneracy: nuisance-level information (distances and inclinations shift radii and velocities differently than Υ shifts masses), and — more decisively — galaxies where Υ does not matter.

## 2. Method and gates

We fit all SPARC rotation-curve points (171 usable galaxies) with the Λ-reading's interpolation, treating per galaxy: distance D (Gaussian prior, catalog value ± its uncertainty; radii scale as D, component velocities as √D), inclination i (Gaussian prior; observed velocities rescale as sin i₀/sin i), and Υ_disk (lognormal, 0.5 ± 0.1 dex; Υ_bul = 1.4 Υ_disk), with a global intrinsic scatter profiled. Validation gates: (i) the published configuration (McGaugh ν, Υ = 0.5, fixed D, i) reproduces a₀ = 1.20×10⁻¹⁰; (ii) the fixed-Υ anchor reproduces the committed 0.108 dex. An independent adversarial verification re-derived the load-bearing numbers with separately written code, and caught one real error — the likelihood's inclination-rescaled errors carried a normalization term without its data-transformation Jacobian, biasing a₀ low. All headline numbers below use the corrected (Li-faithful, data-space) likelihood; the uncorrected variant is reported in the repository as a labeled fork. Because rotation-curve points within a galaxy share systematics, naive Δχ² overstates significance; galaxy-level bootstrap values are quoted alongside throughout.

## 3. The full-sample verdict: the tension is real

With the nuisances marginalized, the full-sample optimum on the Λ-reading's own ν is **a₀ = 1.37×10⁻¹⁰** (1.24×10⁻¹⁰ on the uncorrected fork), with 9.36×10⁻¹¹ disfavored at Δχ² = 270 naive (161 on the fork), **P < 0.002 (≈3.3σ) by galaxy bootstrap**, robust to removing the two most influential galaxies, and *not* rescuable by a global Υ zero-point shift within its plausible band. We state this at full weight: the long-standing defense that the tension was an artifact of missing distance/inclination nuisances is now closed, because the nuisances are included and the tension survives. Three caveats keep it short of a kill, and they are structural rather than statistical: the bulge mass-to-light ratio is locked to the disk's (Υ_bul = 1.4 Υ_disk) while the most influential galaxies are bulge-heavy — an independent-Υ_bul fit is the one unrun knob; the intrinsic-scatter model is a lever; and the population split of §4 means the "full-sample a₀" is an average over sub-populations that genuinely disagree.

## 4. No single a₀ fits SPARC — on any ν

Splitting the sample by dynamical gas fraction, the gas-dominated and star-dominated populations prefer coefficients differing by a factor ≈1.8, and the split persists on McGaugh's interpolation as well as the Λ-reading's. Whatever one's theoretical commitments, this is a model-independent statement about the data: **the RAR coefficient extracted from SPARC is population-dependent at the factor-two level**, and every published single-number a₀ — 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ included — is an average over that disagreement. Any honest use of "the measured a₀" against (or for) a theoretical value must carry this entanglement.

## 5. The mass-to-light-free measurement

Gas-dominated galaxies are the exception to the degeneracy of §1: their baryonic mass is set by HI, not starlight, so their preferred coefficient is insensitive to Υ — the cleanest coefficient measurement the data contain (the same logic that made gas-rich galaxies the classic honest test of the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation; McGaugh 2011). With full nuisances and the corrected likelihood, the gas-dominated cut (N = 37) prefers **a₀ = 9.8×10⁻¹¹, statistically centered on the Λ-derived 9.36×10⁻¹¹** (Δχ² ≤ 1.5 under both conventions; bootstrap P(≤9.36×10⁻¹¹) = 0.39 — i.e., the Λ value is entirely typical). We flag prominently that a preliminary version of this cut, run without the distance/inclination machinery, preferred 7.7×10⁻¹¹ and was briefly recorded as a "low-side" hint; the adversarial verification traced that sign to the Jacobian error, and it is retracted and superseded here. The corrected, convention-robust statement stands: **where the mass-to-light ratio cannot act, SPARC lands on c²√(Λ/32π).**

## 6. The high side coheres as one uplift

The measurements that prefer the high coefficient are precisely those where baryon accounting carries the most freedom, and they miss the Λ value by a *common* factor: the star-dominated SPARC population by a Υ uplift to ≈0.70 (+0.146 dex over the population-synthesis mean), and the independent KiDS-1000 weak-lensing RAR by a +0.20 dex baryon uplift (with the papers' own circumgalactic-gas accounting identified as the one-sided systematic pointing that way). One coherent ×1.4–1.6 uplift across two independent datasets and techniques is a different epistemic object than two unrelated misses — it is either a real, physically-identified baryon undercount (in which case the Λ value survives everywhere), or it is the Λ-reading's coherent failure mode (in which case the high value wins everywhere). Resolved stellar-mass measurements in gas-rich dwarfs (MIGHTEE-HI) currently measure low Υ for *that* population without transferring to SPARC's star-dominated spirals — consistent with either reading of the uplift.

## 7. What actually decides it

More RAR statistics will not: the degeneracy structure guarantees it. The deciding measurements are: (i) **independent bulge mass-to-light ratios** for the influential bulge-heavy galaxies (unlocking Υ_bul is the single unrun knob in the hierarchical fit); (ii) **absolute baryon budgets** — pinning the stellar-population zero-point and the circumgalactic contribution at the 0.05-dex level, which adjudicates the uplift directly; (iii) **larger gas-dominated samples** (the forthcoming ~4000-galaxy BIG-SPARC will multiply the Υ-free cut tenfold); and (iv) any external, non-RAR measurement of a₀-scale physics. Until then the honest summary is the population-split one, and this note commits to it symmetrically: if independent bulge and baryon measurements confirm the population-synthesis values, the full-sample tension of §3 becomes a genuine coefficient exclusion and the Λ reading fails; if they confirm the coherent uplift, the Λ reading survives its sharpest current test with its cleanest evidence already in hand.
