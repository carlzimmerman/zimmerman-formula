(28)–(34)), the strain–budget ratio is exactly $\varepsilon_M = 2\,g_{\mathrm{bar}}/a_{0V}$, so the crossover sits at $g_{\mathrm{bar}} = a_{0V}/2$, i.e. $y_c \equiv g_{\mathrm{bar}}/a_0 = Z/2 = 2.894$. ### 2. Result 1: the response cutoff is necessary, and its location is $y_c = Z/2$

**Necessity.** Model the medium as an isotropic elastic solid whose *only* nonlinearity is volumetric: an energy density $E(\varepsilon) = \tfrac{\mu}{2}J_2 + F(\det(\mathbb{1}+\varepsilon))$, with $J_1 = \mathrm{tr}\,\varepsilon$ the bulk strain and $J_2$ the squared shear. Matching $F$ to the deep-MOND displacement law forces $F$ to be scale-free (a pure power law) — so nothing *inside* the constitutive law can terminate the response at high acceleration. Stronger: the high-acceleration tail required by the Saturn ephemeris bound is *exactly impossible* for any single-valued $F(\det)$ — imposing it makes the stress–strain relation double-valued (a spinodal), i.e. not a constitutive law at all. The termination must therefore come from outside the elasticity: from the entropy budget that defines the medium.

**Location.** Verlinde's budget supplies it, with no free coefficient. From the published version of record (SciPost Phys. **2**, 016 (2017); equation numbers below are SciPost's; the arXiv v2 numbering (4.28)–(4.34) maps onto (28)–(34)):

- eq. (28): the entropy displaced by matter, $S_M(r) = -2\pi M c r/\hbar$ — the $2\pi$ is the Bekenstein/Unruh coefficient;
- eq. (29): the dark-energy (volume-law) entropy, $S_{DE}(r) = (r/L)\,A(r)/4G\hbar$ — the $1/4G\hbar$ is the area law;
- eqs. (32)–(34): the strain–budget identification $\varepsilon_M(r) = V_M(r)/V(r) = (8\pi G/a_{0V})\,\Sigma_M(r)$, with the text after eq. (42) identifying the elastic strain of the medium with this same $\varepsilon_M$;
- eq. (3): the crossover criterion $\Sigma < a_{0V}/8\pi G$.

Combining (28) and (29), the ratio is exactly
$$\varepsilon_M \;=\; \frac{|S_M|}{S_{DE}} \;=\; \frac{2\,G M L}{c^2 r^2} \;=\; \frac{2\,g_{\mathrm{bar}}}{a_{0V}},$$
so the budget saturates ($\varepsilon_M = 1$) at $g_{\mathrm{bar}} = a_{0V}/2$. In the program's units, with $a_0 = a_{0V}/Z$,
$$\boxed{\;y_c \;=\; \frac{g_{\mathrm{bar}}}{a_0}\Big|_{\varepsilon_M=1} \;=\; \frac{Z}{2} \;=\; 2.894\;}$$
(canonical footing; $2.898$ on the total-density footing, the difference pure rounding). The chain was verified against three independent renderings of the paper — the arXiv PDF, the ar5iv LaTeX, and the published SciPost version — all symbol-identical, and re-derived end-to-end in committed symbolic algebra. The one genuine $O(1)$ alternative — taking the strain to be Verlinde's starred volume ratio with the $(d{-}1)/(d{-}2) = 3/2$ factor unabsorbed, which would give $y_c = Z/3 = 1.93$ — is closed *by the paper itself*: eq. (41) absorbs the factor and the surrounding text identifies the strain with $\varepsilon_M$; section 7's independent hydrostatic derivation agrees.

**Consequences.** With the minimal budget-consistent throttle — response unmodified below $y_c$, depleted as $y_c/y$ above (the depletion reading; the alternative "hard cap" reading is computed dead by $4.7$ orders of magnitude at Saturn, and Verlinde's text supports depletion) — the branch passes the Saturn enclosed-mass ephemeris bound with an $18$–$26\times$ margin on the strict bound, Mars and Neptune comfortably, with the baryonic Tully–Fisher zero-point untouched to machine precision (all outermost SPARC points lie below $y_c$) and the deep-regime $0.982$ amplitude preserved exactly.

### 3. Result 2: the medium's shear sector cannot be linearized general relativity

An attractive route to suppressing the medium's Cassini quadrupole would be for its shear sector to *be* linearized GR — shear waves = gravitational waves, Planck-stiff, so the galactic-strain anharmonicity is negligible by hierarchy. This is refuted on the program's own normalizations, with an exact closed form. Linearized GR, read as an elastic medium, pins only the *ratio* $\mu/\rho = c^2$ (the metric perturbation is dimensionless strain; GR supplies no intrinsic modulus), so with the medium's inertial density $\rho_\Lambda$: $\mu_{GW} = \rho_\Lambda c^2 = 5.25\times 10^{-10}$ Pa. But the deep-MOND energy match — using the derived compatibility lemma that *no decaying radial displacement field is shear-free* (for $u\sim r^{-n}$, $J_2/J_1^2 = \tfrac{2}{3}(n{+}1)^2/(2{-}n)^2$; the deep-MOND profile has $J_2 = J_1^2/6$ exactly) — pins the medium's *total* deep stiffness at $K_{\mathrm{eff}} = a_0^2/16\pi G$, capping the shear modulus at least $33$–$134\times$ (convention-dependent) below $\mu_{GW}$:
$$\frac{\rho_\Lambda c^2}{K_{\mathrm{eff}}} \;=\; 6Z^2 \;=\; 201.1 \quad (\mathrm{exact}).$$
The fork is closed in both directions: insisting on GR-stiff shear collapses the deep lensing amplitude from $0.982$ to $0.119$ (excluded by the rotation-curve/lensing data the branch exists to fit); keeping the amplitude forces the medium's shear waves to $v \lesssim 0.17c$ — material sound in a source medium, *not* gravitational waves. (Metric gravitational waves still propagate at $c$ on the one shared metric; GW170817 is untouched. What dies is only the identification, and with it the free suppression of the shear anharmonicity — which therefore remains a bounded, posited quantity, straddling the Cassini requirement; see §5.)
