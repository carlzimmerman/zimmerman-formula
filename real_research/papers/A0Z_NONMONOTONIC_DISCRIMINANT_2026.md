# A Non-Monotonic a₀(z) Signature: An Observational Discriminant Among Evolving-Acceleration-Scale Models in the DESI Era

**Carl P. Zimmerman** · Briar Creek Tech · 2026-07-01

## Abstract

The MOND acceleration scale a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² is numerically close to c√Λ, a coincidence that has long invited a cosmological origin and, with it, the possibility that a₀ *evolves* with redshift. Several distinct couplings have been proposed, and — crucially — they disagree in the **shape** of a₀(z), not merely its amplitude. This note catalogs the published a₀(z) predictions and observes that they are, without exception, **monotonic**: rising (a₀ ∝ cH(z) in emergent-gravity and quantized-inertia models; holographic dark energy; the ΛCDM-simulation-inferred a₀ ∝ (1+z)^{3/4}), constant (standard MOND; a₀ ∝ √Λ with Λ fixed), or monotonically declining (the Limbach–Psaltis–Özel 2008 relation a₀ ∝ √ρ_DE for a constant equation of state w < −1). Evaluating the LPO relation instead on the **DESI DR2 dynamical (w₀–wₐ) dark energy** — where ρ_DE(z) is itself non-monotonic, peaking at the phantom-crossing near z ≈ 0.4 — yields a qualitatively different curve: a **bump** of +3–9% in a₀ at z ≈ 0.4, followed by a decline to ≈0.65–0.78 by z = 3. This non-monotonic shape is absent from every rival prediction we catalog, and a thorough (though not exhaustive) literature search found no prior publication of it. It therefore constitutes a clean, falsifiable **observational discriminant**: at low z it mimics a rising model, at high z a declining one, and only it exhibits the turnover. We are explicit about credit and scope. The relation a₀ ∝ √ρ_DE is Limbach, Psaltis & Özel (2008); the de Sitter–Unruh modified-inertia mechanism is Milgrom (1999), with a concurrent 2026 treatment by Luo; the ρ_DE peak is standard DESI w₀–wₐ cosmology. The contribution here is the **composition** — the DESI-era evaluation and the identification of the resulting non-monotonic signature as a discriminant — together with an honest assessment that the bump lies below current detection floors and is therefore a long-horizon test.

## 1. One relation, several couplings, several shapes

That a₀ is comparable to cH₀ and to c√(Λ/3) is an old observation (Milgrom 2001; Famaey & McGaugh 2012). If it reflects a genuine coupling, a₀ should track its cosmological source through cosmic time, and the *form* of the source fixes the *shape* of a₀(z). Two candidate couplings dominate the literature:

$$a_0(z) \propto cH(z), \qquad a_0(z) \propto \sqrt{\rho_{\rm DE}(z)}.$$

The first ties a₀ to the expansion rate; the second to the dark-energy density. Limbach, Psaltis & Özel (2008) wrote both explicitly (their Eqs. 3–4) and proposed testing them with the redshift evolution of the Tully–Fisher intercept, using M = V⁴/(Gα₀). Because H(z) and ρ_DE(z) have different redshift dependences, the two couplings predict different a₀(z) — and this is exactly the leverage a high-z Tully–Fisher or radial-acceleration measurement provides.

The point of the present note is narrower and, we believe, not previously made: **once dark energy is allowed to be dynamical, the √ρ_DE coupling predicts a shape that no other model produces.**

## 2. The landscape of published a₀(z) predictions — all monotonic

We surveyed the published predictions and measurements of a redshift-dependent MOND scale. They sort cleanly by shape (Table 1), and every one is monotonic or flat.

**Rising** (a₀ larger in the past): emergent gravity and quantized inertia with a₀ ∝ cH(z) (Verlinde 2016; McCulloch 2017), which rise monotonically because H(z) does; holographic dark-energy constructions in which ρ_DE tracks H²(z) (Adolf et al. 2024); and the ΛCDM-hydrodynamical-simulation result that the *fitted* a₀ rises as ≈(1+z)^{3/4} from baryon-fraction evolution (Mayer et al. 2023). The intermediate-z kinematic measurements (MUSE-DARK III, Ciocan et al. 2026; MIGHTEE-HI, Vărăşteanu et al. 2025) also report a *linearly rising* fitted a₀ — a monotonic datum, not a peaked prediction.

**Constant**: standard Milgrom MOND, and constructions in which a₀ ∝ √Λ with Λ a true cosmological constant (Milgrom 2015; Blanchet & Serraille 2025). A recent de Sitter–Unruh modified-inertia treatment (Luo 2026) shares the mechanism used here but yields a *static* a₀ with no dark-energy-density evolution.

**Monotonically declining**: the LPO 2008 √ρ_DE coupling for a constant equation of state w < −1, for which ρ_DE ∝ a^{−3(1+w)} falls into the past.

**Non-monotonic (bump)**: the subject of this note; no other occupant found.

The one genuinely non-monotonic *rival* — the Scale-Invariant Vacuum theory (Maeder & Gueorguiev; Gueorguiev 2024) — is not a match: its turnover is driven by the matter density Ω_m through Weyl scale invariance, not by a dark-energy phantom crossing; it is not tied to DESI, w₀–wₐ, or √ρ_DE; and for standard Ω_m ≈ 0.3 its curve is in practice a monotonic decline with no z ≈ 0.4 peak.

## 3. The non-monotonic signature

Take the LPO relation a₀(z) = a₀(0)·[ρ_DE(z)/ρ_DE(0)]^{1/2} and feed it the DESI DR2 dynamical dark energy in the CPL form w(a) = w₀ + wₐ(1−a), for which

$$\frac{\rho_{\rm DE}(z)}{\rho_{\rm DE}(0)} = (1+z)^{3(1+w_0+w_a)}\exp\!\left(\frac{-3w_a z}{1+z}\right).$$

For the DESI-preferred region (w₀ > −1, wₐ < 0), w(z) crosses −1 near z ≈ 0.4, so ρ_DE(z) **peaks there** — this is standard DESI cosmology. Propagating that peak through the square root gives a₀(z) that **rises to a bump and then falls**:

| DESI DR2 fit | bump in a₀ | z of peak | a₀(z=3)/a₀(0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| DESI+CMB+DESY5 | +6.0% | 0.35 | 0.65 |
| DESI+CMB+Union3 | +8.9% | 0.44 | 0.71 |
| DESI+CMB+Pantheon+ | +3.6% | 0.36 | 0.78 |

At low redshift the curve is indistinguishable from a rising model; at high redshift from a declining one; **only the √ρ_DE-on-dynamical-DE curve turns over**. The turnover location is not free — it is pinned to the phantom-crossing redshift, itself measured by DESI.

## 4. Why the bump is structurally unique

Every rival maps a₀ onto a *monotonic* driver: H(z), a power of (1+z), or a constant-w power-law ρ_DE. A bump requires ρ_DE(z) *itself* to be non-monotonic, and that requires an equation of state that crosses w = −1 with the DESI sign. The two ingredients each have prior art — the √ρ_DE relation (LPO 2008) and the ρ_DE peak (DESI w₀–wₐ cosmology) — but their **composition into an a₀(z) prediction for galaxy dynamics** appears not to have been made. That composition is the entire content of the signature, and it is enough to make it a discriminant: no monotonic-driver model can reproduce a turnover.

## 5. Detectability — an honest floor

The distinctiveness is qualitative; the amplitude is not favorable. The bump translates to a Tully–Fisher/BTFR intercept shift of only ≈0.015–0.037 dex for the robust DESI fits (a₀ ∝ 1/M at fixed V), against systematic floors of ≈0.05 dex (star-formation) to ≈0.20 dex (spatial resolution; cf. the seeing systematic reported in intermediate-z JWST rotation-curve work, 2026). In weak-lensing the bump is ≈2–3% versus a ≈3–5% circumgalactic-gas floor; in direct kinematic a₀(z) it is far below the ≈0.13–0.17 dex per-point scatter of current measurements. The signature is therefore a **long-horizon** discriminant — realistically requiring sub-2% Tully–Fisher-intercept precision at 0.3 < z < 0.6 with star-formation and seeing systematics controlled — not a near-term test. The larger high-z *decline* is more accessible in principle but is the LPO direction, not distinctive, and demands clean deep-MOND kinematics at z ≳ 2 that do not yet exist.

## 6. Scope and credit

This note claims neither a new relation nor a new mechanism. The coupling a₀ ∝ √ρ_DE and its Tully–Fisher test are due to **Limbach, Psaltis & Özel (2008)**; the de Sitter–Unruh modified-inertia reading of MOND is due to **Milgrom (1999, 1994)**, with a concurrent 2026 treatment by **Luo**; the peak of ρ_DE(z) is standard **DESI** dynamical-dark-energy phenomenology; and the high-z decline is already present in LPO for w < −1. The acceleration-scale coefficient used for normalization, a₀ = c²√(Λ/32π) = 9.36×10⁻¹¹ m/s², is a one-parameter modelling choice, not a derivation. The contribution is the **DESI-era composition** and the recognition of the resulting **non-monotonic curve as an observational discriminant** among the a₀(z) hypotheses. Our search for prior publication of this signature was thorough but not exhaustive: forward literature search across the DESI×MOND intersection and the full a₀(z) landscape returned no match, but a complete citation-tree enumeration of LPO 2008 could not be machine-verified, so we state the result as "no duplicate found," not "none exists."
