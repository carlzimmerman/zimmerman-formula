# Dark Matter: A Certainty-Gap Audit of Pedagogy & Outreach

*Compiled 2026-06. LOCAL working document — not for git push.*

## (A) Framing note — what this document IS and IS NOT

This is an **honest science-communication audit**, not a claim that dark matter is wrong.

The relevant distinction is between two very different claims that outreach and
teaching materials routinely **collapse into one**:

1. **The gravitational discrepancy** — that galaxies, clusters, lensing, and the CMB
   reveal far more gravitating mass/energy than visible baryons account for. This is
   **strongly evidenced** and is not what this audit contests.
2. **The new-PARTICLE nature** — that the discrepancy is caused by a new, undiscovered,
   non-baryonic *particle*, and that no alternative (modified gravity / modified inertia)
   is a live contender. This is **genuinely open**: the leading particle candidates
   (WIMPs, axions) remain undetected, and modified-dynamics alternatives exist in the
   frontier literature.

The documentable point is the **certainty gap**: pedagogy and outreach frequently
present claim (2) with the same flat certainty as claim (1). This is a critique of
**overstated certainty in science communication** — a defensible demarcation/updating
argument. It is **NOT** a "dark matter is flat-earth" equivalence, and nothing here
should be read that way. Where pages handle the existence-vs-nature split carefully
(several do), this audit records that too — see the both-ways read in (D).

Classification scheme per item:
- **asserts-as-fact** — states existence and/or particle nature with no hedge.
- **hedged** — explicitly flags the nature/identity as unknown, a model, or a hypothesis.
- **mentions-alternatives** — labels candidates as hypothetical / a "favored model" /
  not-yet-found, acknowledging the inference is provisional.

**Verification rail:** every quote below was pulled via WebFetch from the exact URL
listed and confirmed verbatim (each load-bearing quote re-fetched to guard against
paraphrase/truncation). Only items marked fetched_verified are included; unverifiable
strings were omitted (see (E)).

## (D) The honest both-ways read

The data do **not** show a monolithic "everyone asserts dark-matter particles as
settled fact." They show a more specific, and more defensible, pattern:

1. **The existence/discrepancy claim is asserted as fact almost everywhere, and that
   is appropriate.** Lines like MIT's "strong evidence that more than 80%...", LBL's
   "have clearly established that... one-fifth is dark matter," UChicago's "the evidence
   for dark matter is overwhelming," and Caltech's "hitting us over the head with
   evidence" all target the *gravitational discrepancy*, which is strongly evidenced.
   These are not the overstatement.

2. **The careful frontier-facing pages hedge the NATURE correctly — often on the same
   page.** Harvard/Chandra ("The nature of dark matter is unknown"; "favored model"),
   Caltech ("hypothetical... have not been found"; "do not know what it is"), Berkeley
   Axion Works ("No one knows for certain what the dark matter is made of"), Cambridge
   KICC ("found no viable candidate"), and Oxford ("their nature remains one of the
   biggest unsolved questions") are well-calibrated. Sixteen of 26 items hedge — the
   research-adjacent literature is, on the whole, honest about the open nature.

3. **The certainty gap appears where outreach COLLAPSES existence into nature.** The
   clearest cases: Stanford KIPAC's "85% of the matter in the universe is made up of
   one or more species of dark matter" (presents the new-substance/particle reading as
   settled, with no flag that the inference is a discrepancy and no mention of
   alternatives) and Yale Astronomy's "a detailed case for the existence of cold dark
   matter – sluggish particles" (asserts the *particle* nature — the genuinely contested
   claim — as established). Perimeter's "physicists have discovered that this mysterious,
   unseen material" similarly states discovery of a *material*.

4. **No fetched page in either class presented modified-gravity / modified-inertia as a
   live alternative to the particle hypothesis.** The closest acknowledgments are
   Harvard/Chandra's "favored model" and Caltech's "hypothetical." The *absence* of any
   "alternatives to the particle hypothesis exist" framing in mainstream outreach is
   itself part of the documented certainty gap.

**The defensible thesis:** the *gap between* (a) outreach that asserts the particle
nature as fact and (b) frontier literature that hedges it is real, sourced, and
quotable. That gap — not any "dark matter is false" claim — is exactly what a
demarcation/updating argument can cite. It supports "science communication overstates
certainty about the *nature* of dark matter" **without** the false flat-earth
equivalence. The strongest, most honest framing pairs an asserts-as-fact outreach line
(Stanford KIPAC, Yale Astronomy) directly against a hedged frontier line from a
comparable institution (Cambridge KICC, Berkeley, Oxford) to show the same field saying
both.
