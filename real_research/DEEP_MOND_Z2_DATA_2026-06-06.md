# What the deep-MOND z≥2 data actually says — correcting "untested" to "not decisively tested, leaning framework"

*C. Zimmerman, 2026-06-06. Carl asked, correctly: "I thought we had z≥2 data?" We do — and I'd been imprecise calling
the framework's decline "untested." This pins down exactly what the cleanest deep-MOND-regime data reaching z~2.5
actually shows. Honest result: it **leans the framework's way (declining a₀), but at <1σ — suggestive, not decisive.***

## The precise situation

We have abundant z≥2 kinematics (KMOS³D, de Graaff JADES, Big Wheel, Saldana-Lopez, RC100), but almost all of it is
**high-acceleration** (g ≫ a₀), the wrong regime to measure the deep-MOND scale:

| system | z | g/a₀ | deep-MOND? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Big Wheel | 3.25 | ~2.5 | no (on the local TFR) |
| Genzel disks (6) | 0.9–2.4 | 3–11 | no (Milgrom 2017) |
| de Graaff JADES (6) | 5.5–7.4 | ≳1.5 (DM-dominated) | no |

In that regime `V⁴/(G M_bar)` *overestimates* a₀ — which is why those points read spuriously "rising" (the regime
artifact). They correctly **exclude a strong rise** (Milgrom 2017: the Genzel disks "all but exclude a₀∝(1+z)^{3/2}"),
but they cannot measure the decline.

## The one clean deep-MOND z≥2 dataset — and what it says

There is **exactly one** published a₀(z) fit restricted to genuinely deep-MOND (g<a₀) systems reaching z~2.5:
**Del Popolo & Chan 2024** (arXiv:2405.01841), using the **17** low-acceleration galaxies in the RC100 sample
(Nestor Shachar+2023), z = 0.5–2.5.

| reading | sample | slope (a₀ vs z) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Del Popolo & Chan** (deep-MOND-17, g<a₀) | 17 | Pearson **r = −0.116**; Z-slope **−0.2 ± 0.4** | **declining** (framework direction) — but consistent with 0 |
| **Gueorguiev** (re-analysis, SIV) | 100 (full RC100) | Z-slope **0.01 ± 0.2** | flat; argues the deep-MOND cut is biased (applied to high-z, not low-z) |

**Both readings are statistically consistent with zero.** Del Popolo & Chan's own caveat: *"the error bars in a₀ are
very large… the result here can be used as a reference only."* Gueorguiev: *"the uncertainty in the data is too big for
the clear demonstration of a z-dependence yet."* No second independent deep-MOND z≥1.5 a₀ sample exists.

## Honest verdict (correcting my framing)

- **"Untested" was wrong.** We *have* deep-MOND z≥2 data — the RC100-17 subset.
- **It leans the framework's way:** the deep-MOND-selected a₀ *anti-correlates* with z (declining, r=−0.116), which is
  the framework's predicted direction (and opposite Verlinde).
- **But it is not decisive:** the slope is **−0.2 ± 0.4** — *consistent with flat* at <1σ — and the "declining" lean is
  partly cut-dependent (Gueorguiev gets flat, 0.01±0.2, on the unrestricted sample and contests the cut).
- **It does exclude a strong rise** (Verlinde), consistent with the high-acceleration constraint.

So the accurate one-liner is **"the framework's decline is not *decisively* tested, and the cleanest deep-MOND z≥2 data
weakly lean toward it (declining) while excluding the rising rival"** — *not* "untested." This is slightly more
favorable than my earlier phrasing, and it is the honest read: a <1σ lean in the right direction, consistent with flat,
on the one clean dataset that exists.

## Where this sits in the multi-method picture

It is fully consistent with everything else: at z<1.5 the multi-method data give framework=constant (Δχ²≈0) with
Verlinde excluded; the cleanest deep-MOND point at z~1.5–2.5 (RC100-17, already in the multi-method comparison at
a₀(z=1.5)=1.32±0.66) leans declining but is consistent with flat. **At every redshift the data are consistent with
framework and constant, exclude Verlinde, and — in the one clean deep-MOND z≥2 sample — weakly favor the framework's
decline.** The decline becomes decisive only with a larger deep-MOND z≥2 sample (the lensed-dwarf / unbiased-cut
campaign), where the predicted −26% at z=3 would clear the error bars.
