## A. Does Verlinde clear the interpolation bar? (a controlled interpolation from a mechanism) — NO, and neither do I

**This is the headline result.** The bar he pointed me to is: *derive a controlled
Newtonian → deep-MOND transition from a mechanism, not just match the scale and the deep-MOND limit.*

**What Verlinde actually derives.** His central result (eq. 7.40) is an apparent-DM relation:
M_D²(r) = (cH₀ r²)/(6G) · d/dr[∫ G M_B(r′)/r′² ...]. For a point mass it collapses to
**g_D = √(g_B·a₀/6)** with a₀ = cH₀ — i.e. the deep-MOND √-law and the scale a_M = cH₀/6, **from a
mechanism**: dark energy carries a volume-law (thermal) de Sitter entropy; baryons remove/displace a region
of it; the medium responds *elastically* (a strain field, formally an elastic solid); the elastic memory of
the displaced entropy is the extra "dark" force. The Milgrom scale falls out because the dS entropy density
is set by H₀.

**Where it stops — Verlinde's OWN caveat (sec. 8):** *"these scaling relations are not new laws of gravity
or inertia, but appear as estimates of the strength of the extra dark gravitational force."* He gives a
**transition criterion** (the linear-elastic regime ε_M(r) < 1) but **no smooth interpolating function**
across all r. He has the deep-MOND end + the onset criterion — not the controlled function in between.
Assumptions baked in: spherical symmetry, mass localized near origin, static, sub-Hubble, weak-field.

**Independent literature confirms the gap is real AND distinctive** (not merely incomplete): the effective
Verlinde transition depends not only on y = g_B/a₀ but on the **radial derivative** y′ = dy/dr (the
elastic-strain integral is non-local). Hernandez/Tortora-class tests (Diez-Tejedor, Gonzalez-Morales &

intermediate transition departs by up to ~50% in dwarf-spheroidal interiors, and the **derivative dependence
"represents the stronger deviation from the MOND paradigm."** So Verlinde does NOT reproduce a standard MOND
interpolation — he produces a *different, derivative-dependent, non-local* transition, pinned only at the two
ends.

## Where Verlinde has been FALSIFIED (credit the failures honestly — they are on the gravity/lensing side)

These are *Verlinde's* failures, and they are exactly where modified-GRAVITY is most exposed — the lensing /
cluster mass profile, which a modified-*inertia* theory routes differently:
- **Galaxy clusters (Tamosiunas et al. 2019, JCAP 05(2019)053, arXiv:1901.05505):** Coma + 58 stacked
  clusters (X-ray + weak lensing). EG predictions for cluster mass profiles and baryon fractions disagree
  with observations by a factor of **~2–6 at large radii**; "EG does not reproduce the observed
  characteristics of relaxed galaxy clusters as well as cold DM." The cluster missing-mass problem (the same
  one that has always hurt MOND-class theories at the cluster scale) hits Verlinde too.
- **CLASH cluster lensing:** the NFW dark-matter model fits the sample well; only a few clusters are
  reproduced by VEG.
- **Color-dependent galaxy-galaxy lensing (SDSS DR7):** EG fails to explain the *color dependence* of the
  excess surface density; ΛCDM is favored. (Verlinde's a₀-from-baryons has no handle on a color/formation-
  history dependence.)
- The **first** weak-lensing test (Brouwer et al. 2017, MNRAS 466, 2547) was *consistent* with EG for 33,613
  isolated centrals — but EG there has zero free parameters and ΛCDM (with free M/L) fit equally well, so it
  was non-diagnostic, and the later cluster/color tests went against EG.

**Both-ways correction — Verlinde is NOT uniformly behind MOND; a 2026 result favors him:** Yoon, Han &
Hwang 2026 (arXiv:2601.01715, "Comparison of MOND and Verlinde's emergent gravity in dwarf spheroidals," MNRAS)
find that across **23 dwarf spheroidals**, Verlinde's EG follows the observed radial accelerations more
closely than McGaugh-MOND in **21 of 23**, favoring **EG over MOND at 5.2σ**. So the derivative-dependent /
non-local transition that "departs from MOND by up to ~50% in the interior" is **not** obviously a *defect* —
in dwarf-spheroidal interiors it appears to fit *better* than standard MOND. Do not relay "Verlinde's
interpolation is wrong"; relay "Verlinde's interpolation is *different and derivative-dependent*, and on
current dwarf data that difference currently helps him." The framework, with its posited additive-in-
quadrature ν, sits closer to standard-MOND-ν here and inherits MOND's dwarf-interior behavior — i.e. on this
particular front Verlinde may be ahead of *both* MOND and the framework.
