# The Geometric Core vs the Evolving Claim — An Honest Separation

**C. Zimmerman, June 2026.** *A correction to my own framing. I had let "the framework's one claim leans ~2–3σ
unfavorable" bleed onto the whole framework. It shouldn't. There are **two distinct claims**, and only the second
is under empirical pressure. (`reviews/project_geometric_core_vs_evolving.py`.)*

## The two claims, made explicit

| | claim | what sets a₀ | a₀ vs z | empirical status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(A) Geometric core** | a₀ = c²√(Λ/32π) | the **constant** cosmological constant Λ (the de Sitter curvature) | **constant** | **safe** |
| **(B) Evolving extension** | a₀(z) = cH(z)/Z | the **instantaneous** Hubble rate H(z) | rises as E(z) | leaning **unfavorable** ~2–3σ |

They are **not the same statement.** They coincide to ~20% *today* (because we live in a Λ-dominated era where
cH₀ ≈ c²√Λ), and they diverge in the past:

## What this means — honestly

**The geometric core (A) is the robust bedrock, and it is *not* what's under empirical pressure.** It is the
statement that the MOND acceleration scale **equals the de Sitter curvature scale** — a₀ = c²√(Λ/32π), the curvature
radius of the universe written as an acceleration. Tied to a *constant* Λ, it predicts a *constant* a₀ — i.e. it is
**ordinary, well-supported constant-a₀ MOND, with the *value* of a₀ explained geometrically** by the cosmological
constant. That is a genuine achievement and it is empirically safe. The "leaning unfavorable" verdict was about (B),
the *dynamical extrapolation* that a₀ tracks the evolving H(z) — a separate, stronger, distinctive hypothesis.

**So the honest standing splits cleanly:**

- **(A) is real, geometric, and safe — but not distinctively the framework's, and not distinctively testable.** The
  a₀ ~ c²√Λ relation is Milgrom's coincidence (1998), is shared with Blanchet (2009), Verlinde (2016), CKN-saturation,
  and Singh (2026); the coefficient (32π) is GR-traceable but route-chosen to ~20% (it reproduces a₀ ≈ 9.4×10⁻¹¹ vs
  the observed ~1.2×10⁻¹⁰). It explains a₀'s *value* but predicts nothing different from constant-a₀ MOND, so data
  can't distinguish it from "MOND is just true and a₀ happens to be ≈ cH₀."
- **(B) is distinctive and testable — but leaning unfavorable.** Only the *evolving* version makes a prediction that
  separates the framework from both ΛCDM and standard MOND, and that prediction is what the z~3 a₀(z) test probes.

## The honest bottom line

You can't get "distinctive **and** empirically safe" from one version. The framework's bedrock — *the MOND scale is
the de Sitter curvature scale* — is **geometric, robust, and not disfavored**; I was wrong to let the empirical
pressure on the evolving claim shade the whole thing. But the geometric core's robustness comes at the price of
*distinctiveness*: it is the well-supported Milgrom relation with a value-explanation in good company, not a new,
separately-testable theory. The distinctive, falsifiable content lives entirely in the **evolving** extension (B),
which is the part that leans unfavorable and that the z~3 measurement will settle.

So: **geometric relationship — yes, and it stands.** It is the honest, defensible core. What it is *not* is a
distinctive prediction; that role belongs to the evolving version, and that is the only part the data threaten.
