## 2. What the requirements force

1. **It must change state after z ≈ 2.** It is cold for R1/R2 and hot or expelled for R3. Nothing gravitational does
   this (X22). Single-temperature universal heating is excluded by the cluster terciles (X23_late_heated_cold, X26 A3).
2. **Its "temperature" today is pinned by the clusters** (X26, scenario B: the only allowed region):
   - c_s ≈ 550–770 km/s in KT clusters, which is 1.1–1.5 × σ_KT;
   - almost universal: |r| ≲ 0.6 in c_s² ∝ σ^{2r};
   - weakly hotter where denser: q ≈ −0.1 … +0.9 in ∝ ρ_b^q;
   - steep in time: p ≥ 3, i.e. c_s ∝ a^{2.5} or faster.
   - With both cluster slicings (KT and MGAS500) imposed, two grid points survive: canonical c_* ≈ 1300 km/s,
     q = +0.375; alt c_* ≈ 1800 km/s, q = +0.5 (r = 0).
   - Partial retention near c_s ≈ σ is what makes the cluster excess rise smoothly with KT (R4) rather than step.
3. **The dwarfs are a separate small-scale effect.** No member of the family reproduces R5 (X26 C-dwarfs empty, at every
   point). R5 needs a floor of about 4–5 km/s that scales as g ∝ r^−0.8 (X24, X28). It is not γ_L (X25, wrong sign) and
   not η a₀ (X28).
4. **R6 makes the heat kinetic, not pressure.** A pressure-supported fluid at 600 km/s shocks with the gas in a merger
   (Mach 6–9) and puts lensing mass with the X-ray gas. The hot state must be a velocity dispersion of a medium that can
   pass through itself. The single-stream khronon cannot (X14: caustics generic). This is a requirement on the field's
   kinematics, **not yet tested in any lane.**
5. **Energy.** The heating carries 26–46 keV per baryon in clusters (canonical; 30–49 alt), about 2–4e-6 of ρ_Λ c².
   No action in the record supplies it (X6, X19 step 2, CFG944 Theorem G, CFG741, CFG643).

## 3. One sentence

**The cold sector is a collisionless dust until z ≈ 2. After that it is heated, nearly uniformly and slightly more where
it is denser, to a velocity dispersion of about 600 km/s today. That leaves it bound only in the deepest wells (clusters,
partially, graded by KT) and gone from galaxies. A separate ~4 km/s-scale floor shows up in faint dwarfs.**

## 5. What this replaces

It replaces "the missing piece is a selector" (verdict §6). The data do not want a selector. They want a time change of
state (cold → hot after z ≈ 2), with the cluster terciles fixing the hot temperature. Being hosted plays no role (X24).

## 6. Current answer (after X30)

1. **Added-component routes are closed for clusters.** Hydrostatic or late-heated retention spreads mass outward
   (X30). Gas, feedback and known baryons are short (X21, X31). Clusters need extra mass at 0.3–1 Mpc *and* less than the
   law at 1.4–3 Mpc. That is a change in the **shape** of the gravitating profile: more concentrated than the law's
   phantom, i.e. ZT2B's "selector as a concentration target".
2. **The cosmic requirement is unchanged.** Dust-like clustering to forest scales through z ≈ 2–3, and almost none inside
   isolated galaxies today (X22). Recombination release and early-dark-energy tracking either reduce to ordinary dust-like clumping or stay
   UNDECIDED (X34, X35).
3. **Shear caps any late suppression** at T²(k = 1) ≥ 0.835 (X29, R7).
4. **Dwarfs:** a separate ~4–5 km/s floor scaling as r^−0.8 (X24, X28). It is not in the action (X25).
5. **What survives every frozen test in the record is a dust-like (w ≈ 0), collisionless, concentrated **cold dark energy** in clusters**
   (X32's surviving tracer, ZT2B's concentration target), **together with something that keeps it out of isolated galaxies
   today.** That second part is the unsolved piece. Every candidate for it tested on 10-24 fails: a selector (X19),
   universal heating (X23), the sheet (X26 + X30), vacuum heating (X24b), feedback (X31). This is the precise open problem.
