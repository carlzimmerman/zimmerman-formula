# Limit of the uniform-background susceptibility improvement

The disappearance of KT37's negative k² term at H0=0 is exact for KT38.
It is a statement about that background, not every local tidal field.
This note supplies a sharper limitation without imposing a new physical
rejection criterion or asserting a dynamical instability.

Freeze a smooth background jet (g,H), with x=g.g/a0²>0,
s=ell² H:H/a0²>0 and z=x+s². At fixed g, let K be a Hessian variation.
Writing D=2ell²s xR'(z), direct differentiation of B=DH gives

    delta B = D K
      +(4ell^4 x/a0²)[R'+2s²R''](H:K) H.

For a plane-wave Hessian direction K=nn, |n|=1, the highest-spatial-order
coefficient is therefore

    N(n)=.5 E_HH[nn,nn]
        =2ell²s xR'
         +(4ell^4 x/a0²)[R'+2s²R''](n.H.n)².

If n.H.n=0, then N(n)=2ell²s xR'<0. Such directions occur for nonzero
indefinite vacuum tidal tensors. For example, the local harmonic Newtonian
potential psi=eta a0 z_coordinate+(kappa/2)(x_coordinate²-y_coordinate²)
has, at the origin, g=eta a0 e_z and H=diag(kappa,-kappa,0). Choosing
n=e_z gives the strictly negative coefficient whenever ell>0,kappa!=0.

In the local frozen-coefficient high-wavenumber expansion, the full
source -double_div(delta B) contributes +N(n)|k|² to the potential
transfer after inversion of Delta. Mixed g/H terms and coefficient
gradients have lower spatial order. The negative principal contribution
can thus recur in a tidal background even though it vanishes exactly at
H=0. This is a local short-wave static statement, not a global constant-H
galaxy model or an extrapolation of the uniform-field transfer formula.

The same qualifications as the KT37 susceptibility audit apply: the
perturbation must remain in the linear regime, and no relativistic kinetic
sector or matter dynamics has been supplied. A negative static contribution
does not alone prove a ghost, dynamical instability, or observational
failure. It does mean that the uniform-background improvement must not be
described as eliminating all short-wave density/coarse-graining effects.

This derivation uses only the already pinned KT38 A/B law and its ordinary
first variation. It does not change the tested action, baryons, cut values,
or interpretation of the finite dwarf and Milky Way scans.
