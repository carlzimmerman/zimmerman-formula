### Row 4: what's computed, and what isn't

The two benchmarks' K-shapes are stiff, so the static quadratic mass term H2 used (mu^2 Phi) does not describe them:

| | quadratic mu^-1 at Z = 0 | sqrt(Z0/Q0) | Z = Q0 Psi / Z0 at Psi 1e-6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| PC1 Cosh | 0.1 Mpc (SZ21's bound: at least 1 Mpc) | 30 km/s | 100 |
| PC2 Exp | 70.7 Mpc | 0.09 km/s, upper end | 1e7 |

- Z is far above 1 in any real potential, so these K-shapes are not in the quadratic regime in a galaxy. Reading, not a theorem: the fluid is effectively cold in wells, with a sound speed well below disc velocities, so nothing computed keeps it out of discs. Whether it contracts as a collisionless fluid does (f_Q 0.872) is unknown: it's a barotropic, single-valued field, and no paper or lane has followed it.
- On the quadratic branch with mu^-1 = 1 Mpc, the hydrostatic term is 2.2e-6 Msun/pc^3 at V 200, 67 times the cosmic mean rho_x, and 4.8e6 Msun inside 8 kpc. That is harmless to discs. It also holds only about 67 times the mean, so the question of where the cosmic dust ends up stays open. This sharpens H2's caveat and does not settle it.
- Search of the literature, PROVISIONAL (search summary, not read in full): no AeST N-body paper found, with nothing newer than 2024 surfaced; Durakovic and Skordis, arXiv:2312.00889, build isothermal cluster models and say a quantitative comparison needs more; a companion paper treats each galaxy's chemical potential as free for lack of simulations. These bear on rows 4, 9, 10.
