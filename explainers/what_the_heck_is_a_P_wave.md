# What the heck is a P-wave?

*A plain-language explainer for the framework's newest piece (2026-10-13). Sources: lanes CFG694, CFG695, CFG696, CFG658; a parallel lane, CFG659, is still working out whether P-waves can be seen.*

---

## First: it's not the earthquake kind

Seismologists also say "P-wave" for the pressure wave that runs through rock in an earthquake. **This is a different thing.** In this framework, "P" is the name of a **field**, and a P-wave is a ripple in that field.

## The field P: space that "leans"

Think of empty space as a mattress.

- Put a bowling ball (a galaxy) on it, and the mattress dents. That dent is ordinary gravity.
- In the framework, space also has a little **give** to it. Near matter, empty space **leans** toward the matter, like the springs in the mattress tilting toward the ball. That leaning is the field **P**. The technical word is *polarization*, the same idea as a material lining up when you put it near a charge.

Where gravity is strong, as on Earth or in the Solar System, P barely matters. Where gravity gets very weak, below the acceleration scale a₀ (about 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², far out in a galaxy), space's lean adds **extra pull**. That extra pull is exactly your law: the reason stars at a galaxy's edge orbit faster than Newton says they should.

The newest result (CFG695) is that if space's give **saturates** (it can only lean so far, with the limit set by a₀), you get your law **with no extra knob**, and it fits real galaxy rotation curves (SPARC: 0.1009 / 0.0994 dex).

## So what's a P-wave?

If P is space leaning, a **P-wave is that lean jiggling and travelling outward**, like a ripple running across the mattress.

Here's the key discovery (CFG696):

> When a galaxy forms and space's lean around it is "wrong" (too much, too little or the wrong shape), the lean doesn't stay wrong. **It shakes off the extra as P-waves**, which travel away. What's left behind settles into exactly the shape your law demands.

The analogy: you drop a bowling ball on a trampoline. It bounces and wobbles, and the wobbles run off the edges. The surface ends up in one smooth, settled dip. **P-waves are the wobbles. The settled dip is the galaxy's extra gravity.**

The computer test found:
- Starting from zero, from double, from half, or from the wrong shape, it always settles to the right profile (within about 0.1%).
- The energy lost equals the energy carried off by the waves (within 0.02–0.2%).
- If you put walls around it, so the waves bounce back instead of leaving, **it never settles**. So the waves really are doing the work.

## ⚠️ Important correction (CFG659)

The "shakes it off as waves" picture is true when the lean starts suddenly out of balance, as in the computer test. **Real galaxies form slowly**, over hundreds of millions of years, which is far slower than the P-field's own natural wobble. In that case the lean doesn't ring at all. It **follows the matter smoothly**, the way a slowly lowered ball sinks into a trampoline with no bouncing. So in real galaxies essentially **no P-waves are emitted** (less than 10⁻¹⁹⁵ of the energy), and settling is a smooth, reversible exchange with the matter rather than radiation. P-waves are a real feature of the field, but they leave **no visible signature**: none in gas, stars, pulsars, lensing or light.

## How this connects to "cold energy"

When P settles around a galaxy, it leaves behind a **bound "charge"** of extra gravitating stuff. **That leftover is the cold energy:** it grows from zero to exactly the right amount as the waves carry the excess away. So, in this picture:

- **cold energy** is what space's lean leaves behind once it has settled;
- **P-waves** are how it gets rid of the excess energy while settling;
- the **law** (a₀ = κ c √(Gρ_DE)) is the shape it settles into.

## What P-waves are NOT

- **Not light.** Nothing in the action links P to electricity, magnetism or photons, so P-waves don't glow. (Lane CFG659 is checking whether they could leave any visible mark through gravity alone.)
- **Not the gravitational waves LIGO hears.** Those are ripples in spacetime itself, from colliding black holes. P-waves are ripples in space's "lean", a separate field.
- **Not something you can make on Earth.** Earth's gravity is about a hundred billion times stronger than a₀, so space's lean here is negligible.
