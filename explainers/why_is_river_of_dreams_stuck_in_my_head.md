# Why is "River of Dreams" stuck in my head?

*Earworms, and why Billy Joel's 1993 single is built to be one.*

## It has a name: an earworm

A song that replays in your head without being asked is an **earworm**. Psychologists call it **involuntary musical imagery** (INMI). It is one of the most common things a mind does on its own:

- **Almost everyone gets them.** Surveys find more than 90% of people report an earworm at least once a week.
- **They are short.** Usually it is not the whole song but a 10–20 second loop, most often the chorus or a hook.
- **They are harmless.** For most people they are a mild annoyance. They are not a sign that anything is wrong.

So the question is not "why does my brain do this", it is "why *this* song".

## Why some songs stick and others don't

A 2017 study (Jakubowski, Finkel, Stewart and Müllensiefen) compared about 100 songs people named as earworms against matched songs that were just as popular but did not stick. The sticky ones tended to share three features:

1. **A fairly fast tempo.** Songs you could walk or clap to.
2. **A common, easy melodic shape.** The tune rises and falls in a pattern the ear predicts easily, so it is cheap to replay.
3. **One unusual twist.** A surprising leap, repeated note or rhythmic hiccup that makes the familiar shape memorable rather than generic.

Repetition, a strong chorus and lots of radio play help too.

## "River of Dreams" ticks every box

| earworm feature | in "River of Dreams" |
|---|---|
| fast, steady pulse | an upbeat gospel / doo-wop groove driven by hand claps |
| simple, singable tune | a chorus melody that sits in an easy range and moves in small steps |
| one memorable twist | call-and-response backing vocals and a syncopated, stop-start rhythm |
| heavy repetition | the hook comes back again and again, with stacked vocal layers |
| loop-shaped structure | the song itself feels circular, like a river that keeps flowing |

It was also a top-3 hit in the US in 1993 and still turns up on oldies radio, in stores and in films. If you heard it a lot when you were younger, the melody is stored very deeply.

There is a nice irony too. The song is about a mind wandering at night, half asleep, searching for something it can't name. That is roughly what your brain is doing when an earworm shows up.

## Why it comes back "randomly"

It feels random, but earworms have typical triggers:

- **Recent exposure.** Hearing even a few seconds of the song, sometimes without noticing (a shop, a car, an ad), is the top trigger.
- **Memory cues.** A word, phrase or situation that links to the song. Anything about rivers, dreams, night, sleepwalking or "searching" can do it.
- **An idle mind.** Earworms arrive most often during low-effort tasks: showering, walking, driving, chores, falling asleep. The brain's auditory "rehearsal loop" has spare capacity and fills it.
- **Stress or tiredness.** Both make them more frequent.
- **Unfinished business.** If you only remember a fragment, the brain keeps replaying it, like an unfinished task it wants to close (often linked to the Zeigarnik effect).

## How to get it out

The research on remedies is small, but these have some support:

- **Listen to the whole song once, start to finish.** Giving the loop its ending can close it.
- **Chew gum.** A 2015 study (Beaman and colleagues) found chewing gum reduced earworms, probably because it occupies the same mouth-and-speech circuits used for silently "singing along".
- **Do something moderately hard.** A puzzle, anagrams or a focused task. Too easy leaves room for the loop; too hard and your mind drifts back to it.
- **Swap it.** Deliberately play a different, less sticky song.
- **Don't fight it.** Trying hard to suppress a thought tends to bring it back. Letting it play usually makes it fade sooner.

## The short answer

"River of Dreams" is stuck in your head because it is close to an ideal earworm: fast, simple, repetitive, with one catchy twist, and stored deeply from years of exposure. It returns when something cues it or when your mind is idle. Listen to it all the way through once, or chew some gum, and it will usually let go.

*If an earworm is ever constant, distressing or comes with other symptoms, that is worth mentioning to a doctor. For ordinary earworms it isn't needed.*
