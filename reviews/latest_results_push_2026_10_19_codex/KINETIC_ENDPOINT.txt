RESULT AND EXACT CLAIM
The measured approximately 10 km/s additive circular-speed form is an empirical
hint for a small positive component, not a particle-state or equilibrium
specification. A stationary isotropic kinetic completion must respond to the
TOTAL potential. Under the hypotheses below, a commonly used quadratic-flat
core is inadmissible when the central total acceleration scales as r^alpha,
-1<alpha<1. A positive lowered-Maxwellian distribution instead gives the
required nonanalytic central flattening and admits a finite-support
self-consistent spherical endpoint under an explicit sufficient condition.
This constructs an endpoint CLASS, not an individual dwarf fit, a formation
mechanism, or a universal temperature or normalization.

A. THE ISOTHERMAL TEMPERATURE DICTIONARY
Assume a stationary spherical collisionless component with isotropic velocity
moments, no streaming, mass density rho_x, and constant one-dimensional
specific velocity variance s_c^2. Assume this component obeys the same total
orbital potential as the stellar test tracers, with acceleration
  g_tot(r)=g0(r)+G M_x(<r)/r^2.
The stationary radial Jeans moment is
  s_c^2 d rho_x/dr = -rho_x g_tot.
On any exact isothermal-density segment rho_x=C/r^2 with
M_x(<r)=V_x^2 r/G, this implies
  2 s_c^2 = V_x^2 + r g0(r).                         (A1)
The enclosed-mass equality requires the flat component to extend inward with
no additional integration constant; more generally replace V_x^2 by
G M_x(<r)/r and retain the actual density slope on the left.
Consequently s_c=V_x/sqrt(2) is correct only in the self-dominated limit
r g0 << V_x^2. A common constant s_c and exact r^-2 profile are incompatible
with variable r g0 over a radial interval. The identification quoted in
CFG856 is therefore a self-dominated approximation, not a completed
isothermal equilibrium in the full baryon-plus-retained field. This does not
refute its phenomenological acceleration fit.

B. A QUANTITATIVE PHASE-SPACE NECESSITY
Let Phi(r) be a finite central spherical specific potential, Phi'(r)=g_tot>0.
Suppose the bound kinetic component has ordinary nonnegative isotropic
phase-space density F(epsilon), with
  Psi(r)=E_t-Phi(r)>0,
  epsilon=E_t-[v^2/2+Phi(r)]=Psi-v^2/2,
  F(epsilon)=0 for epsilon<=0.
Here E_t is a fixed specific-energy ceiling defining the selected population;
this is a state input, not a reflecting spatial wall or a derived escape rule.
Let its density be finite and nonzero at the center and suppose the velocity
integral and its first derivative are well defined (the derivative may instead
be positive infinite, which only strengthens the obstruction). Direct velocity
integration gives
  rho(Psi)=4 pi sqrt(2) integral_0^Psi F(eps) sqrt(Psi-eps) d eps,
  rho_Psi=2 pi sqrt(2) integral_0^Psi F(eps)/sqrt(Psi-eps) d eps.
For every 0<eps<Psi,
  1/sqrt(Psi-eps) >= sqrt(Psi-eps)/Psi.
Thus
  rho_Psi >= rho/(2 Psi)>0,                           (B1)
  d ln rho/d Psi >= 1/(2 Psi),
  rho(r)/rho(0) <= sqrt[Psi(r)/Psi(0)].                (B2)
The direction in (B2) follows by integrating from the smaller Psi(r) to
Psi(0), not the reverse. It is a necessary inequality, not sufficient
phase-space consistency or Eddington positivity.

Suppose
  g_tot(r)=g_c r^alpha[1+o(1)], g_c>0, -1<alpha<1,
  0<Psi(0)=Psi_c<infinity.
Then
  Psi_c-Psi(r)=g_c r^(alpha+1)/(alpha+1)[1+o(1)].
Equation (B2) forces
  [rho(0)-rho(r)]/rho(0)
     >= g_c r^(alpha+1)/[2 Psi_c(alpha+1)] [1+o(1)].   (B3)
A quadratic-flat core rho(r)=rho_c-C r^2+o(r^2), C finite, violates this
condition because 2>alpha+1. This includes a Plummer or pseudo-isothermal
quadratic central density core, if maintained to arbitrarily small r under
the stipulated potential and isotropic kinetic description.

The obstruction does not require a finite E_t in its qualitative form. In
ordinary specific energy E, rho(Phi)=4pi sqrt(2) integral_Phi^infinity
f(E)sqrt(E-Phi)dE. A finite interval [E1,E2] strictly above Phi(0) carrying
positive integrated f supplies a strictly positive lower bound on
-d rho/d Phi near the center. Such an interval exists for a nonzero ordinary
finite central density. Therefore rho_c-rho(r) is at least a positive constant
times Phi(r)-Phi(0). Unbounded energy tails cannot turn it into a quadratic
core when alpha<1. The finite-cutoff version above additionally provides the
explicit coefficient in (B3).

Application to one declared baryon model: for Plummer baryons,
  M_b(<r)=M_b r^3/(r^2+a_b^2)^(3/2),
  g_b(r)=(G M_b/a_b^3)r+O(r^3).
If the assumed retained baseline follows the full deep-law scaling at the
center,
  g0(r)=sqrt(a0 g_b(r))[1+o(1)]
        =sqrt(a0 G M_b/a_b^3) r^(1/2)[1+o(1)],
whereas any finite-density extra Newtonian core has g_x=O(r), then
alpha=1/2 and (B3) demands a density drop of at least order r^(3/2).
A core can still have finite central density and zero first radial derivative;
what fails is the stronger quadratic-flat analytic core assumption. This is
not an exclusion of every cored component. A physical baseline that becomes
harmonic g0=O(r) at very small radii avoids this particular obstruction.
The observation range need not resolve the asymptotic central region, so no
observational rejection follows from the mathematical asymptotic alone.

C. CONSTRUCTIVE POSITIVE FINITE ENDPOINT FAMILY
Rather than guessing a density core, choose the nonnegative energy DF
  F(epsilon)=A [exp(epsilon/s^2)-1] for epsilon>0, else 0,
  A>0, s>0.
This is an ordinary collisionless stationary DF in any time-independent
spherical total potential because it is a function of the conserved specific
orbital energy. Isotropy and zero streaming are selected here. Formation,
angular-momentum transport, collisional detailed balance and stability do not
follow from this stationarity statement.
Set W=Psi/s^2 and
  B(W)=exp(W) erf(sqrt(W))
       -(2 sqrt(W)/sqrt(pi))(1+2W/3).
Direct integration over 0<=v<=sqrt(2Psi) gives
  rho_x(W)=A(2pi s^2)^(3/2) B(W).                    (C1)
Its derivatives and edge expansion are
  B'(W)=exp(W)erf(sqrt(W))-2sqrt(W)/sqrt(pi)>0,
  B''(W)=exp(W)erf(sqrt(W))>0,
  B(W)=8 W^(5/2)/(15sqrt(pi))+O(W^(7/2)).             (C2)
Positivity can be seen directly from the velocity integrand; B'>0 also
follows by integrating its positive derivative B'' from B'(0)=0.

For a prescribed attractive baseline g0(r)>=0 and ordinary Newtonian extra
self-gravity, solve the exact integral/ODE system
  M_x'(r)=4pi r^2 rho_x[W(r)],
  s^2 W'(r)=-g0(r)-G M_x(r)/r^2,
  M_x(0)=0, W(0)=W_c>0.                              (C3)
These equations define the stationary self-consistent endpoint; no numerical
solution is claimed here. The origin is regular in its integral formulation
because M_x=O(r^3) and g0 is locally integrable. Assume continuous g0 for r>0,
g0=O(r^alpha), alpha>-1 near zero. Standard local existence/uniqueness applies
to the regular integral solution, with the origin obtained by its Volterra
form. On the W>0 branch W decreases and rho_x<=rho_x(W_c), so no finite-radius
mass blowup occurs. Ordinary continuation therefore reaches any finite radius
unless W first reaches zero. Stop at that first root and set rho_x=0 outside;
all occupied orbits have E<E_t and thus never cross the root with finite
velocity. No reflecting wall is introduced.

An explicit sufficient condition for finite support is the existence of
finite R_b such that
  integral_0^R_b g0(r)dr >= s^2 W_c.                  (C4)
Integrating (C3), the extra self-gravity is nonnegative, so W must reach zero
at or before R_b (strictly before if the inequality is saturated and A>0).
The total extra mass is then finite and obeys
  M_x(R_t)<= (4pi/3) rho_x(W_c) R_b^3.
This sufficient condition constructs a nonempty finite endpoint class for
any baseline with positive finite central-to-outer potential depth by choosing
0<s^2W_c below that depth. It does not prove the arbitrary desired stock or
aperture mass can be realized, or select E_t from a production history.

For g0=g_c r^alpha[1+o(1)] with -1<alpha<1, integration of (C3) gives
  W(r)=W_c-g_c r^(alpha+1)/[s^2(alpha+1)]
            +o(r^(alpha+1)),
  rho_x(r)=rho_c-rho_W(W_c) g_c r^(alpha+1)
                   /[s^2(alpha+1)]+o(r^(alpha+1)).    (C5)
Extra self-gravity first enters the potential at order r^2. It is subleading
at this displayed order; unspecified subleading baseline terms must not be
claimed smaller than the r^2 term. In the deep-law Plummer case, (C5) has the
required r^(3/2) flattening. Near an outer root with g_tot(R_t)>0, (C2) gives
rho_x proportional to (R_t-r)^(5/2), a smooth finite-mass taper.
This family is not globally isothermal: s is its DF velocity scale and
s_c(r) is not identically s near the cutoff. Treating s as the observed
V_x/sqrt(2) without solving (C3) and the stellar forward model would again
misidentify the physical state.
