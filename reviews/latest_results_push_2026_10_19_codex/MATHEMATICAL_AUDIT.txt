DECISIVE INDEPENDENT DERIVATIONS
1. K1. Pair x and -x for an even coefficient b(|x|). The real (cos(tx)-1)/x
part cancels exactly, leaving -2i integral_0^eta b(x)sin(tx)/x dx. For b=1,
this is -2i Si(t eta). For b=1/log(1/x), its total variation on [0,eta] is
b(eta), and integration by parts against Si(tx) gives a bound
2 C_Si b(eta) for the sine integral, uniformly in t. The remainder contributes
at most 2 integral |r|/|x|. Neither |1/log x|/|x| integrability nor separate
absolute-value estimates on the two sides are being claimed. If A1 differs
between signs, the real nonoscillating term no longer cancels and a log-log
obstruction can reappear; the shared-sign coefficient assumption is essential.

2. K2. Multiply (A-L v ell)U=L b v+r by A^-1. Then
U=p+L V(b+ell U), so ell U=(ell p+L a b)/(1-L a), and
U=p+V L(b+ell p)/(1-aL), with denominator 1-aL, not 1+aL.
As |L|->infinity and a->a0!=0,
  L/(1-aL)=-1/a-1/(a^2 L)+O(|L|^-2).
Because L=Lambda+O(1), 1/L=1/Lambda+O(Lambda^-2); the bounded constants may
differ by the sign of x without changing P or Q. Inverse continuity preserves
the Holder errors in p,V,a. This gives the displayed P,Q and a valid weighted
integrable remainder. The two boundary sides may have different P,Q; their
difference still has the SAME two-sided-in-x expansion required by K1.
An arbitrary fixed bounded linear observation and smooth frequency factor
preserve that property. Invertibility alone does not imply a0!=0: in C2 take
v=e1, ell(y)=y1, and A^-1 swapping e1,e2. Then ell(v)=1 but ell(A^-1 v)=0.

3. K3. H_g>=nu_c I because g>0. For real z<nu_c, solving the rank-one equation
gives m_g=m0/(1+g m0). Since m0 tends to +infinity as z increases to nu_c,
monotone convergence yields exactly integral dmu_g/(lambda-nu_c)=1/g.
Thus mu_g({nu_c})=0 and
  integral lambda/(lambda-nu_c) dmu_g=1+nu_c/g.
Using |exp(-i theta)-1|<=2 proves the claimed uniform work bound. This proof
requires no classification of continuous or singular spectrum. The positive
sign of g and nu_c>0 matter for this proof; the report does not claim a bound
uniform as g->0. Direct expansion of the g=0 cosine integral gives the bare
-nu_c log(t), with all other contributions bounded. There is no missing
transport factor: v=H0[1/(nu_c+x)] exactly and the bracket is smooth.

For the Holder extension, on the open band write l(x)=log((1-x)/x) and
m_f^+=f(x)(l+i pi)+R_f(x), m0^+=l+i pi. Taking the imaginary part of
m_f^+/(1+g m0^+) gives exactly [f-gR_f]/[(1+gl)^2+(g pi)^2].
The difference quotient has bound
  |R_f(x)| <= [f]_alpha [x^alpha+(1-x)^alpha]/alpha
             <=2[f]_alpha/alpha.
The resulting density is O(log^-2(1/x)) at x=0, hence has integrable absolute
value after division by x. On compact subintervals of the band the scalar
m_g boundary values are smooth and uniformly bounded, so Stieltjes inversion
has no singular component there. The explicit endpoint limits of m_g are
finite; there are no endpoint atoms. Any remaining singular measure on the
closed band would have to be supported on its two endpoints and hence would
be atomic, so none is missed. Cross measures are absolutely continuous with
respect to the v spectral measure by spectral-projection Cauchy-Schwarz.
Outside the band the scalar logarithm is real; the sole upper-gap zero of
1+g m0 is simple and separated from nu_c (indeed
lambda_*=nu_c+1/(1-exp(-1/g))). No lower-gap eigenvalue occurs because H_g>=nu_c.
This authenticates the boundary-density-to-full-work step for this model;
it is not an assumption that a generic resolvent jump exhausts a generic halo.

4. D1. The radial velocity substitution v^2=2(Psi-epsilon) gives the exact
4 pi sqrt(2) coefficient; differentiation gives 2 pi sqrt(2). Positivity and
(Psi-epsilon)^-1/2 >= (Psi-epsilon)^1/2/Psi give B1. Integrating from Psi(r)
UP to Psi_c gives log rho_c-log rho(r)>=.5 log(Psi_c/Psi(r)), proving the
stated direction of B2. The density ratio can also be checked directly by
comparing the two positive square-root kernels, so allowing an infinite
positive derivative does not create a sign or integration loophole. With
DeltaPsi~g_c r^(alpha+1)/(alpha+1),
1-sqrt(1-DeltaPsi/Psi_c)=DeltaPsi/(2Psi_c)+O(DeltaPsi^2); this is B3.
Since r^2=o(r^(alpha+1)) for alpha<1, every finite-C quadratic-flat core
contradicts the lower bound. This is not an exclusion of all finite-density
cores or of zero first derivative when alpha>0. At alpha=1 the argument gives
an order-r^2 constraint rather than an exponent contradiction. For alpha<=-1
the asserted finite central-potential premise is not supplied by the power law.

Without a finite energy ceiling, finite positive rho_c of an ordinary f(E)
ensures some finite interval E1>E_c=Phi(0) with positive finite integral of f.
Its square-root kernel gives a strictly positive lower density-change rate
for small positive changes of Phi. This verifies the qualitative no-ceiling

5. D2. With rho(W)=A(2pi s^2)^(3/2)B(W), the displayed B',B'' and positive
W^(5/2) edge coefficient check by direct differentiation and power expansion.
The apparent r=0 singularity of the ODE is removed by its exact Volterra form:
  W(r)=W_c-s^-2 integral_0^r g0(u)du
       -(4pi G/s^2) integral_0^r [u-u^2/r]rho(W(u))du.
The kernel is bounded by u and rho is locally Lipschitz on any finite W
interval; the standard contraction estimate is O(r^2). This validates the
local existence/uniqueness invoked in the text. While W>0, monotonicity gives
0<rho(W)<=rho(W_c) and M(r)<=4pi rho(W_c)r^3/3, so finite-radius continuation
has no mass or coefficient blowup.
If no root occurred by R_b, integrating C3 would give
  s^2 W(R_b)=s^2 W_c-integral_0^R_b g0
              -G integral_0^R_b M(r)/r^2 dr < 0,
because A>0,W_c>0 imply positive enclosed mass on every small positive-radius
interval. This contradicts W>0. At equality in C4 the strict self-gravity
term puts the root strictly before R_b. Continue M=M(R_t) outside the root;
the baseline and extra gravity remain attractive, W<=0 there, and the same
energy DF has zero density. At R_t, Phi=E_t; E<E_t orbits cannot reach or cross
that radius. No reflecting-wall boundary rule has entered.
The local central expansion uses M=(4pi/3)rho_c r^3+o(r^3), so self-gravity
changes the potential at order r^2, subleading to r^(alpha+1) for alpha<1.
The author's caution about unspecified subleading baseline terms is correct.
At the outer root the positive self-gravity already implies g_tot(R_t)>0,
under G>0, and W has a simple zero; the W^(5/2) edge gives the stated taper.

6. V1/V2. Spherical Jeans multiplied by r^3 and integrated gives
integral (3-2beta)p r^2 dr=integral nu g r^3 dr after the endpoint term
vanishes. Angular averaging the spherical second-moment tensor contributes
1/3, even for anisotropic velocities, yielding 3S=<rg>_nu. The comparison
therefore uses the same observed tracer density and weighting; it does not
describe arbitrary physical re-equilibration that changes that density.
Constant independent additive LOS variance has exactly the same integrated
second moment for b^2=V_x^2/3. Matching this one moment is not matching the
full velocity likelihood, or proof that such contamination exists.

The isotropic Plummer projection checks independently as follows. In the
dimensionless projected integral set
  u=sqrt(x^2-y^2)/sqrt(1+x^2).
It reduces to the stated positive compact integral. Polynomial division gives
  integral_0^1 (u^2-u^4)/(y^2+u^2)du
   =2/3+y^2-y(1+y^2)atan(1/y),
recovering F exactly. At q^2=0 and 1 this gives 1 and 1/5, respectively;
y=1 gives 5-3pi/2. The global weight transforms by
d(q^2)=2y/(1+y^2)^2 dy. Interchanging these positive analytic integrals gives
(3/2) integral_0^1 [-2 u^2 log u]du=1/3. No numerical quadrature was used.
Finally, for any nonzero ordinary isotropic energy DF, an occupied finite
energy interval [E1,E2] contributes at least
4pi sqrt(2) sqrt(E1-Phi) integral_E1^E2 f(E)dE to rho. This diverges as
Phi->-infinity, ruling out the finite-density isotropic Plummer core in the
unsoftened logarithmic potential. The qualification is substantive and correct.
