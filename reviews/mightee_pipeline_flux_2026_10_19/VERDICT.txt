MIGHTEE PIPELINE FLUX INVESTIGATION -- 2026-10-19 UTC

Outcome
Final image-domain continuum subtraction measurably reduces the line-window flux in these seven galaxies, but accounts for only a minority of their ALFALFA shortfall. Direct earlier-stage tests do not support a large global beam-area or homogenization attenuation as the main cause. The remaining discrepancy cannot be assigned uniquely from the released products. No correction to catalogue or science fluxes has been applied.

Measured stage comparison
Seven previously reliable ALFALFA-code1 matches, including four original science-sample galaxies, were retained without a fresh cube-detection threshold. Downloaded879,749,760 reserved bytes (including retries) of clean_conv cutouts plus small dirty-cube sightlines, safely below the1.5GB cap; raw HTTP range logs record every successful response's SHA256, Content-Range, URL, UTC and ETag. The pre/post celestial and frequency grids and full beam axes were verified identical. Source centres, apertures, line windows, neighbour exclusions and broad robust residual baseline rules were held common.
'Before' means the released clean_conv image, which ALREADY includes visibility-domain continuum subtraction, flagging, imaging/deconvolution, beam homogenization and mosaicking. It is not a raw observation. 'After' means clean_conv_contsub, immediately following final image-domain subtraction.

The sum of before-minus-after line flux is14.59% of the sum of ALFALFA-minus-after deficits. Thus most of the summed benchmark discrepancy precedes this final subtraction. This is a measured reduction in line-window signal after common broad baseline fits; not every removed voxel is certified astrophysical HI. Per-galaxy deficit fractions can exceed100% when the initial shortfall is small or calibration/noise differs (one object does). Individual formal errors were not propagated and these seven selected overlaps do not calibrate a universal correction.
Constant-baseline pre/after median is1.1771 rather than1.2051 for linear fits. Widening the common window by250km/s each side gives1.2101 (constant) or1.2858 (linear); final subtraction remains a contributing stage, not a complete repair. Raw no-additional-baseline variants are stored, but already-reduced pre cubes can retain broad residual backgrounds.

Where final subtraction acts
Exploratory spatial annuli show94.65% of the summed removed component lies at radii above0.5 restoring-beam FWHM (roughly37-38arcsec), within the1.5-FWHM extraction aperture. The bright central half-beam annulus is almost unchanged in the aggregate. This is consistent with pixelwise sigma-clipping masks protecting bright cores while low-surface-brightness emission is treated as background. Exact historical masks and image-subtraction source code have not been recovered, so this is a mechanism-consistent localization rather than an exact rerun.
Median32-channel noise-block variance/(32*single-channel variance) changes from1.224 before to0.338 after, confirming the expected long-range spectral noise suppression. These small-sample block statistics are descriptive, not independent flux uncertainties.
The final subtraction also narrows some source maps. For example, a simple Gaussian fit to MGTH_J100140.0+031207 changes from77.9x68.6arcsec before to50.9x46.1arcsec after, versus a74.9arcsec header beam. Galaxy-map fits are noise-, background- and morphology-sensitive, so these are exploratory effective profile shapes, not calibrated PSF measurements.

Continuum path documented in primary sources
Heywood et al2024 sections3.2-3.6 separate two continuum stages. The visibility-domain stage subtracts a smooth, frequency-interpolated sky model after self-calibration, followed by additional flagging. The image-domain stage uses iterated2.3sigma masking, morphological erosion/dilation and interpolation, then31-channel median and31-channel first-order Savitzky-Golay filters for L2. At our frequencies this spans about175-185km/s, comparable to HI widths, so the protection mask matters. The public unsubtracted cubes allow the latter stage to be tested directly; the former cannot be undone from these images.

Beam tests
The exact Gaussian area conversion used in our extraction agrees with the catalogue paper's equation4: pixel_area divided by pi*bmaj*bmin/(4ln2). The40arcsec uv taper does not specify the final restoring beam, which is about75arcsec here. Beam-table values and actual profile maps support this scale: before-subtraction geometric-mean fitted source FWHM/header FWHM has median1.005, range0.977-1.070 over seven galaxies. Since galaxies may be extended and dirty PSFs non-Gaussian, this is a consistency check, not a complete beam calibration.
A separate earlier-stage test sampled all native line channels and every sixth off-line channel in identical central3x3-pixel sightlines of dirty, clean_conv and clean_conv_contsub cubes. All dirty image headers declare Jy/beam and their spatial/frequency grids match. Clean_conv/dirty central integrated brightness has median0.96183; constant-baseline sensitivity gives0.95957. Six of seven are slightly dimmer, but the range and different PSFs do not reveal a factor-of-two central attenuation. These are sightline brightness ratios, not total source fluxes; extended emission, PSF shoulders and deconvolution limit the inference. They weaken a large uniform homogenization-scale error, without excluding subtle residual normalization errors.

Public code lead, tested and constrained
A public mightee-hi convolution script constructs a target restoring beam but then passes fitted_beam_image to the model convolution while labelling the output with the target beam. The related oxkat utility uses target_beam_image in that call. Both inspected scripts also lack an explicit Gaussian beam-area conversion after the current PyPHER CLI's unit-sum-kernel residual convolution.
Controlled Gaussian calculations demonstrate that an unconverted50->75arcsec smoothing can return0.44444 of the flux when divided by the target area. Correctly normalized controls recover1.0, for five clean/residual mixture fractions. This is a conditional mathematical failure mode, not a measurement of actual production input beams and not a verified cause of this data deficit.
The public mightee-hi branch has a hard-coded0.7 factor associated with robust0.5-style processing, while our cubes are robust1.0. File history dates to2023; the public repository head is January2025, and the release is September2024. No exact production commit, per-pointing PSFs, kernels or model/residual split is identified in the released FITS metadata. The direct dirty-stage brightness result is near unity. These limitations prevent attributing the release discrepancy to the code lead. Full detail and exact source-line references: BEAM_CODE_AUDIT.txt.

What remains unresolved
Most of the ALFALFA comparison shortfall predates the final image-domain subtraction. Central dirty-to-clean_conv sightlines show no large additional brightness loss, but their different PSFs prevent proving that the entire integrated-flux shortfall already exists in dirty cubes. Candidate remaining explanations include loss before image formation, residual-component normalization, interferometric spatial response and uncatalogued diffuse emission, calibration and differences in single-dish blending/flux scale. The central test is particularly limited for low-surface-brightness extended residuals. The present evidence does not select among these. FASHI spectra are still needed for a channel-level three-instrument benchmark; catalogue flux disagreement means ALFALFA cannot simply be assumed to define the true scale for every object.
The next decisive pipeline evidence would be the actual production settings and per-pointing dirty PSFs, CLEAN model/residual images and homogenization kernels for these galaxies, or a controlled line injection before visibility subtraction and imaging. Post-image injections cannot validate those earlier stages. No authors have been contacted, no raw-visibility rerun attempted, and no blanket multiplicative correction inferred.
