# What the cold-particle survival calculation bounds

The state is a residual-U(1) Majorana gaugino of mass 1 MeV at e=.003,
with mu determined by the computed leading pole mass. It is not identified
with an observed photon/photino sector. The action remains rigid SU(5),
expanded about the specified commuting radiative vacuum. The FD distribution
is unpolarized and carries no stipulated coherent rolling phase charge.

The bounded leading low-energy loss set is:

- the source-checked Goldstino--U(1)-photon two-body Ward channel;
- all one-loop heavy box/seagull two-vector transitions covered by the
  independent operator-norm proof and its explicit scope addendum;
- the leading phase-radiation Ward/current completion, including anomaly
  photon splitting and interference;
- light virtual-Goldstino exchange into a lighter gaugino and its gauge
  boson plus the U(1) photon;
- leading nonrelativistic phase-pair annihilation and a conservative crossed
  heavy-box annihilation bound at the explicit halo density.

The transpose symmetry forbids the dangerous scalar/Goldstino two-body
transitions and unbroken-gaugino one-vector dipoles. The three-gaugino
threshold is closed at the computed mass ratios. This does not set every
allowed higher-dimensional coefficient to zero.

For the crossed heavy-box bound use the same generic normalized-external-
gaugino vertices with both external fermions chosen as the U(1) photino.
Every internal cycle remains charged and gapped. Physical external energies
are at most m at threshold; routed sums are below a few m, with m/M=1.5e-9.
They remain far inside the proof's factor-two analytic continuation margin.
The uniform amplitude majorant is 4 B_loop m^3/M^3. With Phi2=1/(8pi) and
the nonrelativistic flux, sigma v is at most the spin/color sum of |M|^2
divided by 32pi m^2. The proof's deliberately enlarged inclusive
c7=512 B_loop yields c7^2 m^4/(16pi M^6) with its 32768-label cap. We use
the following 256-times looser cushion:

    (sigma v)_hard <= 16 c7^2 m^4/(pi M^6).

This exceeds the direct normalized polarization/counting bound, including
identical-initial exchange and ignoring final-identical suppression. Its
loss rate per particle is n sigma v with n=rho/m. The estimate is a leading
vacuum nonrelativistic bound, not a finite-temperature transport calculation.

The resulting survival inequality is for this *specified leading loss set*.
It is not an all-orders inclusive decay theorem. Higher-loop pole/current
and multipole corrections, higher derivative vertices, more general soft
emission, finite-temperature populations, nonperturbative or UV/gravitational
violation of the transpose selection rule, and an actual gravity embedding
remain separate. No thermal production, reheating, abundance or visible-
photon observational fit is supplied by a long vacuum lifetime.

The raw supersymmetry-breaking auxiliary norm F_eff^2=mu^4/31 enters the
Ward coupling even if an independent gravitational vacuum subtraction is
assumed. Replacing it by the observed net dark-energy density would change
the decay calculation incorrectly. Conversely, requiring the raw V0 itself
to equal that density fixes mu and eliminates this MeV example.
