# Exact charge obligation for a heavy-sector producer

The unbroken residual U1 has diagonal generator D/sqrt60. Its broken adjoint
components carry charges +/-sqrt(5/12); the unbroken SU3,SU2,U1 components
and singlet flat direction are neutral. This is an exact gauge charge, not
the transpose selection rule used for the neutral photino.

Every physical decay of a single charged excitation must retain that charge
in its daughters. Hence the lightest charged state is stable against decay
into entirely neutral particles. Pair annihilation and bound-state processes
are separate and must be computed; stability of one charged particle does
not imply conservation of the total particle-plus-antiparticle number.

At the origin, the broken-sector fermion matrix has a zero eigenvalue per
broken generator. At the admitted outer backgrounds all charged physical
tree states are massive. The matrix control classifies scalar, Weyl and
vector modes using the unbroken charge-squared operator, excluding scalar
gauge Goldstones. It reports the leading lightest charged state and compares
it with the photino mass. Weak loop corrections may shift the ordering, but
cannot remove the exact charge obligation; whichever state is lightest
remains protected by charge conservation.

A charged-messenger decay into one photino plus one charged daughter leaves
at least one charged particle per such decay before pair depletion. If that
process supplies all photinos, a10% charged rest-density allowance requires

    N_charged/N_chi <= .1 m_chi/M_charged.

This is a necessary number-depletion ratio for that producer, not a proof
that depletion fails. Resonant neutral-Higgs inverse decays, charged
annihilation, changing background masses and late plasma processes must be
included before claiming thermal production succeeds or is excluded.
An initially populated massless charged branch cannot be omitted when the
field later rolls to its massive state.
