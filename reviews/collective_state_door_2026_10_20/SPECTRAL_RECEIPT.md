# A response-and-restoration test for a physical collective state

20 October 2026. Parent target: [TARGET.md](TARGET.md). This is a necessary
matching test and an executed analytic discriminator, not a constructed new
phase, a lifetime calculation or a global no-go theorem.

## 1. Define the physical operator before counting memory

Let the proposed polarization be one specified microscopic vector operator
`P`, with the same definition before and after release. Its density identity,
source vertex and conversion current must follow from the existing action.
If `div P` represents a selected density rather than the total mass, its
commutators and response must be calculated for that selected operator.
The total-mass sum rule is not automatically its sum rule. A surviving
ordinary mass response is compatible with disappearance of an *additional*
response; the latter must be an observable consequence of the same action.

Use the irreducible material response to an imposed gravitational-potential
probe first. Restore reciprocal self-gravity exactly once afterwards. This
separates a material restoring force from gravitational attraction or a
baryon-host overlap energy already present in that same gravitational term.

## 2. The canonical energy gives a precise response target

Write `K=4piG`, `u=K|P|`, `0<=u<a0`. Up to its zero-point constant,

\[
 W_{\rm rem}(u)={1\over K}
 \left[-a_0^2\log(1-u/a_0)-a_0u-{u^2\over2}\right].
\]

The *material* local energy, before gravitational elimination, is

\[
 e(P)={K|P|^2\over2}+W_{\rm rem}(K|P|)
 ={ -a_0^2\log(1-u/a_0)-a_0u\over K}.
\]

An imposed uniform source `g` couples as `-g dot P`. Its local equilibrium
obeys `g=a0 u/(a0-u)`. The radial and transverse Hessian eigenvalues are

\[
 A_\parallel={K a_0^2\over(a_0-u)^2}
             =K(1+g/a_0)^2,\qquad
 A_\perp={K a_0\over a_0-u}=K(1+g/a_0).
 \tag{1}
\]

At zero background they both tend to `K`. The retained gradient term adds
`gamma k^2`, with the *existing* `gamma=K L*^2/2`. Neither `a0` nor `L*`
is derived by this calculation; their inherited status is unchanged.

Take a longitudinal plane-wave density perturbation `delta rho=i k P_L`.
At an aligned background use `k` parallel or perpendicular to `P0`, so
the selected longitudinal coordinate is a Hessian eigenvector. Its quadratic
material density kernel and susceptibility are necessarily

\[
 h_\alpha(k)={A_\alpha+\gamma k^2\over k^2},\qquad
 \boxed{\chi^{\rm mat}_{\rho,\alpha}(k,0)
       ={k^2\over A_\alpha+\gamma k^2}},
 \quad \delta\rho=-\chi^{\rm mat}_\rho\,\delta\phi.
 \tag{2}
\]

This is more discriminating than finding a positive oscillator curvature.
The microscopic state must have the correct *operator residue*, momentum
dependence, absolute normalization and finite-background dependence. General
wave directions require the appropriate constrained matrix inverse; (2)
does not assume that an anisotropic matrix can always be replaced by one
scalar eigenvalue.

## 3. A conditional spectral frequency with no new fit

For clarity, consider a passive time-reversal invariant ground state, with
positive spectral representation in the chosen reciprocal channel,

\[
 \chi_\rho(k,\omega)=\sum_n{F_n(k)\over
           \Omega_n(k)^2-(\omega+i0)^2},\quad F_n\geq0.
 \tag{3}
\]

Integrals replace sums for a continuum. Zero-frequency poles, instability,
moving backgrounds and conversion require separate treatment; they are
not hidden in (3). For a Galilean nonrelativistic *complete mass density*
of positive particles, coordinate-dependent interactions commute with that
density. Its kinetic double commutator gives, per volume,

\[
 {1\over V\hbar^2}\langle[\rho_{-k},[H,\rho_k]]\rangle
       =\bar\rho k^2=\sum_n F_n(k).
 \tag{4}
\]

For multiple species this requires the same inertial and source masses
and the actual conserved mass operator. In the source/daughter retained
action, the charge-weighted reference density is not automatically the
complete mass when `M!=2m`; its actual moment must be used instead. The
earlier phase note gives that moment directly. Subtracting center-of-mass
motion from a finite object also removes spectral weight: the right side
of (4) must not then be assigned to its internal optical mode.

When (4) *does* apply to the very operator in (2), their ratio gives the
necessary weighted harmonic mean of squared frequencies,

\[
 \Omega_H^2(k):={\sum_nF_n\over\sum_nF_n/\Omega_n^2}
       =\bar\rho\,(A_\alpha+\gamma k^2).
 \tag{5}
\]

No single-mode approximation is needed for this ratio. At zero background
its limit is `4piG bar rho`. A single-pole displacement realization with
the actual kinetic energy `|dot P|^2/(2 bar rho)` would have precisely
this positive material gap. Ordinary gravity contributes the *negative*
longitudinal energy `-K|P_L|^2/2`, so its total-mode frequency is instead

\[
 \Omega_{\rm total}^2(k)=\bar\rho
                  (A_\alpha-K+\gamma k^2).
 \tag{6}
\]

At zero background the two constants cancel. Equation (6) is conditional
on that single inertial coordinate; the static subtraction is general.
A Jeans instability of a fluid does not provide the missing positive
material gap. Nor may an independently chosen oscillator frequency be
set equal to (5) and called a prediction.

For example, acoustic weight `F_n~f_n k^2`, `Omega_n~c_n k`, with
`f_n>0`, leaves a positive constant contribution to static susceptibility.
It cannot satisfy the vanishing `k^2` in (2). Conversely, an internal
finite-object mode can be gapped but have mass-density weight `O(k^4)`
or smaller; its gap alone also does not meet (2). A projected extra-force
operator could differ, but must earn its own vertex and spectral moment.

## 4. A restoration experiment separates a constitutive state from a label

Start from a specified existing-field state `A` in a baryonic source
`rho_b^0`. Apply a stated finite release protocol and restore that source
to `rho_b^0`. Call the resulting microscopic state `B`. Compare matched
present sources, boundaries and total gravitating stock; record residual
energy, flow and mass loss rather than silently declaring them restored.
The microscopic states are allowed to differ: that is the proposed memory.

At both preparations apply the same infinitesimal baryonic probe. Compute
the complete retarded density/force response matrix, including baryon
motion, self-gravity and receiver flux. The same physical `P` map must
reproduce (1)-(2) in the responding state and lose the specified additional
force contribution in the released state. Ordinary Newtonian mass response
is retained and counted once. Restoring the source must not automatically
recreate the original response if permanent release is claimed.

A simple difference `chi_B-chi_A` is not by itself proof of an extra force:
temperature, changed density and Doppler shifts can change ordinary
response. Those terms must be predicted from the same states. There must
also be an absolute match to the canonical source and constitutive kernel.
This is why matched-history measurements and a source-calibrated operator
are both needed.

Then evolve `B` under the restored same action and calculate the time
dependence of that response difference. A conserved winding label can
last indefinitely without turning longitudinal response off. A finite
phase-slip barrier is not a rate, and a vanishing binding energy does not
specify an absorbing boundary. A rate or memory time requires actual
receiver/scattering states, or an exact dynamical selection rule.

## 5. Decision and bounded novelty

The decisive next calculation is thus a finite-state, source-calibrated
response spectrum before release, after release, and after source
restoration. It tests the positive kernel, source residue and memory in
one object. A successful static interpolation refit or a fossil value of
`a0` tests different implications.

The parent packet already proved a static kernel mismatch and constructed
the compensating current. The new contribution here is the finite-background
response target, the conditional spectral-moment normalization and their
combination with a matched restoration test. This does not establish a new
general physical principle or an all-phase exclusion. All equations above
are analytic; no scientific numerical run or fitted parameter is used.
