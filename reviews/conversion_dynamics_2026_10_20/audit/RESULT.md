## Claim card and conventions

The canonical claim concerns a collisionless rest-mass displacement with
`partial_t P=-j` and `div P=rho-rhobar`, together with S4's distinct permanent
release label. The reciprocal two-population extension used in the radial
construction has separately conserved stocks; it is not silently identified
with a reaction already present in S2. The scalar claim concerns complex
charge-one and charge-two fields, with conserved
`Q=N_psi+2N_b`, at a common finite spatial/contact regulator. Scalar mode
wavefunctions may be complex. Discrete pair states use normalized bosonic
Fock states and unordered pair counting. Microscopic coefficients are in
natural units; the displayed `hbar` converts energy to time.

Radial statements quantify over sufficiently regular spherical Hamiltonian
flows on occupied annuli, with no relevant phase-space boundary flux and
the separated shell ordering when separability is asserted. Threshold and
rate statements require a static host with a common continuum zero and the
specific channel stated. Golden-rule and Markov reductions require the
explicit continuum, weak-coupling, state and coarse-time hypotheses. A local
harmonic approximation is not a statement about the complete host spectrum.

## Decisive reconstruction

### 1. Canonical source and radial dynamics

Taking the divergence of `partial_t P=-j` and using continuity preserves
`div P-(rho-rhobar)`. If a projected stock instead obeys
`partial_t rho_t+div j_t=S`, the simultaneous identity
`div P=rho_t-rhobar_t` requires
`div(partial_t P+j_t)=S`. Keeping the old displacement law forces `S=0`.
Crossing zero orbital energy does not remove a parcel from the original
source. The scalar protected charge and the radial collective parcel mass
are different inventories and cannot be equated by a label change.

For every central, possibly time-dependent force,
`d(r cross v)/dt=0`. The original supported family has
`j_t(s)^2=G M0 r0(s)`, with `r0<a`, whereas
`j_f^2=100 G(3M0+m)a`. Hence the squared-action support ratio exceeds
300. No collisionless characteristic, and no corresponding central
one-body quantum propagator after such quantization is adopted, changes
between those disjoint supports. Full nonspherical many-body gravity is
outside this individual-action conservation statement.

For positive finite spherical Newtonian mass, the potential is at least
`-G Mtot/r`. Thus negative instantaneous orbital energy requires
`r>j^2/(2G Mtot)`. With the outer action and the conservative attracting
mass bound `Mtot=3M0+2m`, this is
`r>50(3M0+m)/(3M0+2m) a >= 50(301/302)a` for the stated mass interval.
It excludes the supported core `r<=9a/8` even for a negative-energy
eccentric outer-action orbit. It is an ordinary-gravity statement for the
free parcel; it does not insert the tether response force into that orbit.

Direct shell integration gives
`E_h=Mh jh^2/(2q^2)-G Mh(M0+mn+Mh/2)/q` and
`E_f=m(2-n)[jf^2/(2R^2)-G(M0+Mh+m+mn/2)/R]`.
There is no `q,R` mixed term. Inner shape terms see only the constant
potential of exterior shells. At fixed stock, radial host oscillation
cannot write the exterior circular stock while the ordering holds. A
comparison of separately minimized occupations supplies no missing
occupation velocity or transition operator.

For a shell host, `Phi_h=-G Mh/max(r,q(t))`; its derivative is
`G Mh qdot/q^2` inside and zero outside. Continuity of the potential at
the shell removes a surface-delta term. Contraction lowers a crossing
particle's energy; expansion raises it. For `e=h-e_escape(t)` and a
collisionless distribution, differentiating `Theta(-e)f` gives
`-delta(e)(partial_t h-edot_escape)f`. Splitting this signed integral
into positive and negative parts gives exactly the capture and release
crossing fluxes in radial equations (11)-(12). They are crossing fluxes,
not automatically independent Poisson hazards; recrossings can correlate.

### 2. Local host receiver

Write `A=M0+Mh/2`, `q0=jh^2/(GA)`. Expanding the counted host energy gives
`D=G Mh A/q0^3`, `f=G Mh m/q0^2`, and `Omega^2=D/Mh>0`.
Completion of the square shifts the host minimum by `-fn/D` and yields
the occupation curvature `U_h=-f^2/D=-G^2 Mh m^2/jh^2`.
For adjacent harmonic minima separated by `d=f/D`, the coherent
displacement obeys
`S=Mh Omega d^2/(2hbar)=|U_h|/(2hbar Omega)`.
The unexcited-to-level-l probabilities are `exp(-S)S^l/l!`, summing to
one. The inclusive probability is not suppressed by `exp(-S)`.

These are properties of the stated local harmonic eigenstates. The
factorization language is a separation of scalar/host mode variables;
it must not be read as a proof that an arbitrarily slow driven write
excites the same Franck-Condon distribution. The packet correctly demands
an actual original `v_fi(q)` and its integral if it varies over the host
width/displacement. The overlap neither creates a forbidden operator nor
gives a linewidth. A closed discrete receiver has coherent evolution;
its finite excitation energy can also enable reverse transitions.

### 3. Scalar matching and bosonic factors

Expanding the original positive-square action gives
`c3=g_c/(2m sqrt(2M))` and the positive-frequency
`kc=lambda_tot/(4m^2)`. Direct subtraction gives
`kc-c3^2/delta=lambda_rep/(4m^2)+g_c^2/[8m^2 M(M+2m)]`.
The raw response derivation explicitly decomposes the source propagator;
its removed negative-frequency piece contributes
`delta_k=-g_c^2/[8m^2 M(M+2m)]`. Thus the matched coefficient is
`kR=kc+delta_k` and `kR-c3^2/delta=lambda_rep/(4m^2)`.
The packet uses this specified matched truncation, rather than silently
claiming that MASTER's uncorrected `kc` is exactly threshold matched.

For the cubic, two ordered daughter annihilation terms combine for
distinct modes, giving `g_Aij=2c3 I_Aij`; for one repeated normalized
pair they give `g_Aii=sqrt(2)c3 I_Aii`. Therefore the unified expression
is `2c3 I_Aij/sqrt(1+delta_ij)`. The repeated-mode Fock factors include
`n_i(n_i-1)/2` or `(n_i+1)(n_i+2)/2` when written in terms of `|g_Aii|^2`.
The draft includes both factors. Counting a flat `k,-k` pair once gives
`Gamma_NR=c3^2 m sqrt(m delta)/pi`, consistent to NR order with the
original relativistic two-identical-daughter width.

For `H4=(g2/2) integral psi^dagger2 psi^2`, two creation orders and two
annihilation orders give the distinct-pair matrix element `2g2 I`.
Normalized unordered two-particle states give
`2g2 I/sqrt[(1+delta_ij)(1+delta_kl)]`. The collision route's repeated
incoming-mode coefficient and factorial moment agree with this expression.
Retained-source exchange has coefficient `4c3^2 J_out^* J_in` and the
direct contact `4kR I`; its flat low-energy local limit recovers `2g2 I`.
It is not controlled through the real source pole without its derived
self-energy or explicit source evolution.

For an open bare source in a fixed factorized continuum bath, subtracting
the exact forward/reverse Bose products cancels the `n_X f_i f_j` term:
`(1+n_X)f_i f_j-n_X(1+f_i)(1+f_j)` equals
`f_i f_j-n_X(1+f_i+f_j)`. Integration against the nonnegative vacuum
decay measure gives `d n_X/dt=A-Lambda n_X`, with

does not claim a general quantum information lifetime or a statement

### 4. Thresholds and accessible channels

With daughter ground energy `epsilon0<0` and continuum zero, two-free
and bound-plus-free separated-channel thresholds are respectively zero
and `epsilon0`. Repulsive daughter contact vanishes when clusters are
separated. The stated finite-regulator/channel assumptions support this
threshold ledger. This audit does not promote that ledger to a theorem
about the complete renormalized relativistic spectrum or unprovided
host/radiation channels. Open energy support still requires nonzero
on-shell overlap and occupied incoming modes.

For the Coulomb control, `epsilon0=-m alpha^2/2` and the source ground
excess energy is `d_A=delta-q^3 m alpha^2/2`, `q=M/m>2`.
Solving for `d_A=0`, `d_A=epsilon0`, and `d_A=2epsilon0` gives the three
ordered boundaries printed in cubic equations (9)-(10). Hence closing
two-free decay does not close mixed bound/free release. A normalizable
dressed level below `epsilon0` has no vacuum pair-continuum width in
the stated model, but direct association from that continuum into the
same isolated level also has no energy-conserving support.

The spectator process has charge ledger `1+1+1 -> 2+1`. Its outgoing
spectator can carry `sum epsilon_in-E_B`. On bare Q=3 states,
`V3 G0 V4` can contribute whereas `V4 G0 V3` vanishes because only one
daughter remains after association. These checks establish an allowed
existing interaction route, not positivity of a full dressed channel
amplitude. For dressed objects, residual Q=2 cluster levels, elastic
channels and the stated definition of ionization must be distinguished.
The draft explicitly retains that obligation instead of treating every
loss of bare-source identity as release from the host.

For the contact orbital channel, `epsilon_b=-B` and
`epsilon_b+epsilon_k=epsilon_p+epsilon_q` require
`epsilon_k=epsilon_p+epsilon_q+B>=B`. This fixes both the reverse
evaporation threshold and the energy of the write-produced receiver.
The above-threshold daughter must escape or be evolved as an actual
potential reverse partner; resetting the bath would assume transport.

For two inherited daughter momenta of magnitude `m u_*`, invariant
kinematics gives `s-4m^2=2m^2 u_*^2(1-cos alpha)` and
`x=(1-cos alpha)/2`. Independent isotropic directions fill `[0,1]`;
the entire inherited collision integral is therefore not a threshold
contact calculation. Actual produced-pair correlations are separate
state data. The packet correctly preserves this limitation.

### 5. State dependence and the observable called memory

The threshold-bearing release control has a linear loss functional
`Gamma[F]=integral F(E)K(E)dE`, with `K=0` below `B` and positive on
some accessible interval above `B`. The stated two-point mixture has
weight `w=(E0-El)/(Eh-El)` and exactly matches the single-energy
reservoir's number and mean incoming energy. Its low-energy component
has zero loss and its high component gives `n w K(Eh)>0`, whereas the
single-energy reservoir below threshold has zero loss. Isotropic
asymptotic NR kinetic pressure is `2nE0/3` in both. Compact normalized
bumps with the same means preserve both moments and the zero/positive
distinction. This proves the advertised incoming-reservoir counterexample;
it asserts no self-consistent stationary galaxy pair with identical
local density/pressure profiles.

For contact capture, the same energy shell gives the Bose thermal ratio
`A_b/B_b=exp[-beta(epsilon_b-mu)]`. Birth/death rates
`(n+1)A_b,nB_b` yield
`d<n>/dt=A_b+(A_b-B_b)<n>`. Thus stable mean-occupation contrast decays
with `B_b-A_b`, whereas no jump from `n=1` has hazard `2A_b+B_b`.
A loss-only first-exit reduction instead has hazard `B_b`. These are
different observables and different stated approximations.

For a genuine two-state Markov site, contrast between preparations is
`exp[-(a+b)t]`; occupied-state first release is controlled by `b`.
Solving the same fixed generator from an empty site gives the cubic
write/contrast bound (24). The algebraic formation/retention feasibility
bounds (19)-(20) also follow from their declared mobile-density convention;
the per-site and mobile `L3` coefficients have correctly different units.
They are conditional tests, not measured or evaluated rates.

Finally the entire Q=1 daughter sector is annihilated by the
normal-ordered quartic and both cubic terms. A prepared one-daughter
eigenorbital of the static-host regulated one-body Hamiltonian is
therefore an exact eigenstate with `N_B(t)=1`. This result needs neither
a barrier estimate nor a Markov approximation. It does require that
static host and the absence of partners/sources/additional sectors. It
does not prove dynamical scattering preparation, a read protocol, or
S4 retention in a live halo. Capture from a closed two-daughter state
keeps total charge two: the outgoing partner retains one charge. The
isolated Q=1 protection applies to the separated final bound subsystem;
it is not a finite-time erasure of the other global charge.
