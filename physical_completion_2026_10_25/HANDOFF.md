## The physical candidate

Independent carrier x and matter angle y, with q_T=sin^2(pi y/2) and independent
vacuum-volume q_x=sin^2(pi x/2). Alignment energy is
`J rho sin^2(pi(y-x)/2)`. Canonical matter kinetic energy requires both
sqrt-density stiffness and `kappa pi^2 rho |grad y|^2/8`. The vacuum carrier
also needs a nonzero gradient coefficient at vacuum; here ell_v=.01 kpc.

A local complex vacuum field Sigma=f(1+x)exp(i chi) can supply a massless angular
mode without directly coupling chi to matter or baryons. Its polar kinetic
energy transfers radial oscillation energy into angular modes; outgoing null
waves drive the modulus only through subleading spherical corrections. This
is a physically explicit candidate, with phenomenological coefficients and
additional dynamical assumptions. It is not a microscopic derivation.

## What is still needed, with resumption conditions

**Active physical obligation:** derive one number- and energy-conserving kinetic
conversion closure and F distribution/entropy from the candidate action, then
couple material motion, carrier motion and angular radiation on a free domain.
It must produce the radiative pump, not insert the desired energy into it.
Specify time inertia, normal-state distribution, thermalization/conversion
operators, conserved energy/current and common physical couplings before any
claim of a formation simulation. The existing static data cannot select these
uniquely: different positive inertias and angular couplings have the same
static equilibrium (DERIVATION section11).

Open continuations, not declared exhausted:

1. **Full radiation/formation coupling.** The oscillator/pulse tests establish
   possible components only. Resume after an explicit dynamical Hamiltonian or
   detailed-balance kinetic operator fixes the pump and rate. Test actual
   escaped energy, F heating/conversion and boundary restoration together.
2. **Material constitutive state across galaxies.** Replace the chosen K policy
   with a derived F kinetic state; use common physical inputs and then actual
   galaxy baryon profiles. A temperature proportional to the desired BTFR is
   not a derivation. Prior CFG122 already tested and rejected standard BK and
   temperature-slaved EOS escapes in their stated classes.
3. **Larger-supply branch selection.** A different inside-out initialization or
   energy-verified branch search could differ from PC-19. Resume only with that
   changed premise and record the supply change; extra mass alone has not
   repaired the tested force balance. A high-barrier plus census-supply
   combination is untested, and must not be presented as ruled out or working.
4. **Exact versus approximate bulk law.** PC-28 rules out an interface-only repair
   for its canonical pure-core assumptions. A different microscopic stiffness
   or bulk response can evade it, but must supply its own force derivation.
   An energy penalty written to vanish on a chosen law profile would not do so.
5. **Physical source and stability of the vacuum condensate.** Derive B, binding,
   stiffness, alignment and a globally stable radial potential. Establish a
   consistent time/covariant completion; no cosmological or Solar-System rescue
   follows from the spherical benchmarks.

These are genuine remaining physical dependencies, not hidden completed work.
Further arbitrary coefficient scans cannot establish their joint implication.
The present phase records a concrete candidate and decisive constraints while
leaving the main objective open.
