# Assembly and dissociation from a Coulomb parent

**A physical positively charged bound state and radiative channel are derived;
the requested disk/dwarf/cluster mechanism is still not established.**
This stage tests a concrete molecular completion, not a galaxy simulation.

The previous harmonic lattice cannot determine dissociation energy. Potentials
with identical curvature can have arbitrary well depths. We therefore specified
a mobile Coulomb parent with the same charge species and gauge interaction,
without adding a new binding force. This is a candidate microscopic completion,
not a unique consequence of the earlier effective band Hamiltonian.

## Positive result: a charged assembly channel

Two positive carriers X sharing one negative countercharge C form a molecular
ion T=X2C with net charge+Q. Unlike the earlier neutral atom A=XC, this object
can repel positive charges through the same field. All species and leftover
countercharges remain explicit. Free X also couples to that field: assembly
reduces its charge-to-mass ratio by M/(2M+m), rather than switching charge on.
Calling this molecule a galactic tether still needs a dynamical demonstration.

In an explicit two-orbital Born-Oppenheimer approximation at heavy/light mass
ratio100:

- Electronic well depth: **.06483099 E_H**, separation **2.492830 a_D**.
- Including nuclear zero-point motion: ground dissociation energy
  **.04810609 E_H**, with15 bound rovibrational levels and rotational weight83.
- Halving radial spacing .04 to .02 changes ground binding by2.03e-6 E_H;
  the level count is unchanged. The electronic basis error is not measured
  by this grid agreement; these are not exact molecular energies.
- The same physical dipole operator gives a nonzero g-u radiative matrix
  element and a selected photodissociation cross section. Thermal detailed
  balance fixes the inverse selected association coefficient. Neither has
  an independent normalization or fitted formation energy.
- Curvature is also predicted: K≈.0628331 E_H/a_D². Nuclear vibration
  ≈.0354494 E_H/hbar and electronic g-u gap≈.275599 E_H are not resonant.
  This radial vibration is not automatically the polar mode of the prior
  quantum benchmark; its inversion symmetry differs.

A conservative scattering check illustrates the capture criterion: up to96.3%
probability enters the molecular region while true bound-state probability
remains below5e-32 for a continuum-projected initial state. Matter-energy error
is2.64e-16 E_H. Formation needs an emitted quantum or third-body energy transfer;
local concentration cannot be counted as binding.

## Population test and a uniform reduced-model bound

A restricted four-species network X+,C-,A0,T+ uses the computed spectrum.
Charge and both constituent counts are conserved. Closed-matter endpoints
balance initial kinetic energy against final kinetic, atomic and molecular
binding/internal energy; fixed-temperature endpoints are separately labeled.
These are equilibrium screens, not finite-time capture histories.

For this spectrum and statistical model, an analytic inequality gives
**charged molecular mass fraction <=.48692047 at every density and temperature**.
It eliminates alpha, light mass and environmental scales from the80% operational
screen. It is NOT a bound for the exact Coulomb problem, other species, condensed
phases or nonequilibrium assembly.

The main525-point common-parameter scan had no point satisfying the applicability
screens in all four environments: its cluster temperatures exceeded the declared
ground-atom regime. Its large raw fractions are therefore not physical successes.
An explicit42-point extension to alpha=.15/.2 reaches that missing regime:

| Isolated-matter energy matched |29|1.24095e-6|0|
| Fixed temperature, thermostatted |30|1.27426e-44|0|

The energy-matched maximum occurs at common alpha=.2 and light mass19.9526eV.
Its fractions for the two dwarf proxies, disk proxy and cluster proxy are
1.015e-8,7.598e-8,1.241e-6,1.396e-9. Thus some relative preference exists, but the
absolute charged-molecule abundance is about1.24 parts per million. It is not
a macroscopic tethered component. The screening threshold80% is inherited from
previous tests; it is not an independently derived observational requirement.

Neutral atoms count as non-tethered mass in this comparison, not as unbound
fundamental particles. No galaxy types enter the equations; however, only
mass/radius-derived homogeneous density and temperature proxies are used.
No actual rotating disk, dwarf or cluster is evolved. The approximation omits
neutral molecules, excited atoms, nuclear-spin exchange, photon transport,
nonideal plasma effects and gravity. Passing applicability screens is necessary,
not proof these omissions are all negligible. At alpha=.2 uncomputed electronic
relativistic corrections have characteristic orderalpha²=.04.
