## What this does to the crossing-speed joint

Read with DSC1, the ALPAKA rings neither support nor kill the joint's distinctive statement. All nine z >= 2.1 discs fall
above v*(z) on both footings, but their curves reach about 1 to 3.6 R_e (1.2 by construction for most). Inside 6 kpc the
reference halo adds a few percent to gravity, so shape is silent. The amplitude is not: with the record's class-M gas and
SED stellar masses, the baryons alone already exceed the measured rotation by 0.2 to 0.9 dex, and the halo multiple is
pinned at 0 with an interval reaching 2.3. That is NO POWER as frozen. It is also not a halo statement at all: it says the
baryon inventory of these high-SFR discs, which CFG229 already flagged, cannot be trusted at the 0.3 dex level. Below the
boundary, the z ~ 1.5 discs sit above their baryons, and the law and the reference cannot be told apart.

So DSC1's fragile canonical hit above v*(z) is not repeated or contradicted on a second, larger, higher-redshift sample.
Both samples fail for the same reason: amplitude trades one-for-one with an uncertain baryon mass, and shape needs curves
beyond about 3 R_e that neither sample has. ID 18 is the only ALPAKA disc out that far, and it alone cannot weigh a halo.
The joint's statement stays untested on resolved curves. A decision needs three things:
- gas conversions measured per galaxy, not chosen;
- measured sizes for the 11 convention discs;
- rings beyond about 10 kpc for z > 2 discs above v*(z).
