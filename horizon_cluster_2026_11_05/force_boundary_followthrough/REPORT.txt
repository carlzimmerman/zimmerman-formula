RESULT

The isolated fourth-root force-balance law is correct for the explicitly
specified additive MOND + Lambda model. Its finite-age radial shell prediction
is computable, and the Local Group illustration gives R0 = 2.089 Mpc rather
than the historical measured 0.96 Mpc. This discrepancy is conditional on the
point-mass, fixed-potential, isolated, zero-angular-momentum age prescription;
it is not a general exclusion of MOND or the proposed a0-Lambda relation.

An external-field-dominated QUMOND asymptote restores a cube-root boundary.
For the conversation's illustrative Local Group baryonic mass 1.6e11 Msun,
matching R0 = 0.96 Mpc requires a PHYSICAL external field about 0.077 a0 in
the angular-mean approximation. A smooth radial crossover toy gives 0.074 a0.
This is an inverse requirement, NOT an independently measured external field
or confirmation. The external-dominated approximation is self-consistent near
that boundary: internal/external acceleration is 0.0364 in the asymptote.

A constructive counterfamily proves that the isolated galaxy force law and
the empty de Sitter limit alone do NOT uniquely select the fourth-root outer
law. Those premises also do not select 32pi. No healthy covariant cosmological
completion is established here. The bounded mathematical questions have been
answered; the theory-selection and empirical-validation questions remain open.

2. ISOLATED MODEL: EXACT CONDITIONAL RESULTS

Assume rddot = -A/r + K r.
Potential per unit mass: Phi(r) = A ln(r/r_ref) - K r^2/2.
The arbitrary r_ref cancels from every potential difference.

Static radius Rs = sqrt(A/K) = (G M_b a0/K^2)^(1/4).
It is UNSTABLE: d(rddot)/dr at Rs = 2K > 0.
This is a force-balance radius, not a light horizon and not a merger guarantee.

For a particle initially inside Rs, the radial energy barrier is
  DeltaPhi = A [ln(Rs/r) - 1/2 + r^2/(2 Rs^2)].
The outward radial speed required to reach/cross the barrier obeys
  v_escape^2 = 2 DeltaPhi.
Energy below the barrier traps a trajectory on the inner allowed branch;
the same energy can also label a disconnected OUTER allowed branch. Energy
alone without position/branch information is not a binding test.

Circular orbit angular momentum satisfies l^2 = A r^2 - K r^4.
The radial stability condition is
  Phi_eff'' = 2 A/r^2 - 4K > 0,
so stable circular orbits exist only for r < Rs/sqrt(2).
Neither Rs nor this circular-orbit threshold implies a merger time.

With x=r/Rs and dimensionless time tau=sqrt(K)t:
  x'' = x - 1/x.
For a shell turning at x_t < 1, its travel time is
  tau(x_t) = integral_0^x_t dx /
       sqrt[2 ln(x_t/x) + x^2 - x_t^2].
As x_t -> 0, tau/x_t -> sqrt(pi/2); as x_t -> 1, time diverges.
The integral is strictly increasing: write x=x_t y; increasing x_t increases
the numerator and decreases the positive denominator for 0<y<1.
Thus the first-turning solution at a specified positive age is unique.

At the stated age, x_t = 0.554733098259.
For the inverse-square comparison x''=x-1/x^2, the corresponding result is
x_t = 0.719956357663. The familiar ~0.72 multiplier is not the MOND multiplier.
At turnaround velocity vanishes; attraction is NOT equal to K R0. In the
isolated model g_attract/(K R0)=1/x_t^2; in inverse-square dynamics it is
1/x_t^3. Only at the STATIC boundary is attraction equal to K r.

3. WHAT THE 32pi RELATION DOES AND DOES NOT SUPPLY

Before substituting any coefficient,
  Rs^2/(rM RdS) = sqrt(3) a0/(c^2 sqrt(Lambda)).
The proposed relation gives sqrt(3/(32pi)) = 0.172747074736.
At the fixed age, the analogous R0 ratio is multiplied by x_t^2.

However v_flat^4=GM_b a0 implies Rs=v_flat/sqrt(K). At fixed MEASURED
v_flat the coefficient a0 cancels exactly. Measuring v_flat and Rs alone
cannot select 32pi. Independent baryonic mass is needed to infer a0, and the
external field and history must also be constrained if this is to become a
coefficient test. Substituting a mass obtained from the same v_flat is circular.

4. EXTERNAL FIELD: DERIVATION, RANGE CHECK, AND INVERSE REQUIREMENT

Use the external-field-dominated QUMOND potential of [S1],
  phi = -GM_b nu_e [1 + (K0/2) sin^2(theta)]/r.
Here K0=d ln(nu)/d ln(g_N,e), not the cosmological K above.
In the deep external-field limit, K0=-1/2. If e=g_e/a0 is the PHYSICAL
external field fraction, g_N,e/a0=e^2 and nu_e=1/e.
Thus radial attraction is B(theta)/r^2, where
  B(theta)= GM_b h(theta)/e, h=1-sin^2(theta)/4 in [3/4,1].
The angular mean h_bar=5/6. This is a radial-force average, NOT an exact
spherical potential or an assertion that all directions are radial orbits.
Axis directions have no angular force; elsewhere the full potential has one.

With the same ADDITIVE Lambda prescription,
  Rs,E(theta) = [GM_b h(theta)/(e K)]^(1/3).
The instantaneous radial-force zero is not a complete equilibrium surface.
At fixed external field the mass exponent is 1/3, not 1/4.
The angular-mean inverse-square travel-time model gives R0=x_N Rs,E.

5. COUNTERFAMILY: WHY GALAXY DYNAMICS DO NOT FIX THE OUTER LAW

Let g_N=GM_b/r^2, g_I be any chosen isolated MOND attraction, and
z=g_N/(Kr). For p>0 define
  W_p(z)=z^p/(1+z^p),
  inward net acceleration F_p = g_N + (g_I-g_N) W_p(z) - Kr.
Define the additive comparison separately by W_0=1.

All models have the same isolated-galaxy limit wherever z>>1, the same
Lambda->0 attraction g_I, and the same empty-vacuum acceleration +Kr as
M_b->0 at fixed r. A common high-acceleration interpolation in g_I can be
used in every model. They differ in how the low-acceleration enhancement is
continued into a cosmologically dominated region.

For p=0 and g_I=A/r, Rs^4=GM_b a0/K^2.
For p=1, cancellation gives exactly
  F_1=(g_N g_I - (Kr)^2)/(g_N+Kr),
so the positive outer zero instead obeys
  Rs,1^5 = GM_b A/K^2,
  Rs,1 = (G^3 M_b^3 a0/K^4)^(1/10) proportional to M_b^(3/10).
In the regime z<<1 and g_I>>g_N, general finite p yields exponent
  (p+1/2)/(3p+2), approaching 1/3 as p grows.
The exact p=1 cancellation does not require dropping the g_N term.

At M_b=1e11 Msun, numerical static roots are:
 additive p=0: 3.34884 Mpc; p=1: 1.08870 Mpc;
 p=2: 0.84223 Mpc; p=4: 0.69582 Mpc.
These are counterexamples to UNIQUENESS FROM THE LIMITED PREMISES, not proposed
physical fits. Their covariant actions, conservation laws and perturbation
health are unspecified. They do not prove that a chosen complete gravity theory
has arbitrary predictions. They prove that the two asymptotic limits alone do
not choose the completion. No parameter p has been fitted to observations.
