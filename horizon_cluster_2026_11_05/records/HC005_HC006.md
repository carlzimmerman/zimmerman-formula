## What changed

### Hot-gas association survives stronger checks

The published 85-system sample was divided into 63 blocks using the source's central-host labels and an explicit alias dictionary. Virgo's two central galaxies and ten satellites were kept together. M106/CVnI host aliases were also merged; isolated systems each got their own block. These are source-host blocks, not independently verified spatially independent groups. Ambiguous or missing memberships remain a limitation.

Holding out entire blocks changes prediction RMSE from 0.395 dex without temperature to 0.314 dex with temperature. Equal weighting of blocks gives 0.421 to 0.313 dex. The block-bootstrap temperature coefficient interval is [1.12,2.20]; the bootstrap interval for mean block-level squared-error improvement is [0.0409,0.1252] dex². Because the held-out errors share training data, the latter is an exploratory stability diagnostic, not an external-validation significance claim.

Removing all 12 Virgo entries leaves residual correlation 0.660 (73 objects). A conditional effective-variance likelihood including quoted errors gives temperature coefficients 1.619 with diagonal errors and 1.616 with the declared shared-distance covariance sensitivity. All three optimizer starts agree, converge and avoid scatter bounds. These are sensitivity models, not a full latent-population or selection model. In particular, unequal mass-resolution selection and heterogeneous measurement methods are unresolved.

The association is robust within the tested selected sample. It is not yet evidence for extra gravitational force; hotter halos may feed larger holes, holes may heat gas, and both may follow halo formation history.

### New rotational data provide a narrower, less robust clue

Source: Davis, Graham & Combes, *A Consistent Set of Empirical Scaling Relations for Spiral Galaxies: The (vmax, MDM)–(sigma0, MBH, phi) Relations*, ApJ 877, 64 (2019). Versioned author manuscript arXiv:1901.06509v2, dated 2019-05-25, Table 1: https://arxiv.org/html/1901.06509v2 . The unversioned HTML endpoint resolved to v2, as shown in its header; local source hash is pinned in data/davis.json.

48 entries, 42 finite vmax measurements, 31 matches to the stellar-mass sample, 27 eligible after removing three author-excluded cases and NGC6926 (an upper limit in the later table). We retained the published velocity measurements and did not use their calibrated halo masses. vmax is not necessarily vflat; its radius and full baryonic profile are absent here. The source itself discusses selection and kinematic systematics.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Total stellar mass controlled | 27 | 0.0552 | [0.0132, 0.0901] | 0.0582 / 0.0482 dex |
| Total mass, bulge mass, spheroid size, velocity dispersion controlled | 26 | 0.0328 | [-0.0201, 0.0900] | 0.0533 / 0.0560 dex |

The 27-object coefficient corresponds to about 13.6% higher predicted vmax per factor-ten BH mass at fixed tabulated total stellar mass, within this descriptive linear model. It is not a force law.

Sample changes matter: the 26-object complete-covariate sample excludes the bulgeless NGC4395. On those SAME 26 objects, the mass-only baseline improves only from 0.0519 to 0.0490 dex on adding BH mass. Thus part of the apparent improvement in the 27-object fit comes from the extra object's leverage; the stronger covariate result is not solely a nested-model comparison on an unchanged 27-object set. On the fixed 26-object set, adding BH mass to the full structural baseline worsens cross-validated prediction.

Replacing 2019 BH values with the later compilation barely changes the simple mass-controlled coefficient. The declared inclination cut 30–80 degrees removes none of the eligible 27 objects, so it is NOT an additional robustness result. Missing spheroid sizes were not imputed. The all-matches fit is only a diagnostic and contains excluded/upper-limit entries; it is not the primary result.

Additional bulge/dispersion controls were added after seeing the first rotation fit and are explicitly exploratory. They were motivated by confounding, not used to select a favorable final specification. All fits and exclusions are retained in results.json.

### A non-horizon countermodel reproduces the scale of the residual

In a synthetic common-cause model, true stellar mass drives both BH mass and rotation, with NO BH-to-rotation force. For 28 synthetic objects per draw, assume latent log mass SD 0.55 dex, log BH=2m plus 0.45-dex noise, log v=0.25m plus 0.04-dex noise. Add an independent error to the observed stellar mass. At 0.2-dex mass error, the median fitted BH coefficient at fixed observed stellar mass is about 0.052, close to the real sample's 0.055. The precise simulated intervals are stored in continuation003/results.json.

This construction demonstrates non-identifiability: a residual correlation of this size need not require a BH force. It is not calibrated to the sample's true errors, selection or latent distribution and does not establish the actual cause. A proper errors-in-variables and selection model is still needed for the real rotation sample.

### Direct curvature computation closes another simple mechanism

Starting from the diagonal Schwarzschild–de Sitter metric, curvature.py constructs Christoffel symbols and Riemann components and contracts them exactly. It finds

    R = 4Λ,
    Ricci² = 4Λ²,
    Kretschmann = 48m²/r⁶ + 8Λ²/3,
    Weyl² = 48m²/r⁶,       m = GM/c².

Setting the mass-dependent tidal contribution equal to the vacuum contribution gives r=18^(1/6)(m/Λ)^(1/3). The mass acceleration there scales as c² m^(1/3) Λ^(2/3). It depends on source mass; it cannot by itself select a universal a0 proportional to c² sqrt(Λ), or fix 32π. This rules out this simple curvature-equality prescription within the specified metric, not all horizon mechanisms. 11 exact checks include the flat, Schwarzschild and de Sitter limits.
